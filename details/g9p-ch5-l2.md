# Sound Waves

Sound is made by vibrations. A guitar string vibrates and pushes on the air, and the air passes the vibration along to your ear.

## Key ideas
- **Sound** is a longitudinal wave of **compressions** and **rarefactions**; it needs a medium.
- Speed: about **343 m/s in air**, about 1500 m/s in water and about 5000 m/s in steel.
- **Pitch** depends on **frequency**; **loudness** depends on **amplitude** (measured in decibels, dB).
- Human hearing ranges from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.
- An **echo** is reflected sound. Distance to the wall = speed x time / 2.
- In air, sound travels about 340 m in one second.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Thunder.** Thunder is heard 3 s after lightning (speed of sound 340 m/s).
1. Light arrives almost instantly.
2. d = v t = 340 x 3 = 1020 m.

**Example 2: Echo.** An echo returns 2 s after a shout (speed of sound 340 m/s).
1. The sound travels to the cliff and back: 340 x 2 = 680 m.
2. The cliff is half of that: 340 m away.

**Example 3: Pitch.** A flute plays a higher note than a tuba.
1. A higher note means a higher frequency.
2. The flute produces sound waves with a higher frequency.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking sound travels in space.
- Thinking a louder sound travels faster.
- Using the full echo path as the distance to the wall.
