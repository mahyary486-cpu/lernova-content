# Plant diversity

Plant diversity is the large variety of plant types on Earth, which scientists sort into groups by how their bodies are built and how they reproduce.

## Key ideas

- All plants are made of cells with cell walls. Most plants have chloroplasts with chlorophyll, so they make their own food by photosynthesis. This is why plants are called producers.
- Plants first lived in water. Over a very long time, some of them moved onto land. To live on land, a plant needs a way to take in water, hold itself up and avoid drying out.
- Nonvascular plants have no true tubes for carrying water and food. Water simply moves from cell to cell. Because of this, they stay small, usually only a few centimeters tall, and live in damp, shady places. Mosses are the best-known example. They have no true roots. They use thin threads called rhizoids to hold on to the ground.
- Vascular plants have two kinds of tubes. Xylem carries water and minerals. Phloem carries sugar. These tubes let plants grow tall and live in drier places.
- Vascular plants are divided by how they reproduce:
  - Ferns make tiny spores, not seeds. They need moist conditions for reproduction. Their leaves are called fronds.
  - Gymnosperms make seeds that are not enclosed in a fruit. Most make seeds in cones. Pine and cedar are examples, and many have thin, needle-like leaves.
  - Angiosperms make seeds inside flowers, and the seeds develop inside fruits. About 90% of living plant species are angiosperms. Wheat, rose, apple and pomegranate are examples.
- Angiosperms are divided into monocots and dicots. A monocot seed has one cotyledon (seed leaf), the leaves have parallel veins, and the flower parts usually come in threes. Wheat and corn are monocots. A dicot seed has two cotyledons, the leaves have a net of veins, and the flower parts usually come in fours or fives. Bean and rose are dicots.

## Examples

1. Sorting a moss. A moss plant is 3 cm tall, has no flowers and no seeds, and grows on a wet rock. It has no xylem or phloem. Result: it is a nonvascular plant.
2. Sorting a pine tree. A pine tree is 20 m tall and has woody cones. Its seeds are bare on the cone scales. It has xylem and phloem. Result: it is a vascular plant and a gymnosperm.
3. Sorting a wheat plant. Wheat has narrow leaves with parallel veins. Its seed has one cotyledon. Wheat makes seeds inside grains, which are fruits. Result: it is an angiosperm and a monocot.
4. Sorting a bean plant. Bean leaves show a net of veins. The bean seed splits into two halves, which are two cotyledons. Result: it is an angiosperm and a dicot.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that all plants have flowers. Mosses, ferns and gymnosperms do not have flowers.
- Thinking that mosses have true roots. They have only rhizoids, which hold the plant but do not carry water through tubes.
- Thinking that a fern makes seeds. Ferns make spores.
- Mixing up monocots and dicots. Remember that mono means one and di means two, and this refers to the number of cotyledons.

## Remember

- Nonvascular plants have no tubes and stay small. Vascular plants have xylem and phloem.
- Ferns use spores. Gymnosperms make naked seeds. Angiosperms make seeds in fruits.
- Angiosperms are the biggest plant group and are either monocots or dicots.
