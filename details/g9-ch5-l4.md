# Factoring Trinomials and Common Factors

Factoring writes a polynomial as a product. It undoes multiplication.

## Step 1: Always look for a greatest common factor (GCF)
Find the largest number and the lowest power of each variable that divide every term, then pull it out.
6x^3 + 9x^2 = 3x^2(2x + 3).

## Factoring x^2 + bx + c
Find two numbers that **multiply to c** and **add to b**. Then x^2 + bx + c = (x + p)(x + q).
- If c is positive, p and q have the same sign (the sign of b).
- If c is negative, p and q have opposite signs.

## Factoring ax^2 + bx + c with a not equal to 1
Find two numbers that multiply to a*c and add to b. Split the middle term with them, then factor by grouping.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Factor x^2 + 7x + 12.
1. Need two numbers with product 12 and sum 7: 3 and 4.
2. Answer: (x + 3)(x + 4).

**Example 2.** Factor x^2 - 2x - 15.
1. Product -15 and sum -2: 3 and -5.
2. Answer: (x + 3)(x - 5).

**Example 3.** Factor 2x^2 + 7x + 3.
1. a*c = 6. Numbers with product 6 and sum 7: 1 and 6.
2. Split: 2x^2 + x + 6x + 3.
3. Group: x(2x + 1) + 3(2x + 1).
4. Answer: (2x + 1)(x + 3).

**Example 4.** Factor 3x^2 - 12x - 36 completely.
1. Pull out the GCF 3: 3(x^2 - 4x - 12).
2. Product -12 and sum -4: 2 and -6.
3. Answer: 3(x + 2)(x - 6).

## Check by multiplying
Multiply your factors back out. You should get the original polynomial.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the GCF, or leaving it out of the final answer.
- Choosing numbers with the right product but the wrong sum.
- Getting signs wrong. For x^2 - 5x + 6 the numbers are -2 and -3, so (x - 2)(x - 3).
- Stopping too early when a factor can still be factored.
