# Types of Mixtures

Salt water, milk and muddy water are all mixtures, but they behave differently because their particles have different sizes.

## Key ideas
- A **solution** is a uniform mixture of very small particles; it does not settle or scatter light.
- A **colloid** has medium-sized particles that do not settle but **scatter light** (the **Tyndall effect**).
- A **suspension** has large particles that **settle out** and can be filtered.
- Examples: solution (sugar water), colloid (milk, fog), suspension (muddy water).
- The parts of a mixture keep their own properties.
- Mixtures can be separated by **physical** methods.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Classifying.** Classify sugar water, milk and muddy water.
1. Sugar water is a solution.
2. Milk is a colloid.
3. Muddy water is a suspension.

**Example 2: Tyndall test.** A flashlight beam is visible in a liquid.
1. Particles scatter the light.
2. The liquid is a colloid, not a true solution.

**Example 3: Settling.** A mixture clears if left standing and leaves a layer at the bottom.
1. Large particles settle out.
2. It is a suspension.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a solution is always a liquid.
- Thinking colloids settle out.
- Thinking mixtures are chemically combined.
