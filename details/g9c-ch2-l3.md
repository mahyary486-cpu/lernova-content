# Electron Arrangement

Electrons are not spread randomly. They sit in layers, and the outer layer controls how an atom reacts with others.

## Key ideas
- Electrons are arranged in **shells** (energy levels) around the nucleus.
- Maximum electrons: **2 in the first shell, 8 in the second, 8 in the third** (for the first 20 elements).
- Fill the **lowest shell first**.
- **Valence electrons** are in the outer shell and control chemical behavior.
- Examples: sodium **2, 8, 1**; oxygen **2, 6**; chlorine **2, 8, 7**.
- A **full outer shell** makes an atom stable (noble gases).

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Sodium.** Sodium has 11 electrons.
1. Fill the first shell with 2, the second with 8.
2. 1 electron is left for the third shell: 2, 8, 1.

**Example 2: Valence electrons.** Oxygen has the arrangement 2, 6.
1. The last number is the outer shell.
2. Oxygen has 6 valence electrons.

**Example 3: Stability.** Why is neon unreactive?
1. Neon has 10 electrons: 2, 8.
2. Its outer shell is full, so it is stable.

## Common mistakes
- Putting more than 2 electrons in the first shell.
- Counting all electrons as valence electrons.
- Filling the third shell before the second is full.
