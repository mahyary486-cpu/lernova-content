# Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

Newton realized that the force that pulls an apple down is the same force that keeps the Moon in orbit.

## Key ideas
- **F = G m1 m2 / r^2**, with **G = 6.67 x 10^-11 N m^2/kg^2**.
- Gravity is always **attractive** and acts on both masses with **equal and opposite** forces.
- **Inverse-square law**: doubling the distance makes the force one quarter; tripling makes it one ninth.
- The force is proportional to **each mass**.
- The distance r is measured between the **centers** of the masses.
- Gravity between everyday objects is **very small**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Two masses.** 10 kg and 20 kg masses are 2 m apart.
1. F = G m1 m2 / r^2 = 6.67 x 10^-11 x 200 / 4.
2. F = 3.3 x 10^-9 N.

**Example 2: Changing the distance.** The force between two masses is 12 N. The distance triples.
1. The force changes by 1 / 3^2 = 1 / 9.
2. F = 12 / 9 = 1.33 N.

**Example 3: Doubling the masses.** The force is 12 N. Both masses are doubled.
1. The force is proportional to m1 x m2, so it is multiplied by 2 x 2 = 4.
2. F = 48 N.

## Common mistakes
- Using the distance from the surface instead of from the center.
- Forgetting to square the distance.
- Thinking the heavier object pulls with a larger force. The forces are equal.
