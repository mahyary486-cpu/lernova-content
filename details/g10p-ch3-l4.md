# Newton's Third Law and Weight

Forces always come from interactions between two objects. Newton's third law describes this pairing, and weight is the force of gravity that shows it most clearly.

## Key ideas
- **Third law:** if object A exerts a force on object B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
- The two forces of a pair act on different objects, so they never cancel each other in a free-body diagram.
- Equal forces do not mean equal accelerations. Each object accelerates by a = F / m for its own mass.
- **Mass** (kg) is the amount of matter and does not change with location. **Weight** is the gravitational force: W = mg, measured in newtons.
- On Earth g = 9.8 m/s^2. On the Moon g is about 1.6 m/s^2.
- The **normal force** is the contact force perpendicular to a surface. It is not always equal to the weight.
- In an elevator accelerating upward, N - mg = ma, so N = m(g + a). Accelerating downward, N = m(g - a). The scale reading equals N.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: weight on two worlds.** A 75 kg astronaut.
1. On Earth: W = 75 x 9.8 = 735 N.
2. On the Moon: W = 75 x 1.6 = 120 N.
3. Mass stays 75 kg in both places.

**Example 2: skaters pushing apart.** A 40 kg skater and a 60 kg skater push each other with 120 N on frictionless ice.
1. Third law: each feels 120 N, in opposite directions.
2. a(40 kg) = 120 / 40 = 3.0 m/s^2.
3. a(60 kg) = 120 / 60 = 2.0 m/s^2.

**Example 3: elevator scale.** A 60 kg person rides an elevator accelerating upward at 1.5 m/s^2.
1. N - mg = ma, so N = m(g + a).
2. N = 60 x (9.8 + 1.5) = 60 x 11.3 = 678 N.
3. If the elevator accelerates downward at 1.5 m/s^2, N = 60 x (9.8 - 1.5) = 498 N.

## Common mistakes
- Saying action and reaction cancel. They act on different objects.
- Thinking the larger object pushes harder in a collision. The forces are equal.
- Confusing mass and weight, or giving weight in kilograms.
- Assuming the normal force always equals mg. It changes with acceleration or slope.
