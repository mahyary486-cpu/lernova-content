# Pressure in gases and air pressure

Gases also make pressure, and the air around Earth presses on everything with a large force.

## Key ideas

- Gas particles move quickly in all directions and collide with the walls of their container. Millions of collisions every second make the gas pressure.
- If the same amount of gas is pushed into a smaller space, the particles hit the walls more often and the pressure rises.
- Earth is surrounded by a layer of air called the atmosphere. The weight of this air makes atmospheric pressure. At sea level it is about 101 325 Pa, which is close to 100 kPa. This value is called 1 atmosphere (1 atm).
- Atmospheric pressure acts in all directions. We do not feel it because the fluids inside our bodies push outward with the same pressure.
- Atmospheric pressure decreases as height increases, because there is less air above. On high mountains the pressure is low and breathing is harder.
- Torricelli measured air pressure with a tube of mercury turned upside down in a dish of mercury. The mercury column stays about 76 cm (760 mm) high at sea level. This tool is called a barometer. We can say the pressure is 76 cmHg or 760 mmHg.
- Boyle's law: for a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, pressure and volume are inversely related. P1 × V1 = P2 × V2.
- Everyday uses of air pressure include drinking through a straw, suction cups, syringes and bicycle pumps.

## Examples

**Example 1: Boyle's law.** A gas has a volume of 2 L at a pressure of 100 kPa. It is compressed to 0.5 L at the same temperature. Find the new pressure.
- Step 1: P1 × V1 = P2 × V2, so 100 × 2 = P2 × 0.5.
- Step 2: P2 = 200 / 0.5 = 400 kPa.
- Step 3: The volume became 4 times smaller, so the pressure became 4 times larger.

**Example 2: A weather balloon.** A balloon holds 3 L of gas at 100 kPa. It rises to a height where the pressure is 60 kPa. Find the new volume, assuming the temperature does not change.
- Step 1: 100 × 3 = 60 × V2.
- Step 2: V2 = 300 / 60 = 5 L.
- Step 3: The balloon expands because the outside pressure is lower.

**Example 3: Air force on a table.** A table top has an area of 1.5 m^2. Atmospheric pressure is 100 000 Pa. Find the force of the air on the top.
- Step 1: F = P × A = 100 000 × 1.5.
- Step 2: F = 150 000 N.
- Step 3: The table does not break because air also pushes on the underside with an almost equal force.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that air has no weight, or that air pressure only pushes downward. It acts in all directions.
- Using different units for the two pressures or volumes in Boyle's law. Both sides must use the same units.
- Believing that the mercury in a barometer is held up by nothing. The air pressure on the dish holds the column up.
- Forgetting that Boyle's law only works when the temperature and the amount of gas stay constant.

## Remember

- Atmospheric pressure at sea level ≈ 101 325 Pa ≈ 76 cmHg ≈ 1 atm.
- Air pressure falls as you go higher.
- Boyle's law: P1 × V1 = P2 × V2.
