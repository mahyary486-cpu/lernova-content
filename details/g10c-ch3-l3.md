# Covalent Bonds and Lewis Structures

A **covalent bond** forms when two atoms share a pair of electrons. It usually occurs between nonmetals, and the result is a **molecule**.

## Key ideas
- A **single bond** shares 1 pair, a **double bond** 2 pairs, a **triple bond** 3 pairs. More shared pairs mean a shorter and stronger bond. Typical energies: C-C 347 kJ/mol, C=C 614 kJ/mol, C-triple-C 839 kJ/mol.
- **Lone pairs** are valence pairs on one atom that are not shared.
- Steps to draw a Lewis structure:
  1. Add up all valence electrons (add 1 for each negative charge, subtract 1 for each positive charge).
  2. Put the least electronegative atom (not H) in the center and connect atoms with single bonds.
  3. Fill the octets of the outer atoms with lone pairs.
  4. Put any leftover electrons on the central atom.
  5. If the central atom lacks an octet, turn lone pairs into double or triple bonds.
- **Formal charge** = valence electrons - nonbonding electrons - number of bonds. The best structure has formal charges close to zero.
- Resonance occurs when more than one valid Lewis structure can be drawn, as in NO3-.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Lewis structure of CO2.**
1. Electrons: 4 + 2 x 6 = 16.
2. Place C in the center: O-C-O uses 4; 12 remain.
3. Giving each O three lone pairs leaves C with only 4 electrons, so make two double bonds: O=C=O. Each O has 2 lone pairs.

**Example 2: Valence electrons in NO3-.**
1. N: 5. Three O: 3 x 6 = 18. Charge: +1.
2. Total = 5 + 18 + 1 = 24 electrons.

**Example 3: Energy to break 3.0 mol of H-H bonds (436 kJ/mol).**
1. Energy = 3.0 mol x 436 kJ/mol.
2. Energy = 1308 kJ (about 1.3 x 10^3 kJ).

**Example 4: Formal charge on N in NH4+.**
1. N has 5 valence electrons, 0 nonbonding electrons, 4 bonds.
2. Formal charge = 5 - 0 - 4 = +1.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to adjust the electron count for an ion.
- Making the hydrogen atom the central atom. Hydrogen forms only one bond.
- Giving hydrogen an octet. It holds only 2 electrons.
