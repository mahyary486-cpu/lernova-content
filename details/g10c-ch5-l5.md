# Limiting Reactants and Percent Yield

Reactions rarely start with the exact ratio shown in the equation. One reactant runs out first and stops the reaction. Also, real experiments produce less product than calculated. This lesson covers both ideas.

## Key ideas
- The limiting reactant (limiting reagent) is the reactant that is completely used up first. It controls the amount of product.
- The excess reactant is left over when the reaction ends.
- Theoretical yield is the maximum product calculated from the limiting reactant.
- Actual yield is the amount obtained in the experiment.
- Percent yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) x 100.
- Method: find the product mass from each reactant. The smaller answer comes from the limiting reactant.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Limiting reactant.** For 2Na + Cl2 -> 2NaCl, 4.60 g Na (22.99 g/mol) and 5.00 g Cl2 (70.90 g/mol) are mixed. Find the mass of NaCl (58.44 g/mol).
1. Moles Na = 4.60 / 22.99 = 0.200 mol. Moles Cl2 = 5.00 / 70.90 = 0.0705 mol.
2. NaCl from Na: 0.200 mol (2:2) = 0.200 mol. NaCl from Cl2: 0.0705 x 2 = 0.141 mol.
3. Cl2 gives less, so Cl2 is limiting.
4. Mass NaCl = 0.141 x 58.44 = 8.24 g.

**Example 2: Percent yield.** Theoretical yield is 20.15 g MgO and the student collects 17.2 g.
1. Percent yield = 17.2 / 20.15 x 100.
2. Percent yield = 85.4 percent.

**Example 3: Finding theoretical yield.** The actual yield is 12.6 g and the percent yield is 70.0 percent.
1. Theoretical = actual / (percent yield / 100).
2. Theoretical = 12.6 / 0.700 = 18.0 g.

## Why yield is below 100 percent
Reactants may be impure, side reactions may occur, and some product is lost during transfer, filtering or drying.

## Common mistakes
- Picking the reactant with the smaller mass as limiting. Compare moles after applying the ratio.
- Calculating percent yield as theoretical divided by actual.
- Using the excess reactant to find the product amount.
- Expecting a percent yield above 100 percent, which usually signals a wet or impure product.
