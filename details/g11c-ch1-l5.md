# Empirical and Molecular Formulas

Hydrogen peroxide H2O2 and water H2O look similar but differ in their formulas. Experiments on composition show the formula.

## Key ideas
- **Empirical formula**: simplest whole-number ratio.
- **Molecular formula** = empirical formula x n.
- **n = molar mass / empirical formula mass**.
- Steps: **percent -> grams -> moles -> divide by smallest -> whole numbers**.
- Typical ratios: 1.5 -> multiply by 2; 1.33 -> multiply by 3.
- A **hydrate** contains water of crystallization.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Empirical formula.** A compound is 40% C, 6.7% H and 53.3% O.
1. Moles: C = 3.33, H = 6.7, O = 3.33.
2. Dividing by 3.33 gives 1 : 2 : 1, so CH2O.

**Example 2: Molecular formula.** The molar mass is 180 g/mol and the empirical mass is 30.
1. n = 180 / 30 = 6.
2. Molecular formula = C6H12O6.

**Example 3: Hydrate.** Find the percent water in CuSO4 . 5H2O (M = 250 g/mol, water 90 g).
1. Water mass in one mole = 5 x 18 = 90 g.
2. 90 / 250 x 100% = 36%.

## Common mistakes
- Not converting percent to moles.
- Rounding 1.5 to 1 or 2 instead of doubling.
- Confusing the empirical and molecular formulas.
