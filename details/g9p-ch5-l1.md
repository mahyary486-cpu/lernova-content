# Wave Properties

When a stone is dropped into a pond, circles of ripples spread out from the point where it fell. A floating leaf does not travel along with the waves; it only moves up and down. This shows the main idea of this lesson: a wave carries energy from one place to another, but it does not carry matter.

## Key ideas

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy without transferring matter. Most waves travel through a medium, such as water, air or a rope, and the particles of the medium only move back and forth around their rest position.

There are two basic types. In a transverse wave, the particles of the medium move at right angles to the direction in which the wave travels, like a rope shaken up and down. Water surface waves and light are examples. In a longitudinal wave, the particles move back and forth along the same direction as the wave, as in a stretched spring that is pushed and pulled. Sound is a longitudinal wave.

Waves are described by several measurable quantities. The wavelength (lambda) is the distance between two neighboring crests, or between any two matching points. The amplitude is the maximum displacement of a particle from its rest position, and it is related to the energy of the wave. The frequency (f) is the number of complete waves that pass a point in one second, measured in hertz (Hz). The period (T) is the time for one complete wave, and it is related to the frequency by T = 1 / f.

These quantities are linked by the wave equation: v = f x lambda, where v is the wave speed. The speed of a wave depends on the medium, not on the frequency or the amplitude. In a given medium, if the frequency increases, the wavelength becomes shorter so that the speed stays the same.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Wave speed.** A wave has a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m. What is its speed?
1. Use v = f x lambda.
2. Substitute: v = 5 x 2.
3. The speed is 10 m/s.

**Example 2: Finding the wavelength.** A wave travels at 20 m/s and has a frequency of 4 Hz. What is its wavelength?
1. Rearrange the wave equation: lambda = v / f.
2. Substitute: lambda = 20 / 4.
3. The wavelength is 5 m.

**Example 3: Frequency from the period.** A wave has a period of 0.25 s. What is its frequency?
1. Use f = 1 / T.
2. Substitute: f = 1 / 0.25.
3. The frequency is 4 Hz, which means 4 complete waves pass each second.

## Common mistakes

Many students think that a wave carries matter from one place to another, but only energy travels. Another mistake is to believe that a higher frequency makes a wave faster in the same medium; the speed is set by the medium, and a higher frequency gives a shorter wavelength. A third mistake is mixing up amplitude, which is a height, with wavelength, which is a length along the direction of travel.
