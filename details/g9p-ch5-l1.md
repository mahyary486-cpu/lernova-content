# Wave Properties

Drop a stone into a pond and waves spread out, but the water itself only moves up and down. The waves carry energy, not water.

## Key ideas
- A **wave** transfers energy without transferring matter.
- **Wavelength (lambda)**: distance between two neighboring crests. **Amplitude**: maximum displacement from rest.
- **Frequency (f)**: waves per second, in hertz (Hz). **Period** T = 1 / f.
- **Wave speed**: **v = f x lambda**.
- **Transverse** waves move the medium at right angles to the wave direction; **longitudinal** waves move it back and forth along the direction.
- The speed of a wave depends on the **medium**, not on frequency or amplitude.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Wave speed.** A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m.
1. v = f x lambda = 5 x 2.
2. v = 10 m/s.

**Example 2: Wavelength.** A wave travels at 20 m/s with frequency 4 Hz.
1. lambda = v / f = 20 / 4.
2. lambda = 5 m.

**Example 3: Frequency from period.** A wave has a period of 0.25 s.
1. f = 1 / T = 1 / 0.25.
2. f = 4 Hz.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking waves carry matter from one place to another.
- Thinking a higher frequency makes a wave faster in the same medium.
- Mixing amplitude with wavelength.
