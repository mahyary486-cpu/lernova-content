# Simplifying Radicals

A radical expression such as sqrt(18) asks for the number that, multiplied by itself, gives 18. Many radicals are not whole numbers, but they can often be written in a neater form called simplest radical form.

## Key ideas
- sqrt(a) means the nonnegative square root of a. For example, sqrt(49) = 7.
- A perfect square is the square of a whole number: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144.
- **Product rule:** sqrt(a * b) = sqrt(a) * sqrt(b) for a, b >= 0.
- **Quotient rule:** sqrt(a / b) = sqrt(a) / sqrt(b) for a >= 0, b > 0.
- A radical is in simplest form when the number under the root has no perfect square factor other than 1, there is no fraction under the root, and no radical is left in a denominator.

## Simplifying a square root
Find the largest perfect square factor of the number under the root, then take its root.

**Example 1.** Simplify sqrt(72).
1. Factor 72 = 36 * 2, where 36 is the largest perfect square factor.
2. sqrt(72) = sqrt(36) * sqrt(2).
3. sqrt(72) = 6 sqrt(2).

**Example 2.** Simplify sqrt(50x^3), with x >= 0.
1. Write 50x^3 = 25 * x^2 * 2x.
2. sqrt(50x^3) = sqrt(25) * sqrt(x^2) * sqrt(2x).
3. Result: 5x sqrt(2x).

For variables, each pair of identical factors comes out of the root as one factor.

## Rationalizing the denominator
We do not leave a radical in the denominator. Multiply the top and bottom by the radical.

**Example 3.** Simplify 6 / sqrt(3).
1. Multiply by sqrt(3)/sqrt(3), which equals 1.
2. 6 sqrt(3) / (sqrt(3) * sqrt(3)) = 6 sqrt(3) / 3.
3. Divide: 2 sqrt(3).

**Example 4.** Simplify sqrt(5/16).
1. Use the quotient rule: sqrt(5) / sqrt(16).
2. sqrt(16) = 4, so the answer is sqrt(5) / 4.

## Rational and irrational numbers
The square root of a whole number that is not a perfect square is irrational: its decimal never ends and never repeats. So sqrt(2) is irrational, but sqrt(81) = 9 is rational.

## Common mistakes
- Stopping too early: sqrt(72) = 2 sqrt(18) is true but not simplest, because 18 still has the factor 9.
- Writing sqrt(a + b) = sqrt(a) + sqrt(b). The product rule works for multiplication only.
- Pulling out a whole factor instead of its root: sqrt(36 * 2) is 6 sqrt(2), not 36 sqrt(2).
- Forgetting to simplify the answer after rationalizing.
