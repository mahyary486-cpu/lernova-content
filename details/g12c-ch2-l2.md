# Balancing Redox Equations

The half-reaction method works for every redox equation, however complicated.

## Key ideas
- **Separate** into half-reactions.
- Balance **atoms (not O, H)**, then **O with H2O**, then **H with H+**.
- Balance **charge** with **electrons**.
- Make the **electrons equal**, then add.
- **Basic solution**: add OH- to neutralize H+.
- Always check **atoms and charge**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Simple redox.** Balance Fe^2+ + Ce^4+ -> Fe^3+ + Ce^3+.
1. Fe^2+ -> Fe^3+ + e- and Ce^4+ + e- -> Ce^3+.
2. The electrons are equal, so add: Fe^2+ + Ce^4+ -> Fe^3+ + Ce^3+.

**Example 2: Permanganate.** Balance MnO4- + 5 Fe^2+ (acid).
1. MnO4- + 8 H+ + 5 e- -> Mn^2+ + 4 H2O and 5 (Fe^2+ -> Fe^3+ + e-).
2. MnO4- + 8 H+ + 5 Fe^2+ -> Mn^2+ + 4 H2O + 5 Fe^3+.

**Example 3: Check.** Check the charge of the last equation.
1. Left: -1 + 8 + 10 = +17.
2. Right: +2 + 15 = +17. It is balanced.

## Common mistakes
- Balancing electrons last without equalizing them.
- Forgetting to add H+ or OH- depending on the medium.
- Not checking the charge.
