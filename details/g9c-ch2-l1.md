# Parts of the Atom

Everything is made of atoms, and atoms are made of even smaller particles. The way these particles are arranged decides the element and its chemistry.

## Key ideas
- The **nucleus** is at the center and contains **protons** (+) and **neutrons** (no charge).
- **Electrons** (-) move around the nucleus in the electron cloud.
- Protons and neutrons have almost equal mass; electrons have very little mass.
- A **neutral atom** has equal numbers of protons and electrons.
- Most of an atom is empty space; the nucleus holds almost all of the mass.
- Models changed with evidence: Dalton, Thomson, Rutherford (nucleus), Bohr (energy levels).

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Charges.** An atom has 6 protons and 6 electrons.
1. Charge = +6 and -6.
2. The charges cancel, so the atom is neutral.

**Example 2: Which particle?.** Which particle is found in the nucleus and has no charge?
1. Protons are positive and electrons are outside the nucleus.
2. Neutrons are in the nucleus and have no charge.

**Example 3: Where is the mass?.** Where is most of the mass of an atom?
1. Electrons have very little mass.
2. Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus, so the nucleus holds almost all the mass.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking electrons are in the nucleus.
- Thinking the atom is a solid ball.
- Thinking neutrons carry a charge.
