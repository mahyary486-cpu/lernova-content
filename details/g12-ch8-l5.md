# Rates of Change and the Idea of the Derivative

## Average rate of change
The average rate of change of f from x = a to x = a + h is the slope of the **secant line** through two points on the graph:

**(f(a + h) - f(a)) / h**

For a position function this is average velocity: change in position divided by change in time.

## Instantaneous rate of change
Let h get closer and closer to 0. The secant lines approach the **tangent line** at x = a. The limit of the average rates is the **instantaneous rate of change**, which is the slope of the tangent line:

**f'(a) = lim (h to 0) (f(a + h) - f(a)) / h**

This number is called the **derivative** of f at a. The derivative function is f'(x) = lim (h to 0) (f(x + h) - f(x)) / h.

## Meaning
- Slope of the tangent line to the curve at that point.
- Instantaneous velocity, when f is position.
- A positive derivative means f is increasing there; negative means decreasing; zero means a flat tangent.

## Worked examples
1. A ball's height is s(t) = 5t^2 meters. Find the average velocity from t = 1 to t = 3.
   1. s(1) = 5 and s(3) = 45.
   2. (45 - 5) / (3 - 1) = 20 meters per second.
2. Find the derivative of f(x) = x^2 at x = 3.
   1. f(3 + h) - f(3) = (9 + 6h + h^2) - 9 = 6h + h^2.
   2. Divide by h: 6 + h.
   3. As h approaches 0, the limit is 6, so f'(3) = 6.
3. Find f'(x) for f(x) = x^2.
   1. ((x + h)^2 - x^2) / h = (2xh + h^2) / h = 2x + h.
   2. The limit is 2x, so f'(x) = 2x.
4. Write the tangent line to y = x^2 at x = 3.
   1. The point is (3, 9) and the slope is 6.
   2. y - 9 = 6(x - 3), so y = 6x - 9.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing the average rate (a secant slope over an interval) with the instantaneous rate (a limit).
- Substituting h = 0 before simplifying, which gives 0/0.
- Forgetting to expand (x + h)^2 as x^2 + 2xh + h^2.
- Forgetting units: a rate of change has units of output per unit of input.
