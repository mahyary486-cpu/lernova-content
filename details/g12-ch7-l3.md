# Hyperbolas

A hyperbola is the set of points whose distances to two fixed foci differ by a constant, 2a. It has two separate branches and two asymptotes that the branches approach.

## Key ideas
For a hyperbola centered at (h, k):
- Horizontal transverse axis: (x - h)^2/a^2 - (y - k)^2/b^2 = 1. The branches open left and right.
- Vertical transverse axis: (y - k)^2/a^2 - (x - h)^2/b^2 = 1. The branches open up and down.
- The positive term tells you the direction. Here a^2 is always the denominator of the positive term.
- Vertices are a units from the center along the transverse axis. Foci are c units away, where c^2 = a^2 + b^2.
- Asymptotes: for a horizontal axis, y - k = +-(b/a)(x - h). For a vertical axis, y - k = +-(a/b)(x - h).
- Eccentricity e = c/a is greater than 1.

A quick way to sketch it: draw a rectangle with half-width a and half-height b around the center, draw its diagonals as asymptotes, then draw the branches from the vertices.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Analyze x^2/9 - y^2/16 = 1.**
1. a^2 = 9, b^2 = 16, so a = 3 and b = 4. The branches open left and right.
2. c^2 = 9 + 16 = 25, so c = 5.
3. Vertices (+-3, 0), foci (+-5, 0), asymptotes y = +-(4/3)x.

**Example 2. Write the hyperbola with vertices (+-2, 0) and foci (+-sqrt(13), 0).**
1. a = 2 and c = sqrt(13).
2. b^2 = c^2 - a^2 = 13 - 4 = 9.
3. Equation: x^2/4 - y^2/9 = 1.

**Example 3. Convert 9x^2 - 16y^2 - 36x - 32y - 124 = 0.**
1. Group: 9(x^2 - 4x) - 16(y^2 + 2y) = 124.
2. Complete squares: 9(x - 2)^2 - 36 - 16(y + 1)^2 + 16 = 124, so 9(x - 2)^2 - 16(y + 1)^2 = 144.
3. Divide by 144: (x - 2)^2/16 - (y + 1)^2/9 = 1.
4. Center (2, -1), a = 4, b = 3, c = 5. Foci (7, -1) and (-3, -1).

## Common mistakes
- Using c^2 = a^2 - b^2, which is the ellipse rule.
- Assuming the larger denominator is a^2. In a hyperbola it is the positive term that gives a^2.
- Writing the asymptote slope as a/b for a horizontal hyperbola. It is b/a.
- Forgetting that completing the square with a coefficient outside the bracket multiplies the added number.
