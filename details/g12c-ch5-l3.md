# Fission, Fusion and Nuclear Energy

A single gram of uranium-235 releases about as much energy as three tonnes of coal.

## Key ideas
- **Fission**: heavy nucleus + neutron -> two lighter nuclei + **neutrons** + energy.
- **Control rods** absorb neutrons; **moderator** slows them.
- **Fusion**: light nuclei join; needs about **10^8 K**.
- Energy comes from the **mass defect**: **E = delta m c^2**.
- **1 u = 931.5 MeV**.
- Binding energy per nucleon peaks at **iron-56**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mass-energy.** A reaction has a mass defect of 0.20 u. How much energy is released?
1. E = 0.20 x 931.5 MeV.
2. E = 186 MeV.

**Example 2: Conservation.** In 235-U + n -> 141-Ba + 92-Kr + x n, find x.
1. A: 235 + 1 = 236 = 141 + 92 + x.
2. x = 3 neutrons.

**Example 3: Reactor control.** What happens to the reaction rate when control rods are inserted?
1. The rods absorb neutrons.
2. Fewer fissions occur, so the rate falls.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing fission with fusion.
- Thinking control rods slow neutrons (that is the moderator).
- Forgetting that fusion needs very high temperatures.
