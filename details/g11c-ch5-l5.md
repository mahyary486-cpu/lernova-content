# Le Chatelier's Principle and Equilibrium Calculations

Chemists use Le Chatelier's principle to get the maximum yield in industrial processes.

## Key ideas
- **Adding reactant** or **removing product**: shifts **right**.
- **Higher pressure**: shifts to the side with **fewer gas moles**.
- **Higher temperature**: favors the **endothermic** direction.
- A **catalyst** does **not** shift the equilibrium.
- **ICE table**: Initial, Change, Equilibrium.
- **Haber process**: high P, moderate T, catalyst.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Concentration change.** More N2 is added to N2 + 3 H2 <-> 2 NH3 at equilibrium.
1. The system reduces the stress by using up N2.
2. The equilibrium shifts to the right.

**Example 2: Pressure.** Pressure is increased for N2 + 3 H2 <-> 2 NH3.
1. Reactants have 4 moles of gas and products 2.
2. The equilibrium shifts right, toward fewer moles.

**Example 3: ICE table.** H2 + I2 <-> 2 HI with K = 4. Start: [H2] = [I2] = 1.0 M, [HI] = 0. Let x react.
1. K = (2x)^2 / ((1 - x)^2) = 4, so 2x / (1 - x) = 2.
2. 2x = 2 - 2x, so x = 0.5; [H2] = [I2] = 0.5 M and [HI] = 1.0 M.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a catalyst changes the equilibrium position.
- Forgetting to count only gas moles for pressure changes.
- Thinking K changes when concentrations change.
