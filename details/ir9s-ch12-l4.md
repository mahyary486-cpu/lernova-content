# Reproduction in plants

Reproduction in plants is the way plants make new plants, either by combining sex cells or by growing a new plant from a part of an old one.

## Key ideas

- Sexual reproduction needs two sex cells, male and female, which join in fertilization. The offspring get genes from both parents, so they differ from each other and from the parents. This variety helps a species survive changes in the environment.
- Pollination is the movement of pollen from an anther to a stigma.
  - Self-pollination happens within the same flower or the same plant.
  - Cross-pollination happens between flowers of different plants of the same species. The carriers can be wind, insects, birds, bats or water.
- Fertilization follows. A pollen grain on the stigma grows a thin pollen tube down the style to the ovule. A male sex cell moves along the tube and joins the egg cell. The fertilized egg becomes the embryo in the seed.
- Seed dispersal moves seeds away from the parent so the young plants do not crowd each other. Wind carries light or winged seeds. Water carries seeds that float, such as coconuts. Animals carry seeds that stick to fur or are eaten in fruits. Some pods dry and burst open and throw the seeds.
- Germination is the start of growth of a seed. A seed needs water, oxygen and a suitable temperature. Light is usually not needed at first. The root comes out first, then the shoot grows upward.
- Asexual reproduction needs one parent and no fertilization. The offspring are clones: they have the same genes as the parent.
  - Runners: strawberries send out horizontal stems that form new plants.
  - Tubers: a potato has buds ("eyes") that can grow into new plants.
  - Bulbs: onion and tulip bulbs make new bulbs.
  - Cuttings and grafting: a piece of stem planted in soil can grow roots. Gardeners use this for roses and fruit trees.
- Ferns and mosses make spores. A spore is a tiny cell that can grow into a new plant without being fertilized first.

## Examples

1. Cuttings. A gardener cuts a stem of a rose that makes red flowers and plants it in moist soil. It grows roots. The new rose is a clone, so it will also make red flowers. A rose grown from a seed could make a different color.
2. Germination test. A student puts 20 bean seeds in four dishes. Dish A has wet paper at room temperature. Dish B has dry paper at room temperature. Dish C has wet paper in a refrigerator. Dish D has seeds covered by water with no air. Seeds in dish A germinate. The others do not, because B lacks water, C lacks a warm enough temperature, and D lacks oxygen. If 17 of the 20 seeds in dish A germinate, the rate is 17 / 20 × 100 = 85%.
3. Choosing dispersal. A dandelion fruit has a feathery top. It is carried by wind. A burdock fruit has hooks. It is carried on animal fur.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing pollination with fertilization. Pollination moves pollen to the stigma. Fertilization is the joining of sex cells inside the ovule.
- Thinking that asexual offspring are different from the parent. They are identical copies.
- Thinking that seeds need light to germinate. Most need water, oxygen and warmth.
- Thinking that wind-pollinated plants need bright petals. They usually do not have any.

## Remember

- Sexual reproduction makes variety. Asexual reproduction makes clones.
- Pollination -> fertilization -> seed -> dispersal -> germination.
- Seeds need water, oxygen and a suitable temperature to germinate.
