# The pH Scale

A small change in pH is a big change in acidity. This is why a pH of 3 is much more acidic than a pH of 5.

## Key ideas
- **pH** below 7 is **acidic**, 7 is **neutral**, above 7 is **basic**.
- pH = -log [H+]; each pH step is a **factor of 10** in [H+].
- pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4 and 100 times more than pH 5.
- In pure water at 25 C: [H+] = [OH-] = 1 x 10^-7 mol/L.
- **pH + pOH = 14** at 25 C.
- Examples: lemon juice 2, pure water 7, baking soda 9, ammonia 11.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Comparing.** How much more acidic is pH 2 than pH 5?
1. The difference is 3 pH units.
2. 10 x 10 x 10 = 1000 times more acidic.

**Example 2: Finding pH.** [H+] = 1 x 10^-5 mol/L. What is the pH?
1. pH = -log(1 x 10^-5).
2. pH = 5.

**Example 3: pH from pOH.** [OH-] = 1 x 10^-3 mol/L.
1. pOH = 3.
2. pH = 14 - 3 = 11.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking pH 4 is twice as acidic as pH 8.
- Forgetting that lower pH means more acidic.
- Treating the pH scale as linear.
