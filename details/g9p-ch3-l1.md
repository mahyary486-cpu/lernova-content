# What Is a Force?

Every change in motion is caused by a force. To predict what happens we add up all the forces acting on an object.

## Key ideas
- A **force** is a push or a pull. It is a vector with size and direction.
- The SI unit is the **newton (N)**: 1 N = 1 kg x m/s^2.
- The **net force** is the combination of all forces on an object.
- Forces in the same direction add; forces in opposite directions subtract.
- **Balanced forces** give zero net force. **Unbalanced forces** give a net force and cause acceleration.
- A **free-body diagram** draws one object with an arrow for each force on it.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Opposite forces.** A box is pushed 30 N to the right and 10 N to the left.
1. Opposite directions subtract: 30 - 10 = 20 N.
2. Net force = 20 N to the right.

**Example 2: Balanced forces.** Two students pull a rope with 15 N each in opposite directions.
1. 15 N right and 15 N left give 15 - 15 = 0.
2. The forces are balanced and the rope does not accelerate.

**Example 3: Forces in a line.** A book has 12 N up and 5 N down acting on it.
1. Subtract: 12 - 5 = 7 N.
2. Net force = 7 N upward.

## Common mistakes
- Adding forces without paying attention to direction.
- Thinking balanced forces mean there are no forces.
- Thinking an object needs a net force to keep moving at constant velocity.
