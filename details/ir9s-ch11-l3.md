# Adaptation of animals to their environment

An adaptation is a feature of an animal's body or behavior that helps it survive and reproduce in the place where it lives.

## Key ideas

- The place where an animal lives is its habitat. Each habitat has its own conditions, such as temperature, water, food and predators. Animals need features that suit these conditions.
- There are three kinds of adaptation. **Structural** adaptations are body parts, such as a thick coat, sharp claws or a long beak. **Behavioral** adaptations are actions, such as migration, hibernation or hunting in groups. **Physiological** adaptations are body processes, such as making very little urine to save water.
- Adaptations come from natural selection. In every group of animals, individuals are slightly different. Those with helpful features survive longer and have more young. Their young inherit these features. After many generations, most animals in the group have the helpful feature. This process is slow and takes a very long time. An individual animal does not choose to change its body.
- **Camouflage** helps an animal hide from predators or prey by matching its surroundings, for example a green insect on a leaf.
- **Cold habitats.** Polar bears have thick fur and a thick layer of fat under the skin that keep heat in. Their white fur helps them hide in snow.
- **Desert habitats.** The camel has wide feet that do not sink in sand, long eyelashes that keep out sand, and it can go many days without drinking. Many desert animals are active at night when it is cooler.
- **Water habitats.** Fish have streamlined bodies, fins and gills. Ducks have webbed feet that push against the water.
- **Seasonal behavior.** In winter some animals hibernate, which means they sleep deeply and use very little energy. Some birds migrate to warmer places.

## Examples

**Example 1: The camel in the Iranian desert.** The Iranian desert is hot and dry, with little water. The camel stores fat in its hump, which gives energy when food is rare. Its wide, soft feet spread its weight on loose sand. It loses little water in sweat and urine. These features together let it live in a place where many other animals cannot.

**Example 2: The arctic hare.** The hare lives in a cold place with snow in winter. Its fur turns white in winter and brown in summer. In winter it is hidden against snow, and in summer against soil and plants. Hares that blended in were not caught by predators, so they had more young. Over many generations, this change in color became common.

**Example 3: Brown bear hibernation.** In Iran's northern forests, brown bears eat much food in autumn and store it as fat. In winter, when food is hard to find, they stay in a den and sleep for months. Their body uses the stored fat slowly. This behavior helps them survive the cold season.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that an animal changes its body because it needs to. Adaptations appear through natural selection over many generations, not in one lifetime.
- Thinking an adaptation is always a body part. Behaviors such as migration are adaptations too.
- Believing that adapted animals are "perfect". An animal is adapted to its own habitat only, and may not survive if the habitat changes quickly.
- Mixing up camouflage with changing color on purpose. Most animals cannot change color; the color is inherited.

## Remember

- An adaptation helps an animal survive and reproduce in its habitat.
- Adaptations can be structural, behavioral or physiological.
- Natural selection works slowly, over many generations.
