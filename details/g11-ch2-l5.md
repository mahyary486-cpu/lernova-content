# Complex Numbers

## The imaginary unit
Real numbers cannot solve x^2 = -1. We define the **imaginary unit i** by **i^2 = -1**, so i = sqrt(-1).
- sqrt(-9) = sqrt(9) * i = **3i**
- sqrt(-16) = 4i

## Complex numbers
A **complex number** has the form **a + bi**, where a and b are real. a is the real part and b is the imaginary part. Every real number is also a complex number (with b = 0).

## Powers of i
The powers repeat in a cycle of 4: i^1 = i, i^2 = -1, i^3 = -i, i^4 = 1.
To find i^n, divide n by 4 and use the remainder. For example i^7: 7 = 4 + 3, so i^7 = i^3 = **-i**.

## Adding and subtracting
Combine real parts and imaginary parts separately.
(2 + 3i) + (4 - 5i) = **6 - 2i**.

## Multiplying
Use FOIL and replace i^2 with -1.
(2 + 3i)(1 - i) = 2 - 2i + 3i - 3i^2 = 2 + i + 3 = **5 + i**.

## Conjugates and dividing
The **conjugate** of a + bi is a - bi. The product (a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2 is always a real number.
- The conjugate of 4 - 7i is **4 + 7i**.

To divide, multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom.
(1 + 2i) / (3 - i): multiply by (3 + i) / (3 + i).
Top: (1 + 2i)(3 + i) = 3 + i + 6i + 2i^2 = 1 + 7i. Bottom: 9 + 1 = 10.
Answer: **(1 + 7i) / 10**.

## Solving quadratics with complex solutions
x^2 - 2x + 10 = 0. D = 4 - 40 = -36, so x = (2 ± 6i) / 2 = **1 ± 3i**.
Complex solutions of a quadratic with real coefficients come in conjugate pairs.

## Common mistakes
- Saying i^2 = 1. It is -1.
- Leaving i in a denominator. Multiply by the conjugate.
- Forgetting to change i^2 into -1 after multiplying.
- Mixing real and imaginary parts when adding.
