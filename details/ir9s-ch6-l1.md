# Continental drift and Wegener's theory

Continental drift is the idea that the continents were once joined in one large landmass and slowly moved apart to their present positions.

## Key ideas
- In 1912 the German scientist Alfred Wegener proposed that about 200 to 300 million years ago all continents formed one supercontinent called Pangaea. A giant ocean, Panthalassa, surrounded it.
- Pangaea slowly broke into pieces. These pieces, the continents, drifted over millions of years to where they are now. They are still moving today, by a few centimeters each year.
- Wegener gathered several kinds of evidence:
  - Fit of coastlines: the east coast of South America and the west coast of Africa fit together like puzzle pieces, especially if you use the edge of the continental shelf.
  - Fossils: the fossil of the small reptile Mesosaurus is found only in South America and southern Africa. The fossil plant Glossopteris is found in South America, Africa, India, Australia and Antarctica. These living things could not have crossed a wide ocean.
  - Rocks and mountains: rock layers and mountain chains match across oceans. For example, the Appalachian Mountains in North America match mountains in Scotland and Scandinavia.
  - Climate clues: glacier scratches are found in warm places such as India and Africa, and coal from tropical forests is found in cold Antarctica. This shows that these lands were once in different places on Earth.
- Most scientists rejected the theory at first. Wegener could not explain what force was strong enough to push continents through solid ocean floor. His idea was accepted only after new evidence from the ocean floor appeared in the 1950s and 1960s.

## Examples
- Example 1, fossil evidence: Glossopteris seeds are heavy and could not be carried across thousands of kilometers of sea by wind. Finding the same plant on five continents is best explained if these lands were once touching.
- Example 2, climate evidence: Coal forms from the remains of large swampy forests that grow in warm, wet places. Coal exists in Antarctica, where it is now covered by ice. Therefore Antarctica once lay much closer to the equator.
- Example 3, a drift calculation: Suppose two continents move apart by 3 cm each year. In 1 million years the distance grows by 3 × 1,000,000 = 3,000,000 cm. Since 100,000 cm = 1 km, this is 30 km. In 100 million years the distance grows by 3,000 km. Small yearly movement becomes very large over geological time.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the continents moved quickly. They move about as fast as your fingernails grow.
- Saying Wegener explained why continents move. He gave strong evidence that they moved, but his explanation of the force was wrong.
- Believing Pangaea was the first or only supercontinent. It is only the most recent one that is well known.
- Thinking one kind of evidence is enough. The theory is strong because fossils, rocks, climate and coastlines all agree.

## Remember
- Pangaea was a single supercontinent that broke apart about 200 million years ago.
- Evidence for drift: coastline fit, matching fossils, matching rocks and mountains, and climate clues.
- The theory was accepted later, when scientists found a mechanism: moving plates.
