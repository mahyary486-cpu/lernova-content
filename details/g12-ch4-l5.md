# Graphs of Tangent and Other Trig Functions

Tangent, cotangent, secant and cosecant have graphs with vertical asymptotes, because their formulas divide by sine or cosine.

## Key ideas
- **y = tan x:** period pi. Zeros at x = k*pi. Vertical asymptotes at x = pi/2 + k*pi. It increases between asymptotes, and its range is all real numbers.
- **y = cot x:** period pi. Asymptotes at x = k*pi, zeros at x = pi/2 + k*pi. It decreases between asymptotes.
- **y = sec x = 1/cos x:** period 2pi. Asymptotes where cos x = 0. Range: y <= -1 or y >= 1.
- **y = csc x = 1/sin x:** period 2pi. Asymptotes where sin x = 0. Range: y <= -1 or y >= 1.
- For y = A tan(Bx), the period is pi / |B|. For sec and csc, the period is 2pi / |B|.
- A is a vertical stretch; tangent has no amplitude.
- To graph sec or csc, sketch the matching cosine or sine curve lightly. Draw asymptotes at its zeros and place the U-shaped branches at its peaks and valleys.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Graph y = tan(2x).
1. Period = pi / 2.
2. Asymptotes: 2x = pi/2 gives x = pi/4, and by symmetry x = -pi/4.
3. The zero at the center is x = 0.

**Example 2.** Find the asymptote of y = tan(x - pi/4) + 1 nearest the origin on the right.
1. Set x - pi/4 = pi/2.
2. x = 3pi/4. The graph is the basic tangent shifted right pi/4 and up 1.

**Example 3.** Analyze y = 3 sec(2x).
1. Period = 2pi / 2 = pi.
2. Asymptotes where cos 2x = 0: 2x = pi/2 + k*pi, so x = pi/4 + k*pi/2.
3. Range: y <= -3 or y >= 3, because the related cosine has amplitude 3.

## Common mistakes
- Using a period of 2pi for tangent. It is pi.
- Drawing sec or csc curves that cross the x-axis. They never do.
- Putting asymptotes at the zeros of sec or csc instead of at the zeros of the related cosine or sine.
- Forgetting to divide pi by B when finding the period of tangent.
