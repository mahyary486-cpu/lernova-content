# Arthropods

Arthropods are invertebrates with a hard outer skeleton, a segmented body, and jointed legs, and they form the largest group of animals.

## Key ideas

- Arthropods (phylum Arthropoda) make up more than three quarters of all known animal species. They live on land, in water, and in the air.
- Common features:
  - An **exoskeleton**, a hard covering made of a substance called chitin. It protects the body and prevents water loss.
  - A **segmented body**, often grouped into larger sections.
  - **Jointed legs**, which allow movement and give the group its name (arthro = joint, pod = foot).
  - An **open circulatory system**: blood flows partly in open spaces around the organs.
- The exoskeleton cannot grow, so the animal must **molt**: it sheds the old exoskeleton and grows a new, larger one. During molting it is soft and weak.
- **Insects** are the largest class. The body has three parts: head, thorax, and abdomen. They have six legs attached to the thorax, one pair of antennae, and often two pairs of wings. They breathe with small tubes called tracheae. Examples are bees, flies, beetles, and butterflies.
- Many insects go through **metamorphosis**. In complete metamorphosis, there are four stages: egg, larva, pupa, adult (butterfly). In incomplete metamorphosis, the young (nymph) looks like a small adult (grasshopper).
- **Arachnids** have two body parts (cephalothorax and abdomen), eight legs, and no antennae or wings. Examples are spiders, scorpions, mites, and ticks.
- **Crustaceans** have two pairs of antennae, and usually five or more pairs of legs. Most live in water and breathe with gills. Examples are crabs, shrimp, lobsters, and barnacles.
- **Myriapods** have long bodies with many segments. Centipedes have one pair of legs per segment, and millipedes have two.
- Arthropods are important: bees pollinate crops, but mosquitoes and ticks spread diseases, and some insects destroy crops.

## Examples

**Example 1: Counting legs.** A student finds an animal with a body in three parts and six legs. This is an insect. Another animal has eight legs and two body parts. This is an arachnid. A third has ten legs, two pairs of antennae, and lives in a stream. It is a crustacean, such as a crab.

**Example 2: Butterfly life cycle.** A female butterfly lays eggs on a leaf. The eggs hatch into larvae (caterpillars), which eat leaves and molt several times. The caterpillar then forms a pupa, where its body is rebuilt. After some days, an adult butterfly comes out. This is complete metamorphosis with four stages.

**Example 3: Why a crab hides after molting.** A crab sheds its old shell. For a few days, its new exoskeleton is soft, so the crab hides under rocks. As the new shell hardens with calcium, the crab is safe again. This shows why molting is risky.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a spider an insect. Spiders have eight legs and two body parts.
- Counting legs wrongly: insects have six, arachnids eight.
- Thinking the exoskeleton grows with the animal. It does not, so molting is needed.
- Thinking that all arthropods are harmful. Many are helpful, like bees and silkworms.

## Remember

- Arthropod features: exoskeleton, segmented body, jointed legs.
- Insects: 3 body parts and 6 legs. Arachnids: 2 body parts and 8 legs.
- Growth requires molting.
