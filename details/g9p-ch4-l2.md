# Power

Two students carry identical boxes up the same stairs, but one of them runs and the other walks. They do the same amount of work, yet the student who runs does it in less time. Physics describes this difference with a quantity called power.

## Key ideas

Power is the rate at which work is done, or the rate at which energy is transferred. It is calculated as P = W / t, where W is the work in joules and t is the time in seconds. The SI unit of power is the watt (W), and 1 W = 1 J/s. One watt is a small amount of power. A larger unit is the kilowatt (kW), which equals 1000 W.

A machine with more power can do the same work in less time, or it can do more work in the same time. Doubling the power halves the time that is needed for a given job. A 60 W light bulb transfers 60 J of electrical energy every second, mostly to heat and light.

The formula can be rearranged. To find the work you use W = P x t, and to find the time you use t = W / P. This is the same kind of rearrangement as in the speed formula, and it is worth practicing until it is easy.

Power is not the same as energy or work, and it is not the same as the size of a machine. A small, efficient motor can be more powerful than a large, weak one. The electric energy that homes use is sold in kilowatt-hours (kWh), which is a unit of energy: using 1 kW of power for 1 hour transfers 1 kWh, which equals 3.6 million joules.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Finding power.** A machine does 600 J of work in 10 s. What is its power?
1. Use P = W / t.
2. Substitute: P = 600 / 10.
3. The power is 60 W.

**Example 2: Finding work.** A 500 W motor runs for 20 s. How much work does it do?
1. Rearrange to W = P x t.
2. Substitute: W = 500 x 20.
3. The work is 10,000 J, which is 10 kJ.

**Example 3: Finding time.** How long does a 200 W motor need to do 1000 J of work?
1. Rearrange to t = W / P.
2. Substitute: t = 1000 / 200.
3. The time is 5 s.

## Common mistakes

A common error is to confuse power with energy or work, because they sound related. Power is a rate, how fast the energy is transferred. Another mistake is forgetting to convert the time to seconds when the power is in watts. Finally, some students think that a bigger machine always has more power, but power depends on the rate of doing work, not on size.
