# Power

Two students carry the same box up the same stairs, but one does it faster. They do the same work, but the faster one has more power.

## Key ideas
- **Power** = work / time, **P = W / t**.
- The SI unit is the **watt (W)**: 1 W = 1 J/s.
- More power means the same work in less time (or more work in the same time).
- Rearranged: W = P x t and t = W / P.
- 1 kilowatt (kW) = 1000 W. Electric energy is sold in kilowatt-hours.
- A 60 W bulb transfers 60 J of energy every second.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Finding power.** A machine does 600 J of work in 10 s.
1. P = W / t = 600 / 10.
2. P = 60 W.

**Example 2: Finding work.** A 500 W motor runs for 20 s.
1. W = P t = 500 x 20.
2. W = 10,000 J.

**Example 3: Finding time.** How long does a 200 W motor take to do 1000 J of work?
1. t = W / P = 1000 / 200.
2. t = 5 s.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing power with energy or work.
- Forgetting to use seconds when the power is in watts.
- Thinking a bigger machine always has more power. Power depends on the rate of work.
