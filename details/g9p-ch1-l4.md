# Unit Conversions

Different problems use different units. Converting units correctly is one of the most useful skills in physical science.

## Key ideas
- A **conversion factor** is a fraction equal to 1, such as 1000 m / 1 km. Multiplying by it changes the unit but not the quantity.
- Write the factor so the **unit you want to remove is in the denominator** and cancels.
- Useful facts: 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 L = 1000 mL, 1 h = 3600 s, 1 in = 2.54 cm.
- Speed: km/h to m/s divide by 3.6; m/s to km/h multiply by 3.6.
- For two-step conversions chain the factors in one line and cancel unit by unit.
- Check that your answer is reasonable: a bigger unit gives a smaller number.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Kilometers to meters.** Convert 5.2 km to meters.
1. Use 1000 m = 1 km.
2. 5.2 km x (1000 m / 1 km) = 5200 m.

**Example 2: Speed.** Convert 72 km/h to m/s.
1. 72 km/h x (1000 m / 1 km) x (1 h / 3600 s).
2. = 72,000 / 3600 = 20 m/s.

**Example 3: Hours to seconds.** How many seconds are in 3.5 hours?
1. 3.5 h x (60 min / 1 h) x (60 s / 1 min).
2. = 3.5 x 3600 = 12,600 s.

## Common mistakes
- Putting the factor upside down so the unit does not cancel.
- Converting squared units like linear units. 1 m^2 is 10,000 cm^2, not 100 cm^2.
- Dropping the units during the calculation.
- Not checking whether the size of the answer makes sense.
