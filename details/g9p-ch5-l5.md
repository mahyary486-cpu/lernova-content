# Light, Color and Lenses

A rainbow, a magnifying glass and the lens of your eye all show how light changes direction and splits into colors.

## Key ideas
- **White light** contains all colors: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
- A **prism** separates colors because each color **refracts** by a different amount.
- An opaque object's color is the color it **reflects**; it absorbs the rest.
- A **convex lens** (thicker in the middle) **converges** light; a **concave lens** **spreads** it apart.
- A **concave mirror** can focus light; a **convex mirror** gives a smaller, upright image and a wide view.
- Primary colors of light: red, green and blue.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Why an apple is red.** Why does a red apple look red?
1. The apple reflects red light.
2. It absorbs the other colors.

**Example 2: A prism.** White light enters a glass prism.
1. Each color bends by a different amount.
2. Violet bends the most and red the least, so a spectrum appears.

**Example 3: A magnifying glass.** A magnifying glass is a convex lens.
1. It converges light rays.
2. An object close to the lens looks larger.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a prism adds colors to light.
- Thinking a red object emits red light. It reflects it.
- Mixing up convex and concave lenses.
