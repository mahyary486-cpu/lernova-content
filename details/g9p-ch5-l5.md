# Light, Color and Lenses

A rainbow, a magnifying glass and the lens in your eye all show how light behaves when it meets different materials. In this lesson we see why objects have colors and how lenses and mirrors change the path of light.

## Key ideas

White light is a mixture of all the colors of the spectrum: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet. A glass prism can separate them. When white light enters the prism, each color is refracted by a slightly different amount. Violet bends the most and red bends the least, so the colors spread out into a band called a spectrum. A rainbow forms in a similar way when sunlight is refracted in raindrops. The prism does not add any color; the colors were already in the white light.

The color that we see in an object that does not give off light depends on what it reflects. A red apple looks red because it reflects red light and absorbs most of the other colors. A white object reflects all colors, and a black object absorbs almost all of them. The primary colors of light are red, green and blue, and mixing them in equal amounts makes white light.

A lens is a piece of transparent material that has curved surfaces and bends light by refraction. A convex lens is thicker in the middle than at the edges and converges, or brings together, parallel rays. It is used in magnifying glasses, cameras and the human eye. A concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads rays apart. Mirrors can also have curved surfaces. A concave mirror can focus light and is used in telescopes and in torches, while a convex mirror gives a smaller upright image but a wide field of view, which makes it useful as a car's side mirror.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Why an apple is red.** Why does a red apple look red in white light?
1. White light contains all colors.
2. The apple's surface reflects the red light and absorbs the other colors.
3. Only the red light reaches your eye, so the apple appears red.

**Example 2: A prism.** White light enters a glass prism. What do you see on the other side, and why?
1. Each color is refracted by a different amount in the glass.
2. Violet light bends the most and red light the least.
3. So the colors separate and a spectrum appears, with red at one end and violet at the other.

**Example 3: A magnifying glass.** A magnifying glass is a convex lens. What does it do to light, and what do you see?
1. A convex lens converges the light rays that pass through it.
2. When an object is close to the lens, it looks larger and upright.
3. That is why the lens is useful for reading small print.

## Common mistakes

Many students think that a prism adds colors to white light, while it only separates the colors that are already there. Another mistake is to believe that a red object gives off red light; most objects only reflect it. A third common error is to mix up convex and concave lenses; the convex lens is thicker in the middle and converges light, and the concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads it.
