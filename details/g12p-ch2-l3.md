# Electric Power, Energy and EMF

A bill for electricity is for energy in kilowatt-hours, not for power.

## Key ideas
- **P = I V = I^2 R = V^2 / R**.
- **Energy E = P t**; 1 kWh = **3.6 x 10^6 J**.
- **Terminal voltage V = emf - I r**.
- **I = emf / (R + r)**.
- Transmission loss = **I^2 R**, so use **high voltage**.
- **Fuses** protect against too much current.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Power.** A heater has a resistance of 24 ohm on a 120 V supply.
1. P = V^2 / R = 14,400 / 24.
2. P = 600 W.

**Example 2: Cost.** A 2 kW heater runs for 3 h at 0.15 dollars per kWh.
1. E = 2 x 3 = 6 kWh.
2. Cost = 6 x 0.15 = 0.90 dollars.

**Example 3: Internal resistance.** A battery has emf 12 V and r = 0.5 ohm and supplies 4 A.
1. V = emf - I r = 12 - 4 x 0.5.
2. V = 10 V.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing kilowatts and watts.
- Forgetting the internal resistance.
- Thinking power is the same as energy.
