# Algebraic expressions and polynomials

This lesson explains what polynomials are and how to add, subtract, and multiply them.

## Key ideas

- A variable is a letter that stands for a number. An algebraic expression combines numbers and variables using operations.
- A monomial is a product of a number and variables with whole-number exponents, for example 7x^2 or -3xy. The number part is the coefficient. A plain number such as 5 is also a monomial.
- The degree of a monomial is the sum of the exponents of its variables. The monomial 4x^3 has degree 3, and 2x^2y has degree 3. A nonzero constant has degree 0.
- A polynomial is a sum of monomials. Each monomial is a term. A polynomial with two terms is a binomial, and with three terms is a trinomial.
- The degree of a polynomial is the largest degree of its terms. We usually write terms in descending order of degree.
- Like terms have exactly the same variables with the same exponents. Only like terms can be combined: 3x^2 + 5x^2 = 8x^2, but 3x^2 + 5x cannot be simplified.
- To multiply monomials, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of equal bases: (2x^2)(3x^4) = 6x^6.
- To multiply polynomials, multiply each term of the first by each term of the second. Then combine like terms.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Find the degree and the coefficients of p(x) = 5x^3 - 2x + 7, then find p(2).

Step 1: The powers of x are 3, 1, and 0. The largest is 3, so the degree is 3.
Step 2: The coefficients are 5 for x^3, 0 for x^2 (it is missing), -2 for x, and 7 for the constant term.
Step 3: Substitute x = 2: p(2) = 5(8) - 2(2) + 7 = 40 - 4 + 7 = 43.

**Example 2.** Let A = 3x^2 + 2x - 5 and B = x^2 - 4x + 8. Find A + B and A - B.

Step 1: Add like terms. x^2 terms: 3 + 1 = 4. x terms: 2 + (-4) = -2. Constants: -5 + 8 = 3. So A + B = 4x^2 - 2x + 3.
Step 2: For A - B, change the sign of every term in B first: A - B = 3x^2 + 2x - 5 - x^2 + 4x - 8.
Step 3: Combine: x^2 terms: 3 - 1 = 2. x terms: 2 + 4 = 6. Constants: -5 - 8 = -13. So A - B = 2x^2 + 6x - 13.

**Example 3.** Multiply (2x - 3)(x^2 + 4x - 1).

Step 1: Multiply 2x by each term: 2x * x^2 = 2x^3, 2x * 4x = 8x^2, 2x * (-1) = -2x.
Step 2: Multiply -3 by each term: -3 * x^2 = -3x^2, -3 * 4x = -12x, -3 * (-1) = 3.
Step 3: Write everything: 2x^3 + 8x^2 - 2x - 3x^2 - 12x + 3.
Step 4: Combine like terms: 2x^3 + 5x^2 - 14x + 3.
Check with x = 1: (2 - 3)(1 + 4 - 1) = (-1)(4) = -4, and 2 + 5 - 14 + 3 = -4. The answers match.

## Common mistakes

- Combining unlike terms, such as writing 2x + 3x^2 = 5x^3. These terms are not alike.
- Forgetting to change all signs when subtracting a polynomial. The minus sign applies to every term in the second polynomial.
- Adding exponents when adding terms. Exponents are added only when multiplying equal bases: x^2 + x^2 = 2x^2, not x^4.
- Missing a product when multiplying, so that fewer than all pairs of terms are used.

## Remember

- Only like terms can be added or subtracted.
- When multiplying, every term meets every term, and exponents of equal bases add.
- Check a result by substituting a simple number such as x = 1.
