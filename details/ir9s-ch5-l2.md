# Newton's first and second laws

These two laws describe how an object moves when the net force on it is zero or is not zero.

## Key ideas

- Newton's first law: an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving at constant velocity in a straight line, unless an unbalanced force acts on it.
- Inertia is the tendency of an object to keep its state of motion. The first law is often called the law of inertia.
- Mass measures inertia. An object with more mass has more inertia, so it is harder to start, stop, or turn.
- This is why passengers lean forward when a bus brakes suddenly. Their bodies tend to keep moving forward, while the bus slows down.
- Newton's second law: the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass.
- The formula is a = F / m, which is usually written F = m × a.
- F is the net force in newtons (N), m is the mass in kilograms (kg), and a is the acceleration in m/s^2.
- One newton is the force that gives a 1 kg object an acceleration of 1 m/s^2. So 1 N = 1 kg × m/s^2.
- The acceleration points in the same direction as the net force.
- With the same force, a larger mass gives a smaller acceleration. With the same mass, a larger force gives a larger acceleration.

## Examples

**Example 1: Finding force.** A car has a mass of 1000 kg and accelerates at 2 m/s^2. What net force acts on it?
- Write the formula: F = m × a.
- Put in the numbers: F = 1000 kg × 2 m/s^2.
- F = 2000 N.

**Example 2: Finding acceleration.** A net force of 60 N acts on a trolley with a mass of 12 kg.
- Write the formula: a = F / m.
- Put in the numbers: a = 60 N / 12 kg.
- a = 5 m/s^2, in the direction of the force.

**Example 3: Pushing with friction.** A student pushes a 10 kg box with 50 N. Friction on the floor is 20 N in the opposite direction.
- First find the net force: 50 N - 20 N = 30 N.
- Then use a = F / m = 30 N / 10 kg.
- a = 3 m/s^2.
- Note that we use the net force, not the push alone.

**Example 4: Force needed to speed up.** A 40 kg cyclist (with the bicycle) needs to speed up at 0.5 m/s^2.
- F = m × a = 40 kg × 0.5 m/s^2.
- F = 20 N.

## Common mistakes

- Using one single force in F = m × a when several forces act. Always use the net force.
- Mixing up mass and weight. Mass is in kilograms and does not depend on place. Weight is a force in newtons.
- Thinking that a force is needed to keep an object moving. A force is needed only to change the motion.
- Forgetting to use kilograms. If the mass is given in grams, change it to kilograms first.

## Remember

- No net force means no change in velocity (first law).
- F = m × a, with F in N, m in kg, and a in m/s^2 (second law).
- More mass means more inertia and less acceleration for the same force.
