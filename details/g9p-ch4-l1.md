# Work

In everyday speech work means effort. In physics work has a precise meaning: it is the energy transferred when a force moves something.

## Key ideas
- **Work** is done when a force moves an object in the direction of the force.
- **W = F x d**: work (J) = force (N) x distance (m).
- The SI unit is the **joule (J)**: 1 J = 1 N x 1 m.
- No movement means **no work**, even if you push very hard.
- A force at a right angle to the motion does no work.
- Lifting an object: work = weight x height = m g h.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Pushing a box.** A 20 N force moves a box 5 m in the direction of the force.
1. W = F x d = 20 x 5.
2. W = 100 J.

**Example 2: Pushing a wall.** A student pushes a wall with 200 N but the wall does not move.
1. The distance moved is 0 m.
2. W = 200 x 0 = 0 J. No work is done.

**Example 3: Lifting a book.** A 2 kg book is lifted 1.5 m (g = 9.8 m/s^2).
1. Weight = m g = 2 x 9.8 = 19.6 N.
2. W = 19.6 x 1.5 = 29.4 J.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking effort or tiredness always means work was done.
- Using the total distance when the force and motion are not in the same direction.
- Mixing mass (kg) with weight (N) when lifting.
