# Moving plates and subduction

Earth's outer shell is broken into large moving pieces called tectonic plates, and where plates meet, they can pull apart, collide or slide past each other.

## Key ideas
- The lithosphere is the rigid outer layer of Earth. It includes the crust and the top of the mantle and is about 100 km thick. It is broken into about 15 major and minor plates, such as the Eurasian, African, Arabian, Pacific and South American plates.
- Plates float on the asthenosphere, a hot, soft and slowly flowing layer of the mantle below. Heat from deep inside Earth makes mantle rock flow in slow convection currents. Hot rock rises, cools, and sinks again. This movement, together with the weight of sinking plates, moves the plates by a few centimeters per year.
- There are three types of plate boundary:
  - Divergent: plates move apart. New crust forms. Examples are mid-ocean ridges and the East African Rift.
  - Convergent: plates move toward each other. Mountains, volcanoes and trenches form.
  - Transform: plates slide past each other sideways. Earthquakes are common. An example is the San Andreas Fault in California.
- Subduction happens when two plates collide and one sinks below the other. Oceanic crust is denser than continental crust, so it sinks. This forms a deep ocean trench, such as the Mariana Trench, which is about 11 km deep. The sinking plate melts, and magma rises to form a chain of volcanoes. The Andes mountains formed where the Nazca plate sinks under the South American plate.
- When two continents collide, neither sinks easily because both are light. The crust is crumpled and pushed up into high mountains. The Himalayas are the result of India colliding with Asia. The Zagros Mountains in Iran formed as the Arabian plate pushes into the Eurasian plate.

## Examples
- Example 1, ocean and continent: The Nazca plate is oceanic and the South American plate is continental. The denser ocean plate subducts. A trench forms off the coast, and volcanoes grow on land. This describes the Andes.
- Example 2, continent and continent: India moves north by about 5 cm each year and presses against Asia. Over 50 million years this is 5 × 50,000,000 = 250,000,000 cm, or 2,500 km. The crust piles up and makes the Himalayas, which are still rising.
- Example 3, Iran: Iran lies in the zone where the Arabian plate converges with the Eurasian plate. This explains the folded Zagros Mountains and the many earthquakes in the region.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking plates and continents are the same. A plate can hold both continent and ocean floor.
- Saying the denser plate is the continental one. The oceanic plate is denser and subducts.
- Thinking the plates float on liquid magma. The asthenosphere is solid rock that flows very slowly.
- Thinking volcanoes form at every boundary.

## Remember
- Three boundaries: divergent, convergent, transform.
- Subduction: the dense oceanic plate sinks, forming trenches and volcanoes.
- Mantle convection helps move the plates.
