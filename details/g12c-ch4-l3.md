# Carbohydrates and Lipids

Plants store energy as starch, and animals store it as fat, which holds more than twice as much energy per gram. Carbohydrates and lipids are the main energy stores of living things, and they are also building materials. The structure of each is simple once you see the pattern: carbohydrates are chains of sugar units, and a fat is a molecule of glycerol with three fatty acids attached.

## Key ideas

Carbohydrates have the general formula Cx(H2O)y, which is the origin of their name, hydrates of carbon. The simplest are the monosaccharides, such as glucose and fructose, with the formula C6H12O6. Glucose and fructose are isomers, since they have the same formula but different structures. Two monosaccharides join to form a disaccharide, with the loss of one molecule of water, in a condensation reaction. The link between them is called a glycosidic link. Maltose, which is made of two glucose units, and sucrose, made of glucose and fructose, both have the formula C12H22O11.

Polysaccharides are polymers of many sugar units. Starch is the energy store of plants, and glycogen is the energy store of animals. Cellulose is the structural material of plant cell walls. All three are polymers of glucose, but the way the units are linked differs. Humans have enzymes to break the links of starch, but not those of cellulose, so cellulose is fiber for us, and not food. This is a good example of how a small difference in structure changes function.

Lipids are the family of fats, oils and waxes, and they are nonpolar and insoluble in water. A fat or oil is a triglyceride, which is a triester formed from glycerol, an alcohol with three -OH groups, and three fatty acid molecules. A fatty acid is a carboxylic acid with a long hydrocarbon chain. In a saturated fat, the chains have only single bonds, so they pack closely together and the fat is a solid at room temperature. In an unsaturated fat, double bonds put kinks in the chains, so they pack less closely, and the substance is a liquid oil. Heating a fat with NaOH hydrolyzes the ester links, giving glycerol and soap, in the reaction called saponification.

Fats store more energy than carbohydrates, because their carbon atoms are less oxidized. Fats give about 38 kJ per gram, and carbohydrates about 17 kJ per gram. This is why the body stores long-term energy as fat.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: The formula of maltose.** Two glucose molecules join to form maltose.
1. Two glucose molecules contain 2 x C6H12O6 = C12H24O12.
2. A condensation reaction removes one molecule of H2O, which has 2 H and 1 O.
3. The formula of maltose is C12H22O11.

**Example 2: How many fatty acid chains are in a triglyceride?**
1. Glycerol has three -OH groups, and each of them can form an ester link with one fatty acid.
2. Three ester links are formed, with the loss of three molecules of water.
3. Therefore a triglyceride has three fatty acid chains.

**Example 3: Compare the energy of 10 g of fat and 10 g of carbohydrate.** Use 38 and 17 kJ/g.
1. For the fat: 10 x 38 = 380 kJ. For the carbohydrate: 10 x 17 = 170 kJ.
2. The difference is 380 - 170 = 210 kJ.
3. The fat provides 210 kJ more, which is more than twice as much energy.

## Common mistakes

A common error is to forget the water lost when two sugars join, which gives C12H24O12 for a disaccharide instead of C12H22O11. Another is to think that starch and cellulose are the same, but the links between the glucose units differ, and humans can digest only starch. The third is to confuse saturated and unsaturated fats. The saturated fat has no double bonds in its chains, and the unsaturated fat has at least one.
