# Pressure in liquids and Pascal's principle

Liquids press in all directions, and this lesson shows how to calculate that pressure and how a pressure change moves through a liquid.

## Key ideas

- A liquid presses on the bottom and on the walls of its container, and on any object inside it. At a point inside the liquid, the pressure acts in all directions.
- The pressure caused by a liquid depends on three things: the depth (h) below the surface, the density of the liquid (ρ), and the gravitational field strength (g). The formula is P = ρ × g × h.
- Pressure increases with depth. At the same depth in the same liquid, the pressure is the same in every direction.
- The pressure does not depend on the shape of the container or the total amount of liquid. Only the depth and the density matter.
- Density of fresh water is about 1000 kg/m^3. Seawater is a little denser, about 1030 kg/m^3. Use g ≈ 10 N/kg.
- The air above the liquid also presses on its surface. The total pressure at depth h is the atmospheric pressure plus ρ × g × h.
- Pascal's principle: when a pressure is applied to an enclosed liquid, the change in pressure is passed on without loss to every part of the liquid and to the walls.
- A hydraulic lift uses this idea. A small force on a small piston makes a pressure. The same pressure acts on a large piston, so the force there is larger: F2 / A2 = F1 / A1.
- Dams are built thicker at the bottom because the pressure is larger there.

## Examples

**Example 1: Pressure from water.** Find the pressure caused by water at a depth of 5 m, and the total pressure if the atmospheric pressure is 100 000 Pa.
- Step 1: P = ρ × g × h = 1000 × 10 × 5 = 50 000 Pa.
- Step 2: Total pressure = 100 000 + 50 000 = 150 000 Pa.

**Example 2: Seawater.** A diver is 20 m deep in the sea. Find the pressure caused by the water. Use ρ = 1030 kg/m^3.
- Step 1: P = 1030 × 10 × 20.
- Step 2: P = 206 000 Pa, which is 206 kPa.

**Example 3: A hydraulic lift.** A force of 100 N acts on a small piston of area 0.002 m^2. The large piston has an area of 0.04 m^2. Find the force on the large piston.
- Step 1: Pressure = 100 / 0.002 = 50 000 Pa.
- Step 2: By Pascal's principle the same pressure acts on the large piston.
- Step 3: F2 = P × A2 = 50 000 × 0.04 = 2000 N.
- Step 4: The force is multiplied by 0.04 / 0.002 = 20. A lift can raise a car with a small push.

## Common mistakes

- Using the length of the container or the volume of water instead of the vertical depth h.
- Thinking that a wide container gives a larger pressure at the bottom. If the depth is the same, the pressure is the same.
- Forgetting to add atmospheric pressure when the question asks for the total pressure.
- Thinking that a hydraulic machine gives extra energy. It gives a larger force, but the small piston moves a larger distance than the large piston.

## Remember

- P = ρ × g × h; pressure grows with depth and density.
- Pascal's principle: pressure on an enclosed liquid is passed on equally everywhere.
- In a hydraulic system, F1 / A1 = F2 / A2.
