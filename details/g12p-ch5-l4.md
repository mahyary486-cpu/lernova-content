# Nuclear Physics and Radioactivity

Carbon-14 dating works because the amount of radioactive carbon in a dead organism halves every 5730 years.

## Key ideas
- **Nucleons** = protons + neutrons; **isotopes** have the same Z, different A.
- **Binding energy** E = delta m c^2; **1 u = 931.5 MeV**.
- **Alpha**: A - 4, Z - 2; **beta-minus**: Z + 1; **gamma**: no change in A or Z.
- **N = N0 e^(-lambda t)**; **T(1/2) = 0.693 / lambda**.
- After n half-lives: **(1/2)^n** remains.
- Decay is **random** but predictable in large numbers.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Alpha decay.** Uranium-238 (Z = 92) emits an alpha particle.
1. A: 238 - 4 = 234; Z: 92 - 2 = 90.
2. The daughter is thorium-234.

**Example 2: Half-life.** A sample of 80 g has a half-life of 6 hours. How much remains after 18 hours?
1. 18 h is 3 half-lives.
2. 80 x (1/2)^3 = 10 g.

**Example 3: Binding energy.** A nucleus has a mass defect of 0.03 u.
1. E = 0.03 x 931.5 MeV.
2. E = 27.9 MeV.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up mass number and atomic number.
- Thinking half-life depends on the amount.
- Forgetting that beta decay changes Z by +1.
