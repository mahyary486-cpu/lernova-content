# Circles and Ellipses

Circles and ellipses are the closed conic sections. Both come from cutting a cone with a plane that does not run parallel to its side.

## Circles
A circle is the set of points at distance r from a center (h, k).
- Standard form: (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2.
- Centered at the origin: x^2 + y^2 = r^2.

## Ellipses
An ellipse is the set of points whose distances to two fixed foci add up to a constant, 2a.
- Horizontal major axis: (x - h)^2/a^2 + (y - k)^2/b^2 = 1 with a > b.
- Vertical major axis: (x - h)^2/b^2 + (y - k)^2/a^2 = 1 with a > b.
- a is the semi-major axis, b is the semi-minor axis, and c^2 = a^2 - b^2 gives the distance from the center to each focus.
- Vertices lie a units from the center along the major axis. Eccentricity is e = c/a, and 0 < e < 1. A circle has e = 0.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Find the center and radius of x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0.**
1. Group terms: (x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 12.
2. Complete the squares by adding 9 and 4 to both sides: (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25.
3. Center (3, -2), radius 5.

**Example 2. Analyze x^2/25 + y^2/9 = 1.**
1. a^2 = 25 and b^2 = 9, so a = 5 and b = 3. The larger denominator is under x, so the major axis is horizontal.
2. c^2 = 25 - 9 = 16, so c = 4.
3. Vertices (+-5, 0), foci (+-4, 0), eccentricity 4/5.

**Example 3. Write the ellipse with foci (+-3, 0) and major axis length 10.**
1. 2a = 10, so a = 5. The foci give c = 3.
2. b^2 = a^2 - c^2 = 25 - 9 = 16.
3. Equation: x^2/25 + y^2/16 = 1.

## Common mistakes
- Swapping a and b. The larger denominator is always a^2.
- Using c^2 = a^2 + b^2. That rule belongs to hyperbolas.
- Forgetting to add the same amounts to both sides when completing the square.
- Reading the radius as r^2: in x^2 + y^2 = 49 the radius is 7.
