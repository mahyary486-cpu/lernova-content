# Conservation of Energy

A roller coaster climbs slowly and then rushes down. Energy changes form all along the ride, but the total never disappears.

## Key ideas
- **Law of conservation of energy**: energy is not created or destroyed; it changes form or moves.
- Without friction: **KE + PE = constant** (total mechanical energy).
- Falling: PE decreases and KE increases. Rising: KE decreases and PE increases.
- At the top of a swing PE is greatest and KE is least; at the bottom KE is greatest.
- Friction and air resistance change mechanical energy into **thermal energy** and sound.
- The energy "lost" to friction is not destroyed; it is just not useful any more.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Rolling down a hill.** A ball rolls down a hill without friction.
1. It loses height, so its PE decreases.
2. By conservation of energy the PE becomes KE, so the ball speeds up.

**Example 2: Roller coaster.** A car at the top has 50,000 J of potential energy. Ignore friction.
1. At the bottom all the PE has become KE.
2. KE = 50,000 J.

**Example 3: Bouncing ball.** A ball does not bounce back to its original height.
1. Some energy changes to thermal and sound energy on each bounce.
2. So less mechanical energy remains and the ball rises less.

## Common mistakes
- Saying energy is "used up" or destroyed.
- Forgetting the thermal energy produced by friction.
- Adding PE and KE from different objects without checking the system.
