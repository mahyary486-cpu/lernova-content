# Law of Sines and Cosines

These laws work in **any** triangle. Label the sides a, b, c opposite the angles A, B, C.

## Law of Sines
**a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C**

Use it when you know:
- two angles and any side (**AAS** or **ASA**), or
- two sides and an angle **opposite** one of them (**SSA**, which can be ambiguous: sometimes 0, 1 or 2 triangles).

## Law of Cosines
**c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab cos C**

Use it when you know:
- two sides and the **included** angle (**SAS**), or
- all three sides (**SSS**), to find an angle. Rearranged: cos C = (a^2 + b^2 - c^2) / (2ab).

If C = 90 degrees then cos C = 0 and the law becomes the Pythagorean theorem.

## Worked examples
1. A = 30, B = 70 degrees and a = 10. Law of Sines: b = 10 x sin 70 / sin 30 = 10 x 0.9397 / 0.5, about **18.8**.
2. a = 7, b = 9 and the angle between them C = 60 degrees. c^2 = 49 + 81 - 2 x 7 x 9 x 0.5 = 130 - 63 = 67, so c is about **8.19**.
3. Sides 5, 6, 7: the angle opposite 7 has cos C = (25 + 36 - 49) / (2 x 5 x 6) = 12 / 60 = 0.2, so C is about **78.5 degrees**.
4. Two ships leave a port at an angle of 120 degrees apart, one travelling 30 miles and the other 40 miles. Their distance: c^2 = 900 + 1600 - 2 x 30 x 40 x cos 120 = 2500 + 1200 = 3700, so c is about **60.8 miles**.

## Common mistakes
- Using the Law of Sines when you have SAS or SSS. Use the Law of Cosines instead.
- Forgetting the angle in the Law of Cosines is the one **opposite** the side you find.
- Forgetting that cos of an obtuse angle is negative.
- Overlooking the ambiguous case in SSA.
