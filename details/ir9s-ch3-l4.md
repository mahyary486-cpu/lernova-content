# Petroleum products and polymers

Petroleum is the source of fuels and of raw materials for chemicals, and many of these materials are polymers made by joining small molecules together.

## Key ideas

Fractions of crude oil are used in two main ways: as fuels and as raw materials (feedstock) for the chemical industry. Burning oil for fuel releases CO2 and wastes a valuable raw material, so some people say that oil is too useful to burn.

Cracking: the fractions with long molecules are less in demand than those with short molecules. Cracking breaks long hydrocarbon molecules into shorter ones, using high temperature and often a catalyst. Cracking makes two kinds of product: a shorter alkane and an alkene. Alkenes, such as ethene (C2H4) and propene (C3H6), contain a carbon-carbon double bond and are more reactive than alkanes.

Polymers are very large molecules made of many repeating small units. The small unit is called a monomer. Making a polymer is called polymerization. In addition polymerization, many alkene molecules join in a long chain without losing any atoms.

Examples:
- Ethene -> polyethylene (polyethylene is used for bags, bottles and pipes).
- Propene -> polypropylene (used for food boxes, ropes and car parts).
- Chloroethene -> PVC (used for pipes and window frames).

In simple form: n C2H4 -> (C2H4)n, where n is a very large number.

Other petroleum products include lubricating oils, wax, bitumen, and the raw materials for detergents, paints, medicines and synthetic fibers such as nylon and polyester. Many everyday items come from petroleum.

## Examples

Example 1: A cracking reaction. Decane (C10H22) is cracked into octane and ethene: C10H22 -> C8H18 + C2H4.
Step 1: Count carbon on the left: 10. On the right: 8 + 2 = 10. 
Step 2: Count hydrogen on the left: 22. On the right: 18 + 4 = 22.
Both sides match, so the equation is balanced. The product ethene can be used to make plastic.

Example 2: The mass of one polymer chain. A chain of polypropylene is made from 500 propene monomers (C3H6).
Step 1: Molar mass of C3H6 = 3 × 12 + 6 × 1 = 42 g/mol (relative mass 42).
Step 2: In addition polymerization no atoms are lost, so the polymer has the mass of all monomers added together.
Step 3: Relative mass of the chain = 500 × 42 = 21 000.
The very large mass shows why polymers are called macromolecules.

Example 3: Mass of plastic from ethene. A factory has 1000 kg of ethene (C2H4). In ideal conditions all of it becomes polyethylene.
Step 1: Addition polymerization loses no mass.
Step 2: Mass of polyethylene = 1000 kg.
In real factories a little is lost, so the real mass is slightly below 1000 kg.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing monomer and polymer. The monomer is the small unit; the polymer is the long chain.
- Thinking that cracking is the same as distillation. Cracking is a chemical reaction; distillation is only a physical separation.
- Forgetting that alkenes have a double bond and alkanes do not.
- Thinking that polymerization produces a different substance made of different atoms. The atoms are the same; they are only joined in a new way.

## Remember

- Cracking turns long hydrocarbons into shorter, more useful ones, including alkenes.
- Many small monomers join to form a polymer.
- Oil is valuable as a raw material, not only as a fuel.
