# Radians and the Unit Circle

## Radians
One **radian** is the angle that cuts off an arc whose length equals the radius. A full circle is 2π radians, so
**180° = π radians** and 360° = 2π radians.

## Converting
- Degrees to radians: multiply by **π/180**.
- Radians to degrees: multiply by **180/π**.

Common angles:
| Degrees | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° | 180° | 270° | 360° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radians | π/6 | π/4 | π/3 | π/2 | π | 3π/2 | 2π |

- 150° = 150 * π/180 = **5π/6**.
- 3π/4 = (3π/4)(180/π) = **135°**.

## Arc length and sector area
For a circle of radius r and a central angle θ **in radians**:
- Arc length: **s = rθ**. With r = 5 and θ = 2: s = **10**. With r = 6 and θ = π/3: s = 2π.
- Sector area: **A = (1/2) r² θ**. With r = 4 and θ = π/2: A = (1/2)(16)(π/2) = **4π**.

## The unit circle
The **unit circle** has radius 1 and its center at the origin. For an angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, the point on the circle is
**(cos θ, sin θ)**.
So cos θ is the x-coordinate and sin θ is the y-coordinate.

Key points:
| θ | Point (cos θ, sin θ) |
|---|---|
| 0 | (1, 0) |
| 30° (π/6) | (sqrt(3)/2, 1/2) |
| 45° (π/4) | (sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)/2) |
| 60° (π/3) | (1/2, sqrt(3)/2) |
| 90° (π/2) | (0, 1) |
| 180° (π) | (-1, 0) |
| 270° (3π/2) | (0, -1) |

From the table: cos π = **-1**, sin(π/2) = **1**, and tan θ = sin θ / cos θ.

## Common mistakes
- Using degrees in the arc length formula. The angle must be in radians.
- Switching sine and cosine: the x-coordinate is cosine.
- Forgetting that the unit circle has radius 1.
