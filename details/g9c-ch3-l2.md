# Kinetic Molecular Theory

The behavior of everything from a balloon to boiling water can be explained by moving particles and the energy they carry.

## Key ideas
- All matter is made of **particles in constant motion**.
- Gas particles move fast in straight lines and **collide** with the container walls, causing **pressure**.
- **Temperature** is a measure of the **average kinetic energy** of particles.
- Attractions between particles are strongest in solids and weakest in gases.
- Heating makes particles move faster; cooling makes them slower.
- At **absolute zero (0 K)** particle motion would be at a minimum.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Heating a solid.** What happens to the particles when a solid is heated?
1. They gain energy.
2. They vibrate faster.

**Example 2: Gas pressure.** Why does a sealed balloon stay inflated?
1. Gas particles hit the inside walls many times a second.
2. These collisions push outward and create the pressure.

**Example 3: Temperature scales.** Convert 25 degrees C to kelvins.
1. K = C + 273.
2. 25 + 273 = 298 K.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking temperature measures the total energy.
- Thinking particles stop moving in a cold solid.
- Forgetting to use kelvins in gas calculations.
