# Average Rate of Change

## Definition
The **average rate of change** of f from x = a to x = b is the slope of the **secant line** through the points (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)):

**(f(b) - f(a)) / (b - a)**

It tells how much the output changes, on average, for each 1 unit of input.

## Examples
- f(x) = x^2 from 1 to 3: (9 - 1)/(3 - 1) = 8/2 = **4**.
- f(x) = 3x + 2 on [0, 10]: (32 - 2)/10 = **3**. For a linear function the average rate of change is always the slope.
- f(x) = 2^x from 0 to 3: (8 - 1)/3 = **7/3**.
- f(x) = sqrt(x) from 1 to 9: (3 - 1)/(9 - 1) = 2/8 = **1/4**.
- f(x) = x^2 - 2x from -1 to 2: f(-1) = 3 and f(2) = 0, so (0 - 3)/(2 - (-1)) = **-1**.

## Units
The units are (output units) per (input units).
A ball is at height 0 ft at 0 s and 20 ft at 2 s. The average rate of change is 20/2 = **10 ft per second**.

## Positive, negative or zero
- Positive: the function increases on average over the interval.
- Negative: it decreases on average.
- Zero: the output is the same at both ends (it may still go up and down in between).

## The difference quotient
For a point x and a small step h:
**(f(x + h) - f(x)) / h**
This is the average rate of change from x to x + h.
- f(x) = x^2: (x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - x^2)/h = **2x + h**.
- f(x) = 3x - 1: (3(x + h) - 1 - (3x - 1))/h = **3**.
When h gets very small, 2x + h gets close to 2x. This idea leads to the derivative in calculus.

## Common mistakes
- Subtracting in a different order on the top and bottom. Use f(b) - f(a) over b - a.
- Forgetting parentheses when substituting a negative number.
- Mixing up the average rate of change with the average of the two outputs.
