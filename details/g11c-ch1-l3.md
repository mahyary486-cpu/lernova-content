# Stoichiometry: Mole Ratios and Mass Calculations

A recipe tells you how many eggs per cup of flour. A balanced equation tells you how many moles of one substance per mole of another.

## Key ideas
- The **coefficients** give the **mole ratio**.
- Path: **mass A -> moles A -> moles B -> mass B**.
- Always use a **balanced** equation.
- Gas volumes at the same T and P are in the same ratio as the coefficients.
- Mole ratio = **coefficient wanted / coefficient given**.
- Check that the answer is reasonable.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mass to mass.** How many grams of water form from 4 g of H2 (2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O)?
1. n(H2) = 4 / 2 = 2 mol; ratio 2 : 2 gives 2 mol H2O.
2. Mass = 2 x 18 = 36 g.

**Example 2: Oxygen needed.** How many moles of O2 react with 3 mol of H2?
1. The ratio O2 : H2 is 1 : 2.
2. n(O2) = 3 x 1 / 2 = 1.5 mol.

**Example 3: Gas volume.** What volume of CO2 at STP forms from 1 mol of CH4 burning?
1. CH4 + 2 O2 -> CO2 + 2 H2O gives 1 mol CO2.
2. V = 1 x 22.4 = 22.4 L.

## Common mistakes
- Using an unbalanced equation for the ratio.
- Converting grams directly from one substance to another.
- Using the ratio upside down.
