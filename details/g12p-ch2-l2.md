# Series and Parallel Circuits

If one bulb in a string of series lights fails, all go out, but household lamps are in parallel and work independently.

## Key ideas
- **Series**: same **I**, voltages **add**, **R = R1 + R2**.
- **Parallel**: same **V**, currents **add**, **1 / R = 1 / R1 + 1 / R2**.
- The parallel resistance is **less than the smallest** resistor.
- Two equal resistors R in parallel give **R / 2**.
- **Ammeter** in series; **voltmeter** in parallel.
- Houses use **parallel** wiring.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Series.** 6 ohm and 12 ohm resistors in series across 18 V.
1. R = 6 + 12 = 18 ohm.
2. I = V / R = 1 A.

**Example 2: Parallel.** 6 ohm and 12 ohm resistors in parallel.
1. 1 / R = 1 / 6 + 1 / 12 = 3 / 12.
2. R = 4 ohm.

**Example 3: Mixed.** A 4 ohm resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 6 ohm and 3 ohm.
1. Parallel pair: 1 / R = 1 / 6 + 1 / 3 = 1 / 2, so 2 ohm.
2. Total = 4 + 2 = 6 ohm.

## Common mistakes
- Adding parallel resistors directly.
- Forgetting to invert the sum.
- Connecting an ammeter in parallel.
