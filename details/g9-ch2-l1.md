# The Coordinate Plane and Graphing Points

## Axes, origin and ordered pairs
- The **x-axis** is horizontal and the **y-axis** is vertical.
- They cross at the **origin**, (0, 0).
- A point is written as an **ordered pair** (x, y). The first number tells you how far to move left or right, the second how far to move up or down.

## Quadrants
| Quadrant | Signs of (x, y) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | (+, +) | (3, 2) |
| II | (-, +) | (-3, 2) |
| III | (-, -) | (-3, -2) |
| IV | (+, -) | (3, -2) |

Points on an axis are not in any quadrant. A point on the y-axis has x = 0, such as (0, 5). A point on the x-axis has y = 0, such as (5, 0).

## Reflections
- Reflecting across the x-axis changes the sign of y: (2, -3) becomes (2, 3).
- Reflecting across the y-axis changes the sign of x: (2, -3) becomes (-2, -3).

## Solutions of an equation
An ordered pair is a solution if it makes the equation true.
Is (3, 7) a solution of y = 2x + 1? Substitute: 2(3) + 1 = 7. Yes.

## Using a table
For y = 3x - 2, when x = 4: y = 3(4) - 2 = 10, so (4, 10) is on the graph.

## Distance between two points
For (x1, y1) and (x2, y2), the distance is the square root of (x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2.
From (1, 2) to (4, 6): the square root of 3^2 + 4^2 = the square root of 25 = 5.

## Common mistakes
- Reversing the order and plotting (y, x).
- Forgetting that x comes first, even when it is 0.
- Thinking a point on an axis belongs to a quadrant.
