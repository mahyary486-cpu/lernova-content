# Velocity

Saying only how fast something moves is not always enough. A plane that flies at 250 m/s toward the north ends up in a very different place from a plane that flies at 250 m/s toward the south. To describe motion completely, we need both the speed and the direction. That combination is called velocity.

## Key ideas

Velocity is speed in a given direction. It is a vector quantity, which means it has both a size and a direction, so we write it like "3 m/s north" or "20 m/s to the east". Speed is the size of the velocity, and it has no direction.

To calculate velocity we use displacement instead of distance. Distance is the total length of the path that an object has traveled, and it is never negative. Displacement is the straight-line change in position from the start to the end, together with its direction. The average velocity is the displacement divided by the time taken. If an object walks 30 m north, the displacement is 30 m north. If it then walks back to the start, the displacement for the whole trip is zero, even though the distance traveled is 60 m.

This leads to an important idea: velocity can change even when the speed stays the same. If the direction changes, the velocity changes. A car moving around a circular track at a constant 20 m/s has a constant speed but a continuously changing velocity, because its direction keeps turning.

Velocities from different reference frames can be compared by adding them when they point the same way and subtracting them when they point in opposite directions. A passenger walking forward at 1 m/s on a train that moves at 20 m/s has a velocity of 21 m/s relative to the ground, but only 1 m/s relative to the train.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Velocity with direction.** A student walks 30 m north in 10 s. What is the velocity?
1. Divide the displacement by the time: 30 m / 10 s = 3 m/s.
2. Because velocity is a vector, add the direction.
3. The velocity is 3 m/s north.

**Example 2: A round trip.** A runner runs 200 m out and 200 m back to the start in a total of 100 s. Find the average speed and the average velocity.
1. The distance traveled is 200 m + 200 m = 400 m, so the average speed is 400 m / 100 s = 4 m/s.
2. The runner ended at the starting point, so the displacement is 0 m.
3. The average velocity is 0 m / 100 s = 0 m/s. The speed is 4 m/s, but the average velocity is zero.

**Example 3: Constant speed, changing velocity.** A car goes around a circular track at a steady 20 m/s. Is its velocity constant?
1. The speed is always 20 m/s.
2. At every moment the car faces a different direction.
3. Since the direction changes, the velocity is not constant.

## Common mistakes

Students often use the words "speed" and "velocity" as if they meant the same thing, but only velocity includes direction. A second mistake is to forget that the displacement can be zero after a long trip, which makes the average velocity zero. Finally, remember to state the direction whenever you give a velocity; "3 m/s" is only a speed.
