# Velocity

Saying only how fast something moves is not always enough. Velocity also says which way it moves.

## Key ideas
- **Velocity** = speed + direction. It is a vector.
- **Displacement** is the straight-line change in position with a direction. Average velocity = displacement / time.
- If direction changes, velocity changes, even when the speed is constant.
- A round trip has **zero displacement** and therefore zero average velocity.
- Add velocities in the same direction and subtract opposite ones when comparing frames.
- Unit: m/s with a direction such as north or east.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Velocity with direction.** A student walks 30 m north in 10 s.
1. v = 30 m / 10 s = 3 m/s.
2. Add the direction: 3 m/s north.

**Example 2: Round trip.** A runner runs 200 m out and 200 m back in 100 s.
1. Distance = 400 m so average speed = 400 / 100 = 4 m/s.
2. Displacement = 0 so average velocity = 0 m/s.

**Example 3: Constant speed, changing velocity.** A car goes around a circular track at a steady 20 m/s.
1. The speed stays at 20 m/s.
2. The direction keeps changing, so the velocity keeps changing.

## Common mistakes
- Using "speed" and "velocity" as if they were the same.
- Forgetting that displacement can be zero after a long trip.
- Giving a velocity without a direction.
