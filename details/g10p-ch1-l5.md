# Free Fall and Motion Graphs

An object moving only under the pull of gravity, with air resistance ignored, is in free fall. Graphs give a picture of motion and let us read velocity, acceleration and displacement.

## Key ideas
- **Free fall:** near Earth's surface every object has the same acceleration, g = 9.8 m/s^2 downward, regardless of mass. This applies going up, at the top and coming down.
- Use the constant-acceleration equations with a = -g when up is positive. From rest: v = gt and d = (1/2) g t^2.
- At the highest point of a throw, v = 0 but a is still g.
- **Position-time graph:** slope = velocity. A straight line means constant velocity; a curve means changing velocity.
- **Velocity-time graph:** slope = acceleration, and area between the line and the time axis = displacement.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Dropped stone.** A stone is dropped from rest. After 3.0 s:
1. v = gt = 9.8 x 3.0 = 29.4 m/s, about 29 m/s downward.
2. d = (1/2) g t^2 = 0.5 x 9.8 x 9.0 = 44.1 m.

**Example 2: Thrown upward.** A ball is thrown up at 19.6 m/s.
1. Time to top: 0 = 19.6 - 9.8t, so t = 2.0 s.
2. Height: h = v0^2 / (2g) = 384.16 / 19.6 = 19.6 m.
3. Total time in the air to return to the launch height is 2 x 2.0 = 4.0 s.

**Example 3: Cliff drop.** A rock falls from 78.4 m.
1. d = (1/2) g t^2, so t^2 = 2d / g = 156.8 / 9.8 = 16.
2. t = 4.0 s.

**Example 4: Area under a graph.** Velocity rises uniformly from 0 to 10 m/s in 4.0 s.
1. The area is a triangle: (1/2) x 4.0 x 10 = 20 m.
2. The slope is 10 / 4.0 = 2.5 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking heavier objects fall faster when air resistance is ignored.
- Setting a = 0 at the top of the flight.
- Mixing up the slope and the area of a velocity-time graph.
- Forgetting the square root when solving t^2 = 2d / g.
