# Protecting nature

Protecting nature means using natural resources carefully and taking action to keep ecosystems and species healthy.

## Key ideas

- **Sustainable use.** Resources should be used at a rate that lets them renew, so people today can meet their needs without taking resources from future generations.
- **The three Rs.** Reduce means using less. Reuse means using an item again. Recycle means turning waste into new materials. Recycling paper, glass, metals and plastics saves raw materials and energy and reduces garbage.
- **Protected areas.** National parks, wildlife refuges, protected areas and biosphere reserves are places where hunting, cutting trees and building are controlled. Iran has many protected areas, and they shelter species such as the Asiatic cheetah and the Persian fallow deer.
- **Laws and rules.** Governments make laws that limit hunting, fishing, logging and the release of pollution. Hunting seasons and fishing limits allow populations to recover.
- **Restoring habitats.** Planting trees, restoring wetlands and cleaning rivers help damaged ecosystems recover.
- **Saving water and energy.** Fixing leaks, using water carefully and turning off unneeded lights save resources. Renewable energy sources such as solar, wind and hydropower produce much less pollution than fossil fuels.
- **Cleaner living.** Public transport, bicycles and walking reduce air pollution. Treating wastewater and cutting use of harmful chemicals protect water and soil.
- **Protecting endangered species.** Conservation programs protect habitats, breed endangered animals in captivity and release them into the wild. Seed banks store seeds of rare plants.
- **Education and personal responsibility.** Every person can help by learning, making careful choices and encouraging others.

## Examples

**Example 1: A school recycling plan.** A school collects 40 kg of waste paper every week. If the school year has 30 weeks, the school collects 40 × 30 = 1200 kg of paper. Because this paper is sent to be recycled, fewer trees are needed and less waste is buried. This shows how a small habit becomes a large benefit over time.

**Example 2: Saving a species.** The population of an animal falls because its forest habitat is being cut. A government makes the forest a protected area, bans hunting and plants new trees along its edges. After several years the animal has more food and shelter, and its numbers slowly rise. This shows that protecting the habitat is the best way to protect the species.

**Example 3: Choosing a cleaner way.** A family replaces a short car trip with a bicycle ride. The car would have burned fuel and released carbon dioxide. The bicycle releases none. When many families do this, the air in a city becomes cleaner and people become healthier.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that protecting nature is only the job of governments. Individual choices matter as well.
- Thinking recycling alone is enough. Reducing and reusing come first because they avoid making waste at all.
- Believing that protected areas solve every problem. They also need rules that are enforced and people who support them.
- Believing that renewable energy has no effect at all. It is much cleaner, but building it still uses materials and land.

## Remember

- Reduce, reuse and recycle, in that order.
- Protecting habitats protects the species that live in them.
- Laws, science and personal choices all work together to protect nature.
