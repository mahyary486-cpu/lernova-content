# Reactivity of metals

This topic explains that metals differ in how easily they react, and how we can rank them in a reactivity series.

## Key ideas

Reactivity means how easily and how quickly a substance takes part in chemical reactions. Metals can be arranged in a reactivity series, from the most reactive to the least reactive:

potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminum, zinc, iron, lead, (hydrogen), copper, silver, gold

We can compare metals by watching how they react with oxygen, water and acids.

- With oxygen: Metals form metal oxides. Magnesium burns with a bright white flame: 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO. Iron reacts slowly and forms rust. Gold does not react with oxygen at all.
- With water: Potassium and sodium react very fast with cold water and make hydrogen gas and a metal hydroxide: 2Na + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + H2. Magnesium reacts very slowly with cold water. Copper and gold do not react.
- With dilute acids: Metals above hydrogen react and release hydrogen gas: Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2. Metals below hydrogen, such as copper, do not react with dilute acids.

Displacement reactions: A more reactive metal can take the place of a less reactive metal in a compound. For example, iron is more reactive than copper: Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu. A reddish layer of copper appears on an iron nail placed in copper sulfate solution. The reverse does not happen: copper cannot displace iron.

Reactivity explains how metals are found in nature. Gold and silver are found as pure elements because they are unreactive. Most other metals are found in compounds called ores, and we need chemical processes to obtain the metal. Very reactive metals must be stored carefully; sodium is kept under oil.

## Examples

Example 1: Predicting a reaction.
- Will zinc react with copper sulfate solution?
- Zinc is above copper in the series, so zinc is more reactive.
- Yes: Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu. Copper forms on the zinc.

Example 2: Predicting no reaction.
- Will silver react with dilute hydrochloric acid?
- Silver is below hydrogen in the series, so it cannot displace hydrogen from the acid.
- No reaction happens.

Example 3: Mass of product.
- A strip of magnesium of mass 2.4 g burns completely. What mass of magnesium oxide forms?
- Equation: 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO. Molar mass of Mg ≈ 24 g/mol, MgO ≈ 40 g/mol.
- Moles of Mg = 2.4 g ÷ 24 g/mol = 0.10 mol.
- The ratio Mg : MgO is 2 : 2, so 0.10 mol of MgO forms.
- Mass of MgO = 0.10 mol × 40 g/mol = 4.0 g.
- The extra 1.6 g comes from oxygen in the air.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a displacement reaction when the metal added is less reactive. If it is lower in the series, nothing happens.
- Forgetting to balance equations. For example, sodium and water need 2Na and 2H2O.
- Thinking that a faster reaction means more product. Speed and amount are different ideas.
- Confusing rusting with burning. Both involve oxygen, but rusting is much slower.

## Remember

- The reactivity series ranks metals by how easily they react.
- A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compound.
- Metals above hydrogen react with dilute acids and give off hydrogen gas.
