# Amphibians

Amphibians are vertebrates that usually start life in water and spend their adult life on land and in water.

## Key ideas

- The word amphibian means "double life". Frogs, toads, newts and salamanders are amphibians.
- Amphibians are ectothermic. In cold weather they become inactive and often rest in mud or under leaves.
- The skin is thin, smooth and moist, and has no scales. Oxygen can pass through the wet skin into blood vessels just under it. This is called skin breathing. The skin must stay moist, so amphibians live near water or in damp places.
- Young amphibians, called larvae or tadpoles, live in water and breathe with gills. They have a tail and no legs at first. Adults develop lungs and legs. Adult lungs are simple, so the skin gives extra oxygen.
- The heart of an amphibian has three chambers: two atria and one ventricle. Some oxygen-rich blood and oxygen-poor blood mix in the single ventricle. This is less efficient than the heart of a bird or mammal.
- Fertilization is usually external. The female lays soft eggs without shells in water. The eggs are covered with jelly. Many eggs are laid because many are eaten or dry out.
- Metamorphosis is the change in body form from larva to adult. For a frog, the stages are egg, tadpole with gills, tadpole with back legs, tadpole with four legs and a shrinking tail, and finally a young frog with lungs.
- Adult amphibians catch insects and worms. A frog uses a long sticky tongue. Tadpoles mostly eat plants and algae.
- Amphibians are important in food chains and are good indicators of pollution. Their thin skin takes in harmful substances easily, so a drop in their numbers can show that water or air is polluted.

## Examples

- Life cycle of a frog: A female lays hundreds of eggs in a pond. After about one to three weeks, tadpoles hatch and breathe with gills. Over several weeks they grow back legs, then front legs, and the tail becomes shorter. The young frog has lungs and can leave the water. This is a complete change, so we call it metamorphosis.
- Frog on a hot day: A frog sits in shade near water. If its skin dried, it could not take in oxygen well. By staying wet, it keeps skin breathing working.
- Disappearing frogs in a polluted pond: If farm chemicals enter the pond, tadpoles and adult frogs absorb them through the skin and may die. Scientists who notice that frogs are missing can conclude that the water is unhealthy.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking amphibians never live on land. Most adults spend a lot of time on land, but they must return to water or damp places.
- Thinking a tadpole is a different animal from a frog. It is the same animal at an earlier stage.
- Thinking amphibian eggs have hard shells. Their eggs are soft and jelly-covered.
- Thinking amphibians have scales. Their skin is smooth and moist.

## Remember

- Amphibians have moist skin, three-chamber hearts, and a life cycle with metamorphosis.
- Larvae breathe with gills; adults use lungs and skin.
- They need water or damp places to reproduce and to keep their skin wet.
