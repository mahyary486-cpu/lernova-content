# Distance and displacement

Distance and displacement are two different ways to describe how far an object has moved.

## Key ideas

- Motion means a change of position over time. To say where something is, we need a reference point, such as a house, a school, or the starting line.
- Distance is the total length of the path an object travels. It is a scalar quantity, which means it has size (magnitude) only. It is never negative.
- Displacement is the straight-line change in position from the starting point to the end point. It is a vector quantity, which means it has both size and direction. For example, "50 m north" is a displacement.
- Distance and displacement are measured in metres (m) in the SI system. Kilometres (km) are used for long trips.
- Distance is always greater than or equal to the size of the displacement. They are equal only when the object moves in a straight line without turning back.
- If an object returns to its starting point, its displacement is zero, even if the distance is large.
- When two movements are at right angles, we find the size of the displacement with the Pythagorean theorem: displacement = √(a² + b²).
- On a straight line, we choose one direction as positive (for example, east) and the opposite direction as negative (west). Then displacement can be written with a plus or minus sign.

## Examples

**Example 1: Two legs at right angles.** A student walks 30 m east and then 40 m north.
- Distance = 30 m + 40 m = 70 m.
- The two legs form the sides of a right triangle, so displacement = √(30² + 40²) = √(900 + 1600) = √2500 = 50 m.
- The direction is found from tan θ = 40 / 30 = 1.33, so θ ≈ 53° north of east.
- Answer: distance 70 m, displacement 50 m at about 53° north of east.

**Example 2: A round trip.** Sara walks from her home to a shop 150 m away and then walks back home.
- Distance = 150 m + 150 m = 300 m.
- She ends where she started, so displacement = 0 m.
- Answer: distance 300 m, displacement 0 m.

**Example 3: Moving back on a straight road.** A car drives 5 km east and then 2 km west along the same road.
- Distance = 5 km + 2 km = 7 km.
- Take east as positive: displacement = (+5 km) + (−2 km) = +3 km.
- Answer: distance 7 km, displacement 3 km east.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that distance and displacement are the same thing. They are equal only for straight-line motion in one direction.
- Forgetting to give a direction when writing a displacement. "50 m" is a distance; "50 m north" is a displacement.
- Saying that displacement is zero means the object did not move. It only means that the object ended at its starting point.
- Adding the legs of a right-angle trip to get the displacement. You must use the Pythagorean theorem instead.

## Remember

- Distance = length of the whole path (scalar, never negative).
- Displacement = straight line from start to end, with direction (vector).
- Distance ≥ size of displacement, and a round trip has zero displacement.
