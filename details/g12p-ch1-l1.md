# Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law

A balloon rubbed on hair sticks to a wall because of electric forces between charges, and Coulomb's law tells how strong they are.

## Key ideas
- **Like charges repel; unlike charges attract.**
- **e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C**; charge is quantized and conserved.
- **Coulomb's law**: **F = k q1 q2 / r^2**, k = 9 x 10^9 N m^2/C^2.
- The force is along the line between the charges; it is an **inverse-square** law.
- **Superposition**: add the forces as vectors.
- Conductors let charge move; insulators do not.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Force between charges.** Two charges of 2 x 10^-6 C and 3 x 10^-6 C are 0.3 m apart.
1. F = k q1 q2 / r^2 = 9 x 10^9 x 6 x 10^-12 / 0.09.
2. F = 0.6 N, repulsive.

**Example 2: Changing the distance.** The force between two charges is 8 N. The distance is doubled.
1. F is proportional to 1 / r^2.
2. F = 8 / 4 = 2 N.

**Example 3: Number of electrons.** A body has a charge of -3.2 x 10^-9 C.
1. n = Q / e = 3.2 x 10^-9 / 1.6 x 10^-19.
2. n = 2 x 10^10 extra electrons.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to square the distance.
- Using microcoulombs without converting to coulombs.
- Adding forces as numbers instead of vectors.
