# Matter and the Scientific Method in Chemistry

Chemistry is the study of matter and the changes it undergoes. Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space, so it includes the air, the water, the metals and every living thing. Before chemists can explain why materials behave as they do, they need a shared way of sorting matter, a shared system of measurement, and a reliable method for testing ideas. This lesson introduces all three, and it ends with the use of density and significant figures, which will appear in every calculation of the course.

## Key ideas

Matter is divided into pure substances and mixtures. A pure substance has a fixed composition. An element, such as oxygen gas (O2), contains only one kind of atom, and a compound, such as water (H2O), contains two or more elements bonded in a fixed ratio. A mixture combines substances physically, so the parts keep their own properties and can be present in different amounts. A homogeneous mixture, such as salt water, is uniform throughout, while a heterogeneous mixture, such as granite, has visible parts. Because the parts of a mixture are not bonded, they can be separated by physical methods, while a compound needs a chemical change to be broken down.

Changes are physical or chemical. A physical change, such as melting or dissolving, alters the form or state of a substance, but not its identity. A chemical change, such as rusting or burning, produces new substances with new properties. The question to ask is always whether a new substance has formed.

Measurements in science use SI units: the kilogram for mass, the meter for length, the second for time, the kelvin for temperature and the mole for the amount of substance. Density connects two of these measurements, d = m / V, with units of g/cm^3 or g/mL for solids and liquids. Density is a property of the material and does not depend on the size of the sample, so it helps to identify substances.

Chemists follow the scientific method: they make an observation, ask a question, propose a hypothesis, test it with a controlled experiment in which only one variable is changed, analyze the data and draw a conclusion. A hypothesis is a testable proposed explanation. A theory is a well-tested explanation supported by a large body of evidence, and a law describes a pattern in nature without explaining it.

Finally, every measurement has limited precision, which is shown by significant figures. All nonzero digits count, zeros between nonzero digits count, leading zeros never count, and trailing zeros count only when there is a decimal point. So 0.00340 has three significant figures. In multiplication and division, the answer has as many significant figures as the measurement with the fewest.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Finding density.** A metal sample has a mass of 54.0 g and a volume of 20.0 cm^3. Find its density.
1. Write the definition, d = m / V.
2. Substitute the values: d = 54.0 g / 20.0 cm^3.
3. The density is d = 2.70 g/cm^3, which fits aluminum.

**Example 2: Finding mass from density.** What is the mass of 25.0 mL of ethanol, which has a density of 0.789 g/mL?
1. Rearrange the definition to m = d x V.
2. Substitute: m = 0.789 g/mL x 25.0 mL.
3. The mass is m = 19.7 g, with three significant figures.

**Example 3: Volume by displacement.** An object of mass 28.0 g raises the water in a graduated cylinder from 40.0 mL to 44.0 mL. Find its density.
1. The volume of the object is the change in the level: 44.0 - 40.0 = 4.0 mL.
2. The density is 28.0 g / 4.0 mL.
3. The answer is 7.0 g/mL, with two significant figures because the volume has only two.

## Common mistakes

Students often divide volume by mass instead of mass by volume, which gives the wrong units. They also call a mixture a compound because it looks uniform, but a compound has a fixed composition and a mixture does not. A third mistake is to count leading zeros as significant. In displacement problems, many use the final water level as the volume, when the volume is the change in level. Finally, a hypothesis is not the same as a theory: a hypothesis is a proposed explanation, and a theory has already survived many tests.
