# Matter and the Scientific Method in Chemistry

Chemistry is the study of matter and the changes it undergoes. Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space.

## Key ideas
- **Pure substances** have fixed composition. An element (like O2) has one kind of atom, and a compound (like H2O) has elements bonded in a fixed ratio.
- **Mixtures** combine substances physically. Homogeneous mixtures (salt water) are uniform, and heterogeneous mixtures (granite) are not.
- **Physical change** alters form or state (melting, dissolving). **Chemical change** makes new substances (rusting, burning).
- **Density** = mass / volume, or d = m / V. Units are g/cm^3 or g/mL.
- **SI base units:** kilogram (mass), meter (length), second (time), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount).
- **Scientific method:** observation, question, hypothesis, controlled experiment, analysis, conclusion. A theory is a well-tested explanation, and a law describes a pattern.
- **Significant figures:** all nonzero digits count, zeros between digits count, leading zeros do not, and trailing zeros after a decimal point do.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Finding density.** A metal sample has mass 54.0 g and volume 20.0 cm^3.
1. Write d = m / V.
2. Substitute: d = 54.0 g / 20.0 cm^3.
3. d = 2.70 g/cm^3.

**Example 2: Finding mass from density.** What is the mass of 25.0 mL of ethanol (d = 0.789 g/mL)?
1. Rearrange: m = d x V.
2. m = 0.789 g/mL x 25.0 mL.
3. m = 19.7 g.

**Example 3: Volume by displacement.** An object of mass 28.0 g raises water in a cylinder from 40.0 mL to 44.0 mL.
1. Volume of object = 44.0 mL - 40.0 mL = 4.0 mL.
2. Density = 28.0 g / 4.0 mL.
3. Density = 7.00 g/mL.

## Common mistakes
- Dividing volume by mass instead of mass by volume.
- Calling a mixture a compound because it looks uniform.
- Counting leading zeros as significant (0.00340 has 3 significant figures).
- Using the final water level instead of the change in level when measuring displacement.
- Confusing a hypothesis (a testable proposed explanation) with a theory.
