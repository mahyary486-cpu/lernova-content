# Common types of forces

There are several everyday forces, such as weight, friction, normal force, spring force, and air resistance, and each one has its own cause and direction.

## Key ideas

- **Weight** is the force of gravity on an object. It points toward the center of the Earth. The formula is W = m × g, where g is about 10 N/kg near the Earth's surface (more exactly 9.8 N/kg). Weight is measured in newtons, and mass in kilograms.
- Weight changes from place to place, but mass does not. On the Moon, g is about 1.6 N/kg.
- **Normal force** (support force) is the push of a surface on an object that rests on it. It is perpendicular to the surface.
- **Friction** is a force that opposes sliding between two surfaces in contact. It acts opposite to the motion, or opposite to the push that tries to start motion.
  - Static friction acts when an object is not sliding yet.
  - Sliding friction acts when surfaces slide over each other.
  - Rough surfaces and heavier objects give more friction. Oil or water on a surface reduces friction.
  - Friction is useful for walking and braking, but it also wears parts and wastes energy as heat.
- **Spring (elastic) force** is the force of a stretched or compressed spring that tries to return to its original length. For a spring that is not overstretched, the stretch is proportional to the force.
- **Air resistance** is the friction of air against a moving object. It increases with speed and with the area facing the air.
- **Contact forces** need touching (friction, normal force). **Non-contact forces** act at a distance (gravity, magnetic and electric forces).

## Examples

**Example 1: Weight on Earth and on the Moon.** A student has a mass of 60 kg.
- On Earth: W = m × g = 60 kg × 10 N/kg = 600 N.
- On the Moon: W = 60 kg × 1.6 N/kg = 96 N.
- The mass is 60 kg in both places. Only the weight changes.

**Example 2: Pulling a backpack.** A backpack of mass 3 kg is pulled along the floor with a force of 20 N. Sliding friction is 12 N.
- Weight = 3 kg × 10 N/kg = 30 N. The normal force from the floor is also 30 N, so these balance.
- Net horizontal force = 20 N - 12 N = 8 N in the direction of the pull.
- Acceleration = 8 N / 3 kg ≈ 2.7 m/s^2.

**Example 3: A spring.** A spring stretches by 4 cm when a force of 2 N pulls on it.
- The stretch is proportional to the force.
- For 6 N, the force is 3 times larger (6 N / 2 N = 3).
- The stretch is 3 × 4 cm = 12 cm.

**Example 4: A skydiver.** At the start of a jump, weight is much bigger than air resistance, so the skydiver speeds up. Air resistance grows with speed. When it equals the weight, the net force is zero, and the skydiver falls at a constant speed.

## Common mistakes

- Using "weight" and "mass" as the same thing. Weight is a force in newtons, and mass is an amount of matter in kilograms.
- Thinking friction always acts backward from the direction of motion in every case. It opposes sliding, and sometimes it pushes us forward, such as when we walk.
- Thinking that the normal force always equals the weight. This is true only on a flat surface with no other vertical forces.
- Forgetting that a spring law works only up to a limit. A spring that is overstretched stays deformed.

## Remember

- Weight W = m × g, and it points down.
- Friction opposes sliding, and the normal force is perpendicular to the surface.
- Mass stays the same everywhere, but weight depends on gravity.
