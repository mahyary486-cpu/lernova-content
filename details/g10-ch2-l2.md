# Deductive Reasoning

## Inductive and deductive reasoning
- **Inductive reasoning:** look at several examples, find a pattern and make a **conjecture**. The conclusion is only likely, and one counterexample can show it is wrong.
- **Deductive reasoning:** start from facts, definitions and rules that are true and reach a conclusion that **must** be true.

Example of inductive reasoning: 2, 6, 18, 54, ... Each term is 3 times the one before, so the next term is probably 162.

## Law of Detachment
If **p implies q** is true and **p** is true, then **q** is true.
- "If a student studies, then she passes." Maria studied. So **Maria passes.**

## Law of Syllogism
If **p implies q** is true and **q implies r** is true, then **p implies r** is true.
- "If it snows, school is closed." "If school is closed, we stay home." So **if it snows, we stay home.**

## Counterexamples
One counterexample is enough to show a conjecture is false.
- Conjecture: "The sum of two numbers is greater than each of them." Counterexample: -3 and 5. Their sum is 2, which is not greater than 5.

## Worked examples
1. Pattern 1, 4, 9, 16, ... The terms are squares, so the next term is **25**.
2. Given: "If a polygon is a triangle, then its angles add up to 180 degrees." A polygon ABCD has angles that add up to 360 degrees. Then ABCD is **not a triangle**. (This uses the contrapositive.)
3. Given: "If a figure is a square, then it has four sides." A figure has four sides. Can you conclude it is a square? **No.** Many figures have four sides.

## Common mistakes
- **Affirming the conclusion:** from "if p, then q" and "q is true", you cannot conclude p. The Law of Detachment needs the **hypothesis** p to be true.
- Calling an argument deductive when it only uses a pattern from examples.
- Believing that many examples prove a statement. Only a proof does.
