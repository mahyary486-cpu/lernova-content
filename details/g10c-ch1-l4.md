# Electron Configuration

An electron configuration shows how the electrons of an atom are arranged in shells and subshells. It explains the periodic table and chemical behavior.

## Key ideas
- Subshell capacity: s holds 2, p holds 6, d holds 10 and f holds 14 electrons. Each orbital holds 2.
- A shell n holds 2n^2 electrons, so n = 1 holds 2, n = 2 holds 8, and n = 3 holds 18.
- **Aufbau principle:** fill the lowest energy subshell first. Order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s.
- **Pauli exclusion principle:** an orbital holds at most two electrons, with opposite spins.
- **Hund rule:** electrons fill orbitals of the same subshell one at a time with parallel spins before pairing.
- **Valence electrons** are those in the outermost shell. Noble gas shorthand, such as [Ar] 4s2 3d6, replaces the inner core.
- Exceptions: chromium is [Ar] 4s1 3d5 and copper is [Ar] 4s1 3d10, because half-filled and filled d subshells are extra stable.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Oxygen (8 electrons).**
1. Fill 1s2 (2 electrons) and 2s2 (4 total).
2. Place the remaining 4 in 2p.
3. Configuration: 1s2 2s2 2p4. Valence electrons: 2 + 4 = 6.

**Example 2: Iron (26 electrons).**
1. 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 4s2 uses 20 electrons.
2. The remaining 6 go into 3d.
3. Configuration: 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 4s2 3d6, or [Ar] 4s2 3d6.

**Example 3: Unpaired electrons in oxygen.**
1. The 2p subshell has three orbitals and holds 4 electrons.
2. Hund rule: three orbitals get one electron each, then the fourth pairs up.
3. Two orbitals have a single electron, so there are 2 unpaired electrons.

## Common mistakes
- Filling 3d before 4s. In the Aufbau order, 4s is filled first.
- Pairing electrons in a p subshell before each orbital has one.
- Counting all electrons as valence electrons. Only the outermost shell counts.
- Forgetting that a positive ion loses electrons from the outermost shell first.
