# Operations with Radicals

Radicals can be added, subtracted, multiplied and divided. The rules look like the rules for variables, once the radicals are simplified.

## Adding and subtracting
Only **like radicals** can be combined. Like radicals have the same number under the root, just as like terms have the same variable part. Add or subtract the coefficients and keep the radical.

**Example 1.** Simplify 3 sqrt(5) + 4 sqrt(5).
1. The radicals are alike.
2. Add the coefficients: 3 + 4 = 7.
3. Result: 7 sqrt(5).

**Example 2.** Simplify sqrt(12) + sqrt(27).
1. Simplify each: sqrt(12) = 2 sqrt(3) and sqrt(27) = 3 sqrt(3).
2. Now they are like radicals: 2 sqrt(3) + 3 sqrt(3).
3. Result: 5 sqrt(3).

Always simplify first. Terms such as sqrt(2) + sqrt(3) cannot be combined, so the sum stays as written.

## Multiplying
Multiply the coefficients and multiply the numbers under the roots: a sqrt(b) * c sqrt(d) = ac sqrt(bd).

**Example 3.** Simplify 2 sqrt(6) * 3 sqrt(2).
1. Coefficients: 2 * 3 = 6.
2. Radicands: sqrt(6 * 2) = sqrt(12) = 2 sqrt(3).
3. Result: 6 * 2 sqrt(3) = 12 sqrt(3).

To multiply a sum, use the distributive property or FOIL.

**Example 4.** Expand (3 + sqrt(2))(3 - sqrt(2)).
1. 9 - 3 sqrt(2) + 3 sqrt(2) - 2.
2. The middle terms cancel.
3. Result: 7.

This is the pattern (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2. The expressions 3 + sqrt(2) and 3 - sqrt(2) are called conjugates.

## Dividing
Divide coefficients and divide radicands, then simplify. For example, 12 sqrt(30) / (4 sqrt(6)) = 3 sqrt(5).

## Rationalizing with a conjugate
To remove a radical from a denominator such as 1 + sqrt(2), multiply top and bottom by its conjugate 1 - sqrt(2). The new denominator is 1 - 2 = -1, so 1 / (1 + sqrt(2)) = (1 - sqrt(2)) / (-1) = sqrt(2) - 1.

## Common mistakes
- Adding radicands: sqrt(2) + sqrt(3) is not sqrt(5).
- Adding 3 sqrt(2) + 4 sqrt(3) to get 7 sqrt(5).
- Forgetting to simplify radicals before trying to combine them.
- Squaring a binomial wrongly: (3 + sqrt(2))^2 = 9 + 6 sqrt(2) + 2 = 11 + 6 sqrt(2), not 9 + 2.
