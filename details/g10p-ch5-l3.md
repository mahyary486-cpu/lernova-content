# Conservation of Momentum

When objects interact, they push on each other with equal and opposite forces for the same time (Newton's third law). Their impulses are equal and opposite, so one object gains exactly the momentum the other loses. The total stays the same.

## Key ideas
- **Law:** if the net external force on a system is zero, the total momentum is constant: **p_before = p_after**.
- An **isolated system** has no net external force, such as skaters on very smooth ice or objects in space.
- For two objects: **m1 v1 + m2 v2 = m1 v1' + m2 v2'** (primes mean after).
- Strategy: pick a positive direction, list the momenta before and after with signs, set the totals equal, and solve.
- A system that starts at rest has total momentum zero, so after an explosion or push the pieces move in opposite directions.

## Example 1: Rifle recoil
A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Find the recoil speed.
1. Total p before = 0.
2. 0 = 4.0 v + 0.010 x 400.
3. v = -4.0 / 4.0 = -1.0 m/s, so the rifle recoils at 1.0 m/s.

## Example 2: Colliding carts
A 1.0 kg cart moves right at 6.0 m/s and meets a 2.0 kg cart moving left at 1.0 m/s. Afterward the 1.0 kg cart moves left at 1.0 m/s. Find the velocity of the 2.0 kg cart.
1. p before = 1.0 x 6.0 + 2.0 x (-1.0) = 4.0 kg m/s.
2. p after = 1.0 x (-1.0) + 2.0 x v.
3. 4.0 = -1.0 + 2.0 v, so v = 2.5 m/s to the right.

## Example 3: Astronaut
An 80 kg astronaut at rest throws a 2.0 kg tool at 5.0 m/s.
1. 0 = 80 v + 2.0 x 5.0.
2. v = -10 / 80 = -0.125 m/s.
3. The astronaut drifts backward at 0.125 m/s.

## Why a falling ball seems to break the law
A dropped ball gains momentum because Earth's gravity is an external force on the ball alone. If the system is ball plus Earth, Earth gains an equal and opposite momentum and the total is conserved. The Earth is so massive that its change in velocity is too small to notice.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting negative signs for motion in the negative direction.
- Applying conservation to a system where a large external force, such as friction, acts during the interaction.
- Assuming that equal and opposite momenta mean equal speeds. Speeds are equal only when the masses are equal.
- Using masses or velocities from after the event in the before total.
