# Position, Distance and Displacement

Physics describes motion by saying where an object is and how that changes. In one dimension, motion happens along a straight line, so a single number with a sign is enough to say where something is.

## Key ideas
- **Position (x)** is the location of an object relative to a chosen origin on a number line. One direction is chosen as positive, the other as negative. Position x = -3 m means 3 m on the negative side of the origin.
- **Distance (d)** is the total length of the path traveled. It is a scalar: it has size but no direction, and it is never negative.
- **Displacement (Δx)** is the change in position: Δx = x_final - x_initial. It is a vector: its sign gives the direction.
- Distance is always greater than or equal to the size of the displacement. They are equal only for straight motion with no reversal.
- Unit: the meter (m) in SI.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Out and back.** A hiker starts at x = 0, walks 40 m east (positive), then 15 m west.
1. Signed moves: +40 m and -15 m.
2. Displacement = 40 + (-15) = +25 m (25 m east).
3. Distance = 40 + 15 = 55 m.

**Example 2: Crossing the origin.** A cart moves from x = +12 m to x = -5 m.
1. Δx = x_final - x_initial = -5 - 12.
2. Δx = -17 m, so it moved 17 m in the negative direction.
3. Because it did not reverse, the distance is also 17 m.

**Example 3: A lap.** A runner completes one 400 m lap and stops at the starting line.
1. Final position equals initial position, so Δx = 0 m.
2. The distance traveled is 400 m.
3. This shows that a large distance can have zero displacement.

## Common mistakes
- Adding magnitudes to get displacement. Displacement uses signs, so a move in the opposite direction subtracts.
- Treating displacement as always positive. A negative answer is meaningful: it gives the direction.
- Using the distance in place of the displacement when the path reverses.
- Forgetting to state the origin and positive direction before using signs.
