# Acceleration and Changing Velocity

Whenever velocity changes, either in size or in direction, the object accelerates. Speeding up, slowing down and turning around are all acceleration.

## Key ideas
- **Average acceleration** a = Δv / Δt = (v - v0) / t. It is a vector.
- The SI unit is meters per second squared (m/s^2): m/s of change every second.
- **Sign rule:** if velocity and acceleration point the same way, the object speeds up. If they point opposite ways, it slows down.
- A negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down. It depends on the direction of the velocity.
- Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration. A ball at the top of its flight has v = 0 but still accelerates at 9.8 m/s^2 downward.
- Rearranged: v = v0 + at.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Speeding up.** A cyclist goes from 5.0 m/s to 25 m/s in 4.0 s.
1. Δv = 25 - 5.0 = 20 m/s.
2. a = 20 / 4.0 = 5.0 m/s^2.

**Example 2: Braking.** A car slows from 20 m/s to rest in 5.0 s.
1. Δv = 0 - 20 = -20 m/s.
2. a = -20 / 5.0 = -4.0 m/s^2.
3. The minus sign says the acceleration points opposite to the motion.

**Example 3: Reversing direction.** A ball's velocity changes from +8.0 m/s to -6.0 m/s in 2.0 s.
1. Δv = -6.0 - 8.0 = -14 m/s.
2. a = -14 / 2.0 = -7.0 m/s^2.

**Example 4: Unit conversion first.** A car goes from 0 to 100 km/h in 8.0 s.
1. 100 km/h = 100 / 3.6 = 27.8 m/s.
2. a = 27.8 / 8.0 = 3.5 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes
- Subtracting in the wrong order. Always use final minus initial.
- Believing negative acceleration means slowing. Compare the signs of v and a.
- Thinking v = 0 means a = 0.
- Mixing km/h with seconds without converting.
