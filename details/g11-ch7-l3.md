# Conditional Probability and Independence

## Conditional probability
**P(B | A)** is the probability of B **given that A has happened**.
**P(B | A) = P(A and B) / P(A)**

Example: P(A and B) = 0.2 and P(A) = 0.5. Then P(B | A) = 0.2/0.5 = **0.4**.

Example with a table: 50 students, 30 take Spanish, and 20 of those also take art. P(art | Spanish) = 20/30 = **2/3**. (Not 20/50, which is P(Spanish and art).)

## The multiplication rule
**P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B | A)**

## Independent events
Events are **independent** if one does not change the probability of the other. Then P(B | A) = P(B), and
**P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B)**
- Two coins both heads: 1/2 * 1/2 = **1/4**.
- Two dice both show 6: 1/6 * 1/6 = **1/36**.

**Testing for independence:** P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, P(A and B) = 0.2. Since 0.4 * 0.5 = 0.2, the events **are independent**.

## Dependent events (without replacement)
Drawing without putting back changes the next probability.
- A bag has 5 red and 3 blue marbles. Two red in a row without replacement: 5/8 * 4/7 = 20/56 = **5/14**.
- With replacement: 5/8 * 5/8 = 25/64.
- Two aces from a deck without replacement: 4/52 * 3/51 = **1/221**.

## Common mistakes
- Using P(A) * P(B) when the events are dependent.
- Dividing by the wrong total in conditional probability. The denominator is P(A), the **given** event.
- Confusing "independent" with "mutually exclusive". Mutually exclusive events with positive probabilities are never independent.
