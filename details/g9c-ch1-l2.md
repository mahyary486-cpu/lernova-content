# Physical Properties

You can describe a block of copper without changing it: it is reddish, shiny and heavy. These descriptions are physical properties.

## Key ideas
- A **physical property** can be observed without changing the substance's identity.
- Examples: color, density, melting and boiling point, hardness, solubility, conductivity.
- Properties like **density** and **melting point** do not depend on the amount and help to identify substances.
- **Mass** and **volume** depend on the amount of matter.
- Metals: shiny, **malleable**, **ductile**, good conductors. Nonmetals: dull, **brittle**, poor conductors.
- Magnetism is a physical property used to separate iron from sand.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Identifying a metal.** A shiny solid conducts electricity and can be hammered flat.
1. Shiny, conducting and malleable are properties of metals.
2. The solid is probably a metal.

**Example 2: Separating by a property.** How can iron filings be separated from sand?
1. Iron is magnetic and sand is not.
2. A magnet pulls the iron filings out.

**Example 3: Using melting point.** A solid melts sharply at 0 C.
1. Pure substances have a fixed melting point.
2. A melting point of 0 C is the property of ice (water).

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a physical property can only be seen with the eyes.
- Thinking mass identifies a substance. It depends on the amount.
- Confusing physical properties with chemical ones.
