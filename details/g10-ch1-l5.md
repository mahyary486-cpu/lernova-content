# Dilations

## What a dilation does
A dilation changes the size of a figure but not its shape. It needs
- a **center** (a fixed point), and
- a **scale factor** k.

Every point moves along the line from the center so that its distance from the center becomes k times as large.

## Rule about the origin
If the center is the origin, then **(x, y) becomes (kx, ky)**.

## What changes and what stays the same
| Quantity | Effect of scale factor k |
|---|---|
| Angle measures | stay the same |
| Side lengths | multiplied by k |
| Perimeter | multiplied by k |
| Area | multiplied by k squared |

- If k is greater than 1, the image is an **enlargement**.
- If k is between 0 and 1, the image is a **reduction**.
- If k = 1, the image is the same size (and congruent).
- The image is **similar** to the preimage, because the shape is the same.

## Finding the scale factor
Scale factor = (image length) / (original length).

## Worked examples
1. Dilate P(3, -2) with scale factor 3 about the origin. P' = (9, -6).
2. A side of length 4 is dilated by scale factor 2.5. The new side is 4 x 2.5 = **10**.
3. A triangle has area 12 and is dilated by scale factor 3. The new area is 12 x 3^2 = **108**.
4. A segment of length 6 becomes a segment of length 4. The scale factor is 4 / 6 = **2/3**, which is a reduction.
5. Dilate A(6, 8) with scale factor 1/2 about the origin. A' = **(3, 4)**.

## Common mistakes
- Multiplying the area by k instead of k squared.
- Thinking a dilation keeps the figure congruent. Only a scale factor of 1 does.
- Forgetting that a scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the figure smaller, not larger.
