# Atomic structure and electron arrangement

This topic describes what atoms are made of and how their electrons are arranged in shells.

## Key ideas

An atom has a tiny, dense nucleus in the center and electrons moving around it. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.

- Proton: relative charge +1, relative mass 1.
- Neutron: charge 0, relative mass 1.
- Electron: charge -1, mass almost zero (about 1/1840 of a proton).

Most of the mass of an atom is in the nucleus. Most of the volume of an atom is empty space where electrons move.

Important numbers:
- Atomic number (Z) = number of protons. It identifies the element.
- Mass number (A) = number of protons + number of neutrons.
- Number of neutrons = A - Z.
- In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons, so the total charge is zero.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. For example, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 are both chlorine.

Electron arrangement: Electrons are placed in energy levels called shells. Electrons fill the shell closest to the nucleus first. For the first 20 elements, the shells hold at most 2, 8, 8 and then 2 electrons in the first, second, third and fourth shells (the fourth shell receives its first electrons only after the third has 8). The electrons in the outermost shell are called valence electrons. They decide how an atom reacts. An atom with a full outer shell, such as helium (2) or neon (2,8), is very stable and unreactive.

When an atom gains or loses electrons it becomes an ion. Losing electrons makes a positive ion; gaining electrons makes a negative ion. The nucleus does not change.

## Examples

Example 1: Chlorine-37.
- Atomic number Z = 17, so there are 17 protons and 17 electrons.
- Mass number A = 37, so neutrons = 37 - 17 = 20.
- Electron arrangement: 2 in shell 1, 8 in shell 2, the remaining 17 - 10 = 7 in shell 3. Written as 2,8,7.
- Chlorine has 7 valence electrons.

Example 2: Calcium.
- Z = 20, so 20 electrons.
- Shell 1: 2, shell 2: 8, shell 3: 8, shell 4: the remaining 20 - 18 = 2.
- Arrangement: 2,8,8,2. Calcium has 2 valence electrons.

Example 3: The sodium ion.
- A sodium atom has Z = 11 and arrangement 2,8,1.
- It loses its one valence electron and becomes Na+.
- Na+ has 11 protons and 11 - 1 = 10 electrons, arrangement 2,8, the same as neon.
- Charge = +11 - 10 = +1.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up atomic number and mass number. The atomic number counts only protons.
- Saying that the number of protons changes when an atom becomes an ion. Only electrons change.
- Putting more than 8 electrons in the third shell when working with the first 20 elements.
- Forgetting to subtract the atomic number when calculating neutrons.

## Remember

- Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons.
- Shells fill in the order 2, 8, 8, 2 for the first 20 elements.
- Valence electrons decide chemical behavior.
