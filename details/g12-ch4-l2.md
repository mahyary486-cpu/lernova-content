# The Unit Circle

The unit circle is the circle x^2 + y^2 = 1 centered at the origin. It turns angles into numbers by giving a point for every angle.

## Key ideas
- For an angle theta in standard position, the terminal side meets the unit circle at the point (cos theta, sin theta).
- Therefore tan theta = sin theta / cos theta, defined only when cos theta is not 0.
- Since the point lies on the circle, sin^2 theta + cos^2 theta = 1.
- **Signs by quadrant:** Q I all positive; Q II only sine positive; Q III only tangent positive; Q IV only cosine positive.
- **Reference angle:** the acute angle between the terminal side and the x-axis. Find the trig value of the reference angle, then apply the sign of the quadrant.
- **Special points:** pi/6 gives (sqrt(3)/2, 1/2); pi/4 gives (sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)/2); pi/3 gives (1/2, sqrt(3)/2). The axis angles give (1, 0), (0, 1), (-1, 0), (0, -1) for 0, pi/2, pi, 3pi/2.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Find sin(5pi/6).
1. 5pi/6 is in Quadrant II. Its reference angle is pi - 5pi/6 = pi/6.
2. sin(pi/6) = 1/2, and sine is positive in Quadrant II.
3. So sin(5pi/6) = 1/2.

**Example 2.** Find cos(210 degrees).
1. 210 degrees is in Quadrant III with reference angle 30 degrees.
2. cos 30 degrees = sqrt(3)/2, and cosine is negative in Quadrant III.
3. So cos(210 degrees) = -sqrt(3)/2.

**Example 3.** Given cos theta = -3/5 with theta in Quadrant II, find sin theta and tan theta.
1. sin^2 theta = 1 - 9/25 = 16/25, so sin theta = 4/5 (positive in Quadrant II).
2. tan theta = (4/5) / (-3/5) = -4/3.

## Negative and large angles
Coterminal angles have equal values: sin(-pi/3) = -sin(pi/3) = -sqrt(3)/2, and cos(9pi/4) = cos(pi/4) = sqrt(2)/2.

## Common mistakes
- Swapping the roles: cosine is the x-coordinate and sine is the y-coordinate.
- Forgetting the sign from the quadrant after finding the reference angle.
- Taking the reference angle from the y-axis instead of the x-axis.
- Choosing the wrong sign for the square root when solving sin^2 + cos^2 = 1.
