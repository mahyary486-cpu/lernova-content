# Dispersion, Color and the Electromagnetic Spectrum

A rainbow shows that white light is a mixture of colors, each bent by a slightly different amount as it passes through raindrops. The visible colors are only a tiny part of a much larger family of waves, the electromagnetic spectrum, which runs from radio waves to gamma rays. All of them are the same kind of wave, a vibrating electric and magnetic field, and they differ only in frequency and wavelength.

## Key ideas

The refractive index of a material depends slightly on the wavelength of the light. This is called dispersion. Violet light has the largest index and bends the most, while red light bends the least. When white light enters a prism, the different colors leave at different angles and spread into a spectrum. A rainbow is formed in the same way by water drops, with the colors separated by refraction and reflection inside each drop.

All electromagnetic waves travel through a vacuum at the same speed, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m/s, and they obey the wave relation c = f lambda, where f is the frequency and lambda the wavelength. A higher frequency means a shorter wavelength. The spectrum, in order of increasing frequency, runs through radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays. Visible light covers wavelengths from about 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red).

The energy of a photon, a packet of light, is E = h f, where h is Planck's constant, so higher frequency means higher energy per photon. That is why ultraviolet light can damage skin while infrared only warms it, and why X-rays and gamma rays are the most penetrating and dangerous. Radio waves and microwaves have long wavelengths and very low photon energies, and they are used for communication and cooking. They are electromagnetic waves, not sound waves, and they travel through empty space.

Color depends on how an object treats light. For light, the primary colors are red, green and blue, and mixing all three gives white. A colored object reflects its own color and absorbs the others: a red apple reflects red light and absorbs most of the rest. Pigments work by subtraction, so their primaries, such as cyan, magenta and yellow, are different from those of light.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Wavelength.** A microwave oven uses a frequency of 2.45 x 10^9 Hz. Find the wavelength.
1. Use lambda = c / f = 3.00 x 10^8 / 2.45 x 10^9.
2. The wavelength is lambda = 0.12 m.
3. This is about 12 cm, which explains the size of features in the oven.

**Example 2: Frequency.** Green light has a wavelength of 550 nm. Find its frequency.
1. Convert: lambda = 550 x 10^-9 = 5.5 x 10^-7 m.
2. Use f = c / lambda = 3.00 x 10^8 / 5.5 x 10^-7.
3. The frequency is f = 5.45 x 10^14 Hz.

**Example 3: Comparing photons.** Compare the photon energy of ultraviolet light at 300 nm with infrared light at 1000 nm. Use h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s.
1. For ultraviolet: E = h c / lambda = 6.63 x 10^-34 x 3.00 x 10^8 / 3.0 x 10^-7 = 6.6 x 10^-19 J.
2. For infrared: E = 6.63 x 10^-34 x 3.00 x 10^8 / 1.0 x 10^-6 = 2.0 x 10^-19 J.
3. The ultraviolet photon has about 3.3 times more energy, as its wavelength is 3.3 times shorter.

## Common mistakes

Students often think that red light bends the most in a prism, but violet bends the most. Another mistake is to mix up the primary colors of light with those of pigments. Many treat radio waves as if they were sound waves, but they are electromagnetic waves that need no medium. Finally, remember to convert nanometers to meters before using c = f lambda.
