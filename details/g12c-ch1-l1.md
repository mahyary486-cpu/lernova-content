# Acid-Base Theories and Conjugate Pairs

Acid-base reactions are proton transfers, and every transfer creates a conjugate pair.

## Key ideas
- **Bronsted-Lowry**: acid = **proton donor**; base = **proton acceptor**.
- **Conjugate pair**: differ by one **H+**.
- **Lewis**: acid = electron-pair **acceptor**; base = electron-pair **donor**.
- **Amphoteric** substances act as both (water, HCO3-).
- Strong acid = **weak** conjugate base.
- Water: **2 H2O <-> H3O+ + OH-**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Conjugate pairs.** Identify the pairs in NH3 + H2O <-> NH4+ + OH-.
1. NH3 accepts H+: base, conjugate acid NH4+.
2. H2O donates H+: acid, conjugate base OH-.

**Example 2: Conjugate base.** What is the conjugate base of H2SO4?
1. Remove one H+ from H2SO4.
2. The conjugate base is HSO4-.

**Example 3: Amphoteric.** Write HCO3- acting as an acid with water.
1. It donates a proton.
2. HCO3- + H2O <-> CO3^2- + H3O+.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking conjugates differ by an OH.
- Confusing amphoteric with amphiprotic only in water.
- Forgetting that the conjugate of a strong acid is very weak.
