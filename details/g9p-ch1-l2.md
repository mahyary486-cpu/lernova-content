# SI Units and Measurement

A measurement is a number plus a unit. Using the same system of units lets scientists everywhere compare their results.

## Key ideas
- The **SI base units** used most in physical science: length in meters (m), mass in kilograms (kg), time in seconds (s), temperature in kelvins (K).
- **Metric prefixes**: kilo (k) = 1000, centi (c) = 1/100, milli (m) = 1/1000, micro = one millionth, mega (M) = one million.
- **Derived units** combine base units: area in m^2, volume in m^3 or liters (1 L = 1000 mL = 1000 cm^3), speed in m/s, density in kg/m^3 or g/cm^3.
- Temperature: K = degrees C + 273 (more exactly 273.15). A change of 1 K equals a change of 1 degree C.
- **Accuracy** is how close a measurement is to the true value. **Precision** is how close repeated measurements are to each other.
- Read a scale at eye level. Record every certain digit and estimate one more digit. For a liquid in a graduated cylinder read the bottom of the meniscus.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Choosing a unit.** Which SI unit fits the length of a classroom?
1. A classroom is a few meters long.
2. The meter is the SI base unit of length, so the room is about 8 m long.

**Example 2: Reading a ruler.** A ruler is marked every 0.1 cm. The end of a pencil lies between 4.3 and 4.4 cm, about halfway.
1. The certain digits are 4.3 cm.
2. Estimate one more digit: halfway gives 0.05 cm.
3. Record 4.35 cm. The last digit (5) is the estimated one.

**Example 3: Celsius to kelvin.** Water is at 25 degrees C. What is its temperature in kelvins?
1. K = C + 273.
2. K = 25 + 273 = 298 K.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up mass (kg) and weight (a force in newtons).
- Reading the scale from an angle instead of at eye level.
- Forgetting the prefix, for example treating 5 mm as 5 m.
- Thinking precise and accurate mean the same thing. Repeated readings can agree with each other and still be wrong.
