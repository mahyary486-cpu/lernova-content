# Galaxies and distances in space

A galaxy is a very large group of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity, and this lesson also explains the units used to measure the huge distances in space.

## Key ideas

- Our Sun is one star in the Milky Way galaxy. The Milky Way has hundreds of billions of stars. Its diameter is about 100,000 light-years. The Sun is about 26,000 light-years from the center, in one of the spiral arms.
- From Earth, the Milky Way looks like a pale band of light across the dark sky. We see it from inside the galaxy.
- Galaxies have three main shapes. Spiral galaxies have a bright center and curved arms. Elliptical galaxies are round or oval and contain mostly older stars. Irregular galaxies have no clear shape.
- The universe contains billions of galaxies. Galaxies are often found in groups called clusters.
- The Andromeda galaxy is the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way. It is about 2.5 million light-years away. It can be seen without a telescope in a very dark sky.
- Kilometres are too small to describe distances in space, so astronomers use larger units.
  - Astronomical unit (AU): the average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 1.5 × 10^8 km. It is used inside the solar system.
  - Light-year (ly): the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 × 10^12 km. It is used for stars and galaxies.
- The nearest star after the Sun is Proxima Centauri, about 4.24 light-years away.
- Light needs time to travel. When we see an object that is 1000 light-years away, we see it as it was 1000 years ago. Looking far away means looking back in time.

## Examples

Example 1: How many kilometres are in one light-year?
- Seconds in one year = 365.25 × 24 × 3600 = 31,557,600 s.
- Distance = speed × time = 300,000 km/s × 31,557,600 s.
- Distance ≈ 9.47 × 10^12 km. This is about 9.46 × 10^12 km when the exact speed of light is used.

Example 2: How far is the Andromeda galaxy in kilometres?
- Distance = 2.5 million ly = 2.5 × 10^6 ly.
- Distance = 2.5 × 10^6 × 9.46 × 10^12 km.
- Distance ≈ 2.4 × 10^19 km.
- Light from Andromeda that we see tonight left the galaxy 2.5 million years ago.

Example 3: How long would a spacecraft take to reach Proxima Centauri?
- A fast space probe, Voyager 1, moves at about 17 km/s.
- Distance = 4.24 ly × 9.46 × 10^12 km/ly ≈ 4.0 × 10^13 km.
- Time = 4.0 × 10^13 / 17 ≈ 2.4 × 10^12 s.
- In years: 2.4 × 10^12 / 3.16 × 10^7 ≈ 75,000 years.
- Travel to even the nearest star is not possible with today's technology.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that the Sun is the center of the Milky Way. The Sun is far from the center.
- Mixing up a galaxy and the universe. A galaxy is only one group of stars among billions.
- Using the astronomical unit for distances to stars. The astronomical unit is too small for that, so light-years are used.
- Thinking that all the stars we see belong to other galaxies. The stars seen by the naked eye are in the Milky Way, except for a few very faint objects such as Andromeda.

## Remember

- A galaxy is held together by gravity, and its main types are spiral, elliptical and irregular.
- 1 AU ≈ 1.5 × 10^8 km, and 1 ly ≈ 9.46 × 10^12 km.
- The farther away an object is, the older the light that we see from it.
