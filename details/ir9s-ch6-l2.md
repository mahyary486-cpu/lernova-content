# Seafloor spreading

Seafloor spreading is the process in which new ocean floor forms at underwater mountain chains and slowly moves away on both sides.

## Key ideas
- After the 1940s, ships used sound waves to map the ocean floor. Scientists found a long chain of underwater mountains called the mid-ocean ridge. It runs through all the major oceans, such as the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
- In the early 1960s Harry Hess proposed seafloor spreading. Hot rock from the mantle rises under the ridge, melts partly and erupts as magma. The magma cools and becomes new oceanic crust. The older crust is pushed sideways, away from the ridge.
- Evidence for seafloor spreading:
  - Age of rocks: rocks right at the ridge are young. Rocks become older farther from the ridge on both sides. The oldest ocean floor is only about 180 million years old, much younger than the continents.
  - Magnetic stripes: iron-rich minerals in cooling lava line up with Earth's magnetic field. Earth's field has reversed many times, so the ocean floor shows stripes of normal and reversed magnetism. The pattern is the same on both sides of the ridge, like a mirror image.
  - Sediment: a thin layer of mud lies on the crust near the ridge, and it becomes thicker farther away, because the older crust has collected mud for longer.
- Old ocean floor is destroyed at deep ocean trenches, where it sinks back into the mantle. So the size of Earth stays the same. This gives a reason for continental drift: continents ride on moving plates.
- Typical spreading rates are 2 to 10 cm per year. The Atlantic spreads at about 2.5 cm per year.

## Examples
- Example 1, distance moved: At 2.5 cm per year, how far does the sea floor move in 100 million years? Distance = 2.5 × 100,000,000 = 250,000,000 cm. Divide by 100,000 to get kilometers: 2,500 km.
- Example 2, age of rock: A rock sample is 1,000 km from a ridge. The floor spreads at 5 cm per year. First, 1,000 km = 100,000,000 cm. Time = 100,000,000 / 5 = 20,000,000 years. The rock is about 20 million years old.
- Example 3, magnetic stripes: A ship finds a stripe of reversed magnetism 40 km to the east of a ridge. It also finds a reversed stripe 40 km to the west. This matching is expected because the same lava formed at the ridge and then split in two directions.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the ocean floor is flat and old. It has mountains and trenches, and it is younger than most continental rock.
- Thinking the ocean floor is pushed by the ocean water. It moves with the plate underneath.
- Believing Earth grows bigger as new crust forms. Crust is destroyed at trenches at about the same rate.
- Mixing up the ridge and the trench: crust forms at a ridge and is destroyed at a trench.

## Remember
- New crust forms at mid-ocean ridges and the floor spreads outward.
- Evidence: younger rocks near the ridge, mirror-image magnetic stripes, thinner sediment near the ridge.
- Distance = rate × time, with careful unit conversion.
