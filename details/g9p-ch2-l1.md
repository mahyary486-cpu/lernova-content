# Reference Points and Speed

Everything moves, but we describe motion by comparing with something else. A passenger is at rest compared with the train, yet moving compared with the ground.

## Key ideas
- Motion is a change of position relative to a **reference point**.
- **Speed** = distance / time, v = d / t. SI unit: m/s. Common unit: km/h.
- **Average speed** = total distance / total time. **Instantaneous speed** is the speed at one moment.
- Rearranged: d = v x t and t = d / v.
- For a trip in several parts, add all distances and add all times. Do not average the speeds.
- Speed is a scalar: it has size but no direction.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Average speed.** A car travels 150 km in 2 hours.
1. v = d / t = 150 km / 2 h.
2. v = 75 km/h.

**Example 2: Distance from speed.** A cyclist rides at 6 m/s for 40 s.
1. d = v x t = 6 m/s x 40 s.
2. d = 240 m.

**Example 3: A trip in two parts.** A runner runs 100 m in 20 s and then 100 m in 30 s.
1. Total distance = 100 + 100 = 200 m.
2. Total time = 20 + 30 = 50 s.
3. Average speed = 200 / 50 = 4 m/s (not the average of 5 m/s and 3.33 m/s).

## Common mistakes
- Averaging two speeds instead of using total distance over total time.
- Mixing units, such as km with seconds, without converting.
- Thinking an object at rest in one frame is at rest in every frame.
