# Gears and wheel and axle

Gears and the wheel and axle are machines that turn rotation into a larger force or a larger speed.

## Key ideas

- A wheel and axle is a large wheel fixed to a smaller axle so that both turn together. Examples: a steering wheel, a door knob, a screwdriver handle, a water well winch.
- If the effort acts on the wheel and the load acts on the axle, the mechanical advantage is wheel radius / axle radius. A small effort on the big wheel gives a large force at the axle.
- If the effort acts on the axle and the wheel is the output, the machine gives more speed and distance, but less force. A car wheel works this way.
- A gear is a wheel with teeth. Two gears with meshing teeth pass rotation from one to another.
- The driver gear is the gear that is turned by the motor or by you. The driven gear is the gear that it turns.
- Two meshing gears turn in opposite directions. A middle gear (an idler) makes the first and last gear turn the same way.
- Gear ratio = number of teeth on the driven gear / number of teeth on the driver gear.
- Speed of driven gear = speed of driver × (driver teeth / driven teeth).
- A large driven gear turns slower but gives a larger torque. A small driven gear turns faster but gives a smaller torque.
- Gears on a bicycle let the rider choose between more force (for hills) and more speed (for flat roads).

## Examples

Example 1: A pair of gears. The driver has 20 teeth and turns at 90 rotations per minute. The driven gear has 60 teeth.
- Gear ratio = 60 / 20 = 3.
- Speed of driven gear = 90 × 20 / 60 = 30 rotations per minute.
- The driven gear turns three times slower, gives about three times the torque, and turns in the opposite direction.

Example 2: A wheel and axle. The wheel radius is 0.30 m and the axle radius is 0.05 m. A rope on the axle lifts a bucket weighing 600 N.
- Mechanical advantage = 0.30 / 0.05 = 6.
- Ideal effort on the wheel = 600 / 6 = 100 N.

Example 3: A bicycle. The front gear (chainring) has 48 teeth and the rear sprocket has 16 teeth. The wheel radius is 0.35 m.
- The rear wheel turns 48 / 16 = 3 times for each full turn of the pedals.
- Wheel circumference = 2 × 3.14 × 0.35 ≈ 2.20 m.
- Distance for one pedal turn ≈ 3 × 2.20 ≈ 6.6 m.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the teeth numbers upside down. For speed, use driver teeth / driven teeth. For gear ratio, use driven teeth / driver teeth.
- Forgetting that meshing gears turn in opposite directions.
- Using the diameter in the wheel and axle formula for one part and the radius for the other part. Use the same quantity for both.
- Believing that a gear can increase both speed and torque. If one goes up, the other goes down.

## Remember

- Wheel and axle mechanical advantage = wheel radius / axle radius.
- Gear ratio = driven teeth / driver teeth.
- More torque means less speed, and more speed means less torque.
