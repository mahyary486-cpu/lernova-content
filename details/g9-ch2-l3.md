# Slope-Intercept Form

## The form
**y = mx + b**
- **m** is the slope.
- **b** is the y-intercept, the point (0, b) where the line crosses the y-axis.

## Reading an equation
y = 2x + 5 has slope 2 and y-intercept 5.
2x + y = 7 is not yet in the form. Solve for y: y = -2x + 7. Now the slope is -2 and the y-intercept is 7.

## Writing an equation
**From slope and intercept.** Slope 3 and y-intercept -4 give y = 3x - 4.

**From two points.** Through (0, 1) and (2, 7):
1. Slope = (7 - 1) / (2 - 0) = 3.
2. The point (0, 1) shows b = 1.
3. Equation: y = 3x + 1.

**From a point and a slope.** Slope 2 through (1, 5):
1. Start with y = 2x + b.
2. Substitute x = 1 and y = 5: 5 = 2 + b, so b = 3.
3. Equation: y = 2x + 3.

## Finding intercepts
- y-intercept: set x = 0.
- x-intercept: set y = 0. For y = 2x - 6: 0 = 2x - 6, so x = 3.

## Graphing
Plot (0, b). Then use rise over run: from the point, go up by the numerator and right by the denominator of the slope. Connect the points with a line.

## Real-world model
A gym charges $20 to join plus $15 per month. After m months the total cost is C = 15m + 20. The slope 15 is the monthly rate and the y-intercept 20 is the fixed starting cost.

## Common mistakes
- Swapping m and b.
- Forgetting to solve for y before reading the slope.
- Using the sign of b wrongly: y = 3x - 4 has b = -4.
