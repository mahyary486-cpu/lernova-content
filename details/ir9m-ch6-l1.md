# The coordinate plane

The coordinate plane is a flat surface with two number lines that let us describe every point by a pair of numbers.

## Key ideas

- The horizontal number line is the x-axis. The vertical number line is the y-axis. They cross at the origin O(0, 0).
- Every point has an ordered pair (x, y). The first number tells how far to move right (positive) or left (negative). The second number tells how far to move up (positive) or down (negative). The order matters: (2, 5) and (5, 2) are different points.
- The axes divide the plane into four quadrants, numbered counterclockwise starting at the top right:
  - Quadrant I: x > 0 and y > 0.
  - Quadrant II: x < 0 and y > 0.
  - Quadrant III: x < 0 and y < 0.
  - Quadrant IV: x > 0 and y < 0.
- A point on an axis belongs to no quadrant. A point on the x-axis has y = 0. A point on the y-axis has x = 0.
- Reflection in the x-axis changes (x, y) to (x, -y). Reflection in the y-axis changes (x, y) to (-x, y).
- Distance between A(x1, y1) and B(x2, y2): d = sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2). This comes from the Pythagorean theorem.
- Midpoint of AB: M = ((x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2).

## Worked examples

Example 1. Name the quadrant of A(3, -2) and find its reflection in the x-axis.

Step 1: x = 3 is positive and y = -2 is negative, so A is in Quadrant IV.
Step 2: Reflection in the x-axis keeps x and changes the sign of y.
Step 3: The reflected point is (3, 2).

Example 2. Find the distance between A(1, 2) and B(4, 6), and the midpoint of A and B.

Step 1: The horizontal difference is 4 - 1 = 3. The vertical difference is 6 - 2 = 4.
Step 2: d = sqrt(3^2 + 4^2) = sqrt(9 + 16) = sqrt(25) = 5.
Step 3: The midpoint is ((1 + 4)/2, (2 + 6)/2) = (5/2, 4) = (2.5, 4).

Example 3. Three vertices of a rectangle are A(1, 1), B(5, 1) and C(5, 4). Find the fourth vertex D, the area and the perimeter.

Step 1: A and B have the same y, so AB is horizontal. B and C have the same x, so BC is vertical. Therefore D must have the same x as A and the same y as C.
Step 2: D = (1, 4).
Step 3: The length AB = 5 - 1 = 4. The length BC = 4 - 1 = 3.
Step 4: Area = 4 × 3 = 12 square units.
Step 5: Perimeter = 2 × (4 + 3) = 14 units.
Step 6: Check with the diagonal: AC = sqrt(4^2 + 3^2) = 5 and BD = sqrt((1 - 5)^2 + (4 - 1)^2) = sqrt(16 + 9) = 5. The diagonals are equal, as they must be in a rectangle.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the coordinates in the wrong order. Always give x first and y second.
- Forgetting the sign when moving left or down. For (-3, 2), move 3 units left, not right.
- Saying that a point such as (0, 4) is in Quadrant I. It lies on the y-axis, so it is in no quadrant.
- In the distance formula, forgetting to square the differences or forgetting the square root at the end.

## Remember

- A point is written (x, y): first across, then up or down.
- The four quadrants go counterclockwise, and signs of x and y tell you the quadrant.
- Distance uses the Pythagorean theorem. The midpoint is the average of the coordinates.
