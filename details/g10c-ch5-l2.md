# Molar Mass and Mole Conversions

The molar mass of a substance is the mass in grams of one mole of it. It is the bridge between the mass you weigh on a balance and the number of moles or particles.

## Key ideas
- The molar mass of an element in g/mol equals its atomic mass from the periodic table (C = 12.01 g/mol, O = 16.00 g/mol).
- The molar mass of a compound is the sum of the molar masses of all atoms in its formula.
- Mass to moles: moles = mass (g) / molar mass (g/mol).
- Moles to mass: mass (g) = moles x molar mass (g/mol).
- Mass to particles: grams to moles to particles. Moles are always the middle step.
- Constants used: H = 1.008, C = 12.01, O = 16.00, Na = 22.99, Cl = 35.45 g/mol; N_A = 6.02 x 10^23 per mol.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Molar mass.** Find the molar mass of Ca(OH)2.
1. One Ca: 40.08. Two O: 2 x 16.00 = 32.00. Two H: 2 x 1.008 = 2.016.
2. Total = 40.08 + 32.00 + 2.016 = 74.10 g/mol.

**Example 2: Mass to moles.** How many moles are in 36.0 g of water (18.02 g/mol)?
1. moles = 36.0 g / 18.02 g/mol.
2. moles = 2.00 mol.

**Example 3: Mass to molecules.** How many molecules are in 9.00 g of water?
1. Moles = 9.00 g / 18.02 g/mol = 0.499 mol.
2. Molecules = 0.499 mol x 6.02 x 10^23 = 3.01 x 10^23 molecules.

**Example 4: Moles to mass.** What is the mass of 2.50 mol of NaCl?
1. Molar mass = 22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44 g/mol.
2. Mass = 2.50 mol x 58.44 g/mol = 146 g.

## Common mistakes
- Using the atomic number instead of the atomic mass.
- Ignoring subscripts or the number outside parentheses when adding up atoms.
- Dividing when you should multiply. Check units: g divided by g/mol gives mol.
- Trying to go straight from grams to particles without passing through moles.
