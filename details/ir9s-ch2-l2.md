# Ionic bonds

An ionic bond is the strong electric attraction between positive and negative ions that forms when electrons move from one atom to another.

## Key ideas

An ionic bond usually forms between a metal and a nonmetal. The metal atom loses electrons and becomes a cation. The nonmetal atom gains those electrons and becomes an anion. The oppositely charged ions then attract each other strongly.

- Electron transfer: the metal gives, the nonmetal receives.
- The total number of electrons lost must equal the total number gained.
- The compound as a whole is electrically neutral. The positive and negative charges cancel.
- Ionic compounds do not form separate molecules. Ions pack in a repeating three-dimensional pattern called a crystal lattice. Each ion is surrounded by ions of opposite charge.
- Because of this, we write a formula unit, which shows the simplest ratio of ions. For sodium chloride the formula unit is NaCl.

Steps for writing the formula of an ionic compound:

1. Write the symbol and charge of each ion.
2. Choose numbers of each ion so the total positive charge equals the total negative charge.
3. Write the formula with small numbers (subscripts) for the ratio. A subscript of 1 is not written.

Naming: write the metal name first, then the nonmetal name with the ending "-ide". Example: sodium + chlorine gives sodium chloride.

## Examples

Example 1: Sodium and chlorine.
- Na has electron arrangement 2, 8, 1. It loses 1 electron: Na+.
- Cl has 2, 8, 7. It gains 1 electron: Cl-.
- Charges: (+1) + (-1) = 0, so the ratio is 1 : 1.
- Formula: NaCl.

Example 2: Magnesium and chlorine.
- Mg has 2, 8, 2. It loses 2 electrons: Mg2+.
- Each Cl- gains only 1 electron, so two Cl- are needed.
- Charges: (+2) + 2 × (-1) = 0.
- Formula: MgCl2.

Example 3: Aluminium and oxygen.
- Al forms Al3+ and O forms O2-.
- Find a common total charge: the lowest common multiple of 3 and 2 is 6.
- Ions needed: 6 ÷ 3 = 2 Al3+ and 6 ÷ 2 = 3 O2-.
- Check: 2 × (+3) + 3 × (-2) = +6 - 6 = 0.
- Formula: Al2O3.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the formula as a molecule, such as "one NaCl molecule". Ionic compounds are lattices, not molecules.
- Forgetting to balance charges, for example writing MgCl instead of MgCl2.
- Showing the charges inside the final formula. We write MgCl2, not Mg2+Cl2-.
- Thinking that the electrons are shared in an ionic bond. They are transferred.

## Remember

- Ionic bond = transfer of electrons, then attraction between opposite ions.
- It forms between metals and nonmetals.
- The total charge of an ionic compound is zero.
