# Zeroes and graphs of polynomials

The zeroes of p(x) are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y = p(x) meets the x-axis. A linear polynomial gives a straight line; a quadratic gives a parabola that opens upward if a > 0 and downward if a < 0.

A parabola can cut the x-axis at two points, touch it at one point, or miss it, giving two, one (repeated) or no real zeroes. A cubic graph can cut the x-axis at up to three points.
