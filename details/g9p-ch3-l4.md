# Newton's Third Law

You cannot push without being pushed back. When you push a wall, the wall pushes you with an equal force.

## Key ideas
- **Newton's third law**: forces come in pairs. If A pushes B with a force, B pushes A with an **equal and opposite** force.
- The pair forces act on **different objects**, so they do **not cancel**.
- A rocket pushes gas down and the gas pushes the rocket up.
- A swimmer pushes the water back and the water pushes the swimmer forward.
- Equal forces do not mean equal accelerations: use a = F / m with each object's own mass.
- Name a pair as "A on B" and "B on A".

## Worked examples
**Example 1: A rocket.** Why does a rocket rise?
1. The rocket pushes hot gas downward.
2. By the third law the gas pushes the rocket upward with an equal force.

**Example 2: Two skaters.** Skater A (60 kg) and skater B (40 kg) push apart with 120 N on each.
1. a_A = 120 / 60 = 2 m/s^2.
2. a_B = 120 / 40 = 3 m/s^2.
3. The forces are equal but the lighter skater accelerates more.

**Example 3: Walking.** How do you move forward when you walk?
1. Your foot pushes backward on the ground.
2. The ground pushes forward on your foot, and this force moves you forward.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking action and reaction cancel. They act on different objects.
- Thinking the bigger object feels a bigger force. The forces are equal.
- Confusing a pair with two forces on the same object, such as weight and the table's push.
