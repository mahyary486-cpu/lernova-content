# Triangle Angle Sum

## The angle sum
The three interior angles of every triangle add up to **180 degrees**.

## Classifying triangles
| By angles | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acute | all three angles are less than 90 degrees |
| Right | one angle is exactly 90 degrees |
| Obtuse | one angle is more than 90 degrees |
| Equiangular | all three angles are equal (each 60 degrees) |

| By sides | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scalene | no sides are equal |
| Isosceles | at least two sides are equal |
| Equilateral | all three sides are equal |

In a **right triangle** the two acute angles add up to 90 degrees (they are complementary).

## Exterior angles
Extend one side of a triangle. The angle outside the triangle, next to an interior angle, is an **exterior angle**. It forms a linear pair with its adjacent interior angle.

**Exterior Angle Theorem:** an exterior angle equals the **sum of the two remote interior angles** (the two interior angles that are not next to it).

## Worked examples
1. Two angles of a triangle are 50 and 60 degrees. The third angle is 180 - 110 = **70 degrees**.
2. An exterior angle measures 125 degrees and one remote interior angle is 45 degrees. The other remote interior angle is 125 - 45 = **80 degrees**.
3. The angles of a triangle are x, 2x and 3x. Then 6x = 180, so x = 30 and the angles are **30, 60 and 90** degrees. It is a right triangle.
4. One acute angle of a right triangle is 34 degrees. The other acute angle is 90 - 34 = **56 degrees**.

## Common mistakes
- Using the exterior angle theorem with the adjacent interior angle. You must add the two **remote** angles.
- Forgetting that a triangle can have only one right or obtuse angle.
- Calling a triangle with two equal sides "equilateral". That is isosceles. Equilateral needs three equal sides.
