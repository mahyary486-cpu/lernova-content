# Current, Resistance and Ohm's Law

Current is like water flow in a pipe: voltage is the pressure and resistance is how narrow or long the pipe is.

## Key ideas
- **I = delta Q / delta t**; unit **ampere**.
- **Ohm's law: V = I R**.
- **R = rho L / A**.
- Doubling the **length** doubles R; doubling the **area** halves R.
- Metals: R **increases** with temperature.
- An ohmic conductor gives a **straight V-I line** through the origin.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Current.** A charge of 30 C passes a point in 10 s.
1. I = Q / t = 30 / 10.
2. I = 3 A.

**Example 2: Ohm's law.** A 12 V battery drives a current through a 4 ohm resistor.
1. I = V / R = 12 / 4.
2. I = 3 A.

**Example 3: Resistance of a wire.** A wire has rho = 1.7 x 10^-8 ohm m, L = 10 m and A = 1 x 10^-6 m^2.
1. R = rho L / A = 1.7 x 10^-8 x 10 / 10^-6.
2. R = 0.17 ohm.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the current is used up in a resistor.
- Using diameter in place of area.
- Confusing resistivity with resistance.
