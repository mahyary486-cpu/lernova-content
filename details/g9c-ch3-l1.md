# Solids, Liquids and Gases

Ice, water and steam are the same substance in three different states. What changes is how the particles are arranged and how they move.

## Key ideas
- A **solid** has a definite shape and volume; its particles vibrate in fixed positions.
- A **liquid** has a definite volume but no definite shape; its particles slide past each other.
- A **gas** has no definite shape or volume; its particles are far apart and move quickly in all directions.
- Gases are easily **compressed** because there is a lot of empty space between particles.
- **Plasma** is very hot, ionized gas found in stars.
- Solids and liquids are hard to compress because their particles are already close.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Sorting states.** Classify a rock, juice and air.
1. The rock keeps its shape and volume: a solid.
2. Juice takes the shape of the glass but keeps its volume: a liquid.
3. Air fills any container: a gas.

**Example 2: Compression.** Why can air in a syringe be pushed in, but water cannot?
1. Air particles are far apart with much empty space.
2. Water particles are already close together, so water is almost incompressible.

**Example 3: Particle motion.** Do particles in a solid move?
1. They cannot move from place to place.
2. They vibrate around fixed positions, so they are still moving.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking particles in a solid do not move.
- Thinking a gas weighs nothing.
- Thinking a liquid can be compressed as easily as a gas.
