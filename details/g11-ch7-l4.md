# Describing Data: Center and Spread

## Measures of center
- **Mean:** the sum divided by the number of values.
- **Median:** the middle value of the ordered data (the average of the two middle values if there is an even number).
- **Mode:** the most frequent value.

Data: 2, 4, 4, 6, 9. Mean = 25/5 = **5**, median = **4**, mode = **4**.

## Measures of spread
- **Range:** maximum - minimum. For the data above, 9 - 2 = **7**.
- **Quartiles:** Q1 is the median of the lower half, Q3 is the median of the upper half.
- **Interquartile range (IQR):** IQR = Q3 - Q1.

Data: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15. Lower half 1, 3, 5, 7 has Q1 = 4. Upper half 9, 11, 13, 15 has Q3 = 12. IQR = **8**.

## Standard deviation
The standard deviation measures the typical distance from the mean.
1. Find the mean.
2. Square each deviation from the mean.
3. Average the squares (this is the variance).
4. Take the square root.

Data: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. The mean is 5. The squared deviations are 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16, which add up to 32. The variance is 32/8 = 4, so the standard deviation is **2**.

## Outliers
An **outlier** is a value far from the rest. A common rule: a value is an outlier if it is above **Q3 + 1.5 * IQR** or below **Q1 - 1.5 * IQR**. With Q3 = 12 and IQR = 8 the upper fence is 12 + 12 = **24**.
- The mean and standard deviation are **strongly affected** by outliers.
- The median and IQR are **resistant** to outliers.
For skewed data or data with outliers, use the median and IQR.

## Changing the data
- Add a constant c to every value: the **mean** and median increase by c, the **standard deviation stays the same**.
- Multiply every value by k: the mean and the standard deviation are **both multiplied by k** (by the absolute value of k for the standard deviation).

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to order the data before finding the median.
- Using the mean for data with a big outlier.
- Thinking that adding a constant changes the spread.
