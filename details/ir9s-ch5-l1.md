# Balanced forces and net force

A force is a push or a pull, and when several forces act on one object we add them to find the single overall force called the net force.

## Key ideas

- A force is measured in newtons (N). A force has a size and a direction, so we draw it as an arrow. A longer arrow means a bigger force.
- Forces act on an object at the same time. The object responds to all of them together, not to each one separately.
- The net force is the sum of all forces on an object, taking direction into account.
- If two forces act in the same direction, add them: net force = F1 + F2.
- If two forces act in opposite directions, subtract the smaller from the larger. The net force points in the direction of the larger force.
- Forces are balanced when the net force is zero. Two balanced forces are equal in size and opposite in direction.
- Forces are unbalanced when the net force is not zero. Unbalanced forces change the motion of an object: they can speed it up, slow it down, or change its direction.
- A balanced force does not mean "no force". Many forces can act on an object that stays at rest or moves at constant velocity.
- A book resting on a table has two forces on it: its weight pulls down, and the table pushes up. They are equal, so the book stays at rest.

## Examples

**Example 1: Tug of war.** Team A pulls to the right with 30 N and team B pulls to the left with 20 N.
- The forces are in opposite directions, so subtract.
- Net force = 30 N - 20 N = 10 N.
- The direction is to the right, because team A pulls harder.
- The rope accelerates to the right.

**Example 2: Two students pushing a box.** Two students push a box in the same direction. One pushes with 15 N and the other with 25 N.
- The forces are in the same direction, so add.
- Net force = 15 N + 25 N = 40 N in the direction of the push.

**Example 3: A box at rest on the floor.** A box has a mass of 5 kg. Take the pull of gravity as about 10 N for each kilogram.
- Weight = 5 kg × 10 N/kg = 50 N, pointing down.
- The floor pushes up on the box with a force of 50 N.
- Net force = 50 N - 50 N = 0 N.
- The forces are balanced, so the box stays at rest.

**Example 4: A car on a straight road.** A car moves at a constant velocity of 20 m/s. The engine pushes it forward with 800 N. Friction and air resistance push backward with a total of 800 N.
- Net force = 800 N - 800 N = 0 N.
- The forces are balanced, so the velocity does not change, even though the car is moving.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that an object at rest has no forces on it. Forces such as weight and the support force are still acting, but they cancel.
- Adding forces without checking direction. Forces in opposite directions must be subtracted.
- Believing that a moving object always needs a net force. At constant velocity the net force is zero.
- Forgetting that the net force has a direction, not only a size. Always say which way it points.

## Remember

- Net force is the combined effect of all forces on an object.
- Balanced forces: net force = 0 N, so the motion does not change.
- Unbalanced forces: net force is not zero, so the motion changes.
