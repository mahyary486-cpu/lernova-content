# Periodic Families and Trends

Knowing a few families lets you predict the behavior of many elements at once.

## Key ideas
- **Alkali metals** (Group 1): soft, very reactive, 1 valence electron, form 1+ ions.
- **Alkaline earth metals** (Group 2): 2 valence electrons, form 2+ ions.
- **Halogens** (Group 17): reactive nonmetals, 7 valence electrons, form 1- ions.
- **Noble gases** (Group 18): unreactive, full outer shell.
- **Metals** tend to **lose** electrons; **nonmetals** tend to **gain** electrons.
- **Metalloids** (such as silicon) have properties of both and are used as semiconductors.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Family names.** Name the family of sodium and the family of chlorine.
1. Sodium is in Group 1, the alkali metals.
2. Chlorine is in Group 17, the halogens.

**Example 2: Why helium is unreactive.** Helium has 2 electrons.
1. Its only shell is full.
2. A full outer shell makes it stable and unreactive.

**Example 3: Predicting an ion.** What ion does potassium form?
1. Potassium is in Group 1 with 1 valence electron.
2. It loses 1 electron and forms K+.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking all metals are equally reactive.
- Thinking halogens are metals.
- Forgetting that noble gases are unreactive because their shells are full.
