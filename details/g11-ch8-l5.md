# Trigonometric Identities

An **identity** is an equation that is true for every value of the variable (where it is defined).

## Basic identities
- **Quotient:** tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, and cot θ = cos θ / sin θ
- **Reciprocal:** csc θ = 1 / sin θ, **sec θ = 1 / cos θ**, cot θ = 1 / tan θ
- **Pythagorean:** **sin² θ + cos² θ = 1**
  - 1 + tan² θ = sec² θ
  - 1 + cot² θ = csc² θ
- **Even and odd:** cos(-θ) = cos θ and sin(-θ) = -sin θ

## Using the Pythagorean identity
If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is in quadrant I: cos² θ = 1 - 9/25 = 16/25, so cos θ = **4/5** (positive in QI).
Also 1 - cos² θ = **sin² θ**.

## Simplifying expressions
- sec θ cos θ = (1/cos θ)(cos θ) = **1**.
- sin θ cot θ = sin θ * (cos θ / sin θ) = **cos θ**.
- tan θ cos θ = **sin θ**.
- (1 - sin x)(1 + sin x) = 1 - sin² x = **cos² x**.
- sin² θ / (1 - cos θ) = (1 - cos θ)(1 + cos θ) / (1 - cos θ) = **1 + cos θ**.

## Sum and double angle formulas
- sin(a + b) = sin a cos b + cos a sin b
- cos(a + b) = cos a cos b - sin a sin b
- **sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ**
- cos 2θ = cos² θ - sin² θ

Examples:
- With sin θ = 3/5 and cos θ = 4/5: sin 2θ = 2(3/5)(4/5) = **24/25**.
- sin 75° = sin(45° + 30°) = (sqrt(2)/2)(sqrt(3)/2) + (sqrt(2)/2)(1/2) = **(sqrt(6) + sqrt(2))/4**.

## Proving an identity
Work on **one side** (usually the more complicated one) until it matches the other side. Common steps: change everything to sine and cosine, use the Pythagorean identity, combine fractions.

## Common mistakes
- Writing sin(a + b) = sin a + sin b. It is false.
- Writing sin² θ as sin(θ²). The notation sin² θ means (sin θ)².
- Working on both sides of the equation at the same time when proving an identity.
