# The solar system and its planets

The solar system is the Sun together with all the objects that travel around it, including the planets, their moons, asteroids and comets.

## Key ideas

- The Sun is a star. It contains more than 99% of the mass of the solar system. Its gravity keeps all the other objects in orbit.
- There are eight planets. In order from the Sun they are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.
- The inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are called terrestrial planets. They are small, rocky and dense, with few or no moons.
- Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants. They are made mainly of hydrogen and helium. Uranus and Neptune are ice giants, with much water, ammonia and methane. All four have rings and many moons.
- The asteroid belt is between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. It contains millions of rocky objects.
- A planet is an object that orbits the Sun, is nearly round because of its own gravity, and has cleared other large objects from its orbit. Pluto is not a planet because it has not cleared its orbit. It is a dwarf planet.
- Distances in the solar system are measured in astronomical units (AU). 1 AU is the Earth-Sun distance, about 1.5 × 10^8 km. Jupiter is about 5.2 AU from the Sun and Neptune is about 30 AU.
- Mercury is closest to the Sun and has very big temperature changes between day and night. Venus is the hottest planet, about 465 °C, because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat. Mars is cold and red because of iron oxide in its dust. Jupiter is the largest planet. Saturn has the most visible rings.
- Planets farther from the Sun take more time to complete one orbit. Earth takes about 365 days. Mars takes about 687 days.

## Examples

Example 1: How long does light take to travel from the Sun to Neptune?
- Neptune is about 30 AU from the Sun, and 1 AU ≈ 1.5 × 10^8 km.
- Distance = 30 × 1.5 × 10^8 = 4.5 × 10^9 km.
- Time = distance / speed = 4.5 × 10^9 / 3 × 10^5 = 1.5 × 10^4 s.
- In hours: 15,000 / 3600 ≈ 4.2 hours.

Example 2: What is the speed of Earth in its orbit?
- The orbit is nearly a circle with radius 1.5 × 10^8 km.
- Circumference = 2 × 3.14 × 1.5 × 10^8 ≈ 9.4 × 10^8 km.
- Time for one orbit = 365 days × 24 × 3600 = 3.15 × 10^7 s.
- Speed = 9.4 × 10^8 / 3.15 × 10^7 ≈ 30 km/s.
- We travel at about 30 km/s, or about 107,000 km/h, but we do not feel it.

Example 3: Why is Venus hotter than Mercury?
- Mercury is closer to the Sun, but it has almost no atmosphere, so it cannot keep its heat at night.
- Venus has a very thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide that traps heat, like a strong greenhouse effect.
- So the surface of Venus is hotter, about 465 °C, than the day side of Mercury, about 430 °C.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that the planet closest to the Sun is the hottest. Venus is hotter than Mercury.
- Saying that Pluto is a planet. It is a dwarf planet.
- Mixing up gas giants and ice giants. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, while Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.
- Thinking that planets make their own light. They only reflect sunlight.

## Remember

- Eight planets: four rocky inner planets and four giant outer planets.
- 1 AU ≈ 1.5 × 10^8 km.
- Venus is the hottest planet because of its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere.
