# Electromagnetic Induction and Faraday's Law

Moving a magnet into a coil makes a current flow. That is how electricity is generated in power stations.

## Key ideas
- **Flux Phi = B A cos(theta)**; unit **weber**.
- **Faraday**: **emf = -N delta Phi / delta t**.
- **Lenz's law**: the induced current **opposes the change** in flux.
- Moving rod: **emf = B L v**.
- Generator: **emf = N B A omega sin(omega t)**.
- No change in flux means **no emf**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Induced emf.** A coil of 100 turns sees the flux change from 0.04 Wb to 0.01 Wb in 0.1 s.
1. emf = N delta Phi / delta t = 100 x 0.03 / 0.1.
2. emf = 30 V.

**Example 2: Moving rod.** A 0.4 m rod moves at 5 m/s perpendicular to a 0.3 T field.
1. emf = B L v = 0.3 x 0.4 x 5.
2. emf = 0.6 V.

**Example 3: Flux.** A loop of area 0.2 m^2 is perpendicular to a field of 0.5 T.
1. Phi = B A cos(0) = 0.5 x 0.2.
2. Phi = 0.1 Wb.

## Common mistakes
- Ignoring the number of turns.
- Thinking a constant flux induces an emf.
- Forgetting Lenz's law gives the direction.
