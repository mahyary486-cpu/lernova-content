# Ions and their uses

An ion is an atom or group of atoms that has gained or lost electrons and therefore carries an electric charge.

## Key ideas

Atoms are neutral because they have equal numbers of protons (positive) and electrons (negative). When an atom gains or loses electrons, the numbers no longer match, and the atom becomes an ion.

- A cation is a positive ion. It forms when an atom loses electrons. Metals usually form cations. Example: Na -> Na+ + e-.
- An anion is a negative ion. It forms when an atom gains electrons. Nonmetals usually form anions. Example: Cl + e- -> Cl-.
- The charge of an ion equals protons minus electrons.
- Atoms gain or lose electrons to reach a stable outer shell. Many atoms become stable with 8 electrons in the outer shell (the octet rule). Small atoms such as hydrogen and lithium become stable with 2 electrons in the first shell.
- Group 1 metals form 1+ ions, group 2 metals form 2+ ions, and group 13 metals such as aluminium form 3+ ions. Group 17 nonmetals form 1- ions, and group 16 nonmetals form 2- ions.
- Some ions contain several atoms joined together, called polyatomic ions. Examples: ammonium NH4+, nitrate NO3-, sulfate SO4 2-, carbonate CO3 2-, hydroxide OH-.
- Ions in water can carry electric current. This is why salt water conducts electricity but pure water hardly does.

Ions are important in daily life:

- Sodium (Na+) and potassium (K+) ions carry nerve signals in your body.
- Calcium ions (Ca2+) build bones and teeth and help muscles contract.
- Iron ions (Fe2+) in blood help carry oxygen.
- Fluoride ions (F-) in toothpaste make tooth enamel stronger.
- Nitrate and phosphate ions in fertilizers feed plants.
- Chloride ions in table salt and in the stomach's hydrochloric acid help digestion.

## Examples

Example 1: Magnesium atom (atomic number 12).
- Electron arrangement: 2, 8, 2.
- The outer shell has 2 electrons. Losing them leaves the full second shell (8).
- Protons = 12, electrons after loss = 10.
- Charge = 12 - 10 = +2. The ion is Mg2+.

Example 2: Oxygen atom (atomic number 8).
- Electron arrangement: 2, 6.
- It needs 2 more electrons to have 8 in the outer shell.
- Protons = 8, electrons after gain = 10.
- Charge = 8 - 10 = -2. The ion is O2-.

Example 3: Real-life case, sports drinks.
- After hard exercise you lose water and ions such as Na+ and K+ in sweat.
- Sports drinks contain these ions so that nerves and muscles keep working normally.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that atoms gain or lose protons. Only electrons move; the nucleus does not change.
- Mixing up cation and anion. A cation is positive because it lost electrons.
- Saying that a metal gains electrons. Metals normally lose electrons.
- Writing the charge number wrongly, for example Mg+ instead of Mg2+.

## Remember

- Losing electrons gives a positive ion; gaining electrons gives a negative ion.
- Ion charge = number of protons - number of electrons.
- Ions are essential for the body, for farming, and for dental health.
