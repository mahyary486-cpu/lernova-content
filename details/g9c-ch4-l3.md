# Solubility

Why does sugar dissolve faster in hot tea, and why does warm soda lose its fizz faster? The answers lie in solubility.

## Key ideas
- **Solubility** is the maximum solute that dissolves in a given amount of solvent at a given temperature.
- **Saturated**: no more can dissolve. **Unsaturated**: more can dissolve. **Supersaturated**: holds more than normal.
- Most solids are **more** soluble in hot water; gases are **less** soluble when warm.
- Gas solubility **increases with pressure**.
- Dissolving is faster with **stirring**, **heat** and **small particles** (large surface area).
- On a **solubility curve**, a point below the line is unsaturated and a point on the line is saturated.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Saturated solution.** 36 g of salt dissolves in 100 g of water at 20 C. How much dissolves in 250 g?
1. Scale the amount: 250 / 100 = 2.5.
2. 36 x 2.5 = 90 g.

**Example 2: Gas in warm soda.** Why does warm soda go flat faster?
1. Carbon dioxide is less soluble at higher temperature.
2. The gas leaves the solution more quickly.

**Example 3: Crushed sugar.** Why do sugar crystals dissolve faster than a sugar cube?
1. Crystals expose more surface area to the solvent.
2. More contact means faster dissolving.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking stirring changes how much can dissolve in total.
- Thinking all solutes are more soluble when hot (gases are not).
- Mixing up saturated and concentrated.
