# Separating Mixtures

Chemists, recyclers and water plants all depend on separating mixtures. The right method depends on the property that differs.

## Key ideas
- **Filtration**: separates an insoluble solid from a liquid (particle size).
- **Evaporation**: recovers a dissolved solid from a solution.
- **Distillation**: separates liquids using different **boiling points**.
- **Chromatography**: separates dyes by different **speeds** on paper.
- **Magnet**: separates magnetic materials; **separating funnel**: separates liquids that do not mix.
- Combine methods for complex mixtures, choosing the property that differs.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Sand and salt.** How can sand and salt be separated?
1. Add water: the salt dissolves and the sand does not.
2. Filter out the sand and evaporate the water to get the salt.

**Example 2: Ink colors.** How can the dyes in black ink be separated?
1. Place a drop of ink on filter paper and let water move up.
2. Each dye moves at a different rate: chromatography.

**Example 3: Alcohol and water.** How can alcohol be separated from water?
1. Alcohol boils at a lower temperature than water.
2. Heat the mixture and condense the alcohol vapor: distillation.

## Common mistakes
- Using filtration for dissolved substances.
- Using distillation for liquids with the same boiling point.
- Doing the steps in the wrong order, such as evaporating before filtering.
