# Reflections, Translations and Rotations

## Rigid motions
A **transformation** moves a figure. The new figure is the **image**, and the original is the **preimage**. Reflections, translations and rotations are **rigid motions** (isometries): they keep every length and every angle measure. So the image is always **congruent** to the preimage.

## Translation
A translation slides every point the same distance in the same direction.
- Rule: (x, y) becomes (x + a, y + b).

## Reflection
A reflection flips a figure across a line of reflection. Each point and its image are the same distance from the line.
- Over the x-axis: (x, y) becomes (x, -y)
- Over the y-axis: (x, y) becomes (-x, y)
- Over the line y = x: (x, y) becomes (y, x)
- Over the line y = -x: (x, y) becomes (-y, -x)

## Rotation about the origin
- 90 degrees counterclockwise: (x, y) becomes (-y, x)
- 180 degrees: (x, y) becomes (-x, -y)
- 270 degrees counterclockwise (the same as 90 clockwise): (x, y) becomes (y, -x)

## Worked examples
1. Translate A(3, 2) by 4 units left and 5 units up. The rule is (x - 4, y + 5), so A' = **(-1, 7)**.
2. Reflect P(3, -5) over the x-axis. The y value changes sign, so P' = **(3, 5)**.
3. Reflect Q(2, 7) over the line y = x. The coordinates swap, so Q' = **(7, 2)**.
4. Rotate B(4, 1) by 90 degrees counterclockwise about the origin. B' = (-1, 4).
5. Rotate C(2, -3) by 180 degrees about the origin. C' = **(-2, 3)**.
6. A triangle has area 12. After a reflection its area is still **12**, because a rigid motion keeps size and shape.

## Common mistakes
- Changing the wrong sign in a reflection. Over the x-axis only y changes; over the y-axis only x changes.
- Mixing up clockwise and counterclockwise. 270 degrees counterclockwise equals 90 degrees clockwise.
- Thinking a reflection changes the size of the figure. It does not. It only flips it.
