# Isosceles and Equilateral Triangles

## Parts of an isosceles triangle
- The two equal sides are the **legs**.
- The third side is the **base**.
- The angle between the legs is the **vertex angle**.
- The two angles at the base are the **base angles**.

## Theorems
- **Isosceles Triangle Theorem:** if two sides of a triangle are congruent, then the angles opposite them (the base angles) are congruent.
- **Converse:** if two angles of a triangle are congruent, then the sides opposite them are congruent.
- In an isosceles triangle, the **bisector of the vertex angle** is also the **median** and the **altitude** to the base. It splits the triangle into two congruent right triangles.

## Equilateral triangles
- All three sides are equal.
- All three angles are equal, so each is **60 degrees**.
- An equiangular triangle (three equal angles) is also equilateral.

## Worked examples
1. The vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is 40 degrees. The base angles share the other 140 degrees, so each is **70 degrees**.
2. A base angle is 50 degrees. Both base angles are 50, so the vertex angle is 180 - 100 = **80 degrees**.
3. In triangle ABC, AB = AC, AB = 3x + 2 and AC = 5x - 8. Set them equal: 3x + 2 = 5x - 8, so 10 = 2x and x = 5. Each leg is 17.
4. A triangle has angles of 65, 65 and 50 degrees. Two angles are equal, so the sides opposite them are equal. It is isosceles.
5. Each side of an equilateral triangle is 8, so its perimeter is 24 and each angle is 60 degrees.
6. An isosceles triangle has legs of 10 and a base of 12. The altitude to the base splits it into two right triangles with hypotenuse 10 and base 6, so the altitude is the square root of (100 - 36) = **8**.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the base angles are next to the equal sides. They are the angles **opposite** the equal sides... at the ends of the base, which is the unequal side.
- Thinking an isosceles triangle can have only two equal sides. An equilateral triangle is also isosceles.
- Forgetting that the altitude to the base cuts the base in half.
