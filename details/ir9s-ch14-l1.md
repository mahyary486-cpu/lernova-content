# Fish

Fish are vertebrates that live in water, breathe with gills, and usually move with fins.

## Key ideas

- Vertebrates are animals with a backbone made of many small bones called vertebrae. The backbone protects the spinal cord. Fish were the first vertebrates to appear on Earth.
- Fish are ectothermic, often called cold-blooded. Their body temperature changes with the temperature of the water around them.
- Fish breathe with gills. Water enters the mouth and flows over the gills. The gills are full of tiny blood vessels. Oxygen dissolved in the water moves into the blood, and carbon dioxide moves out into the water.
- Most fish have a streamlined body, which means the body is smooth and pointed at the front so it passes through water with little resistance. Many fish have overlapping scales covered with slimy mucus. The scales protect the body and the mucus helps the fish slide through water.
- Fins have different jobs. The tail fin pushes the fish forward. The pectoral and pelvic fins help steering and stopping. The dorsal fin on the back keeps the fish upright.
- Many bony fish have a swim bladder, a gas-filled bag inside the body. By changing the amount of gas in it, the fish can rise or sink without much effort.
- The lateral line is a row of sense cells along each side of the body. It detects small movements and pressure changes in the water.
- Fish have a heart with two chambers. Blood goes from the heart to the gills, then to the body, and then back to the heart. So blood passes through the heart only once in each full circuit.
- Most fish reproduce by external fertilization. The female lays many eggs in the water and the male releases sperm over them. Because many eggs are eaten, fish lay large numbers of them.
- There are three main groups. Jawless fish (such as lampreys) have no jaws and no scales. Cartilaginous fish (sharks and rays) have a skeleton made of cartilage. Bony fish (most fish, such as carp and trout) have a skeleton made of bone.

## Examples

- Trout in a mountain river: The trout has a streamlined body, so it swims against the current easily. Its gills take oxygen from cold, fast water. Its body temperature is almost the same as the river temperature.
- Shark: A shark has a skeleton of cartilage, which is lighter than bone. It has no swim bladder, so it must keep swimming or store oil in its liver to avoid sinking. This shows that cartilaginous fish solve the buoyancy problem in a different way from bony fish.
- Carp in a pond in winter: When the water gets cold, the carp becomes less active and uses less energy. This is possible because the carp is ectothermic and its body temperature follows the water temperature.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking all fish have a bony skeleton. Sharks and rays have a skeleton of cartilage.
- Thinking fish breathe the water itself. Fish take dissolved oxygen out of the water. They do not split water into oxygen.
- Thinking the swim bladder is used for breathing. It is used for floating and sinking.
- Thinking fish are warm-blooded because they move fast. Almost all fish are ectothermic.

## Remember

- Fish: backbone, gills, fins, scales, two-chamber heart, ectothermic.
- The swim bladder controls floating, and the lateral line senses movement in water.
- Three groups: jawless, cartilaginous and bony fish.
