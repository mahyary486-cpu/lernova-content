# Triangle Inequality

## The Triangle Inequality Theorem
The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle is **greater than** the length of the third side.

To test three lengths, add the two **shorter** ones. If their sum is greater than the longest, a triangle exists.

## Range for the third side
If two sides of a triangle are a and b, the third side c satisfies

**|a - b| < c < a + b**

## Sides and angles
- The **longest side** is opposite the **largest angle**.
- The **shortest side** is opposite the **smallest angle**.
- Equal sides are opposite equal angles.

## Worked examples
1. Can 3, 4 and 8 be the sides of a triangle? 3 + 4 = 7, and 7 is not greater than 8, so **no**.
2. Can 5, 7 and 10 be the sides of a triangle? 5 + 7 = 12, which is greater than 10, so **yes**.
3. Two sides are 5 and 9. The third side is greater than 9 - 5 = 4 and less than 9 + 5 = 14, so **4 < c < 14**.
4. A triangle has angles of 40, 60 and 80 degrees. The longest side is opposite the 80 degree angle, and the shortest side is opposite the 40 degree angle.
5. A triangle has sides of 6, 8 and 10. The largest angle is opposite the side of length 10, which is the right angle because 36 + 64 = 100.

## Common mistakes
- Using "greater than or equal to". If the sum is **equal** to the third side, the three points fall on one line and there is no triangle.
- Comparing the wrong pair of sides. Always compare the two shorter sides with the longest.
- Mixing up the order: the longest side is across from the largest angle, not next to it.
