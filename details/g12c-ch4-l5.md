# Nucleic Acids and Metabolism

The four-letter code of DNA stores the instructions for making every protein in the body.

## Key ideas
- **Nucleotide** = sugar + phosphate + base.
- **DNA**: A-T (2 H-bonds), G-C (3 H-bonds); **RNA**: **uracil** replaces thymine.
- A **codon** (3 bases) codes for one **amino acid**.
- **Transcription**: DNA -> RNA; **translation**: RNA -> protein.
- **ATP -> ADP + P** releases about **30 kJ/mol**.
- **Photosynthesis** and **respiration** are reverse reactions.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Base pairing.** One DNA strand reads ATGC. What is the complementary strand?
1. A pairs with T and G with C.
2. TACG.

**Example 2: Codons.** How many bases code for a protein of 100 amino acids (ignore the stop codon)?
1. Each amino acid needs one codon of 3 bases.
2. 100 x 3 = 300 bases.

**Example 3: Photosynthesis.** How many moles of oxygen form when 1 mol of glucose is made by photosynthesis?
1. 6 CO2 + 6 H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2.
2. 6 mol of O2.

## Common mistakes
- Pairing A with G.
- Using uracil in DNA.
- Confusing transcription with translation.
