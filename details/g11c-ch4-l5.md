# Entropy, Gibbs Free Energy and Spontaneity

Ice melts above 0 degrees C but not below it. The balance between energy and disorder changes with temperature.

## Key ideas
- **Entropy S**: disorder; **gas > liquid > solid**.
- **delta G = delta H - T delta S** (T in K).
- **delta G < 0**: spontaneous; **= 0**: equilibrium; **> 0**: non-spontaneous.
- **-H and +S**: always spontaneous; **+H and -S**: never.
- **Spontaneous** does not mean **fast**.
- Entropy of the universe **increases** in a spontaneous process.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Gibbs energy.** delta H = -100 kJ, delta S = -0.2 kJ/K at 300 K.
1. delta G = delta H - T delta S = -100 - 300 x (-0.2).
2. delta G = -100 + 60 = -40 kJ; spontaneous.

**Example 2: Crossover temperature.** delta H = +40 kJ and delta S = +0.1 kJ/K. When is it spontaneous?
1. delta G = 0 at T = delta H / delta S = 40 / 0.1 = 400 K.
2. Spontaneous above 400 K.

**Example 3: Entropy.** Does entropy increase in 2 H2O(l) -> 2 H2(g) + O2(g)?
1. The number of gas moles rises from 0 to 3.
2. Yes, the entropy increases.

## Common mistakes
- Using Celsius for T.
- Confusing spontaneous with fast.
- Mixing joules and kilojoules in delta H and delta S.
