# Ratios and Proportions

## Ratios
A **ratio** compares two quantities by division. The ratio of a to b is written **a : b** or **a / b** (b is not zero). Ratios should be simplified and the units should match.

- The ratio of 12 cm to 18 cm is 12 / 18 = **2 / 3**, or 2 : 3.

## Proportions
A **proportion** says two ratios are equal: **a / b = c / d**.

**Cross products:** in a proportion, a x d = b x c. This is how you solve for an unknown.

## Using a ratio to share a total
If two parts are in the ratio 2 : 3 and the total is 30, there are 2 + 3 = 5 equal shares. Each share is 30 / 5 = 6, so the parts are 12 and 18.

## Rates and scale
A **rate** compares different units, such as 60 miles per 2 hours. A **scale** on a map or drawing is a ratio of drawing length to real length.

## Worked examples
1. Solve 3 / x = 12 / 20. Cross multiply: 3 x 20 = 12x, so 60 = 12x and x = **5**.
2. The sides of a triangle are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 and its perimeter is 48. The shares total 12, each share is 4, so the sides are **12, 16 and 20**.
3. A map scale is 1 inch = 5 miles. Two cities are 7.5 inches apart on the map, so the real distance is 7.5 x 5 = **37.5 miles**.
4. A car travels 150 miles using 6 gallons. At the same rate, 10 gallons gives 150 / 6 = 25 miles per gallon, so 10 x 25 = **250 miles**.
5. Is 4 / 6 = 10 / 15 a true proportion? Cross products: 4 x 15 = 60 and 6 x 10 = 60. They are equal, so **yes**.

## Common mistakes
- Setting up a proportion with the quantities in a different order on each side. Keep the same kind of quantity on top on both sides.
- Forgetting to convert units before writing a ratio.
- Cross multiplying wrongly: multiply **across** the equals sign, top of one side by bottom of the other.
