# Volume of solids

Volume is the amount of space inside a solid, measured in cubic units.

## Key ideas

- Volume is measured in cubic units, such as cm^3 or m^3. Also, 1 liter = 1000 cm^3.
- Cuboid: V = lwh. Cube with edge a: V = a^3.
- Any prism or cylinder: V = (base area) × height. For a cylinder, V = πr^2h.
- Any pyramid or cone: V = 1/3 × (base area) × height. For a cone, V = 1/3 πr^2h.
- A cone has exactly one third of the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height. A pyramid has one third of the volume of a prism with the same base and height.
- Sphere with radius r: V = 4/3 πr^3.
- The height must be the straight distance between the bases, at a right angle to the base. It is not the slant height.
- For a composite solid, split it into simple solids and add or subtract the volumes.

## Worked examples

Example 1. A prism has a right triangle as its base, with legs 6 cm and 8 cm. The height of the prism is 10 cm. Find the volume.
Step 1: Base area = 1/2 × 6 × 8 = 24 cm^2.
Step 2: V = 24 × 10 = 240 cm^3.

Example 2. A cone has radius 3 cm and height 7 cm. Find its volume. Use π = 3.14.
Step 1: Base area = π × 3^2 = 9π.
Step 2: V = 1/3 × 9π × 7 = 21π.
Step 3: With π = 3.14, V = 21 × 3.14 = 65.94 cm^3.

Example 3. A sphere has radius 6 cm. Find its volume in terms of π, and then with π = 3.14.
Step 1: r^3 = 6 × 6 × 6 = 216.
Step 2: V = 4/3 × π × 216.
Step 3: 216 ÷ 3 = 72, and 72 × 4 = 288, so V = 288π.
Step 4: With π = 3.14, V = 288 × 3.14 = 904.32 cm^3.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the factor 1/3 for cones and pyramids.
- Using the diameter in place of the radius. Always halve the diameter first.
- Using the slant height as the height of a cone.
- In the sphere formula, writing r^2 instead of r^3, or squaring only the number and not the radius.

## Remember

- Prism and cylinder: base area times height. Cone and pyramid: one third of that.
- Sphere: V = 4/3 πr^3.
- Check that all lengths use the same unit, and give the answer in cubic units.
