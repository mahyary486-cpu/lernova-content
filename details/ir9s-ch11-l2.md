# Features used to group animals

Animals are sorted into groups by comparing body features such as a backbone, body shape, body covering, way of reproducing and body temperature.

## Key ideas

- **Backbone.** The first and most important split is between vertebrates and invertebrates. Vertebrates have a backbone (a column of bones called vertebrae) that protects the spinal cord. Invertebrates have no backbone. Most animals, about 95 percent of all known species, are invertebrates, for example insects, worms, snails and jellyfish.
- **Five groups of vertebrates.** Fish live in water and breathe with gills. Amphibians live part of life in water and part on land. Reptiles have dry scaly skin. Birds have feathers and wings. Mammals have hair or fur and feed their young with milk.
- **Body symmetry.** Some animals are bilaterally symmetrical, which means the left and right sides are mirror images, like humans and dogs. Some, like starfish and jellyfish, are radially symmetrical, with parts arranged around a center. Sponges have no clear symmetry.
- **Body covering.** Skin can be covered by scales (fish, reptiles), feathers (birds), fur or hair (mammals), a hard shell, or moist bare skin (amphibians).
- **Reproduction.** Many animals lay eggs. Some give birth to live young. Most mammals give birth to live young, but a few, such as the platypus, lay eggs.
- **Body temperature.** Endotherms, often called warm-blooded animals, keep an almost constant body temperature using energy from food. Birds and mammals are endotherms. Ectotherms, often called cold-blooded animals, get most of their heat from the environment. Fish, amphibians, reptiles and most invertebrates are ectotherms. Their body temperature changes with the surroundings.
- **Breathing.** Animals can breathe with gills, lungs, skin or small tubes in the body, depending on the group.

## Examples

**Example 1: Sorting a frog.** A frog has a backbone, so it is a vertebrate. Its skin is moist and has no scales. It lays eggs in water, and its young (tadpoles) breathe with gills while adults breathe with lungs and skin. Its body temperature follows the environment. These features place it in the amphibians.

**Example 2: A bat.** A bat can fly like a bird, but it has fur, gives birth to live young, feeds them milk and keeps a constant body temperature. Using these features, scientists place the bat among mammals. This example shows that we must use several features and not only one.

**Example 3: A lizard on a cold morning.** A lizard is slow in the early morning and becomes active after lying in the sun. Its body temperature rises because it is an ectotherm and takes heat from the environment. A sparrow in the same cold morning is active at once, because as an endotherm it makes its own heat.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that all animals live on land, or that all animals with wings are birds. Insects and bats also have wings.
- Confusing "cold-blooded" with "always cold". A cold-blooded animal can have a warm body after sitting in the sun.
- Using only one feature to classify an animal. Dolphins live in water like fish, but they breathe with lungs and feed milk to their young, so they are mammals.
- Believing that all vertebrates are large. Many tiny fish and frogs are vertebrates, and many large animals, like some squids, are invertebrates.

## Remember

- Vertebrates have a backbone; invertebrates do not.
- Birds and mammals are endotherms; the other groups are mostly ectotherms.
- Use several features together to place an animal in a group.
