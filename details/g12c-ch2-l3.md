# Galvanic Cells and Standard Electrode Potentials

A battery is a galvanic cell: two different metals in solutions joined by a wire and a salt bridge.

## Key ideas
- **Anode**: oxidation (-); **cathode**: reduction (+).
- Electrons flow **anode -> cathode** in the wire.
- **E cell = E cathode - E anode** (reduction potentials).
- **E cell > 0**: spontaneous.
- **delta G = -n F E**, F = **96,500 C/mol**.
- The **salt bridge** keeps the solutions neutral.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Cell potential.** Zn (-0.76 V) and Ag (+0.80 V): find E cell.
1. Ag is the cathode and Zn the anode.
2. E = 0.80 - (-0.76) = 1.56 V.

**Example 2: Free energy.** For the Zn-Cu cell (n = 2, E = 1.10 V).
1. delta G = -n F E = -2 x 96,500 x 1.10.
2. delta G = -212,300 J = -212 kJ.

**Example 3: Spontaneity.** Is Cu + 2 H+ -> Cu^2+ + H2 spontaneous?
1. E = 0.00 - 0.34 = -0.34 V.
2. E is negative, so it is not spontaneous.

## Common mistakes
- Using oxidation potentials instead of reduction potentials.
- Doubling the potential when multiplying a half-reaction.
- Mixing up the anode and the cathode.
