# The Normal Distribution

## The bell curve
Many measurements (heights, test scores, errors) follow a **normal distribution**. Its graph is a bell-shaped curve that is **symmetric** about the mean μ. The spread is described by the standard deviation σ. The total area under the curve is **1** (100% of the data).

## The Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7)
- About **68%** of the data is within 1 standard deviation of the mean.
- About **95%** is within 2 standard deviations.
- About **99.7%** is within 3 standard deviations.

Because the curve is symmetric, each half has half of these percentages. For example, between the mean and 1 standard deviation above it there is about 34%.

## Example
IQ scores have μ = 100 and σ = 15.
- Between 85 and 115 (within 1 SD): **68%**.
- Above 115: the remaining 32% is split between both tails, so about **16%** is above 115.
- Between 70 and 130 (within 2 SD): **95%**.
- Above 130 (more than 2 SD above the mean): about **2.5%**.
- Between the mean and 2 SD above it: about **47.5%**.

## z-scores
The **z-score** tells how many standard deviations a value is from the mean:
**z = (x - μ) / σ**
- Test scores have μ = 70 and σ = 5. A score of 80 has z = 10/5 = **2**.
- A score of 62 when μ = 70 and σ = 4 has z = -8/4 = **-2** (below the mean).

To find a value from a z-score: **x = μ + zσ**. With μ = 50, σ = 10 and z = 1.5: x = 50 + 15 = **65**.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting that the percentages 68, 95 and 99.7 describe both sides of the mean.
- Giving a negative standard deviation. σ is never negative.
- Mixing up x and z. A z-score of 2 does not mean the value is 2.
- Using the Empirical Rule for data that is clearly not bell-shaped.
