# Points, Lines and Planes

## The three undefined terms
Geometry starts with three ideas that are described but not defined with other words.
- **Point:** an exact location. It has no size. We name it with a capital letter, such as A.
- **Line:** a straight path that goes on forever in both directions. It is named by two of its points (line AB) or by a lowercase letter.
- **Plane:** a flat surface that goes on forever in all directions, like an endless sheet of paper. It is named by three points that are not on one line, or by a capital script letter such as plane P.

## Parts of a line
- **Segment AB:** the part of a line from A to B, including both endpoints. It has a length.
- **Ray AB:** starts at A, passes through B and goes on forever. A is the endpoint.
- **Opposite rays:** two rays on the same line with the same endpoint that go in opposite directions.

## Collinear and coplanar
- Points are **collinear** if they lie on the same line.
- Points are **coplanar** if they lie in the same plane.
- Any two points are always collinear. Any three points are always coplanar.

## Basic postulates
1. Through any two points there is exactly one line.
2. Through any three points that are not collinear there is exactly one plane.
3. If two points are in a plane, then the line through them is in that plane.
4. If two different lines meet, they meet in exactly one point.
5. If two different planes meet, they meet in exactly one line.

## Worked examples
1. How many different lines can you draw through 4 points if no three of them are collinear? Each pair of points gives one line. The pairs are AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD, so there are **6 lines**.
2. Are points A, B and C collinear if A, B and C all lie on line m? Yes, because they all lie on one line.
3. Two walls of a room meet at a corner edge. What does the edge show? Two planes meet in a line.
4. Name the intersection of line AB and line CD if they cross at point E. The intersection is the single point **E**.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a line has endpoints. A line never ends. A segment has two endpoints.
- Mixing up line AB and ray AB. Ray AB starts at A, ray BA starts at B.
- Believing two planes can meet in a single point. Two different planes that meet always meet in a line.
