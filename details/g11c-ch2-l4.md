# Dalton's Law, Gas Mixtures and Gas Stoichiometry

The air we breathe is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Each gas contributes its own share of the pressure.

## Key ideas
- **Dalton**: **P_total = P1 + P2 + ...**.
- **Partial pressure** = **mole fraction x P_total**.
- **Mole fraction** X = n_i / n_total.
- Over water: **P_gas = P_total - P_water vapor**.
- Gas volumes react in the ratio of the **coefficients** (same T and P).
- Use **PV = nRT** to convert moles to volume.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Total pressure.** Gases have partial pressures 0.5 atm, 0.3 atm and 0.2 atm.
1. P_total = 0.5 + 0.3 + 0.2.
2. P_total = 1.0 atm.

**Example 2: Partial pressure.** Air at 1.0 atm is 21% oxygen by moles.
1. P(O2) = X x P_total = 0.21 x 1.0.
2. P(O2) = 0.21 atm.

**Example 3: Collected over water.** Total pressure is 1.00 atm and water vapor is 0.03 atm.
1. P_gas = P_total - P_water vapor.
2. P_gas = 0.97 atm.

## Common mistakes
- Adding mole fractions instead of pressures.
- Forgetting the water vapor correction.
- Using percent mass in place of mole percent.
