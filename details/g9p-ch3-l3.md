# Newton's Second Law: F = ma

How a force changes motion depends on the mass. A shopping cart speeds up quickly with a push; a loaded truck needs a much bigger force to do the same.

## Key ideas
- **Newton's second law**: net force = mass x acceleration, **F = ma**, or a = F / m.
- Acceleration is **in the direction of the net force**.
- Same force, **more mass = less acceleration**. Same mass, **more force = more acceleration**.
- 1 N = 1 kg x 1 m/s^2.
- Always use the **net** force, not just one of the forces.
- Combine with v = v_i + a t to get the speed after some time.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Finding acceleration.** A 10 kg object has a net force of 50 N.
1. a = F / m = 50 / 10.
2. a = 5 m/s^2.

**Example 2: Finding force.** What net force gives a 1200 kg car an acceleration of 2.5 m/s^2?
1. F = m a = 1200 x 2.5.
2. F = 3000 N.

**Example 3: Changing the mass.** 20 N acts on a 4 kg cart and then on an 8 kg cart.
1. First: a = 20 / 4 = 5 m/s^2.
2. Second: a = 20 / 8 = 2.5 m/s^2.
3. Doubling the mass halves the acceleration.

## Common mistakes
- Using the weight instead of the mass in F = ma.
- Using one force instead of the net force.
- Thinking a bigger force always means a bigger speed. It means a bigger acceleration.
