# Kinetic Molecular Theory of Gases and Pressure

A tire, a balloon and a can of spray all hold gases whose behavior follows from moving particles.

## Key ideas
- Particles are **small**, **far apart**, move **randomly** and collide **elastically**.
- Average kinetic energy is proportional to **T in kelvins**.
- **Pressure** comes from collisions with the walls.
- **1 atm = 101.3 kPa = 760 mmHg**.
- **STP**: **273 K** and **1 atm**.
- Gases behave most ideally at **high T** and **low P**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Pressure units.** Convert 380 mmHg to atm.
1. 760 mmHg = 1 atm.
2. 380 / 760 = 0.5 atm.

**Example 2: Temperature.** Convert 25 C to kelvins.
1. K = C + 273.
2. K = 298 K.

**Example 3: Kinetic energy.** The temperature doubles from 300 K to 600 K.
1. Average kinetic energy is proportional to T.
2. It doubles.

## Common mistakes
- Using Celsius in gas calculations.
- Mixing up the pressure units.
- Thinking gas particles attract strongly.
