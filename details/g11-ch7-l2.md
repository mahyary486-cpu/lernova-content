# Probability Rules

## Basics
The **probability** of an event E is a number from 0 to 1:
**P(E) = (number of favorable outcomes) / (number of possible outcomes)**
- 0 means impossible, 1 means certain.
- Rolling a fair die: P(even) = 3/6 = **1/2**. P(prime) = P(2, 3 or 5) = 3/6 = 1/2.
- Two dice have 36 outcomes. P(sum is 7) = 6/36 = **1/6**.

## Experimental probability
Based on what actually happened: (number of times it happened) / (number of trials). If a spinner lands on blue 18 times in 60 spins, the experimental probability is 18/60 = **0.3**.

## Complement
The **complement** of E (E does not happen):
**P(not E) = 1 - P(E)**
- If P(rain) = 0.35, then P(no rain) = **0.65**.
- Three coin flips with at least one head: 1 - P(no heads) = 1 - 1/8 = **7/8**.
"At least one" is almost always easiest with the complement.

## Mutually exclusive events
Events that cannot happen together. Then **P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)**.
Rolling a 2 or a 5: 1/6 + 1/6 = **1/3**.

## The addition rule (general)
**P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)**
- Drawing a heart or a king from a deck: 13/52 + 4/52 - 1/52 = 16/52 = **4/13**. The king of hearts is counted once.
- If P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A and B) = 0.2, then P(A or B) = 0.5 + 0.4 - 0.2 = **0.7**.

## Common mistakes
- Adding the probabilities when the events overlap, without subtracting P(A and B).
- Giving a probability greater than 1 or less than 0.
- Forgetting that the complement of "at least one" is "none".
