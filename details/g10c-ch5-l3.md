# Percent Composition and Empirical Formulas

The formula of a compound shows its atoms, but experiments often give only masses. Percent composition and empirical formulas let you move between mass data and formulas.

## Key ideas
- Percent composition gives the percent by mass of each element in a compound.
- Percent of element = (mass of element in 1 mol of compound / molar mass of compound) x 100.
- The percentages of all elements add to 100 percent.
- The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. The molecular formula is the true number of atoms per molecule.
- Molecular formula = empirical formula x n, where n = molar mass / empirical formula mass.

## Finding an empirical formula
1. Treat percentages as grams (assume a 100 g sample).
2. Convert each mass to moles.
3. Divide all mole values by the smallest one.
4. If a ratio is not whole (such as 1.5), multiply all by a small integer.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Percent composition.** Find the percent of hydrogen in H2O (18.02 g/mol).
1. Mass of H in 1 mol = 2 x 1.008 = 2.016 g.
2. Percent = 2.016 / 18.02 x 100 = 11.2 percent.

**Example 2: Empirical formula.** A compound is 40.0 percent C, 6.7 percent H and 53.3 percent O.
1. Moles in 100 g: C = 40.0 / 12.01 = 3.33; H = 6.7 / 1.008 = 6.65; O = 53.3 / 16.00 = 3.33.
2. Divide by 3.33: C = 1.00, H = 2.00, O = 1.00.
3. Empirical formula: CH2O.

**Example 3: Molecular formula.** The empirical formula is CH2 and the molar mass is 56.1 g/mol.
1. Empirical formula mass = 12.01 + 2(1.008) = 14.03 g/mol.
2. n = 56.1 / 14.03 = 4.
3. Molecular formula = C4H8.

## Common mistakes
- Using the percent numbers as moles without dividing by the atomic masses.
- Rounding too early, or rounding 1.5 to 2 instead of multiplying the ratio by 2.
- Reporting the empirical formula when the question asks for the molecular formula.
- Forgetting that hydrates count the water in the total mass.
