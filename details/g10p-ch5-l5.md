# Circular Motion and Gravitation

An object moving in a circle at constant speed is always changing direction, so its velocity changes and it accelerates. Gravity can supply the force that keeps planets and satellites on curved paths.

## Key ideas
- **Speed in a circle:** v = 2 pi r / T, where T is the period.
- **Centripetal acceleration:** a_c = v^2 / r, directed toward the center.
- **Centripetal force:** F_c = m v^2 / r. It is the net inward force, not a new force: tension, friction, gravity or a normal force can supply it.
- **Universal gravitation:** F = G m1 m2 / r^2, with G = 6.67 x 10^-11 N m^2/kg^2. Gravity is always attractive and follows an inverse-square law.
- Surface gravity: g = G M / R^2. Orbital speed: v = sqrt(G M / r).

## Example 1: Car on a curve
A 1200 kg car rounds a curve of radius 50 m at 15 m/s.
1. a_c = v^2 / r = 225 / 50 = 4.5 m/s^2.
2. F_c = m a_c = 1200 x 4.5 = 5400 N, supplied by friction between tires and road.

## Example 2: Gravity between masses
Two 1000 kg masses are 1.0 m apart.
1. F = 6.67e-11 x 1000 x 1000 / 1.0^2.
2. F = 6.67e-5 N, a tiny force. Doubling the distance would cut it to one fourth.

## Example 3: Orbit of a satellite
Find the orbital speed at r = 6.77 x 10^6 m from Earth's center (M = 5.97 x 10^24 kg).
1. G M = 6.67e-11 x 5.97e24 = 3.98e14 m^3/s^2.
2. G M / r = 3.98e14 / 6.77e6 = 5.88e7.
3. v = sqrt(5.88e7) = 7.7 x 10^3 m/s, about 7.7 km/s.

Check of surface gravity: g = 3.98e14 / (6.37e6)^2 = 3.98e14 / 4.06e13 = 9.8 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes
- Believing centripetal force is an extra force added to the free-body diagram. It is the name for the net inward force.
- Using v instead of v^2 in a = v^2 / r.
- Saying there is no acceleration because the speed is constant.
- Forgetting to square r in the law of gravitation, or using the radius from the surface instead of from the center.
