# Cross sections

A cross section is the shape you see on the cut face when a plane slices through a solid.

## Key ideas

- The cross section depends on the solid and on the angle and position of the plane.
- Cube or cuboid: a cut parallel to a face gives a rectangle (a square for a cube).
- Cylinder: a cut parallel to the bases gives a circle equal to the base. A cut through the axis gives a rectangle with width 2r and height h.
- Cone: a cut parallel to the base gives a smaller circle. A cut through the apex and the axis gives a triangle.
- Sphere: every cross section is a circle. The largest circle passes through the center and has the same radius as the sphere.
- If the plane is at distance d from the center of a sphere with radius R, the circle has radius r = √(R^2 - d^2).
- In a cone, a parallel cut halfway up the height gives a circle with half the base radius.
- Describing the cross section in words helps you decide which formula to use.

## Worked examples

Example 1. A cube has edge 4 cm. A plane cuts it parallel to one face. Describe the cross section and find its area.
Step 1: The cut is parallel to a face, so the cross section is a square.
Step 2: Each side equals the edge, 4 cm.
Step 3: Area = 4 × 4 = 16 cm^2.

Example 2. A cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 10 cm. Find the area of the cross section (a) parallel to the base, and (b) through the axis.
Step 1: For (a), the cross section is a circle with radius 5, so the area = π × 5^2 = 25π cm^2.
Step 2: For (b), the cross section is a rectangle. Its width is the diameter, 2 × 5 = 10 cm, and its height is 10 cm.
Step 3: Area = 10 × 10 = 100 cm^2.

Example 3. A sphere has radius 13 cm. A plane is 5 cm from the center. Find the area of the circular cross section.
Step 1: Let r be the radius of the circle. By the Pythagorean theorem, r^2 + 5^2 = 13^2.
Step 2: r^2 = 169 - 25 = 144, so r = 12 cm.
Step 3: Area = π × 12^2 = 144π cm^2. With π = 3.14, this is 144 × 3.14 = 452.16 cm^2.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that every cross section of a solid has the same shape. The shape changes with the direction of the cut.
- Using the radius of the sphere instead of the radius of the circle made by the cut.
- Forgetting that the rectangle through the axis of a cylinder has width 2r, not r.
- Not squaring both numbers correctly when using the Pythagorean theorem.

## Remember

- Parallel to the base gives a circle for cylinders, cones and spheres.
- For a sphere: r = √(R^2 - d^2).
- First name the shape of the cross section, then calculate its area.
