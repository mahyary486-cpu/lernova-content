# Equation of a Circle

## Standard form
A circle with center **(h, k)** and radius **r**:

**(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2**

This comes from the distance formula: every point (x, y) on the circle is r units from the center. With the center at the origin it becomes **x^2 + y^2 = r^2**.

## From a general equation
If the equation is not in standard form, **complete the square** for x and for y to get the center and radius.

## Position of a point
Compute (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 and compare with r^2:
- less than r^2: the point is **inside**
- equal to r^2: **on** the circle
- greater than r^2: **outside**

## Worked examples
1. Center (3, -2) and radius 5: **(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25**.
2. (x + 1)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 49 has center **(-1, 4)** and radius **7**.
3. Find the equation of the circle with center (0, 0) that passes through (6, 8). r^2 = 36 + 64 = 100, so **x^2 + y^2 = 100**.
4. x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0. Complete the squares: (x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 4y + 4) = 12 + 9 + 4 = 25, so (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25. Center **(3, -2)**, radius **5**.
5. Is (4, 5) inside the circle (x - 1)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 16? 9 + 16 = 25, which is greater than 16, so it is **outside**.
6. A circle has a diameter with endpoints (2, 3) and (8, 7). The center is the midpoint (5, 5) and the radius is half the distance: the distance is the square root of (36 + 16) = square root of 52, so r^2 = 13. The equation is **(x - 5)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 13**.

## Common mistakes
- Getting the sign of the center wrong: (x + 1) means h = **-1**.
- Writing r instead of r^2 on the right side.
- Forgetting to add the same numbers to **both** sides when completing the square.
