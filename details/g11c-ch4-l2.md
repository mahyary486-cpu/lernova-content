# Calorimetry and Specific Heat

To measure how much energy is in a reaction, we measure the temperature change of the water around it.

## Key ideas
- **q = m c delta T**; water **c = 4.18 J/(g C)**.
- **q_rxn = -q_solution**.
- **delta H = q_rxn / n** (per mole).
- **Bomb calorimeter** measures combustion at constant volume.
- Hot object loses = cold object gains.
- A temperature **rise** means an **exothermic** reaction.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Heat absorbed.** 100 g of water warms from 20 C to 30 C.
1. q = m c delta T = 100 x 4.18 x 10.
2. q = 4180 J.

**Example 2: Enthalpy of neutralization.** Mixing acid and base (0.05 mol of water formed) warms 100 g of solution by 6.5 C.
1. q = 100 x 4.18 x 6.5 = 2717 J released.
2. delta H = -2717 / 0.05 = -54,340 J/mol = -54.3 kJ/mol.

**Example 3: Specific heat.** A 50 g metal absorbs 1150 J and warms by 10 C.
1. c = q / (m delta T) = 1150 / (50 x 10).
2. c = 2.3 J/(g C).

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the minus sign for q_rxn.
- Mixing joules and kilojoules.
- Dividing by the wrong number of moles.
