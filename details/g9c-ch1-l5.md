# Density

Why does a heavy ship float while a small stone sinks? The answer is density, not just mass.

## Key ideas
- **Density = mass / volume**: D = m / V.
- Units: **g/cm^3** (or g/mL) and **kg/m^3**. 1 g/cm^3 = 1000 kg/m^3.
- Volume of a box = length x width x height. For an irregular solid use **water displacement**.
- Density is a **physical property** and does not depend on the size of the sample.
- **Floats** if less dense than the liquid; **sinks** if more dense. Water is 1.0 g/cm^3.
- Rearranged: m = D x V and V = m / D.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Finding density.** A metal has a mass of 54 g and a volume of 20 cm^3.
1. D = m / V = 54 / 20.
2. D = 2.7 g/cm^3.

**Example 2: Finding mass.** A liquid has a density of 0.8 g/cm^3. Find the mass of 50 cm^3.
1. m = D x V = 0.8 x 50.
2. m = 40 g.

**Example 3: Water displacement.** A stone of mass 45 g raises the water level from 40 mL to 55 mL.
1. Volume = 55 - 40 = 15 mL = 15 cm^3.
2. D = 45 / 15 = 3.0 g/cm^3.

## Common mistakes
- Dividing volume by mass instead of mass by volume.
- Thinking a bigger piece of the same material has a larger density.
- Forgetting that 1 mL equals 1 cm^3.
