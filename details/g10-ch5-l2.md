# Special Right Triangles

## 45-45-90 triangle
Half of a square, cut along the diagonal. It is isosceles.
- legs: **x** and **x**
- hypotenuse: **x times the square root of 2**

## 30-60-90 triangle
Half of an equilateral triangle, cut along its altitude.
- short leg (opposite 30 degrees): **x**
- long leg (opposite 60 degrees): **x times the square root of 3**
- hypotenuse (opposite 90 degrees): **2x**

| Triangle | Side ratio |
|---|---|
| 45-45-90 | 1 : 1 : square root of 2 |
| 30-60-90 | 1 : square root of 3 : 2 |

## Strategy
1. Identify the triangle type.
2. Find the **short** side x (the leg in the 45-45-90, or the side opposite 30 degrees).
3. Use the ratio to find the other sides.

## Worked examples
1. A 45-45-90 triangle with legs of 7 has a hypotenuse of **7 times the square root of 2**.
2. A 45-45-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 10. Each leg is 10 divided by the square root of 2, which is **5 times the square root of 2**.
3. A 30-60-90 triangle with a hypotenuse of 14: the short leg is 7 and the long leg is **7 times the square root of 3**.
4. A 30-60-90 triangle has a short leg of 5: the hypotenuse is **10** and the long leg is 5 times the square root of 3.
5. The diagonal of a square with side 6 is **6 times the square root of 2**.
6. An equilateral triangle has side 8. Its altitude is the long leg of a 30-60-90 triangle with short leg 4, so the altitude is **4 times the square root of 3**.

## Common mistakes
- Using the long leg as x in a 30-60-90 triangle. x is always the **short** leg.
- Mixing up the square root of 2 and the square root of 3 ratios.
- Forgetting that the hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 triangle is **double** the short leg.
