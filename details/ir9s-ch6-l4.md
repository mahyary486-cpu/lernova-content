# Earthquakes, faults and tsunamis

An earthquake is a sudden shaking of the ground caused by the release of energy when rock breaks and slips along a fault.

## Key ideas
- A fault is a crack in Earth's crust where blocks of rock move. Rocks near plate boundaries are pushed slowly. Stress builds up until the rock breaks suddenly, and stored energy is released as seismic waves.
- Three main kinds of fault exist: in a normal fault, one block slides down as the crust is stretched. In a reverse fault, one block is pushed up as the crust is squeezed. In a strike-slip fault, blocks slide sideways past each other.
- The focus is the point underground where the rock breaks. The epicenter is the point on the surface directly above the focus. Shaking is usually strongest near the epicenter.
- There are three kinds of seismic waves: P waves are the fastest (about 6 km/s in the crust) and travel through solids and liquids. S waves are slower (about 3.5 km/s) and travel only through solids. Surface waves are the slowest and cause the most damage.
- A seismograph records the waves. The time gap between the P and S wave arrivals shows how far the epicenter is. Three stations are needed to find the epicenter by drawing circles that cross at one point.
- Magnitude measures the energy of an earthquake. Each increase of 1 on the scale means the ground moves 10 times more, and about 32 times more energy is released.
- A tsunami is a series of long sea waves, usually caused by an undersea earthquake that suddenly lifts or drops the sea floor. In deep water it can travel at about 700 to 800 km/h, but its height is small. Near the coast the water becomes shallow, the wave slows and rises high. Often the sea first pulls back from the beach.

## Examples
- Example 1, distance to epicenter: The S wave arrives 20 s after the P wave. Use P = 6 km/s and S = 3.5 km/s. The distance d satisfies d/3.5 - d/6 = 20. This gives d × (0.2857 - 0.1667) = 20, so d × 0.1190 = 20, and d ≈ 168 km.
- Example 2, comparing magnitudes: A magnitude 7 quake and a magnitude 5 quake differ by 2 steps. Ground motion is 10 × 10 = 100 times larger. The energy is about 32 × 32 ≈ 1,000 times larger.
- Example 3, tsunami warning: A tsunami starts 4,000 km from a coast and moves at 800 km/h. Time = 4,000 / 800 = 5 hours. This gives time to warn people. A tsunami from a nearby source may reach land in only minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the focus and epicenter are the same point.
- Thinking magnitude 6 is only a little stronger than magnitude 5. It is 10 times larger in ground motion.
- Thinking one seismograph station can find the epicenter. It gives only the distance, not the direction.
- Thinking a tsunami is a normal wind wave. It involves a huge mass of water moving.

## Remember
- Earthquakes happen when stressed rock slips along a fault, mostly near plate boundaries.
- P waves arrive first, then S waves, then surface waves.
- Tsunamis come from sea floor movement and can cross whole oceans.
