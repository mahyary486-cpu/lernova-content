# Organic Synthesis and Analysis Methods

Chemists identify unknown compounds by combining several analytical techniques, each of which tells a different part of the story.

## Key ideas
- **Overall yield** = product of the step yields.
- Purify by **distillation**, **recrystallization**, **chromatography**.
- **Mass spec**: molar mass from the **molecular ion**.
- **IR**: **O-H** (3300 cm^-1), **C=O** (1700 cm^-1).
- **NMR**: number of **H environments**.
- Use **several methods** together.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Overall yield.** A two-step synthesis has yields of 80% and 50%.
1. Overall = 0.80 x 0.50.
2. Overall yield = 40%.

**Example 2: IR.** A compound shows a strong peak at 1700 cm^-1 and no O-H band.
1. A strong peak at 1700 cm^-1 indicates C=O.
2. It could be an aldehyde or a ketone.

**Example 3: Mass spectrum.** The molecular ion peak is at m/z = 46.
1. The peak gives the molar mass.
2. M = 46 g/mol, for example ethanol C2H6O.

## Common mistakes
- Averaging yields instead of multiplying.
- Confusing IR and NMR information.
- Using only one technique.
