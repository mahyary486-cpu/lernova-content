# Limiting Reactant and Percent Yield

If you have 10 wheels and 3 car bodies, the number of complete cars is set by the bodies. The same idea applies to reactants.

## Key ideas
- **Limiting reactant**: used up first, determines the product.
- Find it: **moles / coefficient**; the **smallest** is limiting.
- **Theoretical yield**: maximum product from the limiting reactant.
- **Percent yield = (actual / theoretical) x 100%**.
- The reactant in **excess** is left over.
- Percent yield is always **less than 100%** in practice.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Limiting reactant.** 3 mol H2 and 2 mol O2 for 2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O.
1. H2: 3 / 2 = 1.5; O2: 2 / 1 = 2.
2. H2 has the smaller value, so H2 is limiting.

**Example 2: Theoretical yield.** How much water forms from the above reaction?
1. Moles of H2O = 3 mol (ratio 2 : 2).
2. Mass = 3 x 18 = 54 g.

**Example 3: Percent yield.** Only 45 g of water is obtained.
1. Percent yield = actual / theoretical x 100%.
2. 45 / 54 x 100% = 83.3%.

## Common mistakes
- Choosing the limiting reactant by the smaller mass.
- Forgetting to divide by the coefficient.
- Calculating the yield from the excess reactant.
