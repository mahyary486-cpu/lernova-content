# Roots, stems and leaves

Roots, stems and leaves are the three main organs of a vascular plant, and each one has its own job in keeping the plant alive.

## Key ideas

- Roots anchor the plant in the soil, take in water and dissolved minerals, and sometimes store food.
  - Root hairs are very thin outgrowths of root cells. They give the root a large surface for absorption.
  - A taproot has one thick main root that goes deep, such as in a carrot. A fibrous root system has many thin roots of similar size that spread near the surface, such as in grass and wheat. Dicots often have taproots and monocots often have fibrous roots.
  - The root cap is a small cover at the tip that protects the growing tip as it pushes through soil.
- Stems hold the leaves up to the light and connect the roots with the leaves.
  - Xylem carries water and minerals upward from the roots. Phloem carries the sugar made in the leaves to every part of the plant, including roots and fruits.
  - Some stems store food, such as the potato tuber. Woody stems get wider each year and form growth rings. Herbaceous stems are soft and green.
- Leaves are the main place of photosynthesis. The overall reaction is: carbon dioxide + water -> glucose + oxygen, with light energy absorbed by chlorophyll.
  - A leaf is flat and thin, so light reaches the cells easily and gases do not have to travel far.
  - Stomata are small pores, mostly on the underside. Each is surrounded by two guard cells that open and close it. Carbon dioxide enters and oxygen leaves through the stomata.
  - Transpiration is the loss of water vapor from the leaf through the stomata. It pulls water up the xylem and helps to cool the plant. When the plant has little water, the guard cells close the stomata to save water.
  - Veins in the leaf contain xylem and phloem.

## Examples

1. Choosing a root type. A grass plant has a mass of thin roots about the same size. This is a fibrous root system. It holds the top layer of soil together and helps to prevent erosion. A carrot has one thick, deep main root with small side roots. This is a taproot, and it stores food.
2. Tracing water. Water enters a root hair from the soil. It moves into the xylem of the root, up through the xylem of the stem, into the veins of a leaf, and then to the leaf cells. Some of it is used in photosynthesis, and most of it leaves as water vapor through the stomata.
3. A plant on a hot, dry day. The leaf loses water fast. The guard cells lose water and become limp, so the stomata close. Water loss falls, but less carbon dioxide enters, so photosynthesis slows down.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that phloem carries water. Xylem carries water and minerals, and phloem carries sugar.
- Thinking that plants take in food from the soil. Plants make their own sugar in the leaves. Roots take in water and minerals only.
- Confusing transpiration with photosynthesis. Transpiration is the loss of water vapor. Photosynthesis is the making of glucose.
- Thinking that stomata are only for letting water out. They also let carbon dioxide in and oxygen out.

## Remember

- Roots absorb and anchor. Stems support and carry. Leaves make food.
- Xylem moves water and minerals up, and phloem moves sugar around.
- Stomata are opened and closed by guard cells.
