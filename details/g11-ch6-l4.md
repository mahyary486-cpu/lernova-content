# Geometric Sequences

## Definition
In a **geometric sequence** each term is found by **multiplying** the previous one by the same number, the **common ratio r**.
- 2, 6, 18, 54, ... has r = **3**.
- 80, 40, 20, 10, ... has r = **1/2**.
- 3, -6, 12, -24, ... has r = -2 (the signs alternate).
To find r, **divide** a term by the one before it: r = a_(n+1) / a_n.

## Formulas
- **Recursive:** a_n = r * a_(n-1)
- **Explicit:** **a_n = a_1 * r^(n - 1)**

## Examples
**Find a term.** 2, 6, 18, ... Here a_1 = 2 and r = 3, so a_6 = 2 * 3^5 = 2 * 243 = **486**.

**A decreasing sequence.** 80, 40, 20, ... has a_5 = 80 * (1/2)^4 = 80/16 = **5**.

**From two terms.** a_3 = 12 and a_6 = 96. Between them there are 3 steps, so r^3 = 96/12 = 8 and r = **2**. Then a_1 = 12 / 2^2 = **3**.

**Which term?** For 3, 6, 12, ... which term is 384? 3 * 2^(n-1) = 384 gives 2^(n-1) = 128 = 2^7, so n - 1 = 7 and n = **8**.

**Large term.** 5, 10, 20, ... a_8 = 5 * 2^7 = 5 * 128 = **640**.

## Geometric means
The **geometric mean** of two positive numbers a and b is the square root of ab. The geometric mean of 4 and 16 is the square root of 64, which is **8**, and 4, 8, 16 is geometric (r = 2).

## Applications
- **Compound growth:** money earning 5% a year is multiplied by r = 1.05 each year.
- **Bouncing balls and decay:** a ball that rebounds to 60% of its previous height has r = 0.6.

## Arithmetic or geometric?
- Constant **difference** between terms: arithmetic.
- Constant **ratio** between terms: geometric.
- 1, 4, 9, 16 is neither (the differences are 3, 5, 7 and the ratios keep changing).

## Common mistakes
- Using a_1 * r^n. The exponent is **n - 1**.
- Forgetting a negative ratio makes the signs alternate.
- Finding r by subtracting.
