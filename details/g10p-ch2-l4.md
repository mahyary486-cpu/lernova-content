# Projectile Motion Launched Horizontally

A projectile is an object that moves only under the pull of gravity; we ignore air resistance. A ball rolling off a table, a stone thrown from a cliff and water leaving a horizontal hose are all examples. The path of such an object is a curve called a parabola. The curve looks complicated, but it hides a simple secret: the motion can be split into a horizontal part and a vertical part, and the two parts do not affect each other.

## Key ideas

Horizontal and vertical motions are independent, and they share only one thing: the time t. In the horizontal direction there is no force after the launch, so the acceleration is zero and the velocity stays constant. The horizontal distance is therefore x = vx t. In the vertical direction gravity gives a constant downward acceleration of g = 9.8 m/s^2. For a launch that is exactly horizontal, the object starts with no vertical velocity, so vy = g t and the drop is y = (1/2) g t^2.

An important consequence is the time of the fall. Solving y = (1/2) g t^2 for the time gives t = sqrt(2h / g), where h is the height of the launch point. This time depends only on the height and not on the horizontal speed. A ball rolled off a table and a ball dropped from the same height reach the floor at the same instant, even though one of them travels sideways. The sideways motion does not change how fast the ball falls.

At any instant the velocity has two components, vx and vy, so the speed is found with v = sqrt(vx^2 + vy^2). The horizontal component never changes, while the vertical component keeps growing. The direction of the velocity therefore bends downward as time passes, which draws the parabola. To solve a problem, find the fall time first from the vertical motion, and then use that time in the horizontal formula to find the range.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Off a table.** A ball leaves a 1.5 m high table horizontally at 4.0 m/s. How far from the table does it land?
1. The fall time is t = sqrt(2 x 1.5 / 9.8) = sqrt(0.306) = 0.553 s.
2. The horizontal distance is x = vx t = 4.0 x 0.553.
3. The ball lands 2.2 m from the table edge.

**Example 2: Off a cliff.** A stone is thrown horizontally at 12 m/s from a cliff 45 m high. Find the fall time, the range, and the speed on impact.
1. The fall time is t = sqrt(2 x 45 / 9.8) = sqrt(9.18) = 3.03 s.
2. The range is x = 12 x 3.03 = 36 m.
3. The vertical speed on landing is vy = g t = 9.8 x 3.03 = 29.7 m/s.
4. The impact speed is v = sqrt(12^2 + 29.7^2) = sqrt(1026) = 32 m/s.

**Example 3: Comparing two launches.** Two balls leave a 1.8 m high ledge horizontally, one at 3.0 m/s and one at 6.0 m/s. Compare their flights.
1. The fall time is the same for both: t = sqrt(2 x 1.8 / 9.8) = 0.61 s.
2. The slower ball lands at x = 3.0 x 0.61 = 1.8 m.
3. The faster ball lands at x = 6.0 x 0.61 = 3.6 m, twice as far, because it moves twice as fast sideways for the same time.

## Common mistakes

A very common belief is that a faster horizontal launch makes the object fall more slowly; in fact the fall time is the same. Another mistake is to put the horizontal speed into the fall-time formula, which should contain only the height and g. Students also add gravity to the horizontal motion, but the horizontal acceleration is zero. Finally, remember that the speed at landing includes both components; using only the vertical part, or only the horizontal part, gives a wrong answer.
