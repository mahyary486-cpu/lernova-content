# Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

In every collision in an isolated system the total momentum is conserved. What differs between collisions is what happens to kinetic energy.

## Key ideas
- **Elastic collision:** both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. Objects bounce apart. Collisions between hard steel balls or between gas molecules are nearly elastic.
- **Inelastic collision:** momentum is conserved, but some kinetic energy becomes heat, sound or deformation.
- **Perfectly inelastic collision:** the objects stick together and share one final velocity. The most kinetic energy is lost, though never all of it unless both end at rest.
- Perfectly inelastic: **v = (m1 v1 + m2 v2) / (m1 + m2)**.
- Elastic, second object at rest: **v1' = (m1 - m2) v1 / (m1 + m2)** and **v2' = 2 m1 v1 / (m1 + m2)**. Equal masses exchange velocities.

## Example 1: Carts that stick
A 2.0 kg cart at 4.0 m/s hits a stationary 3.0 kg cart and they couple.
1. p before = 2.0 x 4.0 = 8.0 kg m/s.
2. v = 8.0 / (2.0 + 3.0) = 1.6 m/s.
3. KE before = 0.5 x 2.0 x 4.0^2 = 16 J. KE after = 0.5 x 5.0 x 1.6^2 = 6.4 J.
4. Energy lost = 16 - 6.4 = 9.6 J.

## Example 2: Elastic collision
A 2.0 kg ball at 3.0 m/s strikes a stationary 1.0 kg ball elastically.
1. v1' = (2.0 - 1.0) x 3.0 / 3.0 = 1.0 m/s.
2. v2' = 2 x 2.0 x 3.0 / 3.0 = 4.0 m/s.
3. Check momentum: 2.0 x 1.0 + 1.0 x 4.0 = 6.0 = 2.0 x 3.0. Check KE: 1 + 8 = 9 J, equal to the 9 J before.

## Example 3: Ballistic pendulum
A 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s embeds in a 1.990 kg block that hangs at rest.
1. Collision (momentum): 0.010 x 400 = 2.0 v, so v = 2.0 m/s.
2. Swing (energy): (1/2) v^2 = g h, so h = v^2 / (2g) = 4.0 / 19.6 = 0.20 m, using g = 9.8 m/s^2.
3. Most of the bullet's energy is lost in the collision. Energy conservation applies only after it.

## Common mistakes
- Using kinetic energy conservation for a collision where objects stick or deform.
- Forgetting that momentum is always conserved, even when kinetic energy is not.
- Using the energy method across the collision in a ballistic pendulum instead of after it.
- Averaging the two initial speeds instead of using the momentum equation.
