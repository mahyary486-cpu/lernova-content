# Speed and velocity

Speed tells us how fast an object moves, and velocity tells us how fast it moves in a given direction.

## Key ideas

- Speed is the distance travelled in one unit of time. Formula: speed = distance / time, or v = d / t.
- The SI unit of speed is metres per second (m/s). Kilometres per hour (km/h) is also common.
- To change km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6. To change m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6. This works because 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s.
- Uniform (constant) speed means the object covers equal distances in equal times.
- Most real motion has changing speed, so we use average speed = total distance / total time. We do not average the speeds by simply adding them and dividing.
- Instantaneous speed is the speed at one exact moment, like the number shown on a car speedometer.
- Velocity is displacement divided by time: velocity = displacement / time. It is a vector, so it has a direction.
- Two objects can have the same speed but different velocities if they move in different directions. A car going around a circle at constant speed has a changing velocity, because its direction keeps changing.
- Rearranged formulas: d = v × t and t = d / v.

## Examples

**Example 1: Average speed and conversion.** A bus travels 180 km in 2.5 hours.
- Average speed = 180 km / 2.5 h = 72 km/h.
- Convert to m/s: 72 / 3.6 = 20 m/s.
- Answer: 72 km/h, which is 20 m/s.

**Example 2: Finding distance.** A cyclist rides at a constant speed of 6 m/s for 5 minutes.
- Change the time to seconds: 5 × 60 = 300 s.
- Distance = v × t = 6 m/s × 300 s = 1800 m.
- Answer: 1800 m, which is 1.8 km.

**Example 3: Average speed for a trip with two parts.** A family drives 60 km in the first 1 hour and then 90 km in the next 1.5 hours.
- Total distance = 60 + 90 = 150 km.
- Total time = 1 + 1.5 = 2.5 h.
- Average speed = 150 / 2.5 = 60 km/h.
- Check: the average of the two part-speeds (60 and 60) is also 60 here, but in general you must divide total distance by total time.

**Example 4: Velocity.** A runner has a displacement of 50 m north in 20 s.
- Velocity = 50 m / 20 s = 2.5 m/s north.

## Common mistakes

- Using minutes or hours with metres. Always convert to seconds if you want an answer in m/s.
- Calculating average speed by averaging two speeds. Use total distance divided by total time.
- Using distance when the question asks for velocity. Velocity needs displacement and a direction.
- Multiplying by 3.6 when you should divide, or the other way round. Remember that m/s numbers are smaller than km/h numbers.

## Remember

- Speed = distance / time; velocity = displacement / time, with direction.
- km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s.
- Average speed = total distance / total time.
