# Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

## Parallel lines
Parallel lines never meet. They have the **same slope** (and different y-intercepts).
- y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x - 5 are parallel.

## Perpendicular lines
Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle. Their slopes are **negative reciprocals**: flip the fraction and change the sign. The product of the slopes is -1.
- The negative reciprocal of 2 is -1/2.
- The negative reciprocal of 2/3 is -3/2.
- The negative reciprocal of -4 is 1/4.

A horizontal line (slope 0) and a vertical line (undefined slope) are also perpendicular.

## Writing a parallel line
Through (0, 2), parallel to y = 4x - 1:
- Same slope: 4.
- Equation: y = 4x + 2.

## Writing a perpendicular line
Through (3, 1), perpendicular to y = 3x + 2:
1. The perpendicular slope is -1/3.
2. Point-slope: y - 1 = -1/3 (x - 3).
3. Distribute: y - 1 = -1/3 x + 1, so y = -1/3 x + 2.

## Comparing lines in standard form
Lines 2x + 3y = 6 and 3x - 2y = 4:
- First line: y = -2/3 x + 2, slope -2/3.
- Second line: y = 3/2 x - 2, slope 3/2.
- The product (-2/3)(3/2) = -1, so the lines are perpendicular.

## Finding an unknown
For y = kx + 1 to be perpendicular to y = 4x - 3, we need k x 4 = -1, so k = -1/4.

## Common mistakes
- Changing the sign but forgetting to flip the fraction (or the other way round).
- Comparing y-intercepts instead of slopes.
- Using the original slope for a perpendicular line.
