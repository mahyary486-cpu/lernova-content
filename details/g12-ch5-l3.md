# Double-Angle and Half-Angle Formulas

Setting B = A in the sum formulas gives the double-angle formulas. Solving them backward gives the half-angle formulas.

## Key ideas
**Double-angle**
- sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
- cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A = 1 - 2 sin^2 A = 2 cos^2 A - 1
- tan 2A = 2 tan A / (1 - tan^2 A)

**Power-reducing**
- sin^2 A = (1 - cos 2A) / 2
- cos^2 A = (1 + cos 2A) / 2

**Half-angle**
- sin(A/2) = plus or minus sqrt((1 - cos A) / 2)
- cos(A/2) = plus or minus sqrt((1 + cos A) / 2)
- tan(A/2) = (1 - cos A) / sin A = sin A / (1 + cos A)

The sign in the half-angle formulas depends on the quadrant of A/2, not of A.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** If sin A = 3/5 and A is in Quadrant II, find sin 2A and cos 2A.
1. cos A = -4/5.
2. sin 2A = 2(3/5)(-4/5) = -24/25.
3. cos 2A = 1 - 2(9/25) = 7/25.

**Example 2.** Find the exact value of sin 15 degrees using a half-angle formula.
1. 15 is half of 30, and 15 is in Quadrant I, so the sign is positive.
2. sin 15 = sqrt((1 - cos 30) / 2) = sqrt((1 - sqrt(3)/2) / 2).
3. = sqrt((2 - sqrt(3)) / 4) = sqrt(2 - sqrt(3)) / 2, about 0.2588.

**Example 3.** Find cos(pi/8).
1. pi/8 is half of pi/4 and lies in Quadrant I.
2. cos(pi/8) = sqrt((1 + sqrt(2)/2) / 2) = sqrt(2 + sqrt(2)) / 2.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking sin 2A = 2 sin A. The correct formula is 2 sin A cos A.
- Choosing the sign of a half-angle value using the quadrant of A instead of A/2.
- Forgetting the division by 2 inside the half-angle square root.
- Using cos 2A = 1 - 2 cos^2 A. The correct forms are 1 - 2 sin^2 A and 2 cos^2 A - 1.
