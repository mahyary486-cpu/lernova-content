# Midsegment and Side-Splitter Theorems

## Triangle Midsegment Theorem
A **midsegment** connects the midpoints of two sides of a triangle.
- It is **parallel** to the third side.
- Its length is **half** the length of the third side.

## Side-Splitter Theorem (Triangle Proportionality)
If a line is **parallel** to one side of a triangle and meets the other two sides, it divides those sides **proportionally**.

In triangle ABC with D on AB and E on AC, if DE is parallel to BC, then

**AD / DB = AE / EC**

**Converse:** if a line divides two sides of a triangle proportionally, then it is parallel to the third side.

## Parallel lines and transversals
Three or more parallel lines cut any two transversals into proportional parts.

## Worked examples
1. The midsegment of a triangle is 7 units long. The side it is parallel to is **14** units long.
2. In triangle ABC, D and E are the midpoints of AB and AC and BC = 18. Then DE = **9**, and DE is parallel to BC.
3. DE is parallel to BC with AD = 4, DB = 6 and AE = 6. Then 4 / 6 = 6 / EC, so 4 x EC = 36 and EC = **9**.
4. A triangle has sides 12, 16 and 20. The midsegments are half of each side: 6, 8 and 10. The triangle formed by the midsegments has perimeter 24, which is half of 48.
5. In triangle ABC, AD / DB = 3 / 2 and AE / EC = 3 / 2. By the converse, DE is parallel to BC.

## Common mistakes
- Setting up the proportion with a whole side instead of the part. Compare **part to part** (AD / DB = AE / EC).
- Forgetting that the midsegment is half the length of the third side, not the same length.
- Using the theorem when the line is not known to be parallel.
