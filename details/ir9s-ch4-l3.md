# Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate at which the velocity of an object changes with time.

## Key ideas

- An object accelerates when its speed changes, its direction changes, or both.
- Formula: a = (v − u) / t, where u is the initial velocity, v is the final velocity, and t is the time taken for the change.
- The SI unit of acceleration is metres per second squared (m/s²). An acceleration of 2 m/s² means the velocity increases by 2 m/s every second.
- Acceleration is a vector. On a straight line, we use a plus sign for one direction and a minus sign for the opposite direction.
- If the object speeds up in the positive direction, acceleration is positive. If it slows down, the acceleration is in the opposite direction to the velocity. This is often called deceleration, and it gives a negative value in a straight-line motion toward the positive direction.
- Constant (uniform) acceleration means the velocity changes by the same amount every second.
- Rearranged formula: v = u + a × t.
- Zero acceleration means the velocity is constant. The object is either at rest or moving at a steady speed in a straight line.
- Turning at a corner or moving in a circle is also acceleration, because the direction of the velocity changes.
- Free fall near Earth is an example of nearly constant acceleration, about 9.8 m/s² downward (often rounded to 10 m/s²) when air resistance is small.

## Examples

**Example 1: Speeding up.** A car starts from rest and reaches 20 m/s in 8 s.
- u = 0 m/s, v = 20 m/s, t = 8 s.
- a = (20 − 0) / 8 = 2.5 m/s².
- Answer: 2.5 m/s² in the direction of motion.

**Example 2: Braking.** A train slows from 30 m/s to 10 m/s in 5 s.
- u = 30 m/s, v = 10 m/s, t = 5 s.
- a = (10 − 30) / 5 = −20 / 5 = −4 m/s².
- Answer: −4 m/s². The negative sign shows the acceleration points opposite to the motion, so the train slows down.

**Example 3: Final velocity.** A motorbike has a velocity of 5 m/s and then accelerates at 2 m/s² for 6 s.
- v = u + a × t = 5 + 2 × 6 = 5 + 12 = 17 m/s.
- Answer: 17 m/s.

**Example 4: Time needed.** How long does a ball dropped from rest need to reach 30 m/s if a = 10 m/s²?
- t = (v − u) / a = (30 − 0) / 10 = 3 s.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing speed and acceleration. A fast object can have zero acceleration if its speed does not change.
- Writing the unit as m/s instead of m/s². Acceleration is a change of velocity per second.
- Thinking that a negative acceleration always means slowing down. It means the acceleration points in the negative direction, and slowing down happens only when velocity points the opposite way.
- Forgetting that a change of direction is also an acceleration.

## Remember

- a = (v − u) / t, in m/s².
- Speeding up, slowing down, and turning are all accelerations.
- Constant velocity means zero acceleration.
