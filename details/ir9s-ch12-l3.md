# Flowers, seeds and fruits

Flowers, seeds and fruits are the reproductive parts of angiosperms, and they work together to make new plants.

## Key ideas

- A flower has four main parts, arranged in rings.
  - Sepals are small green leaf-like parts outside the flower. They protect the bud.
  - Petals are often large and colorful. Their color and smell attract insects and other animals.
  - Stamens are the male parts. Each has an anther, which makes pollen grains, and a thin stalk called a filament.
  - The pistil (also called the carpel) is the female part. It has a sticky stigma that catches pollen, a style that is a tube, and an ovary at the base that holds one or more ovules. Each ovule contains an egg cell.
- A flower with both stamens and a pistil is called a bisexual (perfect) flower. Some plants have separate male and female flowers. Flowers pollinated by insects are usually colorful and sweet-smelling. Flowers pollinated by wind are usually small, plain, and make a lot of light pollen.
- After pollination and fertilization, the flower changes. The ovule becomes a seed. The ovary becomes the fruit. The petals and other parts usually dry up and fall off.
- A seed has three main parts. The embryo is the young plant. The stored food feeds the embryo until it has leaves. The seed coat protects the inside. In a dicot such as the bean, the food is stored in two cotyledons. In a monocot such as corn, there is one cotyledon and much of the food is stored in a separate tissue.
- A fruit is the ripened ovary. It protects the seeds and often helps spread them. Fleshy fruits, such as an apple or a cherry, are eaten by animals, and the seeds pass out in droppings. Dry fruits may have wings or hooks, such as the maple fruit, which spins in the wind.

## Examples

1. Following a flower to a fruit. A cherry flower is pollinated by a bee. Pollen reaches the stigma, and fertilization happens in the ovule. The ovule becomes the seed, which lies inside the hard stone. The ovary wall becomes the fleshy fruit and the stone around the seed. Result: one flower makes one cherry with one seed.
2. Counting seeds. A pea pod is a fruit that came from one ovary with 6 ovules. If all 6 ovules were fertilized, the pod has 6 seeds. If only 4 were fertilized, the pod has 4 seeds. Number of seeds = number of fertilized ovules.
3. Choosing the pollinator. Flower A is bright red with a sweet smell and a lot of nectar. Flower B is small, green, and has long, feathery stigmas. Flower A is probably pollinated by animals such as insects or birds. Flower B is probably pollinated by wind.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking the whole flower becomes the fruit. Mainly the ovary becomes the fruit.
- Mixing up the male and female parts. Stamen = male, pistil = female.
- Calling only sweet things fruits. Tomatoes, cucumbers and beans in pods are fruits too, because they develop from the ovary and hold seeds.
- Thinking that the seed is the new plant. The seed holds the embryo, which grows into the new plant.

## Remember

- Stamen: anther + filament. Pistil: stigma + style + ovary.
- Ovule -> seed. Ovary -> fruit.
- A seed has an embryo, stored food and a seed coat.
