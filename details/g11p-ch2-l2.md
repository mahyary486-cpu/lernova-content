# Pressure in Fluids

Divers feel more pressure the deeper they go. Dams are thicker at the bottom for the same reason.

## Key ideas
- **P = rho g h** gives the pressure due to a fluid of depth h.
- Pressure at a point is the **same in all directions**.
- It does **not depend on the shape** of the container.
- Water: about **98,000 Pa per 10 m** of depth.
- **Absolute pressure** = atmospheric pressure + rho g h.
- A **mercury barometer**: P_atm = rho g h with h about 0.76 m.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Pressure at depth.** Find the pressure due to water at a depth of 10 m (g = 9.8).
1. P = rho g h = 1000 x 9.8 x 10.
2. P = 98,000 Pa.

**Example 2: Absolute pressure.** Find the absolute pressure at 10 m depth with P_atm = 1.01 x 10^5 Pa.
1. P = P_atm + rho g h = 101,000 + 98,000.
2. P = 199,000 Pa = 1.99 x 10^5 Pa.

**Example 3: Barometer.** What height of mercury (13,600 kg/m^3) balances 1.01 x 10^5 Pa?
1. h = P / (rho g) = 101,000 / (13,600 x 9.8).
2. h = 0.76 m.

## Common mistakes
- Using the total volume of water instead of the depth.
- Forgetting atmospheric pressure in absolute pressure.
- Thinking a wider container gives more pressure at the same depth.
