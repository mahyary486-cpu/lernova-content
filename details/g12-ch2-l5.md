# Applications and Optimization

## Setting up a model
1. Draw a picture and name the variable.
2. Write the quantity to be maximized or minimized as a function of one variable.
3. Find the **domain** that makes sense.
4. Use a table, a graph or the vertex to find the best value.

## The open box
A box is made from a 12 in by 18 in sheet by cutting a square of side x from each corner and folding up the sides.
- Height: x. Width: 12 - 2x. Length: 18 - 2x.
- **V(x) = x(12 - 2x)(18 - 2x)**.
- Domain: x must be positive and 12 - 2x must be positive, so **0 < x < 6**.

| x | V(x) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 * 10 * 16 = 160 |
| 2 | 2 * 8 * 14 = **224** |
| 3 | 3 * 6 * 12 = 216 |
| 2.5 | 2.5 * 7 * 13 = 227.5 |

The maximum volume is about **228 cubic inches**, when x is about 2.35 in.

## Area problems
- A rectangle with perimeter 40 has sides x and 20 - x, so A = x(20 - x). This is a parabola with vertex at x = 10 and **maximum area 100**.
- A farmer has 100 ft of fence and uses a river as one side. If the two sides perpendicular to the river are x, the side parallel is 100 - 2x, so A = x(100 - 2x). The vertex is at x = 25 and the maximum area is 25 * 50 = **1250 square feet**.

## Rational models
**Average cost.** If a company's cost is 50x + 200 for x items, the average cost per item is C(x) = (50x + 200)/x = 50 + 200/x. As x gets large, C(x) approaches **50**, the horizontal asymptote.

**Mixtures.** The concentration of salt in a tank after t minutes is C(t) = 5t/(t + 10) grams per liter. As t grows large, C approaches **5**: the concentration levels off at 5 g/L.

## Checking the answer
Always ask: is the answer inside the domain? Does it make sense (positive length, whole number of items)?

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the domain, which can give a negative length.
- Optimizing the wrong quantity.
- Reading the maximum from the wrong coordinate. The maximum value is the output V(x), not x.
