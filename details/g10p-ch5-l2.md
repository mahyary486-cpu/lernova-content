# Impulse and the Impulse-Momentum Theorem

A force that acts for some time changes an object's momentum. The product of the force and the time is called impulse. This idea explains why you bend your knees when you land and why cars have airbags.

## Key ideas
- **Impulse:** J = F delta t, in newton seconds (N s). 1 N s = 1 kg m/s.
- **Impulse-momentum theorem:** J = delta p, so **F delta t = m vf - m vi**.
- Average force: **F = delta p / delta t**.
- For a fixed momentum change, a longer contact time gives a smaller force. A shorter time gives a larger force.
- Impulse is a vector. If an object rebounds, the velocity reverses, so the momentum change is the sum of the speeds, not the difference.

## Example 1: Kick
A 0.50 kg ball at rest is kicked to 12 m/s. Find the impulse.
1. J = m(vf - vi) = 0.50 x (12 - 0).
2. J = 6.0 N s.

## Example 2: Stopping a ball
A 0.20 kg ball moving at 25 m/s is caught and stopped in 0.050 s. Find the average force.
1. Momentum change: delta p = 0.20 x 25 = 5.0 kg m/s.
2. F = delta p / delta t = 5.0 / 0.050.
3. F = 100 N, directed opposite to the ball's motion.

## Example 3: Rebound
A 0.060 kg tennis ball arrives at 20 m/s and leaves in the opposite direction at 30 m/s. Contact lasts 0.010 s.
1. Take the arrival direction as positive: vi = +20 m/s and vf = -30 m/s.
2. delta v = -30 - 20 = -50 m/s, so delta p = 0.060 x (-50) = -3.0 kg m/s.
3. F = 3.0 / 0.010 = 300 N in magnitude, directed along the new direction of motion.

## Why safety devices work
A passenger must lose the same momentum in a crash no matter what. An airbag, a crumple zone or a bent knee spreads the stop over a longer time. Since F = delta p / delta t, the force and the damage are smaller. A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s has 20000 kg m/s of momentum. A constant 5000 N braking force needs t = 20000 / 5000 = 4.0 s to remove it.

## Common mistakes
- Subtracting speeds for a rebound instead of adding them.
- Forgetting that impulse is a vector and dropping the sign.
- Mixing up impulse (N s) with force (N) by leaving out the time.
- Thinking an airbag reduces the momentum change. It reduces the force by increasing the time.
