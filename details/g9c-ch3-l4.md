# Heating and Cooling Curves

A graph of temperature against time is a fingerprint of how a substance changes state.

## Key ideas
- On a heating curve, **sloped** sections show temperature **rising** within one state.
- **Flat** sections show a **change of state** at constant temperature.
- During a change of state the energy changes the **potential energy** (particle attractions), not the kinetic energy.
- Water: flat at **0 C** (melting) and **100 C** (boiling).
- The boiling plateau is **longer** than the melting plateau.
- A **cooling curve** is the reverse of a heating curve.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Reading a curve.** A curve is flat at 0 C for 3 minutes.
1. A flat part means a change of state.
2. At 0 C the ice is melting.

**Example 2: Why flat?.** Why does the temperature stay constant during boiling?
1. The added energy breaks the attractions between particles.
2. It does not raise the average kinetic energy.

**Example 3: Longest plateau.** Which plateau of water is longer: melting or boiling?
1. Vaporization needs much more energy than melting.
2. So the boiling plateau is longer.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking temperature must rise whenever heat is added.
- Mixing up the flat and sloped sections.
- Forgetting that a cooling curve has the same plateaus.
