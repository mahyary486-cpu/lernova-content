# Periodic Trends in Atomic Radius

Atomic radius is half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms of the same element. It is usually measured in picometers (1 pm = 10^-12 m).

## Key ideas
- **Down a group:** radius increases, because each period adds a new energy level and the outer electrons sit farther from the nucleus. Inner electrons also shield the outer ones.
- **Across a period (left to right):** radius decreases. Protons are added, but electrons enter the same energy level, so the effective nuclear charge increases and pulls the electron cloud inward.
- **Effective nuclear charge (Zeff):** approximately the number of protons minus the number of inner (core) electrons.
- **Cations** are smaller than their parent atoms, because they lose the outer level or have less electron repulsion. **Anions** are larger than their parent atoms, because added electrons repel one another.
- **Isoelectronic ions** have the same number of electrons. The one with more protons is smaller.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Ranking atoms.** Rank Na, Mg, Si and Cl by increasing radius.
1. All are in period 3, so radius decreases from left to right.
2. Na is largest and Cl smallest.
3. Increasing order: Cl, Si, Mg, Na.

**Example 2: Effective nuclear charge.** Compare Zeff for Na (Z = 11) and Cl (Z = 17), each with 10 core electrons.
1. Na: 11 - 10 = 1.
2. Cl: 17 - 10 = 7.
3. Chlorine pulls its outer electrons about 7 times harder, which is why it is much smaller (99 pm against 186 pm).

**Example 3: Ion versus atom.** The radius of Cl is 99 pm and of Cl- is 181 pm.
1. Ratio = 181 pm / 99 pm = 1.83.
2. The ion is about 1.8 times larger, because there are 18 electrons but only 17 protons.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking that radius increases across a period because more electrons are added.
- Forgetting that anions are larger and cations smaller than their atoms.
- Ignoring the number of protons when comparing isoelectronic ions: O2- is larger than Mg2+.
- Confusing the group trend with the period trend.
