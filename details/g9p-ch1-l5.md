# Graphs and Data Analysis

Data in a table are easier to understand on a graph. The shape of the graph tells you how one quantity depends on another.

## Key ideas
- Put the **independent variable on the x-axis** and the **dependent variable on the y-axis**.
- A good graph has a **title**, **labeled axes with units**, an **even scale** and plotted points with a best-fit line or curve.
- **Line graphs** show how something changes; **bar graphs** compare categories.
- **Slope** = rise / run = change in y / change in x. Its unit is the y-unit divided by the x-unit.
- A straight line through the origin means the variables are **directly proportional**: y = kx.
- **Interpolation** reads a value between data points; **extrapolation** estimates beyond them and is less reliable.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Finding a slope.** A distance-time graph is a straight line through (0 s, 0 m) and (4 s, 12 m). Find its slope.
1. Rise = 12 m - 0 m = 12 m. Run = 4 s - 0 s = 4 s.
2. Slope = 12 m / 4 s = 3 m/s.
3. The slope is the speed.

**Example 2: Reading an equation.** A line through the origin has slope 3 m/s. What distance is reached at 10 s?
1. The line is y = 3x, so d = 3 m/s x 10 s.
2. d = 30 m.

**Example 3: Line not through the origin.** A cart is at 5 m when t = 0 and at 25 m when t = 4 s. Find the speed.
1. Change in distance = 25 - 5 = 20 m.
2. Speed = 20 m / 4 s = 5 m/s.
3. The line starts at 5 m, so the cart did not start at the origin.

## Common mistakes
- Putting the independent variable on the y-axis.
- Choosing an uneven scale or leaving out units.
- Connecting every dot with straight segments instead of drawing a best-fit line.
- Calculating slope with points that are not on the line.
