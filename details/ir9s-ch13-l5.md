# Echinoderms

Echinoderms are spiny-skinned marine invertebrates, such as sea stars and sea urchins, that move with tube feet.

## Key ideas

- Echinoderms (phylum Echinodermata) means "spiny skin". All of them live in the sea, from shallow water to the deep ocean. None live in fresh water or on land.
- **Skeleton:** They have an internal skeleton made of hard plates of calcium carbonate under the skin. Many have spines that stick out. The skin may feel rough or spiky.
- **Symmetry:** Adults have radial symmetry, usually with five parts arranged around a center (like the five arms of a sea star). Their larvae are bilaterally symmetrical, which shows they are related to animals with bilateral symmetry.
- **Water vascular system:** This is a network of canals filled with seawater. It ends in hundreds of small tube feet. When the animal pushes water into the tube feet, they extend and grip surfaces with tiny suckers. This is used for walking, holding on to rocks, catching food, and breathing.
- **Nervous system:** They have a nerve ring and nerves along the arms, but no brain.
- **Digestion:** Most have a mouth on the underside and an anus on the top. A sea star pushes its stomach out of its mouth over prey, such as a clam, and digests it outside the body.
- **Regeneration:** Many can regrow lost arms. A sea star can sometimes regrow a whole body from one arm that has part of the central disc.
- **Reproduction:** Most have separate sexes and release eggs and sperm into the water, where fertilization takes place.
- Main groups: sea stars (starfish), brittle stars, sea urchins and sand dollars, sea cucumbers, and sea lilies.
- Echinoderms are important in the sea. Sea urchins eat algae, and sea stars hunt mussels. Sea cucumbers clean the sea floor by eating waste. Some people eat sea urchin eggs and sea cucumbers.

## Examples

**Example 1: A sea star opens a clam.** A sea star wraps its five arms around a clam. Its tube feet grip both shells and pull steadily. After some time the clam's muscle tires and a small gap opens. The sea star pushes its stomach into the gap and digests the clam. This uses the water vascular system.

**Example 2: Counting arms and regrowth.** A sea star has five arms. A fish bites off one arm, leaving four. Over several months the star grows a new arm. The animal has five arms again. This is regeneration.

**Example 3: Telling groups apart.** A round animal covered in long sharp spines, moving slowly on tube feet, is a sea urchin. A long, soft, leathery animal that looks like a cucumber lying on the sea floor is a sea cucumber. An animal with a central disc and five thin, flexible arms that move fast is a brittle star.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that "starfish" are fish. They have no backbone and are echinoderms.
- Believing that adults have bilateral symmetry. Adults are radial; only larvae are bilateral.
- Thinking that tube feet are used only for walking. They also help with feeding and breathing.
- Saying that echinoderms live in fresh water. All are marine.

## Remember

- Spiny skin, internal plate skeleton, radial symmetry.
- Water vascular system with tube feet.
- All marine, with no brain, and many can regenerate.
