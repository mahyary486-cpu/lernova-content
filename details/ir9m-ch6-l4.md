# Linear equations in practice

This lesson shows how to turn real situations into linear equations or systems of linear equations and solve them.

## Key ideas

- Follow these steps for a word problem:
  - Read carefully and decide what the unknown quantities are.
  - Give each unknown a letter, for example x and y, and write down what each letter means, with units.
  - Translate each sentence into an equation.
  - Solve the equation or system.
  - Check the answer against the words of the problem and ask whether it is sensible (for example, a number of people cannot be negative or fractional).
- Many situations follow the pattern total = fixed amount + rate × quantity. This is a linear equation y = mx + b, where b is the fixed part and m is the rate.
- When two unknowns appear, you need two equations. A system of two equations is the natural tool.
- The point where two such lines meet tells us when two options are equal, for example two prices or two plans.

## Worked examples

Example 1. A taxi charges a fixed fee of 20 units plus 5 units for every kilometer. Write a rule for the cost C of a trip of d kilometers, and find the distance when the cost is 70.

Step 1: Fixed part is 20 and the rate is 5 per kilometer, so C = 5d + 20.
Step 2: Put C = 70: 5d + 20 = 70.
Step 3: Subtract 20: 5d = 50, so d = 10.
Step 4: Check: 5 × 10 + 20 = 70. The trip is 10 kilometers.

Example 2. The sum of two numbers is 25 and their difference is 7. Find them.

Step 1: Let x be the larger number and y the smaller number. Then x + y = 25 and x - y = 7.
Step 2: Add the equations: 2x = 32, so x = 16.
Step 3: Then y = 25 - 16 = 9.
Step 4: Check: 16 + 9 = 25 and 16 - 9 = 7. The numbers are 16 and 9.

Example 3. A theater sold 40 tickets. Adult tickets cost 8 units and child tickets cost 5 units. The total income was 245 units. How many tickets of each kind were sold?

Step 1: Let a be the number of adult tickets and c the number of child tickets.
Step 2: Number of tickets: a + c = 40. Income: 8a + 5c = 245.
Step 3: From the first equation, c = 40 - a.
Step 4: Substitute: 8a + 5(40 - a) = 245, so 8a + 200 - 5a = 245, so 3a = 45, so a = 15.
Step 5: Then c = 40 - 15 = 25.
Step 6: Check: 8 × 15 + 5 × 25 = 120 + 125 = 245. The theater sold 15 adult tickets and 25 child tickets.

Example 4. Plan A costs 10 units plus 2 units per hour. Plan B costs 4 units per hour with no fixed fee. When do both plans cost the same?

Step 1: Plan A: y = 2x + 10. Plan B: y = 4x.
Step 2: Set them equal: 4x = 2x + 10, so 2x = 10, so x = 5.
Step 3: The cost is 4 × 5 = 20 units. Check Plan A: 2 × 5 + 10 = 20.
Step 4: For more than 5 hours Plan A is cheaper, and for fewer than 5 hours Plan B is cheaper.

## Common mistakes

- Starting to write equations before deciding what each letter means.
- Mixing units, such as minutes and hours, in the same equation.
- Forgetting to put the fixed fee in the rule, or adding it to the rate instead of to the total.
- Not checking whether the answer makes sense, for example 2.5 people.

## Remember

- Define the unknowns first, then write one equation for each fact.
- Total = fixed part + rate × quantity is a line.
- Always check your answer in the words of the problem.
