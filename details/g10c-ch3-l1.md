# Valence Electrons and the Octet Rule

Atoms bond to reach more stable arrangements of electrons. Only the outermost electrons, the valence electrons, take part in bonding.

## Key ideas
- **Valence electrons** are the electrons in the outermost energy level. For main-group elements, the group number (1, 2, then 13 to 18 as units digit) gives the count: group 1 has 1, group 2 has 2, group 14 has 4, group 15 has 5, group 16 has 6, group 17 has 7, group 18 has 8 (helium has 2).
- **Octet rule:** atoms tend to gain, lose or share electrons until they have 8 valence electrons, like a noble gas. Hydrogen and helium follow the **duet rule** (2 electrons).
- Metals tend to lose electrons and form **cations** (positive ions). Nonmetals tend to gain electrons and form **anions** (negative ions).
- Ions with the same electron count as a noble gas are called isoelectronic with it.
- Electron-dot (Lewis) symbols show valence electrons as dots around the element symbol.

Number of electrons in an ion = atomic number - charge. (For a cation, subtract the charge; for an anion, add its size.)

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Valence electrons of sulfur (Z = 16).**
1. Configuration: 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p4.
2. Outer level n = 3 holds 2 + 4 = 6 electrons.
3. Answer: 6 valence electrons (group 16). Sulfur gains 2 electrons to form S2-.

**Example 2: Electrons in an aluminum ion.**
1. Aluminum is Z = 13 with 3 valence electrons (3s2 3p1).
2. It loses these 3 electrons to reach the octet of the second level, forming Al3+.
3. Electrons = 13 - 3 = 10, the same as neon.

**Example 3: Which noble gas is isoelectronic with Cl-?**
1. Chlorine has Z = 17, so Cl- has 17 + 1 = 18 electrons.
2. Argon has 18 electrons.
3. Cl- is isoelectronic with argon.

## Exceptions
Some molecules do not follow the octet rule. Boron in BF3 has only 6 electrons around it, and elements in period 3 or below, such as sulfur in SF6 (12 electrons), can have more than 8.

## Common mistakes
- Counting all electrons, not only the outermost ones.
- Forgetting that a cation has fewer electrons than the neutral atom.
- Thinking atoms gain or lose protons when they form ions. Only electrons move.
