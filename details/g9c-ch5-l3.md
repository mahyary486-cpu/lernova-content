# Indicators

A strip of paper can tell you whether a liquid is acidic or basic. This is how indicators work.

## Key ideas
- An **indicator** changes color with the pH of a solution.
- **Litmus**: red in acid, blue in base.
- **Phenolphthalein**: colorless in acid, **pink** in base.
- **Universal indicator**: red (strong acid) - yellow - green (neutral) - blue - purple (strong base).
- **Red cabbage juice** is a natural indicator.
- A **pH meter** is more accurate than any indicator.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Litmus test.** Blue litmus paper turns red.
1. Blue litmus turns red in acid.
2. The solution is acidic.

**Example 2: Phenolphthalein.** A solution turns phenolphthalein pink.
1. It turns pink in base.
2. The solution is basic.

**Example 3: Universal indicator.** A solution turns universal indicator green.
1. Green shows a neutral solution.
2. The pH is about 7.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking phenolphthalein turns pink in acid.
- Thinking an indicator shows the exact pH.
- Mixing up the litmus colors.
