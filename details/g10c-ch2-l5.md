# Metals, Nonmetals and Metalloids

The periodic table is divided by a stair-step line running from boron to astatine. Elements on the left are metals, those on the right are nonmetals, and those along the line are metalloids.

## Key ideas
- **Metals** (about 80 percent of the elements): shiny (luster), malleable, ductile, good conductors of heat and electricity, and mostly solid at room temperature (mercury is liquid). They lose electrons to form cations, and their oxides are basic.
- **Nonmetals:** dull, brittle as solids, poor conductors (insulators), lower melting points. They gain or share electrons, and their oxides are acidic. Bromine is the only liquid nonmetal at room temperature.
- **Metalloids:** B, Si, Ge, As, Sb and Te. They have mixed properties and are semiconductors, so their conductivity increases with temperature. Silicon is used in electronics.
- **Alloys** are mixtures of metals, such as brass (copper and zinc) and steel (iron and carbon), designed for better strength.
- **Metallic bonding:** valence electrons move freely among metal cations, which explains conductivity and malleability.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mass in an alloy.** Brass is 70.0 percent copper by mass. How much copper is in 250 g of brass?
1. Copper mass = 0.700 x 250 g.
2. Copper mass = 175 g.

**Example 2: Moles of a metal.** How many moles are in 5.40 g of aluminum (molar mass 26.98 g/mol)?
1. Moles = mass / molar mass.
2. 5.40 g / 26.98 g/mol = 0.200 mol.

**Example 3: Mass from density.** Iron has a density of 7.87 g/cm^3. What is the mass of 10.0 cm^3?
1. Mass = density x volume.
2. 7.87 g/cm^3 x 10.0 cm^3 = 78.7 g.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking that every metal is hard or solid. Mercury is a liquid, and sodium is soft.
- Classifying aluminum as a metalloid. It is a metal, even though it is next to the staircase.
- Calling hydrogen a metal because it is in group 1. It is a nonmetal.
- Saying metalloids conduct as well as metals. They are only semiconductors.
