# Types of Chemical Reactions

Many reactions fall into a few patterns. Recognizing the pattern from the reactants lets you classify a reaction and begin to predict what forms.

## Key ideas
**Synthesis (combination).** Two or more substances combine into one product: A + B -> AB. Example: 2Na + Cl2 -> 2NaCl.

**Decomposition.** One compound breaks into two or more simpler substances: AB -> A + B. Example: 2HgO -> 2Hg + O2. Decomposition usually needs heat, light or electricity.

**Single replacement.** One element takes the place of another in a compound: A + BC -> AC + B. Example: Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2.

**Double replacement.** Two compounds exchange ions: AB + CD -> AD + CB. It often forms a precipitate (a solid), a gas or water. Example: AgNO3 + NaCl -> AgCl + NaNO3. A neutralization is a double replacement between an acid and a base that makes a salt and water.

**Combustion.** A substance reacts rapidly with oxygen and releases heat and light. Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon gives carbon dioxide and water.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: classify Cl2 + 2KBr -> 2KCl + Br2**
1. An element (Cl2) reacts with a compound (KBr).
2. The element replaces bromine in the compound.
3. Type: single replacement.

**Example 2: classify Pb(NO3)2 + 2KI -> PbI2 + 2KNO3**
1. Two compounds swap partners: Pb pairs with I and K pairs with NO3.
2. Type: double replacement. PbI2 is a yellow solid precipitate.

**Example 3: combustion of ethane**
1. Reactants: C2H6 and O2, so it is combustion.
2. Products: CO2 and H2O.
3. Balanced: 2C2H6 + 7O2 -> 4CO2 + 6H2O. Check O: 14 on the left, 8 + 6 = 14 on the right.

## Common mistakes
- Calling every reaction with oxygen a combustion; 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO is better described as synthesis unless a fuel burns.
- Confusing single and double replacement: count the number of reactants that are compounds.
- Forgetting that complete combustion gives CO2, not CO.
- Treating a decomposition as a synthesis because the arrow direction is misread.
