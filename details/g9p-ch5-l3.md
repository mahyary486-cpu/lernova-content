# Reflection and Refraction

A straw in a glass of water looks bent, and your face appears in a mirror. Both effects happen because of what a wave does when it reaches the boundary between two materials. The wave can bounce back, which is reflection, or it can pass through and change direction, which is refraction.

## Key ideas

Reflection is the bouncing of a wave off a surface. To describe it, we draw the normal, an imaginary line at 90 degrees to the surface at the point where the ray hits. The angle of incidence is the angle between the incoming ray and the normal, and the angle of reflection is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal. The law of reflection says that these two angles are equal. It is very important that both angles are measured from the normal and not from the surface.

A plane mirror forms an image that is upright, the same size as the object, and appears to be as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. The image is not really on the mirror's surface, and it is reversed left to right.

Refraction is the bending of a wave when it passes from one material into another in which it travels at a different speed. When light enters a material in which it moves more slowly, such as glass or water, it bends toward the normal. When it leaves that material into air, it bends away from the normal. If the ray hits the boundary along the normal, it does not bend at all. Refraction explains why a pool looks shallower than it really is and why a straw seems broken at the water surface. It is also how lenses work, which the last lesson of this chapter discusses.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: The reflection angle.** A ray strikes a mirror at an angle of incidence of 30 degrees to the normal. What is the angle of reflection?
1. The law of reflection says that the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence.
2. The angle of incidence is 30 degrees.
3. The angle of reflection is also 30 degrees.

**Example 2: An angle given with the surface.** A ray makes an angle of 40 degrees with the surface of a mirror. Find the angle of reflection.
1. The angle to the normal is 90 - 40 = 50 degrees, because the normal is perpendicular to the surface.
2. This is the angle of incidence.
3. The angle of reflection is therefore 50 degrees.

**Example 3: Bending into water.** A ray of light travels from air into water. Which way does it bend?
1. Light moves more slowly in water than in air.
2. When a ray slows down as it enters a new material, it bends toward the normal.
3. So the ray in the water is closer to the normal than the ray in the air.

## Common mistakes

A very frequent mistake is to measure angles from the surface of the mirror instead of from the normal. Another is to bend the ray away from the normal when it enters a material where it slows down. Some students also believe that the image in a mirror is formed on the mirror's surface, while it appears behind the mirror.
