# Reflection and Refraction

A straw looks bent in a glass of water, and you can see your face in a mirror. Both effects come from what light does at a surface.

## Key ideas
- **Reflection**: a wave bounces off a surface.
- **Law of reflection**: angle of incidence = angle of reflection (measured from the normal).
- The **normal** is a line at 90 degrees to the surface.
- **Refraction**: a wave bends when it changes speed in a new material.
- Air to glass or water: bends **toward** the normal. Glass or water to air: bends **away** from the normal.
- A plane mirror image is upright, the same size and appears behind the mirror.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Reflection angle.** A ray hits a mirror at 30 degrees to the normal.
1. The angle of incidence is 30 degrees.
2. The angle of reflection is also 30 degrees.

**Example 2: Angle with the surface.** A ray makes 40 degrees with the mirror surface.
1. The angle to the normal is 90 - 40 = 50 degrees.
2. The angle of reflection is 50 degrees.

**Example 3: Bending into water.** A ray travels from air into water.
1. Light slows down in water.
2. It bends toward the normal.

## Common mistakes
- Measuring angles from the mirror surface instead of from the normal.
- Bending the ray away from the normal when it slows down.
- Thinking a mirror image is on the mirror's surface.
