# Astronomy and observing the sky

Astronomy is the science that studies the objects in space and the events that happen there.

## Key ideas

- Astronomy studies stars, planets, moons, comets, galaxies and the whole universe. Astronomers use observation, measurement and mathematics to test ideas.
- The oldest tool is the naked eye. Ancient people used the sky to make calendars, to know when to plant crops, and to find their way on journeys.
- A telescope collects more light than the eye, so faint and far objects can be seen. A refracting telescope uses lenses to collect light. A reflecting telescope uses a curved mirror. Most large modern telescopes are reflecting telescopes.
- Radio telescopes collect radio waves from space. Some objects send out waves that we cannot see, so we need different instruments to study them.
- Telescopes in space, such as the Hubble Space Telescope, work above Earth's atmosphere. The air blurs the light and blocks some kinds of waves, so images from space can be sharper.
- Light travels at about 300,000 km/s. A light-year is the distance light travels in one year: about 9.46 × 10^12 km. It is a unit of distance, not of time.
- Astronomy is a science, because its ideas are checked with evidence. Astrology claims that stars control human life. Its claims cannot be tested and do not agree with evidence, so it is not a science.
- Many Iranian scholars worked in astronomy. Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi described the brightness and positions of stars in his Book of Fixed Stars. Nasir al-Din Tusi built the Maragheh observatory. Omar Khayyam helped to design the Jalali calendar, which is very accurate.

## Examples

Example 1: How long does sunlight take to reach Earth?
- Distance from the Sun to Earth: 1.5 × 10^8 km.
- Speed of light: 3 × 10^5 km/s.
- Time = distance / speed = 1.5 × 10^8 / 3 × 10^5 = 500 s.
- 500 s / 60 ≈ 8.3 minutes. So we see the Sun as it was about 8 minutes ago.

Example 2: How long does light take to travel from the Moon to Earth?
- Distance: 384,400 km.
- Time = 384,400 / 300,000 ≈ 1.28 s.
- Light from the Moon reaches us in a little more than one second.

Example 3: Magnification of a simple telescope.
- Magnification = focal length of the objective lens / focal length of the eyepiece.
- A telescope has an objective of focal length 1000 mm and an eyepiece of focal length 20 mm.
- Magnification = 1000 / 20 = 50.
- The telescope makes objects look 50 times larger.

Example 4: Choosing a good place to observe.
- A student in a large city sees only a few stars because street lights make the sky bright. This is called light pollution.
- The same student in a desert village far from the lights sees thousands of stars.
- This is why large observatories are built on high mountains and in dry places with dark and clear skies.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that a light-year is a unit of time. It is a unit of distance.
- Thinking that a telescope mainly makes things bigger. Its most important job is to collect more light.
- Mixing up astronomy and astrology. Only astronomy is a science.
- Thinking that stars seen at night are all the same distance away. They are at very different distances.

## Remember

- A light-year is about 9.46 × 10^12 km.
- Telescopes can use lenses (refracting) or mirrors (reflecting), and we also use radio telescopes and space telescopes.
- Time = distance / speed also works for light.
