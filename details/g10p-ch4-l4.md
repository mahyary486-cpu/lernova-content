# Conservation of Mechanical Energy

Mechanical energy is the total of kinetic and potential energy. In many situations this total stays constant, which gives a powerful shortcut for finding speeds and heights.

## Key ideas
- **E = KE + PE.**
- If only conservative forces (gravity, spring force) do work, mechanical energy is conserved: **KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f**.
- Falling objects convert potential energy into kinetic energy, and rising objects do the reverse.
- For free fall from rest through height h: v = sqrt(2 g h). The mass cancels.
- If friction or air resistance acts, mechanical energy decreases. The lost energy becomes thermal energy: E_lost = E_initial - E_final. Total energy is still conserved.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Dropped ball.** A ball falls from rest from 20 m. Ignore air resistance.
1. m g h = 1/2 m v^2, so v^2 = 2 g h.
2. v^2 = 2 x 9.8 x 20 = 392.
3. v = 19.8 m/s.

**Example 2: Thrown upward.** A 0.2 kg ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s.
1. At the top, all kinetic energy has become potential energy: 1/2 m v^2 = m g h.
2. h = v^2 / (2 g) = 225 / 19.6.
3. h = 11.5 m.

**Example 3: Slide with friction.** A 50 kg child starts at rest at the top of a 4 m high slide and reaches the bottom at 5 m/s.
1. PE at top = 50 x 9.8 x 4 = 1960 J.
2. KE at bottom = 0.5 x 50 x 25 = 625 J.
3. Energy turned into thermal energy = 1960 - 625 = 1335 J.

## Common mistakes
- Using conservation of mechanical energy when friction is present without adding the lost energy.
- Thinking a heavier object falls faster in the absence of air resistance.
- Using the total height instead of the height fallen between two points.
- Forgetting the square root when solving for speed.
