# Solutions: Solute and Solvent

When sugar disappears into tea, it has not vanished. Its particles have spread evenly among the water particles.

## Key ideas
- A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture of a **solute** in a **solvent**.
- The **solvent** is present in the larger amount and does the dissolving.
- Water is the **universal solvent**: it dissolves many ionic and polar substances.
- Solutions can be solid (alloys), liquid (sea water) or gas (air).
- Dissolving is a **physical change**; the solute can be recovered by evaporation.
- **Like dissolves like**: polar dissolves polar; nonpolar dissolves nonpolar.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Naming parts.** Salt is dissolved in water.
1. Salt is the substance dissolved: the solute.
2. Water does the dissolving: the solvent.

**Example 2: Recovering the solute.** How can salt be recovered from salt water?
1. Evaporate the water.
2. The salt remains, because dissolving is a physical change.

**Example 3: Oil and water.** Why does oil not dissolve in water?
1. Oil is nonpolar and water is polar.
2. Like dissolves like, so they do not mix.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the solute disappears or is destroyed.
- Thinking the solvent is always a liquid.
- Thinking dissolving is a chemical change.
