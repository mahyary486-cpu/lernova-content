# Relative Velocity

Velocity is always measured relative to something. A passenger who sits still on a moving train has zero velocity relative to the train, yet moves quickly relative to the ground.

## Key ideas
- The velocity of object A relative to object C equals the velocity of A relative to B plus the velocity of B relative to C. In symbols: v(AC) = v(AB) + v(BC).
- Boat in a river: velocity relative to shore = velocity relative to water + velocity of the water (current).
- Aircraft in wind: ground velocity = air velocity + wind velocity.
- Same direction: add speeds. Opposite directions: subtract speeds.
- Perpendicular velocities: use the Pythagorean theorem, v = sqrt(v1^2 + v2^2).
- Motion along perpendicular directions is independent. A boat aimed straight across a river crosses in time = width / (speed across), and the current only carries it downstream.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: upstream and downstream.** A boat moves at 5.0 m/s in still water. The river flows at 2.0 m/s.
1. Downstream: 5.0 + 2.0 = 7.0 m/s relative to the shore.
2. Upstream: 5.0 - 2.0 = 3.0 m/s relative to the shore.

**Example 2: crossing a river.** A boat heads straight across a 100 m wide river at 5.0 m/s relative to the water. The current is 2.0 m/s.
1. Crossing time = 100 m / 5.0 m/s = 20 s.
2. Downstream drift = 2.0 m/s x 20 s = 40 m.
3. Speed relative to shore = sqrt(5.0^2 + 2.0^2) = sqrt(29) = 5.4 m/s.

**Example 3: two cars.** Car A moves at 30 m/s east.
1. If car B moves at 20 m/s east, the velocity of A relative to B is 30 - 20 = 10 m/s east.
2. If car B moves at 20 m/s west, the velocity of A relative to B is 30 - (-20) = 50 m/s east.

## Common mistakes
- Adding speeds when the objects move in opposite directions.
- Using the full boat speed to find crossing time when the boat is not aimed straight across.
- Forgetting the reference frame: always ask "relative to what?".
- Adding perpendicular velocities directly instead of using the Pythagorean theorem.
