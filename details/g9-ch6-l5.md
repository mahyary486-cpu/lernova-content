# Quadratic Word Problems

Quadratic models appear in motion under gravity, area, revenue and number puzzles. The vertex gives a maximum or minimum, and the zeros give where the quantity equals 0.

## Strategy
1. Define the variable and write the model.
2. Decide what is being asked: a maximum or minimum (vertex), a zero (when does it hit the ground), or a value at a given input.
3. Solve, then reject answers that do not make sense, such as negative time or length.

## Projectile model
Height after t seconds is often h = -16t^2 + v*t + s in feet, where v is the starting upward speed and s the starting height.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Projectile.** A ball is thrown up from the ground: h = -16t^2 + 48t.
1. When does it land? Set h = 0: -16t(t - 3) = 0, so t = 0 or t = 3. It lands after 3 seconds.
2. Maximum height: the vertex is at t = -48/(2*(-16)) = 1.5. Then h = -16(2.25) + 72 = 36 feet.

**Example 2: Area.** A farmer has 40 m of fence for a rectangle. Find the largest area.
1. Width w, length 20 - w (since the perimeter is 40).
2. A = w(20 - w) = -w^2 + 20w.
3. The vertex is at w = 10, and A = 100.
4. The best shape is a 10 m by 10 m square with area 100 square meters.

**Example 3: Number problem.** The product of two consecutive positive integers is 56. Find them.
1. n(n + 1) = 56, so n^2 + n - 56 = 0.
2. (n + 8)(n - 7) = 0, so n = -8 or n = 7.
3. Reject -8 because the numbers must be positive. The integers are 7 and 8.

**Example 4: Revenue.** R = -5p^2 + 100p. The best price is p = -100/(2*(-5)) = 10 dollars, giving R = -500 + 1000 = 500 dollars.

## Common mistakes
- Giving the y-value when the question asks for the x-value, or the reverse. Time at the maximum is different from the maximum height.
- Keeping both roots when one is not physically possible.
- Forgetting units.
- Writing the wrong second dimension in perimeter problems; use half the perimeter minus the first side.
