# Electrolytes, Ionic Equations and Precipitation

Tap water conducts electricity because it contains dissolved ions, but pure sugar water does not.

## Key ideas
- **Strong electrolytes** fully dissociate; **weak** partly; **non-electrolytes** not at all.
- **Complete ionic equation**: all ions shown.
- **Net ionic equation**: only the ions that react.
- **Spectator ions** are unchanged.
- Example: **Ag+ + Cl- -> AgCl(s)**.
- Use **solubility rules** to predict precipitates.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Net ionic equation.** Write the net ionic equation for Pb(NO3)2 + 2 KI -> PbI2 + 2 KNO3.
1. PbI2 is the precipitate; K+ and NO3- are spectators.
2. Pb2+(aq) + 2 I-(aq) -> PbI2(s).

**Example 2: Precipitate mass.** 50 mL of 0.1 M AgNO3 reacts with excess NaCl (AgCl 143.5 g/mol).
1. n(Ag+) = 0.1 x 0.05 = 0.005 mol = n(AgCl).
2. Mass = 0.005 x 143.5 = 0.72 g.

**Example 3: Conductivity.** Which solution conducts better: 0.1 M NaCl or 0.1 M sugar?
1. NaCl gives ions; sugar does not.
2. NaCl conducts better.

## Common mistakes
- Writing spectator ions in the net ionic equation.
- Splitting solids or weak electrolytes into ions.
- Not balancing charges.
