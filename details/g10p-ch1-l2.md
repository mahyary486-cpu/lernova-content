# Average Speed and Velocity

Speed tells how fast something moves; velocity also tells which way. Both are rates, meaning a quantity divided by time.

## Key ideas
- **Average speed** = total distance / total time. It is a scalar and is never negative.
- **Average velocity** = displacement / time, or v_avg = Δx / Δt. It is a vector, so its sign gives the direction.
- The SI unit of both is meters per second (m/s). To convert, divide km/h by 3.6 to get m/s.
- If the object returns to its start, the average velocity is zero even though the average speed is not.
- **Instantaneous velocity** is the velocity at one moment. For constant velocity, position follows x = x0 + vt.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Unit conversion.** Express 90 km/h in m/s.
1. 90 km = 90 000 m and 1 h = 3600 s.
2. 90 000 / 3600 = 25 m/s.

**Example 2: Lap of a track.** A runner covers a 400 m lap in 80 s and ends at the start.
1. Average speed = 400 m / 80 s = 5.0 m/s.
2. Displacement is 0 m, so average velocity = 0 m / 80 s = 0 m/s.

**Example 3: Two-part trip.** A car drives 60 km at 30 km/h and then 60 km at 60 km/h.
1. Time of part one = 60 / 30 = 2.0 h. Time of part two = 60 / 60 = 1.0 h.
2. Total distance = 120 km and total time = 3.0 h.
3. Average speed = 120 / 3.0 = 40 km/h.
4. Notice this is not the simple average (45 km/h) of the two speeds.

**Example 4: Position at a later time.** A car starts at x = 5 m with velocity +12 m/s.
1. x = x0 + vt = 5 + 12 x 6.0.
2. x = 77 m after 6.0 s.

## Common mistakes
- Averaging two speeds without weighting by time. Always use total distance divided by total time.
- Calling speed and velocity the same thing. A negative velocity is a legitimate answer.
- Forgetting to convert km/h to m/s before mixing with meters and seconds.
- Dropping the starting position when finding where an object is, not how far it moved.
