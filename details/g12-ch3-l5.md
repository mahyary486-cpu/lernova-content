# Compound Interest, Annuities and Loans

## Compound interest review
**A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)**, and with continuous compounding **A = P e^(rt)**.

## Effective annual rate
The **effective annual rate** is the single yearly rate that gives the same growth: **(1 + r/n)^n - 1**.
- 6% compounded monthly: (1.005)^12 - 1 = 0.0617, so about **6.17%**.
- 6% compounded continuously: e^0.06 - 1, about **6.18%**.
At the same nominal rate, more frequent compounding gives more, and continuous compounding gives the most.

## Present value
**PV = A / (1 + r/n)^(nt)** is how much to invest now to have A later.
To have 10,000 dollars in 5 years at 4% per year: PV = 10000/1.04^5, about **8,219 dollars**.

## Rule of 72
The doubling time is roughly **72 divided by the percent rate**. At 6%: 72/6 = **12 years**. (The exact value is ln 2 / ln 1.06, about 11.9.)

## Annuity (regular deposits)
The future value of equal payments PMT made at the end of each period, with rate i per period and N periods:
**FV = PMT * ((1 + i)^N - 1) / i**
Example: 100 dollars per month, 6% per year compounded monthly (i = 0.005), for 2 years (N = 24):
FV = 100 * (1.005^24 - 1)/0.005, about 100 * 25.43 = **2,543 dollars**.

## Loan payment
The payment that pays off a loan of P dollars with N equal payments at rate i per period:
**PMT = P * i / (1 - (1 + i)^(-N))**
Example: a 1,000 dollar loan, 12 months, 1% per month: PMT = 1000(0.01)/(1 - 1.01^(-12)), about **88.85 dollars** per month.
The total paid is about 12 * 88.85 = 1066.2 dollars, so the interest is about 66 dollars.

## Common mistakes
- Using the yearly rate when the payments are monthly. Use i = r/12.
- Mixing up N (number of payments) with t (years).
- Forgetting to convert the percent to a decimal.
