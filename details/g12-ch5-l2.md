# Sum and Difference Formulas

Trigonometric functions do not distribute over addition: sin(A + B) is not sin A + sin B. The sum and difference formulas give the correct expansions.

## Key ideas
- sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B
- sin(A - B) = sin A cos B - cos A sin B
- cos(A + B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B
- cos(A - B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B
- tan(A + B) = (tan A + tan B) / (1 - tan A tan B)
- tan(A - B) = (tan A - tan B) / (1 + tan A tan B)

Notice that the sign inside cosine is opposite to the sign on the left, while sine keeps the same sign.

## Uses
- Find exact values for angles such as 15 degrees, 75 degrees, and 105 degrees by writing them as sums or differences of special angles (30, 45, 60, 90).
- Simplify expressions such as sin 40 cos 20 + cos 40 sin 20 = sin 60.
- Prove other identities, such as the cofunction identities.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Find the exact value of cos 75 degrees.
1. Write 75 = 45 + 30.
2. cos 75 = cos 45 cos 30 - sin 45 sin 30.
3. = (sqrt(2)/2)(sqrt(3)/2) - (sqrt(2)/2)(1/2) = (sqrt(6) - sqrt(2)) / 4.

**Example 2.** Find sin 15 degrees.
1. Write 15 = 45 - 30.
2. sin 15 = sin 45 cos 30 - cos 45 sin 30.
3. = (sqrt(6) - sqrt(2)) / 4.

**Example 3.** Given sin A = 3/5 (A in Quadrant I) and cos B = 5/13 (B in Quadrant I), find sin(A + B).
1. cos A = 4/5 and sin B = 12/13.
2. sin(A + B) = (3/5)(5/13) + (4/5)(12/13) = 15/65 + 48/65.
3. = 63/65.

## Common mistakes
- Believing sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B. Test A = B = 30 degrees: sin 60 is not 1.
- Using the wrong sign in the cosine formulas.
- Forgetting to find the missing sine or cosine value first, using the Pythagorean identity.
- Taking the wrong sign for a square root. Check the quadrant.
