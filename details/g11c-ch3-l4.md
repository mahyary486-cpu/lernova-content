# Osmosis and Osmotic Pressure

Salty water makes a fresh cucumber slice shrink because water flows out of its cells.

## Key ideas
- **Osmosis**: solvent flows toward the **more concentrated** solution.
- **pi = i M R T**.
- **Isotonic**: equal; **hypotonic**: lower; **hypertonic**: higher concentration.
- Cells **swell** in hypotonic and **shrink** in hypertonic solutions.
- **Reverse osmosis**: pressure above pi pushes water out of the salty side.
- Uses: **IV fluids**, food preservation, **desalination**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Osmotic pressure.** A 0.10 M sugar solution at 300 K (R = 0.0821).
1. pi = i M R T = 1 x 0.10 x 0.0821 x 300.
2. pi = 2.46 atm.

**Example 2: Direction.** Water and a salt solution are separated by a semipermeable membrane.
1. Solvent moves toward the higher solute concentration.
2. Water flows into the salt solution.

**Example 3: Red blood cells.** A red blood cell is placed in pure water.
1. The water is hypotonic to the cell.
2. Water enters and the cell swells and may burst.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking solute moves in osmosis. The solvent moves.
- Using Celsius for T.
- Mixing up hypotonic and hypertonic.
