# Groups, Periods and Families

Position in the periodic table tells you the electron arrangement of an atom, and the electron arrangement controls chemical behavior.

## Key ideas
- **Group number (main groups):** elements in the same group have the same number of **valence electrons** (outer-level electrons). Group 1 has 1, group 2 has 2, and groups 13 to 18 have 3 to 8, so valence electrons = group number minus 10 for groups 13 to 18.
- **Period number:** equals the highest occupied energy level n.
- **Families:** group 1 alkali metals (very reactive, form 1+ ions), group 2 alkaline earth metals (form 2+ ions), group 17 halogens (form 1- ions), group 18 noble gases (full outer level, very unreactive), groups 3 to 12 transition metals.
- **Ion charges:** metals lose valence electrons to reach a noble gas configuration, and nonmetals gain electrons. Group 16 forms 2- ions and group 15 forms 3- ions.
- **Formula rule:** the total positive charge must equal the total negative charge.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Position from configuration.** An atom has the configuration 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p3.
1. The highest level is n = 3, so it is in period 3.
2. The valence electrons are 3s2 3p3, which is 5 electrons.
3. For the p-block, group = 10 + 5 = 15. The atom has 15 electrons, so it is phosphorus.

**Example 2: Formula of a compound.** Magnesium (group 2) reacts with nitrogen (group 15).
1. Mg forms Mg2+, and N forms N3-.
2. The lowest common multiple of 2 and 3 is 6, so use 3 Mg (+6) and 2 N (-6).
3. The formula is Mg3N2.

**Example 3: Reaction amounts.** 2Na + Cl2 -> 2NaCl. How many moles of NaCl form from 0.50 mol Cl2?
1. The ratio is 1 mol Cl2 to 2 mol NaCl.
2. Moles of NaCl = 0.50 mol x 2 = 1.0 mol.

## Common mistakes
- Using the period number as the number of valence electrons. The group tells you that.
- Writing a formula such as MgN instead of balancing charges.
- Thinking that hydrogen belongs with the alkali metals. It is a nonmetal that is placed above group 1.
- Believing that elements in a group have the same number of energy levels. They have the same valence electrons but different levels.
