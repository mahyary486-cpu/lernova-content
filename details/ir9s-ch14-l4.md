# Birds

Birds are warm-blooded vertebrates with feathers and wings that lay hard-shelled eggs.

## Key ideas

- Birds are endothermic, or warm-blooded. They keep a constant body temperature, usually between 40 °C and 42 °C, even when the air is cold. This needs a lot of food energy, so birds eat often.
- Feathers are a unique feature of birds. Flight feathers on the wings and tail help with flying and steering. Soft down feathers close to the body trap air and keep the bird warm. Feathers also give color and help in water-proofing.
- Many bird features help flight. The body is light and streamlined. Many bones are thin and hollow, with air spaces, but they are strong. Birds have no teeth, and a light beak replaces them. The strong chest muscles attach to a large breastbone called the keel and move the wings.
- Birds have a very efficient breathing system. Besides lungs, they have air sacs that make air flow through the lungs in one direction. This gives plenty of oxygen to the muscles during flight.
- The heart has four chambers, and oxygen-rich blood is fully separated from oxygen-poor blood. This supports the high energy needs of flight.
- Fertilization is internal. The female lays eggs with a hard shell made of calcium carbonate. Most birds incubate their eggs, which means they keep them warm with the body. Parents feed and protect the chicks.
- Beaks and feet match the way a bird lives. A hawk has a hooked beak and sharp claws for catching meat. A duck has webbed feet for swimming. A sparrow has a short strong beak for seeds. A heron has long legs and a long beak for catching fish in shallow water.
- Not all birds fly. Ostriches and penguins cannot fly. The ostrich runs fast and the penguin swims using its wings as flippers.
- Many birds migrate, meaning they travel long distances each year to find food or a warm place to breed.

## Examples

- Why a bird eats so much: A small bird must keep its body near 41 °C on a cold morning. Its body loses heat to the air, so it must produce heat from food all the time. This is why a small garden bird may eat a large part of its own mass in food each day.
- Beak and food: A finch has a short, thick beak, which is good for cracking seeds. A hummingbird has a long thin beak, which reaches into flowers for nectar. From the shape of a beak, we can guess what the bird eats.
- Nesting: A hen sits on her eggs for about 21 days. Her body keeps the eggs at around 37 °C to 38 °C, and the embryos develop in the shell with the food of the yolk. After hatching, chicks of some birds are able to walk at once, while others need feeding in the nest.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking all birds can fly. Ostriches, emus and penguins cannot.
- Thinking hollow bones are weak. They are light and strong at the same time.
- Thinking feathers are only for flight. They also keep birds warm.
- Thinking birds are reptiles with the same temperature control. Birds are warm-blooded.

## Remember

- Birds: feathers, wings, hard-shelled eggs, four-chamber heart, warm-blooded.
- Light bodies and air sacs make flight possible.
- Beak and foot shape match the bird's food and habitat.
