# Chemical Equations and Balancing

A chemical equation is a short way to describe a reaction. Reactants are on the left, products on the right, and an arrow means "yields". State symbols show physical states: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.

## Key ideas
**Conservation of mass.** Atoms are not created or destroyed in a chemical reaction, so the number of each kind of atom must be the same on both sides. The total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.

**Coefficients and subscripts.** Balance an equation only by changing coefficients, the numbers in front of formulas. Changing a subscript changes the substance: H2O is water, H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide.

**Balancing steps.**
1. Write the correct formulas.
2. Count atoms of each element on both sides.
3. Add coefficients, usually leaving hydrogen and oxygen for last. Treat a polyatomic ion that stays unchanged as one unit.
4. Recount and reduce to the smallest whole numbers.

**Mole ratios.** The coefficients give the ratio of moles of each substance.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: C3H8 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O**
1. Balance C: 3 CO2.
2. Balance H: 8 H needs 4 H2O.
3. Count O on the right: 3 x 2 + 4 = 10, so 5 O2.
4. Result: C3H8 + 5O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O. The sum of coefficients is 1 + 5 + 3 + 4 = 13.

**Example 2: Al + O2 -> Al2O3**
1. Oxygen is 2 on the left and 3 on the right, so use 3 O2 and 2 Al2O3 for 6 O each side.
2. Right side has 4 Al, so use 4 Al.
3. Result: 4Al + 3O2 -> 2Al2O3.

**Example 3: mass conservation**
2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O. If 4.0 g of H2 react with 32.0 g of O2:
1. Total reactant mass = 4.0 + 32.0 = 36.0 g.
2. The mass of water formed is 36.0 g.

## Common mistakes
- Changing subscripts to make atoms match.
- Counting atoms only on one side.
- Forgetting that a coefficient multiplies the whole formula: 2Al2(SO4)3 contains 4 Al, 6 S and 24 O.
- Leaving coefficients that can still be reduced.
