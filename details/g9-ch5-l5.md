# Factoring Special Cases and by Grouping

Recognizing patterns makes factoring much faster. These are the special-product rules from the earlier lesson, run backwards.

## Patterns
- **Difference of squares:** a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)
- **Perfect square trinomials:** a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a + b)^2 and a^2 - 2ab + b^2 = (a - b)^2
- **Grouping:** for four terms, factor a common factor out of the first pair and out of the second pair. If the same binomial appears, factor it out.
- A **sum** of squares such as x^2 + 9 cannot be factored over the real numbers.

## Recognizing a perfect square trinomial
The first and last terms are perfect squares, and the middle term is twice the product of their square roots. For 4x^2 + 20x + 25: sqrt(4x^2) = 2x, sqrt(25) = 5, and 2(2x)(5) = 20x. It matches.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Factor 25x^2 - 49.
1. This is (5x)^2 - 7^2.
2. Answer: (5x + 7)(5x - 7).

**Example 2.** Factor x^2 - 10x + 25.
1. x^2 and 25 are squares, and 2(x)(5) = 10x.
2. Answer: (x - 5)^2.

**Example 3.** Factor x^3 + 3x^2 + 2x + 6 by grouping.
1. Group: (x^3 + 3x^2) + (2x + 6).
2. Factor each group: x^2(x + 3) + 2(x + 3).
3. Factor out the common binomial: (x + 3)(x^2 + 2).

**Example 4.** Factor 2x^3 - 50x completely.
1. GCF first: 2x(x^2 - 25).
2. Difference of squares: 2x(x + 5)(x - 5).

## Common mistakes
- Trying to factor a sum of squares such as x^2 + 16.
- Calling x^2 + 10x + 25 a perfect square without checking the middle term.
- Forgetting to take out the GCF first, which hides the pattern.
- In grouping, a sign slip when the third term is negative. Factor out a negative so the binomials match: x^2(x - 2) - 3(x - 2).
