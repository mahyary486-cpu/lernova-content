# Addition Polymers

Plastic bags, pipes and non-stick pans are all addition polymers built from small alkene molecules.

## Key ideas
- **Monomers** with **C=C** form **addition polymers**; no atoms are lost.
- Ethene -> **poly(ethene)**; chloroethene -> **PVC**; tetrafluoroethene -> **PTFE**.
- The repeat unit: **-(CH2-CH2)n-**.
- **Thermoplastics** soften; **thermosetting** plastics do not.
- **Branched** chains: flexible, low density; **linear**: stronger, high density.
- Most are **not biodegradable**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Repeat unit.** Draw the repeat unit of poly(propene).
1. Propene is CH2=CH-CH3.
2. The repeat unit is -(CH2-CH(CH3))n-.

**Example 2: Monomer.** What monomer gives PVC?
1. PVC is poly(chloroethene).
2. The monomer is chloroethene, CH2=CHCl.

**Example 3: Mass of polymer.** 1000 ethene molecules form a polymer. What is the molar mass per mole of chains (C2H4 = 28)?
1. Mass = 1000 x 28.
2. 28,000 g/mol.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking atoms are lost in addition polymerization.
- Confusing thermoplastics and thermosets.
- Forgetting that the double bond is opened.
