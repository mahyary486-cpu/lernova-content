# Magnetic Materials, Motors and Applications

Loudspeakers, electric motors, MRI machines and hard drives all rely on magnetic materials and the forces on currents.

## Key ideas
- **Ferromagnetic** materials (Fe, Ni, Co) have aligned **domains**.
- Above the **Curie temperature** a ferromagnet loses its magnetism.
- An **iron core** greatly strengthens an electromagnet.
- Motor torque on a coil: **tau = N I A B sin(theta)**.
- A **commutator** keeps the torque in the same direction.
- **Mass spectrometer**: **r = m v / (q B)** separates ions by mass.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Coil torque.** A coil of 50 turns, area 0.02 m^2 carries 2 A in a 0.4 T field with sin(theta) = 1.
1. tau = N I A B = 50 x 2 x 0.02 x 0.4.
2. tau = 0.8 N m.

**Example 2: Mass spectrometer.** Ions (m = 3.3 x 10^-26 kg, q = 1.6 x 10^-19 C) move at 4 x 10^5 m/s in a 0.5 T field.
1. r = m v / (q B) = 3.3 x 10^-26 x 4 x 10^5 / (1.6 x 10^-19 x 0.5).
2. r = 0.165 m.

**Example 3: Heating a magnet.** Why does a magnet lose its strength when heated strongly?
1. Heating makes the domains vibrate and become random.
2. Above the Curie temperature the alignment is lost.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking all metals are attracted to magnets.
- Forgetting the commutator in a DC motor.
- Confusing the Curie temperature with the melting point.
