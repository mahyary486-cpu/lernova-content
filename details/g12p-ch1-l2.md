# Electric Fields

The field tells what force a charge would feel at a point, even if no charge is there.

## Key ideas
- **E = F / q**; unit **N/C = V/m**.
- Point charge: **E = k Q / r^2**.
- Field lines go from **positive** to **negative** and never cross.
- **F = q E**; a positive charge accelerates **along** the field.
- Between parallel plates the field is **uniform**: E = V / d.
- Inside a conductor in equilibrium **E = 0**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Field of a charge.** Find the field 0.5 m from a 5 x 10^-9 C charge.
1. E = k Q / r^2 = 9 x 10^9 x 5 x 10^-9 / 0.25.
2. E = 180 N/C.

**Example 2: Force on a charge.** A 2 x 10^-6 C charge is in a field of 300 N/C.
1. F = q E = 2 x 10^-6 x 300.
2. F = 6 x 10^-4 N.

**Example 3: Acceleration of an electron.** An electron (9.1 x 10^-31 kg) is in a field of 1000 N/C.
1. F = e E = 1.6 x 10^-19 x 1000 = 1.6 x 10^-16 N.
2. a = F / m = 1.76 x 10^14 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a negative charge moves along the field.
- Forgetting that the field is a vector.
- Confusing E with the force.
