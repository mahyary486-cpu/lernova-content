# Gauss's Law and Conductors

Gauss's law shows why a car acts as a shield during lightning: the field inside a conductor is zero.

## Key ideas
- **Flux** = E A cos(theta).
- **Gauss's law**: **flux = Q_enclosed / epsilon_0**.
- **epsilon_0 = 8.85 x 10^-12 C^2/(N m^2)**; **k = 1 / (4 pi epsilon_0)**.
- Outside a charged sphere: **E = k Q / r^2**.
- Near a conductor's surface: **E = sigma / epsilon_0**.
- A **Faraday cage** shields its inside from external fields.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Flux.** A field of 200 N/C passes perpendicularly through 0.5 m^2.
1. Flux = E A cos(0) = 200 x 0.5.
2. Flux = 100 N m^2/C.

**Example 2: Enclosed charge.** The flux through a closed surface is 1.13 x 10^3 N m^2/C.
1. Q = flux x epsilon_0 = 1.13 x 10^3 x 8.85 x 10^-12.
2. Q = 1.0 x 10^-8 C.

**Example 3: Surface charge.** A conductor has sigma = 2 x 10^-6 C/m^2.
1. E = sigma / epsilon_0 = 2 x 10^-6 / 8.85 x 10^-12.
2. E = 2.26 x 10^5 N/C.

## Common mistakes
- Using Gauss's law with a surface that is not closed.
- Counting charge outside the surface.
- Forgetting the angle in the flux.
