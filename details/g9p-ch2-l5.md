# Free Fall and Gravity

Objects that are dropped speed up as they fall. Aristotle thought that heavy objects fall faster than light ones, but Galileo showed that, when air does not interfere, a heavy object and a light one fall at the same rate. This lesson explains how objects move when gravity is the only force acting on them.

## Key ideas

An object is in free fall when gravity is the only force acting on it. Near the surface of the Earth, every object in free fall has the same downward acceleration, called the acceleration due to gravity, g = 9.8 m/s^2 (about 10 m/s^2 for quick estimates). This does not depend on the mass. A hammer and a feather dropped in a vacuum land at the same moment. On Earth a feather falls more slowly only because the air pushes against it much more strongly than against a hammer.

Because the acceleration is constant, the motion is easy to describe. If an object is dropped from rest, its speed after t seconds is v = g x t, and the distance it has fallen is d = 1/2 x g x t^2. Each second adds about 9.8 m/s to the downward speed. After 1 s the speed is 9.8 m/s, after 2 s it is 19.6 m/s, and so on. The distance, however, grows with the square of the time, so the object falls much farther in the second second than in the first.

Air resistance opposes the motion, and it grows as the speed grows. When it becomes equal to the weight of the falling object, the net force is zero, the acceleration stops, and the object falls at a constant speed called terminal velocity. A skydiver reaches terminal velocity after some time, and opening a parachute increases the air resistance so the terminal velocity becomes much smaller.

When a ball is thrown upward, gravity slows it down on the way up, it stops for an instant at the top, and then it speeds up on the way down. Through the entire flight the acceleration is the same: 9.8 m/s^2 downward. At the top the velocity is zero, but the acceleration is not.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Speed of a falling object.** A ball is dropped from rest. What is its speed after 3 s?
1. Use v = g x t.
2. Substitute: v = 9.8 x 3.
3. The speed is 29.4 m/s downward.

**Example 2: Distance fallen.** A stone falls from rest for 2 s. How far does it fall?
1. Use d = 1/2 x g x t^2.
2. Substitute: d = 0.5 x 9.8 x 2^2 = 0.5 x 9.8 x 4.
3. The distance is 19.6 m.

**Example 3: Height of a bridge.** A stone dropped from a bridge hits the water after 3 s. How high is the bridge?
1. The stone falls from rest, so d = 1/2 x g x t^2.
2. Substitute: d = 0.5 x 9.8 x 3^2 = 0.5 x 9.8 x 9.
3. The bridge is about 44.1 m high.

## Common mistakes

Many students believe that heavier objects fall faster in a vacuum, but all objects have the same acceleration when only gravity acts. Another common error is to say that the acceleration of a ball is zero at the top of its flight; the velocity is zero there, but the acceleration remains 9.8 m/s^2 downward. Finally, do not use d = g x t for the distance; the correct formula contains the square of the time and the factor 1/2.
