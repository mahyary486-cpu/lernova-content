# Free Fall and Gravity

Dropped objects speed up as they fall. Galileo showed that, without air, a heavy object and a light one fall at the same rate.

## Key ideas
- **Free fall**: only gravity acts. Acceleration is **g = 9.8 m/s^2** downward (about 10 for estimates).
- g does not depend on mass: without air resistance a hammer and a feather land together.
- From rest: **v = g t** and **d = 1/2 g t^2**.
- **Air resistance** opposes motion. When it equals the weight, the object falls at **terminal velocity**.
- A ball thrown up has velocity zero at the top but its acceleration is still 9.8 m/s^2 downward.
- Each second of free fall adds about 9.8 m/s to the downward speed.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Speed of a falling object.** A ball is dropped from rest for 3 s.
1. v = g t = 9.8 x 3.
2. v = 29.4 m/s.

**Example 2: Distance fallen.** A stone falls for 2 s from rest.
1. d = 1/2 g t^2 = 0.5 x 9.8 x 2^2.
2. d = 19.6 m.

**Example 3: Height of a bridge.** A stone dropped from a bridge hits the water after 3 s.
1. d = 1/2 x 9.8 x 3^2 = 0.5 x 9.8 x 9.
2. d = 44.1 m.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking heavier objects fall faster in a vacuum.
- Thinking acceleration is zero at the top of a throw.
- Using d = g t instead of d = 1/2 g t^2.
