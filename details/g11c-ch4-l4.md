# Bond Energies and Energy Changes

Breaking bonds costs energy and making bonds gives energy back. The balance decides whether a reaction is exothermic or endothermic.

## Key ideas
- **Breaking bonds absorbs energy; forming bonds releases energy.**
- **delta H = sum(bonds broken) - sum(bonds formed)**.
- More energy released than absorbed = **exothermic**.
- Higher bond energy = **stronger** bond.
- Values are **averages**, so the result is an **estimate**.
- Count **all** the bonds in each molecule.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Formation of HCl.** H2 + Cl2 -> 2 HCl (H-H 436, Cl-Cl 243, H-Cl 431).
1. Broken: 436 + 243 = 679 kJ. Formed: 2 x 431 = 862 kJ.
2. delta H = 679 - 862 = -183 kJ.

**Example 2: Water formation.** 2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O (H-H 436, O=O 498, O-H 463).
1. Broken: 2 x 436 + 498 = 1370. Formed: 4 x 463 = 1852.
2. delta H = 1370 - 1852 = -482 kJ.

**Example 3: Sign.** A reaction breaks 1000 kJ of bonds and forms 1200 kJ.
1. delta H = 1000 - 1200 = -200 kJ.
2. It is exothermic.

## Common mistakes
- Reversing the subtraction.
- Forgetting to count all bonds.
- Treating bond energies as exact.
