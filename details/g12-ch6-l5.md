# Complex Numbers in Polar Form and De Moivre's Theorem

## The idea
A complex number z = a + bi is a point (a, b) in the complex plane. Its **modulus** is r = |z| = sqrt(a^2 + b^2), and its **argument** theta satisfies tan theta = b / a (check the quadrant).

**Polar form: z = r (cos theta + i sin theta)**, often written r cis theta.

## Key ideas
- **Multiply:** r1 cis theta1 * r2 cis theta2 = r1 r2 cis(theta1 + theta2). Multiply moduli, add angles.
- **Divide:** (r1 cis theta1) / (r2 cis theta2) = (r1/r2) cis(theta1 - theta2).
- **De Moivre's Theorem:** [r cis theta]^n = r^n cis(n theta).
- **nth roots:** the n roots of r cis theta are
  **r^(1/n) cis((theta + 360 k)/n)**, for k = 0, 1, ..., n - 1.
  They lie on a circle and are spaced 360/n degrees apart.

## Worked example 1: polar form
z = 1 + i sqrt(3).
1. r = sqrt(1 + 3) = 2.
2. tan theta = sqrt(3), and the point is in quadrant I, so theta = 60 degrees.
3. z = 2 cis 60 degrees.

## Worked example 2: a power
Compute (1 + i)^8.
1. r = sqrt(2) and theta = 45 degrees.
2. r^8 = (sqrt 2)^8 = 16, and 8 * 45 = 360 degrees.
3. (1 + i)^8 = 16 cis 360 = 16.

## Worked example 3: roots
Find the cube roots of 8i.
1. 8i = 8 cis 90 degrees. The cube root of 8 is 2.
2. Angles: (90 + 360k)/3 = 30, 150, 270 degrees.
3. Roots: 2 cis 30, 2 cis 150, 2 cis 270 (which is -2i).

## Common mistakes
- Multiplying the angles when multiplying complex numbers (add them).
- Raising only the angle to the power n, not the modulus.
- Finding only one root instead of all n.
- Placing the argument in the wrong quadrant, for example giving -1 - i the angle 45 degrees instead of 225 degrees.
