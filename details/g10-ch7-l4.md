# Cross Sections of Solids

## What is a cross section?
A **cross section** is the 2D shape where a plane cuts through a 3D solid.

## Common cross sections
| Solid | Slice | Cross section |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangular prism | parallel to a base | rectangle congruent to the base |
| Cylinder | parallel to the bases | **circle** the same size as the base |
| Cylinder | through the axis (vertical) | **rectangle** |
| Cone | parallel to the base | smaller **circle** |
| Cone | through the apex and axis | **triangle** |
| Pyramid | parallel to the base | polygon **similar** to the base |
| Sphere | any plane | **circle** (largest when the plane goes through the center) |

## Solids of revolution
Spin a 2D figure around a line (axis) to make a solid.
- A rectangle spun about one side gives a **cylinder**.
- A right triangle spun about a leg gives a **cone**.
- A semicircle spun about its diameter gives a **sphere**.

## Scaling and similar solids
For similar solids with scale factor k: lengths scale by k, surface areas by **k^2** and volumes by **k^3**.

## Worked examples
1. A vertical slice through the axis of a cylinder with r = 3 and h = 10 is a rectangle **6 by 10**.
2. A horizontal slice of a cone halfway up has half the radius of the base. For a cone with base radius 8, the slice has radius **4**.
3. A plane cuts a sphere of radius 5 at distance 3 from the center. The cross section is a circle with radius the square root of (25 - 9) = **4**.
4. A right triangle with legs 3 and 4 is spun around the leg of length 3. The result is a cone with r = **4** and h = **3**.
5. Two similar cubes have sides 2 and 6 (scale factor 3). Their volumes have ratio **27 : 1**, and the surface areas have ratio **9 : 1**.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming every slice of a solid is the same shape. It depends on the **angle** of the plane.
- Using the scale factor for areas or volumes without squaring or cubing it.
- Forgetting the axis when imagining a solid of revolution.
