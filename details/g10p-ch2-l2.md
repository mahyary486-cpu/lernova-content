# Vector Addition and Components

Adding vectors means finding the single vector, called the **resultant**, that has the same effect as all of them together. Because vectors have direction, ordinary addition does not always work.

## Key ideas
- **Tip-to-tail method:** draw the first vector, start the second at the tip of the first, and so on. The resultant runs from the tail of the first to the tip of the last.
- Same direction: magnitudes add. Opposite directions: magnitudes subtract.
- Perpendicular vectors: R = sqrt(A^2 + B^2), with tan(theta) = B / A.
- **Components:** any vector A at angle theta above the x-axis splits into Ax = A cos(theta) and Ay = A sin(theta).
- **Component method:** add all x components to get Rx, add all y components to get Ry, then R = sqrt(Rx^2 + Ry^2) and theta = tan^-1(Ry / Rx).
- The magnitude of a resultant of two vectors lies between the difference and the sum of their magnitudes.
- Forces are in equilibrium when their resultant is zero.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: components.** A 40 N force acts at 30 degrees above the horizontal.
1. Fx = 40 cos(30) = 40 x 0.866 = 34.6 N.
2. Fy = 40 sin(30) = 40 x 0.500 = 20 N.

**Example 2: perpendicular forces.** A 12 N force acts east and a 5.0 N force acts north.
1. R = sqrt(12^2 + 5.0^2) = sqrt(169) = 13 N.
2. theta = tan^-1(5.0 / 12) = 22.6 degrees north of east.

**Example 3: component method.** A plane's velocity is 10.0 m/s east plus a second velocity of 8.0 m/s at 50 degrees above east.
1. Rx = 10.0 + 8.0 cos(50) = 10.0 + 5.14 = 15.14 m/s.
2. Ry = 0 + 8.0 sin(50) = 6.13 m/s.
3. R = sqrt(15.14^2 + 6.13^2) = 16.3 m/s.
4. theta = tan^-1(6.13 / 15.14) = 22 degrees above east.

## Common mistakes
- Using sine for the horizontal component when the angle is measured from the horizontal. Cosine goes with the side next to the angle.
- Adding magnitudes of vectors that are not in the same direction.
- Forgetting to track signs: a component pointing west or south is negative.
- Setting a calculator to radians instead of degrees.
