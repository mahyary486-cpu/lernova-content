# Human impact on the environment

Human activities change the land, water and air, and these changes can damage ecosystems and reduce the variety of life.

## Key ideas

- **Population growth.** The human population has grown quickly in the last 200 years. More people need more food, water, energy, housing and goods, so more resources are used and more waste is produced.
- **Deforestation.** People cut down forests for farmland, wood and cities. Habitats are destroyed, soil is washed away by rain, and fewer trees remain to take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen.
- **Air pollution.** Burning fossil fuels in cars, factories and power stations releases carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides and soot. Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas. More greenhouse gases trap more heat and contribute to global warming. Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides cause acid rain.
- **Water pollution.** Sewage, factory waste, oil spills, plastic and farm chemicals reach rivers, lakes and seas. Fertilizers can cause algae to grow too much; when the algae die, bacteria use up the oxygen in the water and fish die.
- **Soil pollution and waste.** Pesticides, garbage and chemicals stay in the soil and can enter food chains. Some poisons become more concentrated in animals at higher levels of a food chain.
- **Overuse of resources.** Overhunting and overfishing reduce populations faster than they can recover. Some species become endangered or extinct.
- **Introduced species.** A species moved by people to a new area may have no natural predators, so it can spread and harm native species.
- **Loss of biodiversity.** Biodiversity is the variety of species in an area. Habitat loss is the main reason for its decline.

## Examples

**Example 1: Fertilizer in a lake.** A farmer adds too much fertilizer to a field. Rain washes it into a nearby lake. The extra nutrients make algae grow very fast. When the algae die, bacteria decompose them and use up the oxygen in the water. Fish and other animals cannot breathe and die. One human action has changed the whole food web.

**Example 2: Cutting a forest.** A forest is cleared for farming. The birds and mammals lose their homes and must move or die. Without tree roots, rain washes away the topsoil, so the farmland becomes less fertile. Fewer trees also mean less carbon dioxide taken up from the air.

**Example 3: Burning fuel.** A car burns petrol and releases carbon dioxide. If thousands of cars do this every day, the amount of carbon dioxide in the air rises. Because carbon dioxide traps heat, average temperatures slowly rise, and this changes rainfall and living conditions for many species.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking all human activity harms nature. People can also repair damage, for example by planting forests.
- Mixing up global warming with acid rain. Global warming is linked to greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide; acid rain is linked to sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
- Believing that pollution stays where it is made. Wind and water carry pollution far away.
- Thinking that extinction does not matter if only one species is lost. Each species is linked to others in food webs.

## Remember

- Pollution, habitat destruction and overuse of resources are the main human impacts.
- Habitat loss is the biggest cause of lost biodiversity.
- Damage to one part of an ecosystem affects the other parts.
