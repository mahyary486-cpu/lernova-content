# Distance-Time and Speed-Time Graphs

Graphs show motion at a glance. Knowing what the slope and area mean lets you read speed, acceleration and distance from a picture.

## Key ideas
- **Distance-time graph**: slope = speed. Horizontal line = at rest. Steeper line = faster.
- A distance-time curve that gets steeper means the object is **speeding up**.
- **Speed-time graph**: slope = acceleration. Horizontal line = constant speed.
- The **area under a speed-time graph** equals the distance traveled.
- Rectangle area = base x height; triangle area = 1/2 x base x height.
- Always check the axis labels and units before reading a graph.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Speed from a graph.** A distance-time line goes from (0 s, 0 m) to (5 s, 20 m).
1. Slope = 20 m / 5 s.
2. Speed = 4 m/s.

**Example 2: Distance from a rectangle.** A speed-time graph is flat at 6 m/s for 10 s.
1. The area is a rectangle: 6 x 10.
2. Distance = 60 m.

**Example 3: Distance from a triangle.** Speed rises steadily from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s.
1. The area is a triangle: 1/2 x 5 s x 10 m/s.
2. Distance = 25 m.

## Common mistakes
- Reading a distance-time graph as if it were a path on the ground.
- Using the height of the line instead of the slope to find speed.
- Forgetting that a flat line on a distance-time graph means rest, but on a speed-time graph means constant speed.
