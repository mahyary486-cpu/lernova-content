# Distance-Time and Speed-Time Graphs

A graph can show the whole story of a motion at a glance. Once you know what the slope and the area of a motion graph mean, you can read speed, acceleration and distance directly from the picture, without any formula.

## Key ideas

On a distance-time graph, time is on the horizontal axis and distance is on the vertical axis. The slope of the line is the speed, because slope is the change in distance divided by the change in time. A steeper line means a faster object. A horizontal line means that the distance does not change, so the object is at rest. A straight line that slopes upward means constant speed. A curve that becomes steeper and steeper means the object is speeding up.

On a speed-time graph, the vertical axis shows speed. The slope of this graph is the acceleration, because it is the change in speed divided by the change in time. A horizontal line means constant speed, so there is no acceleration. A line that rises means the object is speeding up, and a line that falls means it is slowing down.

A very useful fact about speed-time graphs is that the area between the line and the time axis equals the distance traveled. For a rectangle, the area is base times height. For a triangle, the area is one half times base times height. When a graph has both shapes, you can add the areas.

Be careful with the two kinds of graph. A flat line means "at rest" on a distance-time graph but "constant speed" on a speed-time graph. Always read the labels and units on the axes before you interpret a picture.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Speed from a distance-time graph.** A line goes from (0 s, 0 m) to (5 s, 20 m). Find the speed.
1. The slope is the rise divided by the run: 20 m / 5 s.
2. The speed is 4 m/s.
3. Since the line is straight, the speed is constant.

**Example 2: Distance from a rectangle.** A speed-time graph is flat at 6 m/s for 10 s. How far does the object travel?
1. The area under the line is a rectangle with a height of 6 m/s and a base of 10 s.
2. Multiply: 6 x 10.
3. The distance is 60 m.

**Example 3: Distance from a triangle.** On a speed-time graph the speed rises steadily from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s. How far does the object travel?
1. The area is a triangle with a base of 5 s and a height of 10 m/s.
2. Use one half times base times height: 1/2 x 5 x 10.
3. The distance is 25 m. The slope of the line is 10 / 5 = 2 m/s^2, so the acceleration is 2 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes

A common error is to read a distance-time graph as if it were a picture of the path on the ground; a hill in the graph does not mean the object climbed a hill. Another is to use the height of the line instead of its slope when finding speed on a distance-time graph. Students also confuse the meaning of a flat line on the two graphs, so always check which graph you are looking at.
