# Adding and Subtracting Polynomials

A polynomial is a sum of terms, where each term is a number times a variable raised to a whole-number power. Working with polynomials mostly means combining like terms.

## Key ideas
- **Monomial:** one term, such as 5x^2. **Binomial:** two terms. **Trinomial:** three terms.
- **Degree of a term:** the exponent of the variable (for several variables, add the exponents). **Degree of a polynomial:** the largest degree of any term.
- **Standard form:** write the terms from the highest degree to the lowest. The coefficient of the highest-degree term is the **leading coefficient**.
- **Like terms** have exactly the same variable part. 3x^2 and -7x^2 are like terms; 3x^2 and 3x are not.

## Adding polynomials
Remove the parentheses and combine like terms.

## Subtracting polynomials
Subtracting means adding the opposite, so change the sign of every term in the second polynomial.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Add (3x^2 + 2x - 5) + (x^2 - 4x + 9).
1. Group like terms: (3x^2 + x^2) + (2x - 4x) + (-5 + 9).
2. Combine: 4x^2 - 2x + 4.

**Example 2.** Subtract (5x^2 - 3x + 2) - (2x^2 + x - 6).
1. Distribute the minus sign: 5x^2 - 3x + 2 - 2x^2 - x + 6.
2. Group like terms: (5x^2 - 2x^2) + (-3x - x) + (2 + 6).
3. Combine: 3x^2 - 4x + 8.

**Example 3.** Write 4x - 7 + 6x^3 - x^2 in standard form and state its degree.
1. Order by exponent: 6x^3 - x^2 + 4x - 7.
2. The degree is 3 and the leading coefficient is 6.

## Common mistakes
- Changing only the first term when subtracting. The sign of every term in the second polynomial flips.
- Combining unlike terms, such as writing 2x + 3x^2 = 5x^3.
- Changing exponents when adding: 3x^2 + 4x^2 = 7x^2, not 7x^4.
- Forgetting that a term like x has coefficient 1 and that -x has coefficient -1.
