# Gravitational Fields and Weight

The same astronaut weighs less on the Moon, but her mass is unchanged. Weight depends on the local gravitational field.

## Key ideas
- **Field strength**: **g = G M / r^2** (N/kg = m/s^2).
- **Weight** W = m g; **mass** is constant, weight depends on location.
- g decreases with the square of the distance from the center of the planet.
- Earth: g = **9.8 N/kg**; Moon: **1.6 N/kg**.
- Orbiting astronauts are **in free fall**, so they feel weightless.
- g on a planet is proportional to M / R^2.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Weight.** A 50 kg person on Earth (g = 9.8).
1. W = m g = 50 x 9.8.
2. W = 490 N.

**Example 2: Earth's g.** Check g from M = 5.97 x 10^24 kg and R = 6.37 x 10^6 m.
1. g = G M / R^2 = 6.67 x 10^-11 x 5.97 x 10^24 / (6.37 x 10^6)^2.
2. g = 9.8 N/kg.

**Example 3: Another planet.** A planet has twice Earth's mass and twice Earth's radius.
1. g is proportional to M / R^2 = 2 / 4.
2. So g is half of Earth's: 4.9 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking astronauts in orbit are beyond Earth's gravity.
- Mixing up mass and weight.
- Using r as the height above the surface.
