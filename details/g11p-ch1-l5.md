# Orbits and Kepler's Laws

Planets, moons and satellites obey the same laws. Knowing them lets us predict where a satellite will be.

## Key ideas
- A satellite is in **free fall around** Earth; gravity is the **centripetal force**: **v = sqrt(G M / r)**.
- **Kepler 1**: orbits are **ellipses** with the Sun at one focus.
- **Kepler 2**: equal **areas** in equal times; fastest when **closest**.
- **Kepler 3**: **T^2 is proportional to r^3**.
- Higher orbits have **lower speed** and a **longer period**.
- A **geostationary** satellite has a period of 24 h.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Orbital speed.** At r = 8.0 x 10^6 m, g = 4.9 m/s^2.
1. v = sqrt(g r) = sqrt(4.9 x 8.0 x 10^6).
2. v = 6.3 x 10^3 m/s.

**Example 2: Kepler's third law.** A planet has a period of 8 years where Earth has 1 year at 1 AU.
1. T^2 = r^3, so r^3 = 64.
2. r = 4 AU.

**Example 3: Period and height.** A satellite is moved to a higher orbit.
1. A larger r means a longer period by Kepler's third law.
2. It also moves more slowly.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a satellite needs engines to stay in orbit.
- Thinking higher orbits are faster.
- Forgetting to square the period or cube the radius.
