# Simplifying Algebraic Expressions

## Terms and like terms
A **term** is a number, a variable, or a product of both, like 5, x or 3x. The number in front of the variable is the **coefficient**.

**Like terms** have exactly the same variable part. 3x and 7x are like terms. 3x and 3x^2 are not, and neither are 3x and 3y.

## Combining like terms
Add or subtract the coefficients and keep the variable part.
- 4x + 3x = 7x
- 5a + 2b - 3a + b = (5a - 3a) + (2b + b) = 2a + 3b

## The distributive property
a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the outside number by **every** term inside.
- 2(x + 5) = 2x + 10
- 3(2x - 4) = 6x - 12

## Negative signs in front of parentheses
A minus sign means multiply by -1, so every sign inside changes.
- -(x - 6) = -x + 6
- 7 - 2(3x - 1) = 7 - 6x + 2 = 9 - 6x

## Worked example
Simplify 4(x + 2) - 2(x - 3).
1. Distribute: 4x + 8 - 2x + 6.
2. Combine like terms: (4x - 2x) + (8 + 6) = 2x + 14.

## Common mistakes
- Multiplying only the first term inside the parentheses.
- Forgetting that -2 x -3 = +6.
- Combining unlike terms, such as 3x + 2 = 5x.
