# Physical and Chemical Changes

Ice melting and wood burning are both changes, but only one makes new substances. Knowing the difference is a basic skill in chemistry.

## Key ideas
- **Physical change**: the form changes, the substance stays the same (melting, dissolving, breaking).
- **Chemical change** (reaction): new substances with new properties form (burning, rusting, souring).
- Evidence of a chemical change: **gas**, **precipitate**, **color change**, **light or heat**, **odor**.
- Physical changes can usually be reversed; many chemical changes cannot easily be.
- **Conservation of mass**: total mass before = total mass after in a closed system.
- A change of state is a physical change.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Baking soda and vinegar.** Bubbles form when baking soda is mixed with vinegar.
1. A gas is produced.
2. This is evidence of a chemical change.

**Example 2: Melting ice.** Ice melts into liquid water.
1. The substance is still water.
2. Melting is a physical change.

**Example 3: Rusting nails.** Rusted nails weigh more than the original nails.
1. Iron combines with oxygen from the air.
2. The extra mass came from oxygen, so mass is conserved.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking every change of color is chemical. Mixing paint is physical.
- Thinking dissolving sugar is a chemical change.
- Forgetting that gases have mass too.
