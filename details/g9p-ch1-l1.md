# The Scientific Method and Lab Safety

Science is a way of answering questions about the natural world by testing ideas against evidence. Good scientists are careful, honest about their data and ready to change their minds.

## Key ideas
- The **scientific method** is a flexible set of steps: ask a question, research, form a hypothesis, test it with an experiment, analyze the data, draw a conclusion and share the results.
- A **hypothesis** is a testable explanation or prediction, often written as "If ... then ...".
- In a **controlled experiment** only one variable is changed on purpose: the **independent variable**. The **dependent variable** is what you measure. All other conditions are **controlled variables** and stay the same.
- A **control group** receives no treatment (or the standard treatment) so results can be compared.
- A **theory** is a well-tested explanation supported by a large amount of evidence. A **law** describes a pattern in nature, often with an equation.
- Lab safety: wear safety goggles, tie back long hair, never taste or smell chemicals directly, know where the exits and the eyewash are, and tell the teacher about every spill or injury.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Naming the variables.** A student grows bean plants under different amounts of light each day and measures plant height after two weeks.
1. The student changes the amount of light, so this is the independent variable.
2. Plant height is measured as the result, so it is the dependent variable.
3. Water, soil, pot size and temperature must stay the same: these are the controlled variables.

**Example 2: Writing a hypothesis.** Does water temperature affect how fast sugar dissolves?
1. Make a testable prediction: "If the water is hotter, then the sugar will dissolve faster."
2. Plan the test: same mass of sugar, same volume of water, same stirring; change only the temperature.
3. Measure the dissolving time for each temperature.

**Example 3: Drawing a conclusion.** Sugar dissolves in 60 s at 10 C, in 30 s at 30 C and in 15 s at 50 C.
1. As the temperature rises, the dissolving time falls.
2. The data support the hypothesis: sugar dissolves faster in warmer water.
3. The conclusion is limited to these conditions; it does not prove the same result for every substance.

## Common mistakes
- Changing more than one variable at a time, so the result cannot be traced to one cause.
- Calling a hypothesis "just a guess". A hypothesis must be testable and based on research.
- Saying data "prove" a hypothesis. Data support it or fail to support it.
- Thinking a theory is weak. In science a theory is a strongly supported explanation.
- Ignoring a safety rule because an experiment looks harmless.
