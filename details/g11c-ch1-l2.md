# The Mole and Molar Mass

Atoms are far too small to count one by one, so chemists count them in moles and weigh them in grams.

## Key ideas
- **1 mol = 6.02 x 10^23** particles.
- **Molar mass** (g/mol) = sum of atomic masses.
- **n = mass / molar mass**.
- **Particles = n x 6.02 x 10^23**.
- **1 mol of gas = 22.4 L** at STP.
- **Percent composition** = (mass of element / molar mass) x 100%.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mass to moles.** How many moles are in 36 g of water (18 g/mol)?
1. n = mass / molar mass = 36 / 18.
2. n = 2 mol.

**Example 2: Particles.** How many molecules are in 0.5 mol of water?
1. N = n x 6.02 x 10^23 = 0.5 x 6.02 x 10^23.
2. N = 3.01 x 10^23.

**Example 3: Percent composition.** Find the percent of oxygen in water.
1. Oxygen is 16 g of the 18 g in one mole.
2. 16 / 18 x 100% = 88.9%.

## Common mistakes
- Using atomic mass instead of molar mass for molecules.
- Forgetting to multiply the atoms by their subscripts.
- Using 22.4 L away from STP.
