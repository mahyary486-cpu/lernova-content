# Arc Length and Sector Area

## Circle formulas
- Circumference **C = 2 pi r**
- Area **A = pi r^2**

## Arc length and sector area
A **sector** is a slice of a circle between two radii. If the central angle is **n degrees**:

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Arc length | (n / 360) x 2 pi r |
| Sector area | (n / 360) x pi r^2 |

If the angle theta is in **radians**:
- arc length = **r x theta**
- sector area = **(1/2) x r^2 x theta**

Conversion: 180 degrees = pi radians.

## Segment of a circle
The region between a chord and its arc. **Segment area = sector area - triangle area.**

## Worked examples
1. r = 6 and the central angle is 60 degrees. Arc length = (60 / 360) x 2 x pi x 6 = **2 pi**, about 6.28.
2. r = 6 and the central angle is 60 degrees. Sector area = (1/6) x pi x 36 = **6 pi**, about 18.85.
3. An arc is 10 long in a circle of radius 5. The angle is 10 / 5 = **2 radians**, about 114.6 degrees.
4. A pizza of radius 8 inches is cut into 8 equal slices. Each slice has area pi x 64 / 8 = **8 pi**, about 25.1 square inches.
5. A sector has area 12 pi and radius 6. The fraction is 12 pi / 36 pi = 1/3, so the central angle is **120 degrees**.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up the formulas for length (uses r) and area (uses r squared).
- Using the diameter instead of the radius.
- Forgetting to convert between degrees and radians.
- Writing area in units of length. Area is in **square** units.
