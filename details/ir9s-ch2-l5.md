# Conservation of mass

The law of conservation of mass says that in a chemical reaction the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.

## Key ideas

In a chemical reaction atoms are rearranged. They are not created and they are not destroyed. Bonds between atoms break and new bonds form, but the number and kind of atoms stay the same. So the total mass stays the same.

- Law: mass of reactants = mass of products.
- A chemical equation shows reactants on the left and products on the right, separated by an arrow (->). Example: 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O.
- A balanced equation has the same number of each kind of atom on both sides. Balancing is done by changing the big numbers in front of formulas (coefficients). Never change the small subscripts, because that changes the substance.
- The law was established by careful measurements, notably those of Antoine Lavoisier in the 18th century, who weighed substances in closed containers.
- Apparent exceptions: when a gas escapes (an open container) or when a gas from the air joins a solid, the measured mass seems to change. For example, burning wood leaves ash that weighs less, because carbon dioxide and water vapour escape. A piece of iron that rusts gets heavier, because it joins with oxygen from the air.
- To check the law, use a closed container (a sealed flask) so nothing enters or leaves.

Balancing steps:

1. Write the correct formulas.
2. Count atoms of each element on both sides.
3. Add coefficients until every count matches.
4. Check the final count again.

## Examples

Example 1: Burning magnesium.
- 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO.
- Suppose 24 g of magnesium reacts with 16 g of oxygen.
- Total reactant mass = 24 + 16 = 40 g.
- So the magnesium oxide formed has a mass of 40 g.

Example 2: Heating limestone.
- CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2.
- Mass of CaCO3 = 100 g, and 56 g of CaO remains.
- Mass of CO2 released = 100 - 56 = 44 g.
- Check: 56 + 44 = 100 g. The law holds. The solid seems lighter only because the gas escaped.

Example 3: Balancing hydrogen with oxygen.
- Unbalanced: H2 + O2 -> H2O. Left has 2 O atoms, right has 1.
- Put 2 in front of H2O: H2 + O2 -> 2H2O. Now O is 2 and 2, but H is 2 on the left and 4 on the right.
- Put 2 in front of H2: 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O. H is 4 and 4, O is 2 and 2. Balanced.
- Mass check: 2 × 2 g + 32 g = 36 g and 2 × 18 g = 36 g.

## Common mistakes

- Balancing by changing subscripts, for example writing H2O2 instead of H2O.
- Believing that mass is lost when a candle burns. The products are gases that leave.
- Forgetting that reactants from the air, such as oxygen, also count.
- Not checking the atom counts on both sides at the end.

## Remember

- Atoms are only rearranged in a reaction, so total mass stays the same.
- A balanced equation has equal numbers of each atom on both sides.
- Use a closed system to test the law.
