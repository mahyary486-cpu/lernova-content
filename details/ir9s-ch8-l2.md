# Pressure in solids

This lesson explains how solid objects press on surfaces and how we change that pressure in daily life.

## Key ideas

- A solid object presses on the surface under it because of its weight. The pressure is P = F / A, where F is the weight (when the object rests on a flat horizontal surface) and A is the contact area.
- A solid transmits a force in the direction in which the force is applied. It does not spread pressure in all directions like a liquid or gas does.
- To increase pressure, we can increase the force or reduce the contact area. To reduce pressure, we can reduce the force or increase the contact area.
- Sharp tools such as knives, needles, nails and axes have a very small edge or tip area. Even a small force makes a very large pressure, so they cut or pierce easily.
- Wide objects such as snowshoes, skis, camel feet, tank tracks and the wide foundations of buildings have a large contact area. They spread the weight and make the pressure low, so they do not sink into soft ground.
- The same object can make different pressures depending on which face touches the surface. A brick lying flat makes less pressure than the same brick standing on its end.
- The contact area is only the area that really touches the surface, not the whole area of the object.

## Examples

**Example 1: A brick on different faces.** A brick has a mass of 3 kg and sides 0.2 m, 0.1 m and 0.05 m. Find the pressure on the table when it lies on its largest face and on its smallest face. Use g = 10 N/kg.
- Step 1: Weight = 3 × 10 = 30 N.
- Step 2: Largest face area = 0.2 × 0.1 = 0.02 m^2. P = 30 / 0.02 = 1500 Pa.
- Step 3: Smallest face area = 0.1 × 0.05 = 0.005 m^2. P = 30 / 0.005 = 6000 Pa.
- Step 4: The pressure on the smallest face is 4 times larger because its area is 4 times smaller.

**Example 2: A knife edge.** A person pushes a knife with a force of 20 N. The edge touches the food over an area of 0.000002 m^2 (2 × 10^-6 m^2). Find the pressure.
- Step 1: P = F / A = 20 / 0.000002.
- Step 2: P = 10 000 000 Pa = 1 × 10^7 Pa, which is 10 000 kPa.
- Step 3: This is a huge pressure, so the knife cuts easily.

**Example 3: A tractor on soft ground.** A tractor has a weight of 40 000 N. First it has two wide tracks with a total contact area of 3 m^2. Then it has four small wheels with a total contact area of 0.4 m^2. Compare the pressures.
- Step 1: With tracks, P = 40 000 / 3 ≈ 13 333 Pa.
- Step 2: With wheels, P = 40 000 / 0.4 = 100 000 Pa.
- Step 3: The wheels make about 7.5 times more pressure, so they sink more easily into soft soil.

## Common mistakes

- Using the total surface area of the object instead of the area that touches the surface.
- Thinking that a solid sends pressure equally in all directions. A solid mainly sends force in the direction of the push.
- Forgetting that weight, not mass, is the force in the formula. Multiply mass by 10 N/kg first.
- Believing that a sharp knife needs more force. It needs less force because the area is smaller.

## Remember

- Smaller contact area gives larger pressure; larger contact area gives smaller pressure.
- Sharp tools increase pressure; wide supports decrease pressure.
- For an object at rest on a flat surface, F is its weight.
