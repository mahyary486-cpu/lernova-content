# Atomic Number, Mass Number and Isotopes

Carbon atoms all have 6 protons, but some have 6 neutrons and some 8. They are the same element and different isotopes.

## Key ideas
- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of protons; it defines the element.
- **Mass number (A)** = protons + neutrons. **Neutrons = A - Z**.
- **Isotopes**: same Z, different numbers of neutrons (and different A).
- A neutral atom has **electrons = protons**.
- **Ions**: losing electrons gives a **positive** ion; gaining electrons gives a **negative** ion.
- The atomic mass on the periodic table is an **average** of the isotope masses.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Counting neutrons.** An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23.
1. Neutrons = A - Z = 23 - 11.
2. Neutrons = 12.

**Example 2: Isotopes.** Carbon-14 has atomic number 6.
1. Neutrons = 14 - 6 = 8.
2. Carbon-12 has 6 neutrons, so the two are isotopes.

**Example 3: An ion.** An ion has 12 protons and 10 electrons.
1. Charge = (+12) + (-10).
2. The charge is 2+.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing atomic number with mass number.
- Thinking isotopes are different elements.
- Changing the number of protons when an atom becomes an ion.
