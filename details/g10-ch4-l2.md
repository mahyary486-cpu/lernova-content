# Similar Polygons

## Definition
Two polygons are **similar** (written with the symbol ~) if
1. all pairs of corresponding **angles** are congruent, and
2. all pairs of corresponding **sides** are proportional.

The common ratio of corresponding sides is the **scale factor** k.

## Ratios in similar figures
| Quantity | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Side lengths (and other lengths like heights and diagonals) | k |
| Perimeters | k |
| Areas | **k squared** |

So if the scale factor is 3 : 2, the areas are in the ratio 9 : 4.

## How to check
1. Match the corresponding vertices in order.
2. Write the ratios of corresponding sides. They must all be equal.
3. Check that corresponding angles are equal.

## Worked examples
1. Are a 4 by 6 rectangle and a 10 by 15 rectangle similar? 4 / 10 = 0.4 and 6 / 15 = 0.4. All angles are 90 degrees, so **yes**, with scale factor 0.4 (or 2.5 the other way).
2. Triangle ABC has sides 6, 8, 10 and is similar to triangle DEF with sides x, 12, 15. The ratio is 12 / 8 = 1.5, so x = 6 x 1.5 = **9**.
3. Two similar figures have a scale factor of 3 : 2. The larger has area 90. The areas are in the ratio 9 : 4, so the smaller has area 90 x 4 / 9 = **40**.
4. Two similar triangles have perimeters of 24 and 36. The scale factor is 24 / 36 = 2 / 3.
5. A rectangle is 5 by 8. A similar rectangle has its short side equal to 15. The scale factor is 3, so the long side is 24.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking two rectangles are always similar. They are only similar when their side ratios match.
- Using the scale factor for the area ratio instead of its square.
- Matching sides that do not correspond. Match the shortest to the shortest and the longest to the longest.
