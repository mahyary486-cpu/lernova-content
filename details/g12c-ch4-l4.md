# Proteins and Amino Acids

The shape of a protein decides what it does, and the shape comes from the order of its amino acids.

## Key ideas
- **Amino acid**: **H2N-CH(R)-COOH**.
- **Peptide bond** formed with loss of **H2O**.
- **Primary**: sequence; **secondary**: helix / sheet; **tertiary**: 3D fold; **quaternary**: several chains.
- **Enzymes** are protein **catalysts** with an **active site**.
- **Denaturation** by heat or extreme pH changes the shape.
- A **zwitterion** has both + and - charges.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Dipeptide.** Two glycine molecules (H2N-CH2-COOH) form a dipeptide. What else forms?
1. A peptide bond is made by condensation.
2. One molecule of water.

**Example 2: Peptide bonds.** A polypeptide has 50 amino acids. How many peptide bonds does it contain?
1. Each bond joins two amino acids.
2. 50 - 1 = 49 peptide bonds.

**Example 3: Denaturation.** Why does cooking an egg change the white?
1. Heat breaks hydrogen bonds and other interactions.
2. The protein is denatured and its shape changes.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking the peptide count equals the amino acid count.
- Thinking denaturation breaks peptide bonds.
- Confusing the levels of structure.
