# Changes of State

Water can freeze in a freezer, melt on a counter, boil in a pot and condense on a cold glass. Each is a change of state.

## Key ideas
- **Melting** (solid to liquid), **freezing** (liquid to solid), **vaporization** (liquid to gas), **condensation** (gas to liquid).
- **Sublimation**: solid to gas (dry ice). **Deposition**: gas to solid (frost).
- **Evaporation** happens at the surface; **boiling** happens throughout the liquid.
- Water melts at **0 C** and boils at **100 C** at sea level.
- **Melting and vaporization absorb energy; freezing and condensation release energy.**
- The identity of the substance does not change.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Naming the change.** Dry ice turns directly into carbon dioxide gas.
1. The solid changes to a gas without becoming a liquid.
2. This is sublimation.

**Example 2: A cold glass.** Droplets form on the outside of a glass of cold water.
1. Water vapor in the air touches the cold glass.
2. The gas changes to a liquid: condensation.

**Example 3: Evaporation cooling.** Why does sweat cool you?
1. The fastest particles leave the liquid.
2. The average kinetic energy left behind is lower, so the skin cools.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking evaporation only happens at 100 C.
- Thinking melting is a chemical change.
- Forgetting that freezing releases energy.
