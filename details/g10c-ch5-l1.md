# The Mole and Avogadro's Number

Atoms and molecules are far too small to count one by one, so chemists count them in huge groups called moles. The mole (symbol mol) is the SI unit for amount of substance, much as a dozen means 12 items.

## Key ideas
- One mole of any substance contains 6.02 x 10^23 representative particles. This number is Avogadro's number, N_A.
- A representative particle is an atom, a molecule, a formula unit or an ion, depending on the substance.
- Moles to particles: particles = moles x 6.02 x 10^23.
- Particles to moles: moles = particles / (6.02 x 10^23).
- The conversion factor is 6.02 x 10^23 particles per 1 mol.
- A subscript in a formula counts atoms inside one particle. In 1 mol of H2O there are 2 mol of H atoms and 1 mol of O atoms.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Atoms from moles.** How many atoms are in 2.50 mol of aluminum?
1. Multiply moles by Avogadro's number.
2. 2.50 mol x 6.02 x 10^23 atoms/mol = 1.51 x 10^24 atoms.

**Example 2: Moles from particles.** A sample contains 3.01 x 10^24 atoms of zinc. How many moles is this?
1. Divide the number of atoms by Avogadro's number.
2. 3.01 x 10^24 / 6.02 x 10^23 = 5.00 mol.

**Example 3: Atoms inside molecules.** How many atoms are in 0.500 mol of CO2?
1. Molecules = 0.500 x 6.02 x 10^23 = 3.01 x 10^23 molecules.
2. Each molecule has 3 atoms (1 C and 2 O).
3. Atoms = 3 x 3.01 x 10^23 = 9.03 x 10^23 atoms.

## Why the mole matters
Equal numbers of moles contain equal numbers of particles, whatever the substance. One mole of helium and one mole of copper each hold 6.02 x 10^23 atoms, but their masses differ. The mole links the invisible world of particles to measurable masses in the lab.

## Common mistakes
- Multiplying when you should divide (or the reverse). Check: a few moles should give a very large number of particles.
- Forgetting that a mole of molecules holds more atoms than particles. 1 mol of CO2 contains 3 mol of atoms.
- Mixing up the sign of the exponent. Avogadro's number is 10^23, not 10^-23.
- Treating the mole as a mass. A mole is a count, not a number of grams.
