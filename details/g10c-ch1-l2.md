# Subatomic Particles and the Nuclear Atom

Atoms are made of three subatomic particles. How scientists found them is a good example of evidence changing a model.

## Key ideas
- **Proton:** charge +1 (+1.602 x 10^-19 C), mass 1.673 x 10^-27 kg, about 1 amu. Found in the nucleus.
- **Neutron:** charge 0, mass 1.675 x 10^-27 kg, about 1 amu. Found in the nucleus.
- **Electron:** charge -1, mass 9.11 x 10^-31 kg, about 1/1836 amu. Found outside the nucleus.

A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons.

**Thomson (1897)** used cathode rays bent by electric fields. They moved toward the positive plate, so they were negative particles, now called electrons. He proposed the plum pudding model.

**Rutherford (1911)** fired alpha particles at thin gold foil. Most went straight through, a few bounced back at large angles. So the atom is mostly empty space with a tiny, dense, positive nucleus holding nearly all the mass.

The atom is about 1.0 x 10^-10 m across, and the nucleus about 1.0 x 10^-15 m.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Nuclear charge.** A nucleus has 8 protons and 9 neutrons.
1. Only protons carry charge.
2. Charge = 8 x 1.602 x 10^-19 C.
3. Charge = 1.28 x 10^-18 C.

**Example 2: Size ratio.**
1. Divide atom diameter by nucleus diameter.
2. 1.0 x 10^-10 m / 1.0 x 10^-15 m = 1.0 x 10^5.
3. The atom is about 100,000 times wider than its nucleus.

**Example 3: Mass comparison.**
1. Proton mass / electron mass = 1.673 x 10^-27 / 9.11 x 10^-31.
2. The ratio is about 1836.
3. So electron mass adds almost nothing to the mass of an atom.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking electrons contribute much to atomic mass.
- Saying the nucleus is negative: it contains protons, so it is positive.
- Giving Thomson credit for the nucleus. He found the electron, and Rutherford found the nucleus.
- Forgetting that neutrons are neutral and add no charge.
