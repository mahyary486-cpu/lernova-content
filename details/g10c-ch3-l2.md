# Ionic Bonds and Compounds

An **ionic bond** is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. It usually forms when a metal transfers electrons to a nonmetal.

## Key ideas
- Metal atoms lose valence electrons to form cations; nonmetal atoms gain electrons to form anions.
- Ionic compounds form a repeating 3-D **crystal lattice**, not separate molecules. The **formula unit** is the lowest whole-number ratio of ions, such as NaCl or MgCl2.
- The total positive charge equals the total negative charge, so the compound is neutral.
- The **criss-cross method** gives formulas: the charge size of one ion becomes the subscript of the other. Al3+ and O2- give Al2O3.
- Properties: high melting and boiling points, hard but brittle, do not conduct electricity as solids, but conduct when molten or dissolved in water because the ions can move.
- **Lattice energy** is the energy released when gaseous ions form a solid lattice. It is larger when the ion charges are larger and the ions are smaller. MgO (2+ and 2-) has a much higher melting point than NaCl (1+ and 1-).

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Formula of magnesium chloride.**
1. Magnesium forms Mg2+ and chlorine forms Cl-.
2. Two Cl- are needed to cancel one Mg2+: 2+ and 2 x (1-) = 0.
3. Formula: MgCl2.

**Example 2: Mass of 0.50 mol CaCl2.**
1. Molar mass: 40.08 + 2 x 35.45 = 110.98 g/mol, about 111.0 g/mol.
2. Mass = 0.50 mol x 111.0 g/mol = 55.5 g.

**Example 3: Moles in 117 g of NaCl.**
1. Molar mass of NaCl = 22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44, about 58.5 g/mol.
2. Moles = 117 g / 58.5 g/mol = 2.00 mol.
3. That is 2.00 x 6.02 x 10^23 formula units.

## Common mistakes
- Calling NaCl a molecule. It is a lattice of ions.
- Writing charges in the formula, such as Mg2Cl2 or Mg2+Cl-2.
- Saying ionic solids conduct electricity. The ions are fixed in place until the solid melts or dissolves.
- Forgetting to reduce the ratio: Mg2O2 must be written MgO.
