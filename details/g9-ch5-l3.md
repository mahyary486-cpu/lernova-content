# Special Products

Some products appear so often that it pays to remember their patterns.

## The three patterns
- **Square of a sum:** (a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2
- **Square of a difference:** (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2
- **Difference of squares:** (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2

In a perfect square, the middle term is twice the product of the two terms. In a difference of squares, the middle terms cancel, so only two terms remain.

## Why the middle term matters
(a + b)^2 means (a + b)(a + b). FOIL gives a^2 + ab + ab + b^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2. The two copies of ab are the "2ab" that students often forget.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Expand (x + 6)^2.
1. a = x and b = 6.
2. a^2 = x^2, 2ab = 12x, b^2 = 36.
3. Result: x^2 + 12x + 36.

**Example 2.** Expand (3x - 5)^2.
1. a = 3x and b = 5.
2. a^2 = 9x^2, 2ab = 2(3x)(5) = 30x, b^2 = 25.
3. Result: 9x^2 - 30x + 25.

**Example 3.** Multiply (4x + 7)(4x - 7).
1. This is a sum times a difference with a = 4x and b = 7.
2. a^2 - b^2 = 16x^2 - 49.

## Mental math
Use the difference of squares for 31 * 29 = (30 + 1)(30 - 1) = 900 - 1 = 899. Use the square of a sum for 52^2 = (50 + 2)^2 = 2500 + 200 + 4 = 2704.

## Common mistakes
- Writing (a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2.
- Using a plus sign on the last term of (a - b)^2. The last term b^2 is always positive.
- Squaring only part of the first term: (3x)^2 = 9x^2, not 3x^2.
- Using the difference of squares pattern when the signs are the same, such as (x + 3)(x + 3).
