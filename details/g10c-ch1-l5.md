# Atomic Models and Light

Our picture of the atom changed as new evidence appeared. Light gave scientists a way to study electrons.

## Atomic models
- **Dalton:** atoms are tiny solid spheres, different for each element.
- **Thomson:** plum pudding, with electrons embedded in a positive sphere.
- **Rutherford:** a small, dense, positive nucleus with electrons around it.
- **Bohr:** electrons move in fixed orbits with set energy levels.
- **Quantum mechanical model:** electrons are found in orbitals, regions where they are likely to be.

## Light
Light is an electromagnetic wave.
- **c = wavelength x frequency**, where c = 3.00 x 10^8 m/s. Write it as c = lambda x f.
- **E = h x f**, where h = 6.626 x 10^-34 J s. Also E = hc / lambda.
- Visible light runs from red (longest wavelength, lowest energy) to violet (shortest wavelength, highest energy).
- When an electron falls from a higher level to a lower one, it emits a photon whose energy equals the difference. This gives a line spectrum unique to each element.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Frequency of green light (500 nm).**
1. Convert: 500 nm = 5.00 x 10^-7 m.
2. f = c / lambda = 3.00 x 10^8 / 5.00 x 10^-7.
3. f = 6.00 x 10^14 Hz.

**Example 2: Photon energy at that frequency.**
1. E = h x f.
2. E = 6.626 x 10^-34 x 6.00 x 10^14.
3. E = 3.98 x 10^-19 J.

**Example 3: Energy of a 400 nm photon.**
1. Convert: 400 nm = 4.00 x 10^-7 m.
2. E = hc / lambda = (6.626 x 10^-34 x 3.00 x 10^8) / 4.00 x 10^-7.
3. E = 4.97 x 10^-19 J.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to convert nanometers to meters.
- Thinking longer wavelength means more energy. It means less.
- Confusing absorption (electron moves up) with emission (electron falls down).
- Mixing up the Thomson and Rutherford models.
