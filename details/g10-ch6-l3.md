# Tangents and Secants

## Definitions
- A **tangent** touches the circle at exactly one point (the **point of tangency**).
- A **secant** is a line that crosses the circle at **two** points.

## Tangent theorems
- A tangent is **perpendicular** to the radius at the point of tangency.
- Two tangents drawn from the same outside point are **congruent**.

## Angles formed outside the circle
For two tangents, two secants, or a tangent and a secant meeting outside the circle, the angle equals **half the difference** of the two intercepted arcs (far arc minus near arc).

## Segment lengths
From an outside point P:
- **Two secants:** (whole secant 1) x (outside part 1) = (whole secant 2) x (outside part 2).
- **Tangent and secant:** (tangent length)^2 = (whole secant) x (outside part).
- **Two chords crossing inside:** the products of the pieces of each chord are equal (a x b = c x d).

## Worked examples
1. A tangent from a point 13 away from the center touches a circle of radius 5. The tangent length is the square root of (169 - 25) = **12**.
2. Two tangents from P touch a circle at A and B and PA = 9. Then PB = **9**.
3. Two tangents form an angle at P and cut arcs of 220 degrees (far) and 140 degrees (near). The angle is (220 - 140) / 2 = **40 degrees**.
4. A tangent from P has length 6 and a secant from P has an outside part of 4. Then 6^2 = 4 x whole, so the whole secant is 9 and the part inside the circle is **5**.
5. Two chords cross. One is cut into 3 and 8 and the other has a piece of 4. The other piece is 3 x 8 / 4 = **6**.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the radius to the point of tangency makes a right angle.
- Using the **whole** secant for one product and only the outside part for the other.
- Using the sum of arcs for angles outside the circle. Outside, you subtract.
