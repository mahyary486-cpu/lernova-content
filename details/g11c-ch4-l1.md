# Energy, Heat and Enthalpy

A hand warmer releases heat and a cold pack absorbs it. Both are chemical reactions with different signs of delta H.

## Key ideas
- **Exothermic**: heat released, **delta H < 0**.
- **Endothermic**: heat absorbed, **delta H > 0**.
- **delta H = H(products) - H(reactants)**.
- Reversing the reaction **changes the sign** of delta H.
- Multiplying the equation **multiplies** delta H.
- **Activation energy** is the energy barrier for the reaction.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Sign of delta H.** Burning methane releases 890 kJ per mole.
1. Heat leaves the system.
2. delta H = -890 kJ/mol; exothermic.

**Example 2: Reversing.** The reaction A -> B has delta H = -50 kJ. What is delta H for B -> A?
1. Reversing changes the sign.
2. delta H = +50 kJ.

**Example 3: Scaling.** How much heat is released when 2 mol of CH4 burn?
1. Each mole releases 890 kJ.
2. 2 x 890 = 1780 kJ.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up the sign of delta H.
- Forgetting to multiply delta H when scaling.
- Confusing activation energy with delta H.
