# Half-Life, Dating and Radiation Safety

The nuclear clock in carbon-14 lets us date objects up to about 50,000 years old.

## Key ideas
- **N = N0 (1/2)^(t / T(1/2))**.
- **lambda = 0.693 / T(1/2)**.
- **C-14** half-life **5730 years**.
- Activity in **becquerels** (decays/s); dose in **sieverts**.
- Protection: **time, distance, shielding**.
- Uses: **tracers**, **radiotherapy**, **dating**, **sterilization**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Half-lives.** A 40 g sample has a half-life of 3 days. How much is left after 12 days?
1. 12 days is 4 half-lives.
2. 40 x (1/2)^4 = 2.5 g.

**Example 2: Carbon dating.** A bone has 25% of its original C-14. How old is it?
1. 25% = (1/2)^2, so 2 half-lives.
2. Age = 2 x 5730 = 11,460 years.

**Example 3: Decay constant.** Find lambda for a half-life of 6 hours.
1. lambda = 0.693 / 6.
2. lambda = 0.116 per hour.

## Common mistakes
- Treating half-life as the time for all to decay.
- Using minutes with a half-life in hours.
- Thinking half-life depends on the amount.
