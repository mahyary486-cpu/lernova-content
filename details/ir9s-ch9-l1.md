# Levers

A lever is a simple machine made of a stiff bar that turns around a fixed point called the fulcrum.

## Key ideas

- A lever has three important points: the fulcrum (the pivot), the effort (the force you apply) and the load (the force you want to overcome).
- The effort arm is the distance from the fulcrum to the point where the effort acts. The load arm is the distance from the fulcrum to the load.
- The law of the lever says that a lever is balanced when effort × effort arm = load × load arm. In symbols: F1 × d1 = F2 × d2.
- If the effort arm is longer than the load arm, a small effort can balance a large load. The mechanical advantage is effort arm / load arm.
- A lever does not give you free energy. A small effort needs a long movement, and a large effort needs only a short movement.
- There are three classes of levers:
  - First class: the fulcrum is between the effort and the load. Examples: a seesaw, scissors, a crowbar.
  - Second class: the load is between the fulcrum and the effort. Examples: a wheelbarrow, a nutcracker, a bottle opener.
  - Third class: the effort is between the fulcrum and the load. Examples: tweezers, a fishing rod, the human forearm.
- Second class levers always give a mechanical advantage greater than 1. Third class levers always give a mechanical advantage less than 1, but they make the load move fast and far.

## Examples

Example 1: A seesaw. A child who weighs 300 N sits 1.5 m from the fulcrum. Where must a 200 N child sit to balance?
- Write the law: F1 × d1 = F2 × d2.
- 300 N × 1.5 m = 450 N·m on the first side.
- 200 N × d2 = 450 N·m, so d2 = 450 / 200 = 2.25 m.
- The lighter child must sit 2.25 m from the fulcrum.

Example 2: A crowbar. A stone weighs 900 N. The load arm is 0.2 m and the effort arm is 1.5 m. Find the effort needed to just lift the stone.
- Load × load arm = 900 × 0.2 = 180 N·m.
- Effort × 1.5 m = 180 N·m.
- Effort = 180 / 1.5 = 120 N.
- Mechanical advantage = 1.5 / 0.2 = 7.5. Check: 900 / 120 = 7.5.

Example 3: A wheelbarrow. The load is 400 N and its center is 0.6 m from the wheel axle (the fulcrum). The handles are 1.5 m from the axle. Find the effort.
- 400 × 0.6 = 240 N·m.
- Effort = 240 / 1.5 = 160 N.
- The wheelbarrow is a second class lever, so the effort is less than the load.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring the arms from the wrong point. Both arms are measured from the fulcrum, not from the end of the bar.
- Mixing units, for example using cm for one arm and m for the other. Convert first.
- Thinking a lever reduces the work. It only changes the size of the force and the distance moved.
- Forgetting that third class levers have mechanical advantage less than 1: they need more effort than the load.

## Remember

- Balance condition: effort × effort arm = load × load arm.
- The class of a lever depends on which of the three points is in the middle.
- A long effort arm means a small effort, but the effort must move a longer distance.
