# Logarithmic Scales: pH, Decibels and Earthquakes

## Why logarithmic scales?
Some quantities range over a huge span of values. A base-10 logarithm compresses them into small numbers. On a log scale, a step of **1 unit means 10 times**.

## pH
**pH = -log[H+]**, where [H+] is the hydrogen ion concentration in moles per liter.
- [H+] = 10^-5 gives pH = **5**.
- Lower pH means more acid. A solution with pH 3 has [H+] = 10^-3, and a solution with pH 6 has [H+] = 10^-6. The pH 3 solution has **1000 times** more hydrogen ions.

## Sound level (decibels)
**L = 10 log(I / I0)**, where I0 is the faintest sound people hear.
- I = 1000 I0 gives L = 10 log(1000) = **30 dB**.
- L = 100 dB means log(I/I0) = 10, so I = **10^10 I0**.
- Every **+10 dB** multiplies the intensity by 10, and **+20 dB** multiplies it by **100**.
- Doubling the intensity adds 10 log 2, about 3 dB.

## Earthquakes (Richter scale)
The magnitude is a logarithm of the amplitude of the ground motion. **Each whole number up multiplies the amplitude by 10.**
- A magnitude 7 earthquake has **100 times** the amplitude of a magnitude 5 earthquake (10^2).
- The energy released grows even faster: about 32 times more for each step.

## Reading a log-scale ratio
If two values on a base-10 log scale differ by d, the ratio of the underlying quantities is **10^d**.
Example: 60 dB and 40 dB differ by 20, so the intensity ratio is 10^(20/10) = 100.

## Finding a quantity
Undo the log with an exponent. If pH = 4.5, then [H+] = 10^(-4.5), about 3.2 * 10^-5.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking that 2 points on the scale mean twice as much. They mean 100 times as much.
- Forgetting the negative sign in the pH formula.
- Using ln instead of log (base 10) in these scales.
