# Combining Transformations

## The general form
**g(x) = a * f(b(x - h)) + k**
| Part | Effect on the graph of f |
|---|---|
| **a** | vertical stretch (|a| > 1) or shrink (|a| < 1); if a is negative, reflect over the x-axis |
| **b** | horizontal shrink by a factor of 1/|b| (if |b| > 1) or stretch (if |b| < 1); if b is negative, reflect over the y-axis |
| **h** | shift **right** h units |
| **k** | shift **up** k units |

## Moving a point
If (x, y) is on the graph of f, then the point on g is
**(x / b + h, a * y + k)**

Take (4, 6) on the graph of f:
- g(x) = f(x - 3) + 2: **(7, 8)**
- g(x) = -f(x): **(4, -6)**
- g(x) = f(-x): **(-4, 6)**
- g(x) = f(x/2): the horizontal factor is 1/b = 2, so **(8, 6)**
- g(x) = f(2x): a horizontal shrink by 1/2, so (2, 6)
- g(x) = -3 f(2x) + 1: x = 4/2 = 2 and y = -3(6) + 1 = -17, so **(2, -17)**

## Order of transformations
1. Horizontal changes inside the parentheses (stretch/shrink, reflect, then shift).
2. Vertical changes outside (stretch/shrink, reflect, then shift).
Do stretches and reflections before shifts.

## Domain and range
- g(x) = sqrt(-x) is the graph of sqrt(x) reflected over the y-axis. Its domain is **x ≤ 0**.
- y = -2 sqrt(x + 1) + 3 starts at the point **(-1, 3)** (the starting point of sqrt(x) is (0, 0), moved left 1 and up 3). It opens downward from there.
- g(x) = 3(x - 1)^2 - 2 has its vertex at (1, -2) and is narrower than x^2.

## Reading a transformation from a graph
Find how the key points moved. If the vertex went from (0, 0) to (2, -1) and the curve is twice as steep, the equation is y = 2(x - 2)^2 - 1.

## Common mistakes
- Moving f(x - 3) to the left. Subtracting inside means right.
- Mixing up the effect of b: f(2x) makes the graph narrower, not wider.
- Doing the shift before the stretch.
