# Rational Inequalities

## Why we cannot just multiply
When we multiply both sides of an inequality by an expression with x, we do not know if it is positive or negative. If it is negative the inequality must be **reversed**. So instead we move everything to one side and study the **sign of a single fraction**.

## Method
1. Move all terms to one side so the other side is 0.
2. Combine into **one fraction** with a common denominator.
3. Find the **critical numbers**: zeros of the numerator and of the denominator.
4. Test the intervals.
5. Denominator zeros are **always excluded**. Numerator zeros are included only for ≤ or ≥.

## Example 1
(x - 1)/(x + 2) ≥ 0. Critical numbers: -2 (denominator) and 1 (numerator).
- x < -2: (-)/(-) = positive
- -2 < x < 1: (-)/(+) = negative
- x > 1: (+)/(+) = positive
Solution: **(-infinity, -2) and [1, infinity)**.

## Example 2
x/(x - 3) < 0. Critical numbers 0 and 3. The fraction is negative between them: **(0, 3)**.

## Example 3
(x + 1)/(x - 2) ≤ 0. Critical numbers -1 and 2. The fraction is negative on (-1, 2) and 0 at x = -1. Solution: **[-1, 2)**. The number 2 is excluded because it makes the denominator 0.

## Example 4: a number on the right side
2/(x - 1) > 1. Subtract 1: 2/(x - 1) - 1 > 0. Common denominator: (2 - (x - 1))/(x - 1) = (3 - x)/(x - 1) > 0. Critical numbers 1 and 3. At x = 2 the value is 1/1 > 0. Solution: **(1, 3)**.

## Example 5
1/x ≤ 2. Subtract 2: (1 - 2x)/x ≤ 0. Critical numbers 0 and 1/2. The fraction is negative for x < 0 and for x > 1/2, and 0 at x = 1/2. Solution: **(-infinity, 0) and [1/2, infinity)**.

## Common mistakes
- Multiplying both sides by the denominator without worrying about its sign.
- Including a number that makes the denominator 0.
- Forgetting to write the fraction as a single fraction before testing.
- Testing only one interval.
