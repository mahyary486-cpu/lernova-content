# Distance and Midpoint Formulas

## The distance formula
For two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) the distance between them is

**d = square root of [ (x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2 ]**

It comes from the Pythagorean theorem. The horizontal change and the vertical change are the two legs of a right triangle, and the distance is the hypotenuse.

## The midpoint formula
The midpoint is the point exactly halfway between the two points. Average the x values and average the y values:

**M = ( (x1 + x2) / 2 , (y1 + y2) / 2 )**

## Finding a missing endpoint
If M is the midpoint and A is one endpoint, then the other endpoint B is found by doubling and subtracting:
- x of B = 2 x (x of M) - (x of A)
- y of B = 2 x (y of M) - (y of A)

## Worked examples
1. Find the distance from A(1, 2) to B(7, 10). The changes are 6 and 8. Distance = square root of (36 + 64) = square root of 100 = **10**.
2. Find the midpoint of A(1, 2) and B(7, 10). M = (8 / 2, 12 / 2) = **(4, 6)**.
3. The midpoint of AB is M(3, -1) and A = (5, 2). Then B = (2 x 3 - 5, 2 x (-1) - 2) = **(1, -4)**.
4. Find the distance from (0, 0) to (5, 12). Distance = square root of (25 + 144) = square root of 169 = **13**.
5. A triangle has vertices P(0, 0), Q(6, 0) and R(6, 8). Its sides are PQ = 6, QR = 8 and PR = 10, so the perimeter is 24.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to square the differences, or forgetting the square root at the end.
- Adding instead of subtracting the coordinates in the distance formula. The order does not matter because the differences are squared, but you must be consistent for x and for y.
- Using the distance formula for the midpoint, or the other way around. The midpoint is an average and gives a point. The distance gives a single number.
