# Mole Ratios and Stoichiometry Calculations

Stoichiometry uses a balanced chemical equation to calculate how much of one substance reacts with or forms from another. The coefficients tell you the ratio of moles, never the ratio of grams.

## Key ideas
- A mole ratio compares the coefficients of two substances in a balanced equation. In N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3, the ratio of H2 to NH3 is 3 mol H2 : 2 mol NH3.
- Always balance the equation first.
- Mole-to-mole problems: multiply by the mole ratio.
- Mass-to-mass problems use the path: grams of A to moles of A to moles of B to grams of B.
- Use molar mass for the gram/mole steps and the mole ratio for the A/B step.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mole to mole.** For 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O, how many moles of water form from 3.0 mol of O2?
1. Ratio: 2 mol H2O per 1 mol O2.
2. Moles H2O = 3.0 mol O2 x (2 / 1) = 6.0 mol.

**Example 2: Mass to mass.** For 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO, find the mass of MgO from 12.15 g of Mg (24.31 g/mol). MgO = 40.31 g/mol.
1. Moles Mg = 12.15 / 24.31 = 0.4998 mol.
2. Moles MgO = 0.4998 x (2 / 2) = 0.4998 mol.
3. Mass MgO = 0.4998 x 40.31 = 20.1 g.

**Example 3: Reactant needed.** For CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O, how many grams of O2 burn 8.0 g of CH4 (16.04 g/mol)?
1. Moles CH4 = 8.0 / 16.04 = 0.499 mol.
2. Moles O2 = 0.499 x (2 / 1) = 0.998 mol.
3. Mass O2 = 0.998 x 32.00 = 31.9 g.

## Why mass is conserved
In Example 2, the 12.15 g of Mg gains oxygen, and the extra mass of about 8 g is the O2 that joined. Matter is conserved even though the masses of reactants and products are not equal one by one.

## Common mistakes
- Using an unbalanced equation.
- Applying the coefficient ratio directly to grams.
- Putting the ratio upside down. Write it so the unit you want is on top.
- Skipping the molar mass step, so units never cancel properly.
