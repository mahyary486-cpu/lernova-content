# Plastics and recycling

Plastics are useful synthetic polymers, but they cause waste problems, so people reduce, reuse and recycle them.

## Key ideas

Most plastics are made from petroleum. They are light, strong, cheap, waterproof and do not rust, so they are used in packaging, pipes, toys, clothes and medical tools.

Two main groups of plastics:
- Thermoplastics soften when heated and harden again when cooled. They can be melted and shaped again, so they are easy to recycle. Examples are polyethylene and PET.
- Thermosetting plastics keep their shape when heated and do not melt again. They cannot easily be reshaped. They are used for items like electrical sockets and pan handles.

The problem of plastic waste: most plastics are not broken down by bacteria, so they stay in nature for hundreds of years. Plastic in the sea harms fish, birds and turtles, which may swallow it or get trapped. Big pieces break into tiny pieces called microplastics, which enter food chains. Burning plastic can release poisonous gases, and landfills take up land.

The best approach is the rule of the "3 Rs", with a fourth step added:
- Reduce: use less plastic, for example carry a reusable bag.
- Reuse: use the same item many times, such as a refillable bottle.
- Recycle: collect used plastic and make it into new products.
- Refuse: say no to items you do not need, such as extra packaging.

Recycling steps: collect, sort by type, wash, shred, melt, and form into new products. Sorting is important because different plastics should not be mixed. Many plastic items carry a number from 1 to 7 inside a triangle. For example, 1 is PET (drink bottles), 2 is HDPE (milk jugs and detergent bottles), 3 is PVC and 5 is polypropylene.

Recycling saves oil, saves energy and lowers waste. Alternatives include paper, cloth and glass, and also bioplastics that can break down more easily.

## Examples

Example 1: A school plastic count. A class of 30 students each uses 2 single-use plastic bags per week.
Step 1: Bags per week = 30 × 2 = 60.
Step 2: A school year has about 36 school weeks, so bags = 60 × 36 = 2160.
If every student uses a cloth bag instead, the class avoids about 2160 bags in a year.

Example 2: Plastic in drink bottles. A family drinks 12 bottles of water per month, and each empty bottle has a mass of 20 g.
Step 1: Mass per month = 12 × 20 g = 240 g.
Step 2: Mass per year = 240 g × 12 = 2880 g = 2.88 kg.
By using a refillable bottle, this family would avoid 2.88 kg of plastic each year.

Example 3: Sorting a collection. A recycling center receives a drink bottle with the number 1, a margarine tub with the number 5 and a milk jug with the number 2. It sends each one to a different group, because PET, polypropylene and HDPE melt at different temperatures and should not be mixed.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that all plastics can be melted again. Thermosetting plastics cannot.
- Throwing mixed and dirty plastic into the recycling bin, which can spoil a whole batch.
- Believing that plastic "disappears" in nature. It only breaks into smaller pieces.
- Thinking that recycling alone solves the problem. Using less plastic is better than recycling.

## Remember

- Thermoplastics can be melted and reshaped; thermosetting plastics cannot.
- Plastic waste lasts for hundreds of years, so reduce and reuse come first.
- Recycling saves oil and energy and helps the environment.
