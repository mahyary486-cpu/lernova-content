# Gravity, Weight and Friction

Gravity pulls every object toward the center of Earth, and friction opposes sliding. Together they decide whether and how things move.

## Key ideas
- **Weight** is the force of gravity on an object: **W = m g**. Unit: newton (N).
- **Mass** is the amount of matter (kg) and does not change with location.
- On Earth g = 9.8 m/s^2; on the Moon g is about 1.6 m/s^2.
- **Friction** opposes the motion (or attempted motion) between surfaces in contact.
- Types: **static** (not moving), **sliding** (kinetic), **rolling** and **fluid** (air or liquid).
- More pressing force and rougher surfaces give more friction; wheels, smooth surfaces and oil reduce it.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Weight on Earth and the Moon.** Find the weight of a 60 kg person on Earth and on the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s^2).
1. On Earth: W = 60 x 9.8 = 588 N.
2. On the Moon: W = 60 x 1.6 = 96 N.
3. The mass is 60 kg in both places.

**Example 2: Constant velocity.** A box slides at constant velocity when pushed with 40 N.
1. Constant velocity means the net force is zero.
2. So the friction force is also 40 N, in the opposite direction.

**Example 3: Net force with friction.** A 50 kg box is pushed with 100 N and friction is 60 N.
1. Net force = 100 - 60 = 40 N.
2. a = F / m = 40 / 50 = 0.8 m/s^2.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up mass and weight.
- Thinking friction only acts when objects move. Static friction acts on objects at rest.
- Forgetting to subtract friction before using F = ma.
