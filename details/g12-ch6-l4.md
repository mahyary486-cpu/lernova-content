# Polar Coordinates

## The idea
A point in the plane can be named by **(r, theta)**: its distance r from the origin (the pole) and the angle theta from the positive x-axis.

## Conversions
- Polar to rectangular: **x = r cos theta, y = r sin theta**.
- Rectangular to polar: **r^2 = x^2 + y^2, tan theta = y / x** (choose theta in the correct quadrant).

## Key ideas
- Polar names are not unique: (r, theta) = (r, theta + 360 degrees) = (-r, theta + 180 degrees).
- A negative r means move in the opposite direction of the angle.
- **r = k** is a circle of radius k centered at the origin.
- **theta = k** is a line through the origin.
- **r = a sin theta** and **r = a cos theta** are circles through the origin.
- **r = a + b cos theta** is a limacon; if a = b it is a cardioid.
- **r = a sin(n theta)** is a rose with n petals when n is odd and 2n petals when n is even.

## Worked example 1: polar to rectangular
(4, 60 degrees).
1. x = 4 cos 60 = 2.
2. y = 4 sin 60 = 2 sqrt(3).
3. The point is (2, 2 sqrt(3)), about (2, 3.46).

## Worked example 2: rectangular to polar
(-3, 3).
1. r = sqrt(9 + 9) = 3 sqrt(2), about 4.24.
2. tan theta = 3/(-3) = -1. The point is in quadrant II, so theta = 135 degrees.
3. Polar form: (3 sqrt(2), 135 degrees).

## Worked example 3: converting an equation
r = 2 sin theta.
1. Multiply both sides by r: r^2 = 2 r sin theta.
2. Substitute: x^2 + y^2 = 2y.
3. Complete the square: x^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 1, a circle with center (0, 1) and radius 1.

## Common mistakes
- Using theta = arctan(y/x) without checking the quadrant.
- Forgetting that (-r, theta) is a different location from (r, theta).
- Using radians and degrees in the same problem.
- Losing the factor r when converting r = a sin theta.
