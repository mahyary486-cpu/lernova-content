# Potential Energy

Potential energy is energy stored because of an object's position or shape. It can later be changed into kinetic energy.

## Key ideas
- **Gravitational potential energy: PE = m g h**, with g = 9.8 m/s^2 near Earth's surface. The height h is measured from a reference level you choose.
- Only the change in potential energy has physical meaning, so the choice of zero level does not affect the results.
- Lifting an object at constant speed stores energy equal to the work done against gravity.
- Only the vertical height change matters, not the path taken.
- **Elastic potential energy: PE = 1/2 k x^2**, where k is the spring constant in N/m and x is the stretch or compression in meters.
- Doubling the stretch of a spring makes the stored energy four times larger.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Height.** A 4 kg object is held 6 m above the floor.
1. PE = m g h = 4 x 9.8 x 6.
2. PE = 235.2 J relative to the floor.

**Example 2: Spring.** A spring with k = 250 N/m is stretched 0.12 m.
1. PE = 1/2 k x^2 = 0.5 x 250 x 0.12^2.
2. 0.12^2 = 0.0144, so PE = 0.5 x 250 x 0.0144 = 1.8 J.

**Example 3: Ramp.** A 2 kg block is carried up a 3 m long ramp that makes 30 degrees with the ground.
1. The height gained is h = 3 x sin 30 = 1.5 m.
2. Delta PE = 2 x 9.8 x 1.5 = 29.4 J.

## Common mistakes
- Using the length of a ramp instead of the vertical height.
- Forgetting to square x in the spring formula, or forgetting the factor 1/2.
- Thinking PE has one absolute value. It depends on the reference level you pick.
- Using centimeters instead of meters for x or h.
