# Solving Systems by Graphing

## What is a system?
A **system of linear equations** is two or more linear equations with the same variables. A **solution** is an ordered pair (x, y) that makes every equation true. On a graph, the solution is the point where the lines cross.

## Three possible outcomes
- **One solution:** the lines intersect at exactly one point. The slopes are different.
- **No solution:** the lines are parallel. The slopes are equal and the y-intercepts are different, for example y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x - 4.
- **Infinitely many solutions:** both equations describe the same line, for example y = x + 2 and 2y = 2x + 4.

## Method
1. Write each equation in slope-intercept form y = mx + b if needed.
2. Graph both lines using the y-intercept and the slope.
3. Find the intersection point.
4. Check the point in both original equations.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Solve y = 2x - 1 and y = -x + 5.
1. Line 1 has y-intercept -1 and slope 2. Line 2 has y-intercept 5 and slope -1.
2. The lines cross at (2, 3).
3. Check: 2(2) - 1 = 3 and -2 + 5 = 3. Both are true.
Solution: (2, 3).

**Example 2.** Solve x + y = 4 and x - y = 2.
1. Rewrite: y = -x + 4 and y = x - 2.
2. Graph both lines. They meet at (3, 1).
3. Check: 3 + 1 = 4 and 3 - 1 = 2. Both are true.
Solution: (3, 1).

**Example 3.** How many solutions do y = 2x + 3 and 4x - 2y = -6 have?
1. Solve the second equation for y: -2y = -4x - 6, so y = 2x + 3.
2. Both equations give the same line.
3. There are infinitely many solutions.

## Limits of graphing
Graphing is quick and visual, but it can be inaccurate when the intersection does not fall on grid points, such as (1/3, 5/2). Then use an algebraic method.

## Common mistakes
- Graphing carelessly and reading the intersection point wrongly. Always check in both equations.
- Forgetting to put an equation in slope-intercept form before graphing.
- Saying parallel lines have no solution only when the slopes look close. They must be exactly equal.
- Reversing the coordinates and writing (y, x) instead of (x, y).
