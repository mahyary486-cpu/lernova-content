# Kirchhoff's Rules and Complex Circuits

Kirchhoff's rules are just two conservation laws applied to circuits: charge and energy.

## Key ideas
- **Junction rule**: currents in = currents out.
- **Loop rule**: sum of potential changes around a loop = **0**.
- Through a battery - to +: **+emf**; through a resistor along the current: **-I R**.
- A **negative** current means the assumed direction was wrong.
- **Voltage divider**: V_out = V_in R2 / (R1 + R2).
- One equation per independent **loop** and **junction**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Junction.** Currents of 3 A and 2 A enter a junction and one wire carries the current out.
1. The current out equals the current in.
2. I = 3 + 2 = 5 A.

**Example 2: Loop.** A 12 V battery drives a current through 2 ohm and 4 ohm resistors in series.
1. 12 - I x 2 - I x 4 = 0.
2. I = 2 A.

**Example 3: Voltage divider.** 9 V is across 3 ohm and 6 ohm resistors in series. Find the voltage across the 6 ohm.
1. V = 9 x 6 / (3 + 6).
2. V = 6 V.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up the signs in the loop rule.
- Forgetting the junction equation.
- Treating a negative current as an error.
