# Projectile Motion Launched at an Angle

When a projectile is launched at an angle theta above the horizontal with speed v, the launch velocity is split into components, and each is analyzed separately.

## Key ideas
- Initial components: vx = v cos(theta), vy = v sin(theta).
- Horizontal: constant velocity, x = vx t.
- Vertical: acceleration -g. The vertical velocity is vy - g t, and it is zero at the highest point.
- Time to the top: t = vy / g. Total time of flight (landing at the launch height): T = 2 vy / g.
- Maximum height: H = vy^2 / (2g).
- Range on level ground: R = vx T = v^2 sin(2 theta) / g.
- The range is largest at 45 degrees. Angles that add to 90 degrees, such as 30 and 60, give the same range.
- At the top the velocity is horizontal and equals vx, not zero.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: full analysis.** A ball is launched at 25 m/s at 40 degrees above level ground.
1. vx = 25 cos(40) = 19.2 m/s and vy = 25 sin(40) = 16.1 m/s.
2. Time to the top: 16.1 / 9.8 = 1.64 s, so T = 3.28 s.
3. Maximum height: H = 16.1^2 / (2 x 9.8) = 13.2 m.
4. Range: R = 19.2 x 3.28 = 62.8 m.

**Example 2: complementary angles.** A ball is kicked at 20 m/s.
1. At 30 degrees: R = 20^2 x sin(60) / 9.8 = 35.3 m.
2. At 60 degrees: R = 20^2 x sin(120) / 9.8 = 35.3 m.
3. At 45 degrees: R = 400 / 9.8 = 40.8 m, the maximum.

**Example 3: speed at the top.** A projectile is launched at 50 m/s at 60 degrees.
1. Only vy becomes zero at the top.
2. Speed at the top = vx = 50 cos(60) = 25 m/s.

## Common mistakes
- Saying the velocity is zero at the top of the path. Only the vertical part is zero.
- Mixing up sine and cosine for the components.
- Using the range formula when the landing height differs from the launch height.
- Believing a heavier projectile travels a different path. Without air resistance mass does not matter.
