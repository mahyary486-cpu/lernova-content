# Rates of Reaction and Collision Theory

Food spoils slowly in a refrigerator and quickly on a hot day because temperature controls reaction rate.

## Key ideas
- **Rate** = change in concentration / time (mol/(L s)).
- Collisions need **enough energy** and the right **orientation**.
- Rate rises with **concentration**, **temperature**, **surface area** and **catalysts**.
- A **catalyst** lowers the **activation energy**, is not consumed and does not change delta H.
- A **10 C rise** roughly **doubles** the rate.
- Particles with energy above the **activation energy** can react.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Average rate.** [A] falls from 0.80 M to 0.50 M in 30 s.
1. rate = -delta[A] / delta t = 0.30 / 30.
2. rate = 0.010 M/s.

**Example 2: Temperature.** The rate doubles every 10 C. By how much does it increase for a 30 C rise?
1. 2 x 2 x 2 = 8.
2. The rate is 8 times faster.

**Example 3: Catalyst.** What does a catalyst do to the activation energy and delta H?
1. It lowers the activation energy.
2. delta H is unchanged.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a catalyst is used up.
- Thinking a catalyst shifts the equilibrium.
- Forgetting that rate is a change over time.
