# Moons, asteroids and space travel

This lesson is about natural satellites called moons, small bodies such as asteroids and comets, and how humans travel and send machines into space.

## Key ideas

- A moon is a natural satellite that orbits a planet. Mercury and Venus have no moons. Earth has one. Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos. Jupiter and Saturn have dozens of moons.
- Earth's Moon has a diameter of about 3,474 km and is about 384,400 km from Earth. It orbits Earth in about 27.3 days. It has no air and no liquid water. Its surface gravity is about one sixth of Earth's.
- The Moon shows phases because we see different parts of its sunlit half as it orbits. The cycle from one new moon to the next takes about 29.5 days.
- Asteroids are rocky or metallic bodies that orbit the Sun. Most are in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. Ceres, about 940 km wide, is the largest object in the belt and is a dwarf planet.
- Comets are balls of ice, dust and rock. When a comet comes near the Sun, the ice turns to gas and forms a glowing head and a tail. The tail always points away from the Sun because of the solar wind and sunlight.
- A meteoroid is a small piece of rock in space. If it burns in Earth's atmosphere, we see a meteor. If a part reaches the ground, it is called a meteorite.
- To leave Earth, a rocket must reach the escape velocity, about 11.2 km/s. A satellite in orbit moves around Earth at a high speed so that it keeps falling around the planet instead of falling to the ground.
- Important events: Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite, in 1957; Yuri Gagarin, the first human in space, in 1961; Apollo 11, the first humans on the Moon, in 1969. Iran launched its first satellite, Omid, in 2009.
- The International Space Station (ISS) orbits at about 400 km above Earth at about 7.7 km/s. Satellites are used for communication, weather forecasts, navigation and studying Earth.

## Examples

Example 1: How long does the ISS take to orbit Earth once?
- Radius of the orbit = 6371 + 400 = 6771 km.
- Circumference = 2 × 3.14 × 6771 ≈ 42,500 km.
- Time = distance / speed = 42,500 / 7.66 ≈ 5,550 s.
- In minutes: 5,550 / 60 ≈ 92.5 minutes. The ISS orbits Earth about 15 times a day.

Example 2: Weight of an astronaut on the Moon.
- Mass of the astronaut with equipment: 60 kg (mass does not change).
- Weight on Earth = 60 × 9.8 = 588 N.
- Weight on the Moon = 60 × 1.62 = 97 N.
- The astronaut feels about one sixth of the weight, so he can jump higher.

Example 3: A radio message to a robot on Mars.
- Suppose Mars is 2.25 × 10^8 km away.
- Radio waves travel at the speed of light, 3 × 10^5 km/s.
- Time = 2.25 × 10^8 / 3 × 10^5 = 750 s = 12.5 minutes.
- A reply takes another 12.5 minutes, so a robot on Mars must be able to work alone for some time.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that the Moon makes its own light. It reflects sunlight.
- Mixing up mass and weight. The mass of an astronaut is the same on Earth and on the Moon, but the weight is different.
- Mixing up meteors and meteorites. A meteor is the light in the sky and a meteorite is a rock that lands.
- Thinking that the tail of a comet shows the direction in which it moves. It points away from the Sun.

## Remember

- Moons orbit planets, asteroids and comets orbit the Sun.
- Escape velocity from Earth is about 11.2 km/s.
- Moon gravity is about 1.62 m/s^2, about one sixth of Earth's.
