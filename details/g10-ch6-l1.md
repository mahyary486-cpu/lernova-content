# Arcs and Chords

## Parts of a circle
- **Radius:** center to any point on the circle.
- **Diameter:** a chord through the center, equal to twice the radius.
- **Chord:** a segment with both endpoints on the circle.
- **Central angle:** vertex at the center.
- **Arc:** a piece of the circle. A **minor arc** is less than 180 degrees, a **major arc** is more than 180 degrees and a **semicircle** is exactly 180 degrees.

## Key facts
- The measure of an arc equals the measure of its **central angle**.
- The whole circle is **360 degrees**.
- Adding arcs: if two arcs share an endpoint, their measures add.
- In the same circle (or congruent circles), **congruent chords** have congruent arcs and are the same distance from the center.
- A **diameter (or radius) perpendicular to a chord bisects** the chord and its arc.

## Worked examples
1. A central angle is 80 degrees, so its minor arc is **80 degrees** and the major arc is 360 - 80 = **280 degrees**.
2. The radius is 10 and a chord is 12 long. The perpendicular from the center to the chord splits it into 6 and 6, so its distance from the center is the square root of (100 - 36) = **8**.
3. In a circle with arcs AB = 100 degrees and BC = 70 degrees, the arc AC through B is **170 degrees**.
4. Two chords are the same distance from the center. They are **congruent**.
5. A chord is 16 long and is 6 from the center. The radius is the square root of (64 + 36) = **10**.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing arc **measure** (degrees) with arc **length** (a distance).
- Forgetting that the perpendicular from the center cuts the chord in half.
- Adding arcs that do not share an endpoint.
