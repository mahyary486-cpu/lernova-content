# Polynomial Functions and End Behavior

## What is a polynomial function?
A **polynomial function** is a sum of terms of the form a * x^n, where n is a whole number (0, 1, 2, ...). In **standard form** the terms are written from the highest power to the lowest:
f(x) = a_n x^n + ... + a_1 x + a_0

- The **degree** is the highest power of x.
- The **leading coefficient** is the coefficient of that highest power.
- The **constant term** a_0 is the y-intercept.

Example: f(x) = 3x^2 - 8x^5 + 1. In standard form it is -8x^5 + 3x^2 + 1. The degree is **5** and the leading coefficient is **-8**.

## Names by degree
| Degree | Name |
|---|---|
| 1 | linear |
| 2 | quadratic |
| 3 | cubic |
| 4 | quartic |
| 5 | quintic |

## End behavior
The ends of the graph depend on only two things: the degree (even or odd) and the sign of the leading coefficient.
| | Leading coefficient positive | Leading coefficient negative |
|---|---|---|
| Even degree | both ends **up** | both ends **down** |
| Odd degree | left **down**, right **up** | left **up**, right **down** |

## Turning points
A polynomial of degree n has **at most n - 1 turning points** (places where the graph changes from rising to falling or the other way). A degree 5 polynomial has at most 4. If a graph has 3 turning points, its degree is at least 4.

## Worked examples
1. f(x) = x^3 - 5x. Odd degree, positive leading coefficient, so the left end goes down and the right end goes up.
2. f(x) = -2x^4 + x. Even degree, negative leading coefficient, so **both ends go down**.
3. The degree of 5x^4 - 3x^2 + 7 is **4**.

## Common mistakes
- Reading the leading coefficient before putting the polynomial in standard form.
- Using the sign of the constant term to decide end behavior. Only the leading term matters for the ends.
- Thinking a degree n graph must have exactly n - 1 turning points. It has at most that many.
