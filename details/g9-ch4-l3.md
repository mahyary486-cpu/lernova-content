# Scientific Notation

## Definition
A number is in **scientific notation** when it is written as **a x 10^n**, where 1 <= a < 10 and n is an integer. It is used to write very large and very small numbers in a short way.

Examples: 45,000 = 4.5 x 10^4 and 0.00032 = 3.2 x 10^(-4).

## Converting
- **To scientific notation:** move the decimal point until the number is between 1 and 10. Count the moves. Moving left gives a positive exponent (large numbers). Moving right gives a negative exponent (numbers smaller than 1).
- **To standard form:** move the decimal point n places right if n is positive, or n places left if n is negative.

## Operations
- **Multiply:** multiply the decimal parts and add the exponents of 10.
- **Divide:** divide the decimal parts and subtract the exponents.
- **Adjust:** if the decimal part ends up 10 or more, or less than 1, rewrite so it is between 1 and 10.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Write 7,200,000 in scientific notation.
1. Move the decimal point 6 places left to get 7.2.
2. Result: 7.2 x 10^6.

**Example 2.** Multiply (3 x 10^5)(4 x 10^3).
1. 3 x 4 = 12 and 10^5 x 10^3 = 10^8.
2. This gives 12 x 10^8, which is not yet in proper form.
3. Rewrite 12 as 1.2 x 10: the answer is 1.2 x 10^9.

**Example 3.** Divide (6 x 10^2) / (2 x 10^(-3)).
1. 6 / 2 = 3.
2. 10^2 / 10^(-3) = 10^(2 - (-3)) = 10^5.
3. Result: 3 x 10^5.

## Comparing
Compare exponents of 10 first. 2 x 10^7 is bigger than 9 x 10^6 because 10^7 is larger than 10^6.

## Common mistakes
- Using a decimal part of 10 or more, such as 12 x 10^8.
- Getting the sign of the exponent backwards: 0.0005 = 5 x 10^(-4), not 5 x 10^4.
- Counting the moves of the decimal wrongly.
- Multiplying the exponents of 10 instead of adding them in a product.
