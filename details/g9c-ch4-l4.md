# Concentration

A drink can be a little sweet or very sweet depending on how much sugar is dissolved in it. Concentration puts a number on this.

## Key ideas
- **Mass percent** = (mass solute / mass solution) x 100%.
- **g/L** = mass of solute / volume of solution.
- **Molarity** M = moles of solute / liters of solution. Unit: mol/L or M.
- **Moles** n = mass / molar mass.
- **Dilution**: M1 V1 = M2 V2 (the moles of solute do not change).
- Mass of solution = mass of solute + mass of solvent.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mass percent.** 20 g of salt is dissolved in 80 g of water.
1. Mass of solution = 20 + 80 = 100 g.
2. Mass percent = 20 / 100 x 100% = 20%.

**Example 2: Molarity.** 58.5 g of NaCl (molar mass 58.5 g/mol) is dissolved to make 2.0 L of solution.
1. n = 58.5 / 58.5 = 1 mol.
2. M = n / V = 1 / 2.0 = 0.5 M.

**Example 3: Dilution.** 100 mL of a 4 M solution is diluted to 400 mL.
1. M1 V1 = M2 V2, so M2 = 4 x 100 / 400.
2. M2 = 1 M.

## Common mistakes
- Using the mass of the solvent instead of the solution for mass percent.
- Using milliliters instead of liters in molarity.
- Thinking that dilution changes the amount of solute.
