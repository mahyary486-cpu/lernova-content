# Graphing Polynomials and the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

## Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
A polynomial of degree n (n ≥ 1) has **exactly n complex zeros**, counting multiplicity. A degree 5 polynomial has 5 zeros, some of which may be repeated or complex.
A polynomial of degree n has at most n real zeros, so a cubic can cross the x-axis **at most 3 times**.

## Zeros and multiplicity on a graph
- **Odd multiplicity** (1, 3, ...): the graph **crosses** the x-axis.
- **Even multiplicity** (2, 4, ...): the graph **touches** the x-axis and turns around.

## Steps to sketch a polynomial
1. Factor to find the zeros and their multiplicities.
2. Find the y-intercept f(0).
3. Use the degree and leading coefficient to get the end behavior.
4. Plot the zeros and the y-intercept, then connect them, crossing or touching at each zero.

## Example 1
f(x) = (x + 1)(x - 2)^2.
- Zeros: -1 (multiplicity 1, **crosses**) and 2 (multiplicity 2, **touches**).
- y-intercept: f(0) = (1)(4) = **4**.
- Degree 3 with positive leading coefficient: left end down, right end up.

## Example 2
f(x) = -(x + 2)(x - 1)^2.
- The leading coefficient is -1 and the degree is 3, so the left end is **up** and the right end is **down**.
- It **crosses** at x = -2 and **touches** at x = 1.

## Writing a polynomial from its zeros
Zeros 1, -2 and 3 with leading coefficient 1: f(x) = (x - 1)(x + 2)(x - 3) = **x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6**.

Zeros 2 and 1 + i with real coefficients: the zero 1 - i must also be there.
f(x) = (x - 2)(x - (1 + i))(x - (1 - i)) = (x - 2)(x^2 - 2x + 2) = **x^3 - 4x^2 + 6x - 4**.

## Common mistakes
- Making the graph cross at a zero of even multiplicity.
- Forgetting the conjugate zero when the coefficients are real.
- Ignoring the leading coefficient's sign. A negative factor in front flips the ends.
