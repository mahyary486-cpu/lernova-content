# Newton's First Law and Inertia

Newton's first law explains what happens when no unbalanced force acts. Before Newton, many people thought a force was needed to keep things moving. In fact a force is needed only to change motion.

## Key ideas
- **First law:** an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving at constant velocity in a straight line, unless a net external force acts on it.
- **Inertia** is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its motion. Mass is the measure of inertia: more mass means more inertia.
- If F_net = 0 the object is in **equilibrium**: it is at rest or moves at constant velocity. Acceleration is zero either way.
- Balanced forces cancel, so their sum is zero. Unbalanced forces give a nonzero net force and change the velocity.
- Equilibrium on a vertical line gives upward forces = downward forces. For a hanging object at rest, tension = weight = mg (g = 9.8 m/s^2).
- Everyday effects: passengers lurch forward when a bus brakes, and a tablecloth can be yanked from under dishes. The objects keep their original motion.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: hanging lamp.** A 5.0 kg lamp hangs at rest from a cord.
1. At rest, F_net = 0, so tension T = weight.
2. T = mg = 5.0 x 9.8 = 49 N.

**Example 2: car at constant velocity.** A car drives at a steady 25 m/s. Air drag and rolling resistance total 700 N backward.
1. Constant velocity means a = 0 and F_net = 0.
2. The road must push forward with a force equal to the backward force: 700 N.
3. If the forward force were larger, the car would speed up.

**Example 3: crate on a rope.** A 30 kg crate is lifted at a constant 0.5 m/s by a rope.
1. Constant velocity means F_net = 0.
2. Tension = weight = 30 x 9.8 = 294 N.
3. The speed does not change the answer, because only acceleration matters.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a moving object needs a net force to keep moving. It needs a net force only to change its velocity.
- Calling inertia a force. It is a property of matter, measured by mass.
- Believing that "no motion" is the only equilibrium. Constant velocity is equilibrium too.
- Forgetting that a force acting alone is not enough. Equal opposite forces together give zero net force.
