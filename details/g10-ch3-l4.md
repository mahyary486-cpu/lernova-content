# Medians, Altitudes and Bisectors

## Four special segments
| Name | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Median** | joins a vertex to the **midpoint** of the opposite side |
| **Altitude** | a segment from a vertex **perpendicular** to the opposite side (or its extension) |
| **Perpendicular bisector** | passes through the midpoint of a side at 90 degrees |
| **Angle bisector** | splits an angle of the triangle into two equal angles |

## Points of concurrency
Three lines that meet at one point are **concurrent**. Each set of special lines in a triangle is concurrent.

| Lines | Meeting point | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Medians | **Centroid** | the balance point; always inside the triangle |
| Perpendicular bisectors | **Circumcenter** | equally far from the three vertices; center of the circle through the vertices |
| Angle bisectors | **Incenter** | equally far from the three sides; center of the circle inside the triangle |
| Altitudes | **Orthocenter** | can be inside, on or outside the triangle |

## The centroid rule
The centroid is **two thirds** of the way from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. So the part from the vertex is **twice** as long as the part from the centroid to the midpoint.

## Worked examples
1. The distance from a vertex to the centroid is 8. The whole median is 8 x 3 / 2 = **12**, and the part from the centroid to the midpoint is 4.
2. Find the centroid of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0) and (0, 9). Average the coordinates: ((0 + 6 + 0) / 3, (0 + 0 + 9) / 3) = **(2, 3)**.
3. Point P is on the perpendicular bisector of segment AB. If PA = 10, then PB = **10**, because every point on the perpendicular bisector is equally far from A and B.
4. Point Q is on the bisector of angle ABC and is 5 units from side BA. It is also **5** units from side BC.
5. In a right triangle the orthocenter is at the vertex of the right angle, because the two legs are altitudes.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up a median (to the midpoint) and an altitude (perpendicular).
- Forgetting that an altitude may fall outside an obtuse triangle.
- Using the centroid ratio the wrong way: the longer part is next to the vertex.
