# Vectors in the Plane

## The idea
A **vector** has both magnitude (size) and direction. A scalar has size only. We write a vector in component form as **<a, b>**.

## Key ideas
- From initial point (x1, y1) to terminal point (x2, y2): **<x2 - x1, y2 - y1>**.
- **Magnitude:** |<a, b>| = sqrt(a^2 + b^2).
- **Direction:** tan theta = b / a, adjusting the quadrant.
- From magnitude r and direction angle theta: **<r cos theta, r sin theta>**.
- **Unit vector** in the direction of v: v / |v|.
- Add and subtract component by component. Scalar multiplication multiplies each component.
- **Dot product:** <a, b> . <c, d> = ac + bd. It is a number, not a vector.
- **Angle between vectors:** cos theta = (u . v) / (|u||v|).
- If u . v = 0, the vectors are perpendicular (orthogonal).

## Worked example 1: component form and unit vector
P(1, 2), Q(4, 6).
1. v = <4 - 1, 6 - 2> = <3, 4>.
2. |v| = sqrt(9 + 16) = 5.
3. Unit vector = <3/5, 4/5>.

## Worked example 2: from magnitude and direction
A force of 10 N acts at 30 degrees above the x-axis.
1. x-component = 10 cos 30 = 8.66.
2. y-component = 10 sin 30 = 5.
3. The force is <8.66, 5>.

## Worked example 3: operations and angle
u = <2, 3>, v = <4, -1>.
1. u + v = <6, 2>.
2. 3u - 2v = <6 - 8, 9 + 2> = <-2, 11>.
3. u . v = 8 - 3 = 5.

For u = <3, 4> and v = <4, 3>: u . v = 24, |u| = |v| = 5, so cos theta = 24/25 and theta = 16.26 degrees.

## Common mistakes
- Subtracting initial minus terminal instead of terminal minus initial.
- Treating the dot product as a vector.
- Forgetting the square root in the magnitude.
- Ignoring the quadrant when finding the direction angle.
