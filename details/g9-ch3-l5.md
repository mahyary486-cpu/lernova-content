# Linear Inequalities and Systems of Inequalities

## Graphing one inequality
A **linear inequality in two variables** uses <, >, <= or >=, for example y > 2x - 1.
1. Graph the **boundary line** by replacing the inequality sign with an equals sign.
2. Use a **solid** line for <= or >= (points on the line are solutions) and a **dashed** line for < or > (they are not).
3. Shade the correct side. For y > or y >=, shade above. For y < or y <=, shade below. Or test a point not on the line, such as (0, 0).

If you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, **reverse the inequality sign**.

## Systems of inequalities
A **system of inequalities** is two or more inequalities. The solution is the region where all shaded areas overlap. Any point in that region satisfies every inequality.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Is (1, 4) a solution of y > 2x + 1?
1. Substitute: 4 > 2(1) + 1 = 3.
2. This is true, so (1, 4) is a solution.

**Example 2.** Graph 3x - 2y > 6.
1. Solve for y: -2y > -3x + 6. Divide by -2 and reverse the sign: y < (3/2)x - 3.
2. Draw a dashed line with y-intercept -3 and slope 3/2.
3. Shade below the line.
4. Check (0, 0): 0 > 6 is false, and (0, 0) is above the line, so shading below is correct.

**Example 3.** Is (2, 5) a solution of the system y >= x + 1 and y < 7 - x?
1. First: 5 >= 3 is true.
2. Second: 5 < 5 is false.
3. The point fails one inequality, so it is not a solution.

## Modeling
"At most $40 for shirts at $8 and hats at $5" gives 8s + 5h <= 40. Words like "at most" mean <=, and "at least" means >=. In real problems, quantities usually cannot be negative, so also use s >= 0 and h >= 0.

## Common mistakes
- Using a solid line for < or >, or a dashed line for <= or >=.
- Forgetting to reverse the sign when dividing by a negative.
- Shading the wrong side. Test a point to be sure.
- Thinking a solution only needs to satisfy one inequality of a system.
