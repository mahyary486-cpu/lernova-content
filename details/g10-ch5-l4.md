# Solving Right Triangles

## What it means
To **solve** a right triangle means to find all of its sides and angles. You need one side and one other part (a second side or an acute angle).

## Tools
- **Pythagorean theorem** to find a missing side when two sides are known.
- **sin, cos, tan** to find a side when an angle and a side are known.
- **Inverse functions** (sin^-1, cos^-1, tan^-1) to find an angle when two sides are known.
- The two acute angles add up to 90 degrees.

## Angles of elevation and depression
- **Angle of elevation:** from the horizontal **up** to the line of sight.
- **Angle of depression:** from the horizontal **down** to the line of sight.
These two are equal for the same line of sight (alternate interior angles).

## Worked examples
1. Legs of 5 and 12. The hypotenuse is 13. The angle opposite the 5 is tan^-1 (5/12), about **22.6 degrees**. The other angle is about 67.4 degrees.
2. Hypotenuse 10 and angle 35 degrees. Opposite side = 10 x sin 35, about **5.74**. Adjacent side = 10 x cos 35, about **8.19**. Other angle = 55 degrees.
3. From a point 50 feet from a building, the angle of elevation of the top is 50 degrees. Height = 50 x tan 50, about **59.6 feet**.
4. A plane 3000 feet high sees an airport at an angle of depression of 10 degrees. The ground distance is 3000 / tan 10, about **17,000 feet**.
5. A ramp rises 2 feet over a horizontal run of 24 feet. The angle is tan^-1 (2/24), about **4.8 degrees**.

## Common mistakes
- Using a trig function when the Pythagorean theorem is simpler (two sides known).
- Using sin instead of sin^-1 when you need an angle.
- Measuring the angle of elevation from the vertical instead of the horizontal.
- Rounding too early. Keep extra decimals until the final answer.
