# Mollusks

Mollusks are soft-bodied invertebrates, such as snails, clams, and octopuses, that often have a hard shell.

## Key ideas

- Mollusks belong to the phylum Mollusca, the second-largest animal phylum after arthropods. They live in oceans, fresh water, and on land.
- The body has bilateral symmetry. It is made of three main parts:
  - The **foot**, a strong muscle used for moving, digging, or catching prey.
  - The **visceral mass**, which holds the organs such as the stomach, heart, and gills.
  - The **mantle**, a thin layer of tissue covering the visceral mass. In many mollusks it produces the shell from calcium carbonate.
- Most mollusks have a radula, a tongue covered with tiny teeth, used to scrape food from rocks or plants. Bivalves do not have a radula.
- Aquatic mollusks breathe with gills. Land snails have a lung-like area in the mantle cavity.
- Most have an open circulatory system. Cephalopods have a closed one.
- **Gastropods** ("stomach foot") are the largest class. Examples are snails, slugs, and limpets. They have one shell (or none, as in slugs) and move on a broad, flat foot that makes a slippery mucus trail.
- **Bivalves** have a shell made of two halves joined by a hinge and closed by strong muscles. Examples are clams, oysters, mussels, and scallops. They filter tiny food from water and usually stay in one place. Some oysters make pearls when a small particle irritates the mantle.
- **Cephalopods** ("head foot") include octopuses, squid, and cuttlefish. The foot is divided into arms or tentacles around the head. They have a large brain, good eyes, and move fast by pushing water out of a tube. Many have only a small inner shell or none. The nautilus has an outer shell.
- Mollusks are food for people and animals. Their shells are used for decoration and for making lime.

## Examples

**Example 1: Classifying by shell.** A shell has two matching halves joined at a hinge, and the animal lives buried in sand. It is a bivalve, such as a clam. A second animal has one coiled shell and crawls on a broad foot. It is a gastropod. A third animal has no outer shell, eight arms, and large eyes. It is a cephalopod, an octopus.

**Example 2: How an oyster feeds.** An oyster lies fixed on a rock. It opens its shell a little and draws water over its gills. Tiny food particles stick to mucus and move to the mouth. One oyster can filter many liters of water in one hour, so oysters help clean the water.

**Example 3: An octopus escapes.** An octopus sees a shark. It sends out a dark ink cloud, pushes water through its tube to move away fast, and changes the color of its skin to blend in with the rocks. This is possible because cephalopods have a developed brain and nervous system, unlike most other mollusks.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that all mollusks have shells. Slugs, octopuses, and squid have none (or only a small inner one).
- Thinking that the octopus is related to fish. It is a mollusk with no backbone.
- Believing that mollusks all live in the sea. Many snails live on land or in fresh water.
- Confusing the mantle with the shell. The mantle is soft tissue that makes the shell.

## Remember

- Three parts: foot, visceral mass, mantle.
- Three main classes: gastropods, bivalves, cephalopods.
- Cephalopods are the most developed mollusks.
