# Crude oil and its separation

Crude oil is a natural liquid mixture of many hydrocarbons, and it is separated into useful parts by fractional distillation.

## Key ideas

Crude oil formed over millions of years from the remains of tiny sea organisms that were buried under mud and heated under pressure. It is a fossil fuel and a non-renewable resource. In Iran, large oil fields are found in the south-west of the country and in the Persian Gulf region.

Crude oil is a mixture, not a pure substance. It contains many hydrocarbons, which are compounds made only of carbon and hydrogen. Many of them are alkanes, with the general formula CnH(2n+2). For example, methane is CH4, ethane is C2H6 and octane is C8H18.

Raw crude oil is not directly useful, so a refinery separates it by fractional distillation:
- The oil is heated until most of it becomes vapor.
- The vapor enters a tall column that is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top.
- Each hydrocarbon rises until it reaches a level where the temperature is below its boiling point. There it condenses to a liquid and is collected.
- Each collected group of hydrocarbons with similar boiling points is called a fraction.

The main fractions, from the top of the column to the bottom, are:
- Refinery gases (1 to 4 carbon atoms): used as fuel gas, such as LPG.
- Gasoline (about 5 to 10 carbon atoms): fuel for cars.
- Kerosene (about 10 to 16 carbon atoms): jet fuel and heating.
- Diesel (about 14 to 20 carbon atoms): fuel for trucks and buses.
- Fuel oil and lubricating oil: fuel for ships and power stations, and oil for machines.
- Bitumen: the thick residue at the bottom, used for roads.

The longer the carbon chain, the higher the boiling point, the thicker the liquid (more viscous), and the harder it is to ignite. Short chains are runny, easy to ignite and have low boiling points.

## Examples

Example 1: Finding the formula of an alkane. An alkane has 8 carbon atoms. Using CnH(2n+2) with n = 8, the number of hydrogen atoms is 2 × 8 + 2 = 18. The formula is C8H18, which is octane, a part of gasoline.

Example 2: Molar mass and carbon dioxide from octane. Octane burns completely: 2C8H18 + 25O2 -> 16CO2 + 18H2O.
Step 1: Molar mass of C8H18 = 8 × 12 + 18 × 1 = 114 g/mol.
Step 2: Two moles of octane give 16 moles of CO2, so one mole gives 8 moles.
Step 3: Mass of CO2 from 1 mol = 8 × 44 = 352 g.
Step 4: Ratio = 352 ÷ 114 ≈ 3.09, so each 1 g of octane makes about 3.1 g of CO2.

Example 3: Which fraction rises highest? A refinery has two samples: one with molecules of 4 carbon atoms and one with molecules of 18 carbon atoms. The 4-carbon sample has the lower boiling point, so it stays as vapor higher in the column. The 18-carbon sample has a higher boiling point, so it condenses lower down.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that distillation changes one substance into another. It only separates substances by boiling point; no chemical reaction happens.
- Thinking that crude oil is one pure substance.
- Mixing up the position of the fractions: the lightest fractions come out at the top, not the bottom.
- Believing that oil is renewable. It forms far too slowly to replace what we use.

## Remember

- Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons separated by differences in boiling point.
- Longer chains: higher boiling point, thicker, harder to ignite.
- Oil is a limited fossil fuel, so using it wisely matters.
