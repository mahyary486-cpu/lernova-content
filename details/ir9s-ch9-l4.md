# Work and torque

Work measures the energy transferred by a force that moves an object, and torque measures how strongly a force turns an object.

## Key ideas

- In science, work is done when a force moves an object in the direction of the force. Work = force × distance, or W = F × d.
- The unit of work is the joule (J). One joule is the work done by a force of 1 N moving an object 1 m: 1 J = 1 N·m.
- If there is no movement, no work is done, even if you push very hard. Carrying a bag along a level road also does no work on the bag by your lifting force, because the force is upward and the movement is sideways.
- Power is the rate of doing work: P = W / t. The unit of power is the watt (W), which is 1 J/s.
- Torque is the turning effect of a force. Torque = force × perpendicular distance from the turning point, or τ = F × d.
- The unit of torque is the newton meter (N·m). Torque is not the same as energy, even though the unit looks similar.
- A longer wrench gives a larger torque for the same force. This is why a long handle helps to loosen a tight nut.
- The force must act at a right angle to the arm to give the largest torque. A push along the arm gives zero torque.
- Weight of an object = mass × g, where g ≈ 10 N/kg is used in these examples.

## Examples

Example 1: Pushing a box. A person pushes a box with a force of 50 N for 6 m along the floor.
- W = F × d = 50 × 6 = 300 J.

Example 2: Using a wrench. A force of 80 N acts at right angles on a wrench of length 0.25 m.
- Torque = 80 × 0.25 = 20 N·m.
- A nut needs 40 N·m. With a 0.50 m wrench, the force needed is F = 40 / 0.50 = 80 N.
- With the 0.25 m wrench, the force would be 40 / 0.25 = 160 N.

Example 3: Lifting a bag. A person lifts a 20 kg bag by 1.5 m in 5 s.
- Weight = 20 × 10 = 200 N.
- Work = 200 × 1.5 = 300 J.
- Power = 300 / 5 = 60 W.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that work is done when you hold a heavy object still. The distance is zero, so the work is zero.
- Using the length of the wrench along a slanted direction instead of the perpendicular distance.
- Mixing mass and weight. Multiply the mass by 10 N/kg before using it as a force.
- Mixing up work (J) and power (W). Power also includes the time.

## Remember

- W = F × d, with the unit joule.
- Torque = F × d (perpendicular), with the unit N·m.
- P = W / t, with the unit watt.
