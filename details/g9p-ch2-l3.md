# Acceleration

When the velocity of an object changes, we say that the object accelerates. In everyday life we use this word only for speeding up, but in physics it is much wider. Pressing the gas pedal, pressing the brake and turning the steering wheel all make a car accelerate, because each one changes the velocity.

## Key ideas

Acceleration is the rate at which velocity changes. It is calculated as the change in velocity divided by the time it takes: a = (v_final - v_initial) / t. The SI unit is meters per second squared (m/s^2). This unit means that the velocity changes by that many meters per second during each second. An acceleration of 4 m/s^2 means that the speed grows by 4 m/s every second.

An object accelerates in three situations: when it speeds up, when it slows down, and when it changes direction. The direction of the acceleration relative to the direction of the velocity tells which of these happens. If the acceleration points the same way as the velocity, the object speeds up. If it points opposite to the velocity, the object slows down. When the velocity is positive and the object slows down, the acceleration comes out negative, and this is sometimes called deceleration. The negative sign does not mean the quantity is "less"; it means the acceleration points in the negative direction.

When the acceleration is constant, the velocity changes by the same amount every second, and the final velocity can be found from v_final = v_initial + a x t. If the acceleration is zero, the velocity does not change, which means the object is at rest or moves at constant velocity in a straight line.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Speeding up.** A car goes from 0 to 20 m/s in 5 s. What is its acceleration?
1. The change in velocity is 20 m/s - 0 m/s = 20 m/s.
2. Divide by the time: a = 20 / 5.
3. The acceleration is 4 m/s^2, which means the car gains 4 m/s each second.

**Example 2: Braking.** A car slows from 25 m/s to 5 m/s in 4 s. What is its acceleration?
1. The change in velocity is 5 - 25 = -20 m/s.
2. Divide by the time: a = -20 / 4 = -5 m/s^2.
3. The negative sign shows that the acceleration points opposite to the motion, so the car is slowing down.

**Example 3: Final speed.** A cart moving at 3 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s^2 for 6 s. What is its final speed?
1. Use v_final = v_initial + a x t.
2. Substitute: v_final = 3 + 2 x 6 = 3 + 12.
3. The final speed is 15 m/s.

## Common mistakes

Many students think that acceleration is the same as speed, but a fast object can have no acceleration at all if the speed is steady. Another mistake is to think that a negative acceleration always means slowing down; if the velocity is also negative, a negative acceleration makes the object speed up in that direction. Finally, do not forget that turning at constant speed is also an acceleration, because the direction of the velocity changes.
