# Acceleration

Whenever velocity changes, there is acceleration. Pressing the gas pedal, the brake pedal and turning the wheel all make a car accelerate.

## Key ideas
- **Acceleration** = change in velocity / time: a = (v_f - v_i) / t. SI unit: m/s^2.
- Speeding up, slowing down and changing direction are all accelerations.
- Slowing down in the positive direction gives a **negative acceleration** (deceleration).
- Constant acceleration means the velocity changes by the same amount each second.
- Final velocity: v_f = v_i + a x t.
- Zero acceleration means constant velocity (or rest).

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Speeding up.** A car goes from 0 to 20 m/s in 5 s.
1. a = (20 - 0) / 5.
2. a = 4 m/s^2.

**Example 2: Braking.** A car slows from 25 m/s to 5 m/s in 4 s.
1. a = (5 - 25) / 4.
2. a = -5 m/s^2. The negative sign means it slows down.

**Example 3: Final speed.** A cart at 3 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s^2 for 6 s.
1. v_f = v_i + a t = 3 + 2 x 6.
2. v_f = 15 m/s.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking acceleration is the same as speed.
- Thinking a negative acceleration always means slowing down. It depends on the direction of the velocity.
- Forgetting that turning at constant speed is also acceleration.
