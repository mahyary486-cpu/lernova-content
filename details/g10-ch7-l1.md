# Area of Polygons

## Area formulas
| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | length x width |
| Square | side^2 |
| Parallelogram | base x height |
| Triangle | (1/2) x base x height |
| Trapezoid | (1/2) x (b1 + b2) x height |
| Rhombus or kite | (1/2) x d1 x d2 (diagonals) |
| Regular polygon | (1/2) x apothem x perimeter |
| Equilateral triangle | (square root of 3 / 4) x side^2 |

The **height** is always perpendicular to the base. The **apothem** of a regular polygon is the distance from its center to the middle of a side.

## Strategy for irregular shapes
Split the shape into rectangles, triangles and so on, then **add** areas. Or take a big shape and **subtract** the removed part.

## Coordinates
For a polygon on a grid you can use rectangles and triangles, or compute the area of a triangle with vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2), (x3, y3) with one half of the absolute value of x1(y2 - y3) + x2(y3 - y1) + x3(y1 - y2).

## Worked examples
1. A triangle with base 10 and height 6 has area (1/2) x 10 x 6 = **30**.
2. A trapezoid has bases 8 and 12 and height 5. Area = (1/2) x 20 x 5 = **50**.
3. A rhombus has diagonals 10 and 14. Area = (1/2) x 10 x 14 = **70**.
4. A regular hexagon has perimeter 36 and apothem about 5.2. Area = (1/2) x 5.2 x 36, about **93.6**.
5. A rectangle 12 by 9 has a 4 by 3 rectangular hole. Area = 108 - 12 = **96**.
6. An equilateral triangle with side 6 has area (square root of 3 / 4) x 36 = **9 times the square root of 3**, about 15.6.

## Common mistakes
- Using the slanted side as the height of a parallelogram or triangle.
- Forgetting the one half in triangle, trapezoid and rhombus formulas.
- Giving the answer in linear units instead of **square** units.
