# Chemical Properties

Paper can burn and iron can rust. Each is a chemical property because the substance changes into something new.

## Key ideas
- A **chemical property** describes the ability of a substance to change into a new substance.
- Examples: **flammability**, **reactivity with acid**, **reactivity with oxygen** (rusting), decomposing when heated.
- It is observed only when the substance **reacts**.
- A substance has the property even when it is not reacting.
- Chemical properties are different from physical properties such as color or density.
- Unreactive substances (noble gases) have few chemical properties.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Flammability.** Wood burns in air.
1. Burning changes wood into ash, gases and heat.
2. Flammability is a chemical property.

**Example 2: Rusting.** An iron nail rusts in moist air.
1. Iron reacts with oxygen and water to form rust.
2. Ability to rust is a chemical property of iron.

**Example 3: Telling properties apart.** Is the boiling point of water a physical or chemical property?
1. Boiling water changes only its state.
2. It is still water, so the boiling point is a physical property.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking color change always means a chemical property.
- Thinking a chemical property exists only while the reaction happens.
- Mixing up changes and properties.
