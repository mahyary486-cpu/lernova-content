# Predicting Products and Activity Series

To predict products, identify the reaction type, swap partners using correct charges, write correct formulas, and balance. For single and double replacement you must also decide whether the reaction actually happens.

## Key ideas
**Activity series of metals** (most to least reactive): Li, K, Ba, Ca, Na, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Ni, Sn, Pb, (H), Cu, Ag, Au.

**Single replacement rule.** A metal replaces another metal in a compound only if it is higher in the series. A metal above hydrogen replaces H from an acid; a metal below hydrogen, such as Cu, does not. If the free element is lower, there is no reaction.

**Double replacement rule.** The reaction occurs only if a product is a precipitate, a gas or water (a weak or nonionic product). Use solubility rules: nitrates and Group 1 salts are soluble; most chlorides are soluble except AgCl and PbCl2; most sulfates are soluble except BaSO4 and PbSO4. If all products stay dissolved, there is no reaction.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Mg + HCl**
1. Mg is above hydrogen, so a reaction occurs.
2. Mg forms Mg 2+ and pairs with Cl-: MgCl2. Hydrogen is released as H2.
3. Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2.

**Example 2: Al + CuCl2**
1. Al is above Cu, so Al replaces Cu.
2. Al 3+ with Cl- gives AlCl3.
3. Balance: 2Al + 3CuCl2 -> 2AlCl3 + 3Cu (6 Cl and 3 Cu on the right, 2 Al on each side).

**Example 3: BaCl2 + Na2SO4**
1. Swap partners: BaSO4 and NaCl.
2. BaSO4 is insoluble, so it is a precipitate and the reaction occurs.
3. BaCl2 + Na2SO4 -> BaSO4 + 2NaCl.

**Example 4: moles of CO2 from propane**
C3H8 + 5O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O. The mole ratio is 1 : 3, so 2 mol of C3H8 give 2 x 3 = 6 mol of CO2.

## Common mistakes
- Predicting a reaction when the free metal is lower in the activity series.
- Using wrong charges for the new formulas, such as AlCl2.
- Forgetting to balance after writing the products.
- Predicting a double replacement even though all products are soluble: KNO3 and NaCl mixed give no reaction.
