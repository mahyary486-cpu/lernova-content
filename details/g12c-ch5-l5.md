# Water Chemistry, Green Chemistry and Sustainability

Chemistry helps to clean water and, through green chemistry, to design processes that make less waste in the first place.

## Key ideas
- **Hard water**: Ca^2+, Mg^2+; **temporary** hardness removed by **boiling**.
- **Eutrophication**: nutrients -> algae -> oxygen depletion (**high BOD**).
- Water treatment: **coagulation, filtration, chlorination**.
- **Heavy metals** bioaccumulate.
- **Atom economy** = desired product mass / total reactant mass x 100%.
- Green chemistry: **less waste**, **safer solvents**, **catalysts**, **renewables**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Atom economy.** In C2H4 + H2O -> C2H5OH, the masses are 28 + 18 = 46 and the product is 46.
1. All of the reactant mass ends up in the product.
2. Atom economy = 46 / 46 x 100% = 100%.

**Example 2: Atom economy 2.** CaCO3 (100) -> CaO (56) + CO2 (44). Find the atom economy for CaO.
1. Desired product = 56 of 100 total.
2. Atom economy = 56%.

**Example 3: Temporary hardness.** How is temporary hardness removed?
1. Boiling decomposes Ca(HCO3)2.
2. CaCO3 precipitates and is removed.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing percent yield with atom economy.
- Thinking boiling removes permanent hardness.
- Forgetting that eutrophication depletes oxygen.
