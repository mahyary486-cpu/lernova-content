# Mammals

Mammals are warm-blooded vertebrates with hair or fur whose females feed their young with milk.

## Key ideas

- The main features of mammals are mammary glands that produce milk, hair or fur on the body, a constant body temperature (endothermy), a four-chamber heart, and lungs for breathing.
- A diaphragm is a sheet of muscle under the lungs that helps mammals breathe in and out.
- Hair or fur traps air and keeps the body warm. Many mammals also have a layer of fat under the skin that acts as insulation. Whales have almost no hair but have a thick fat layer called blubber.
- Mammals have different kinds of teeth: incisors for cutting, canines for tearing, and molars for grinding. The shape of the teeth depends on the diet. Carnivores have large canines, herbivores have broad flat molars, and rodents have long incisors that keep growing.
- Mammals have a large brain compared to their body size, and most show complex behavior. They learn from experience and many care for their young for a long time.
- There are three groups based on reproduction. Monotremes (such as the platypus) lay eggs. Marsupials (such as kangaroos) give birth to very small young that grow in a pouch. Placental mammals (such as humans, cats and whales) keep the young inside the mother's body, where an organ called the placenta passes food and oxygen from mother to baby until birth. Most mammals are placental.
- Mammals live in many habitats. Bats fly, whales and dolphins swim in the sea, moles dig under the soil, and camels live in deserts.

## Examples

- Cat and kitten: A mother cat gives birth to live kittens and feeds them with milk for several weeks. This shows two mammal features, live birth (placental) and milk. The kittens stay warm because of their fur and the warmth of their mother.
- Whale as a mammal: A whale lives in water and looks like a fish. But a whale breathes air with lungs through a blowhole, keeps a warm body temperature with blubber, and feeds its calf with milk. So a whale is a mammal and not a fish.
- Teeth and diet: A wolf has sharp canines and cutting teeth, which suit a meat diet. A sheep has flat molars, which grind grass. If you find a skull with large canines and no flat molars, you can conclude that the animal ate meat.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking all mammals give birth to live young. Monotremes such as the platypus lay eggs.
- Thinking whales and dolphins are fish. They breathe air and feed milk to their young.
- Thinking bats are birds. Bats have fur and feed milk to their young, so they are mammals.
- Thinking all mammals have a lot of hair. Whales and dolphins have very little.

## Remember

- Mammals: milk, hair or fur, warm-blooded, four-chamber heart, diaphragm.
- Three groups: monotremes, marsupials, placental mammals.
- Teeth show the diet of a mammal.
