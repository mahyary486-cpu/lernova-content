# Pyramids, Cones and Spheres

## Pyramids
A **pyramid** has one base and triangular faces meeting at the apex.
- **Volume V = (1/3) B h**, where h is the perpendicular height.
- **Lateral area (regular pyramid)** L = (1/2) x base perimeter x slant height.
- **Total surface area** S = L + B.

## Cones
- **Volume V = (1/3) pi r^2 h**
- **Lateral area L = pi r l**, where l is the **slant height**.
- **Total surface area S = pi r l + pi r^2**
- Relation: **l^2 = r^2 + h^2**.

## Spheres
- **Volume V = (4/3) pi r^3**
- **Surface area S = 4 pi r^2**
A hemisphere has half the volume of a sphere with the same radius.

## Worked examples
1. A square pyramid has base side 6 and height 8. V = (1/3) x 36 x 8 = **96**.
2. A cone has r = 3 and h = 4. The slant height is 5, V = (1/3) x pi x 9 x 4 = **12 pi**, and the total surface area is pi x 3 x 5 + pi x 9 = **24 pi**.
3. A sphere of radius 6 has V = (4/3) x pi x 216 = **288 pi**, and S = 4 x pi x 36 = **144 pi**.
4. A sphere has surface area 100 pi. Then 4 r^2 = 100, so r = **5**.
5. A cone and a cylinder have the same base and height. The cone's volume is **one third** of the cylinder's.
6. A regular square pyramid has base side 10 and slant height 13. Lateral area = (1/2) x 40 x 13 = **260**, and the total surface area is 260 + 100 = **360**.

## Common mistakes
- Using the slant height instead of the vertical height in volume formulas.
- Forgetting the factor one third.
- Mixing up the sphere formulas: volume has r^3, surface area has r^2.
