# Exponential Growth and Decay

## The model
An **exponential function** has the form **y = a x b^x**, where a is the starting value (the y-intercept) and b is the **growth factor**, with b > 0 and b not equal to 1.
- If b > 1, the function shows **exponential growth**.
- If 0 < b < 1, it shows **exponential decay**.

In a linear function, you add the same amount each step. In an exponential function, you multiply by the same factor each step.

## Percent change
- Growth by rate r (as a decimal): y = a(1 + r)^t. The factor is 1 + r.
- Decay by rate r: y = a(1 - r)^t. The factor is 1 - r.

A 5% increase has factor 1.05. A 20% decrease has factor 0.80.

## Compound interest
When money earns interest once a year, A = P(1 + r)^t, where P is the principal, r is the annual rate and t is in years.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** A town has 2,000 people and grows 3% per year. Find the population after 10 years.
1. Model: P = 2000(1.03)^t.
2. For t = 10: 1.03^10 is about 1.3439.
3. P is about 2000 x 1.3439 = 2,688 people.

**Example 2.** A car worth $20,000 loses 15% of its value each year. What is it worth after 3 years?
1. Factor is 1 - 0.15 = 0.85.
2. V = 20000(0.85)^3 = 20000 x 0.614125 = $12,282.50.

**Example 3.** Is y = 5(0.5)^x growth or decay? Find y at x = 3.
1. The factor 0.5 is between 0 and 1, so it is decay.
2. y = 5 x 0.125 = 0.625.

## Graph features
Growth graphs rise faster and faster. Decay graphs fall toward the x-axis but never touch it. All graphs of y = a x b^x pass through (0, a).

## Common mistakes
- Using the rate as the factor: 5% growth needs 1.05, not 0.05.
- Multiplying a by b before the exponent: 3(2)^4 is 3 x 16 = 48, not 6^4.
- Treating decay as subtracting a fixed amount each time.
- Forgetting that the factor for a 20% decrease is 0.8, not 0.2.
