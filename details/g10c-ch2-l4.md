# Ionization Energy and Electronegativity

Two trends describe how strongly atoms hold on to electrons: ionization energy and electronegativity.

## Key ideas
- **First ionization energy (IE1):** the energy needed to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom, A -> A+ + e-. It is usually given in kJ/mol.
- **Trend in IE:** it generally increases across a period (stronger nuclear pull, smaller radius) and decreases down a group (outer electron is farther away and shielded). Small dips occur from group 2 to 13 and from group 15 to 16.
- **Successive ionization energies:** each one is larger than the last. A huge jump shows that the next electron comes from an inner level, so it reveals the number of valence electrons.
- **Electronegativity (EN):** the ability of an atom to attract the shared electrons in a bond. It follows the same trend as IE, with fluorine the highest (4.0) and francium among the lowest. Noble gases are usually left out.
- **Bond type from the EN difference:** below about 0.5 is nonpolar covalent, 0.5 to 1.7 is polar covalent, and above 1.7 is ionic.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Energy for a sample.** IE1 of sodium is 496 kJ/mol. What energy is needed to ionize 0.250 mol of Na atoms?
1. Energy = moles x IE1.
2. 0.250 mol x 496 kJ/mol = 124 kJ.

**Example 2: Energy for one atom.** IE1 of lithium is 520 kJ/mol. Find the energy per atom.
1. Convert: 520 kJ = 5.20 x 10^5 J.
2. Divide by Avogadro's number: 5.20 x 10^5 / 6.022 x 10^23.
3. Energy = 8.63 x 10^-19 J per atom.

**Example 3: Bond type.** Decide the bond type in H2O (EN of H = 2.1, O = 3.5).
1. Difference = 3.5 - 2.1 = 1.4.
2. 1.4 is between 0.5 and 1.7, so the O-H bond is polar covalent.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up ionization energy (energy absorbed to remove an electron) with electron affinity (energy change when an electron is gained).
- Forgetting to convert kJ to J when working with a single atom.
- Saying that IE decreases across a period.
- Assuming noble gases have high electronegativity. They are normally given no value.
