# Linear Momentum

Momentum measures how hard it is to stop a moving object. A loaded truck is harder to stop than a bicycle at the same speed, and a fast bullet is hard to stop even though it is light. Momentum combines both mass and velocity.

## Key ideas
- **Definition:** momentum equals mass times velocity: **p = mv**.
- Momentum is a **vector**. It points in the same direction as the velocity, so signs matter: choose a positive direction and give leftward or downward motion a negative sign.
- SI unit: **kg m/s**.
- Newton's second law in momentum form: **F_net = delta p / delta t**. A net force is what changes momentum.
- Kinetic energy and momentum are linked: **KE = p^2 / (2m)**, so **p = sqrt(2 m KE)**.
- Doubling the mass doubles p. Doubling the speed doubles p but multiplies KE by 4.

## Example 1: Computing momentum
A 1500 kg car moves at 20 m/s. Find its momentum.
1. Write the formula: p = mv.
2. Substitute: p = 1500 kg x 20 m/s.
3. Result: p = 30000 kg m/s in the direction of travel.

## Example 2: Direction matters
A 0.50 kg cart moves left at 4.0 m/s. Take right as positive.
1. The velocity is v = -4.0 m/s.
2. p = 0.50 x (-4.0) = -2.0 kg m/s.
3. The negative sign tells us the momentum points left.

## Example 3: Momentum from kinetic energy
A 0.40 kg object has 50 J of kinetic energy. Find its momentum.
1. Use p = sqrt(2 m KE).
2. p = sqrt(2 x 0.40 x 50) = sqrt(40).
3. p = 6.3 kg m/s.

## Comparing objects
A 2000 kg truck at 10 m/s has p = 20000 kg m/s. A 1000 kg car at 25 m/s has p = 25000 kg m/s. The lighter car has more momentum because its speed is much greater. Neither mass nor speed alone decides it.

## Common mistakes
- Treating momentum as a scalar and ignoring direction.
- Confusing momentum (mv) with kinetic energy (1/2 mv^2) or with force (ma).
- Forgetting to convert units, such as km/h to m/s or grams to kilograms, before multiplying.
- Believing that two objects with equal speeds must have equal momentum.
