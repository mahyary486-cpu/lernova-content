# Centripetal Force and Acceleration

A car turning a corner needs a force pointing toward the inside of the curve. Without enough friction the car goes straight on.

## Key ideas
- **Centripetal acceleration**: **a_c = v^2 / r**, directed toward the center.
- **Centripetal force**: **F_c = m v^2 / r**, directed toward the center.
- It is **provided by** tension, gravity, friction or the normal force.
- Doubling the speed makes the force **4 times** larger; doubling the radius halves it.
- On a flat curve: **v_max = sqrt(mu g r)**.
- If the force disappears, the object moves in a **straight line** along the tangent.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Acceleration.** A car moves at 10 m/s on a circle of radius 50 m.
1. a_c = v^2 / r = 100 / 50.
2. a_c = 2 m/s^2.

**Example 2: Force.** A 2 kg ball moves at 3 m/s in a circle of radius 1 m.
1. F = m v^2 / r = 2 x 9 / 1.
2. F = 18 N.

**Example 3: Curve.** A car rounds a curve of radius 100 m with mu = 0.5 (g = 9.8 m/s^2).
1. v_max = sqrt(mu g r) = sqrt(0.5 x 9.8 x 100).
2. v_max = sqrt(490) = 22 m/s.

## Common mistakes
- Treating centripetal force as an extra force on a free-body diagram.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius.
- Thinking an outward force acts on the object.
