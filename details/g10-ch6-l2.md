# Inscribed Angles

## Definition
An **inscribed angle** has its vertex **on** the circle and its sides are chords. It intercepts an arc.

## Theorems
- **Inscribed Angle Theorem:** the measure of an inscribed angle is **half** the measure of its intercepted arc.
- Inscribed angles that intercept the **same arc** are congruent.
- An angle inscribed in a **semicircle** is a **right angle** (90 degrees).
- In a **cyclic quadrilateral** (inscribed in a circle), opposite angles are **supplementary** (add up to 180 degrees).

## Angles formed by two chords inside a circle
The angle equals **half the sum** of the arcs intercepted by the angle and its vertical angle.

## Worked examples
1. An arc is 110 degrees. An inscribed angle that intercepts it is **55 degrees**.
2. An inscribed angle is 38 degrees. Its intercepted arc is **76 degrees**.
3. Triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle and AB is a diameter. Angle C is **90 degrees**.
4. A cyclic quadrilateral has angles of 80 and 105 degrees. The opposite angles are 180 - 80 = **100** degrees and 180 - 105 = **75** degrees.
5. Two chords cross inside a circle, and they intercept arcs of 70 and 50 degrees. The angle is (70 + 50) / 2 = **60 degrees**.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to **halve** the arc for an inscribed angle (but a central angle equals its arc).
- Using the wrong arc. The intercepted arc is the one **inside** the angle.
- Assuming that all quadrilaterals in a circle have equal opposite angles. They are supplementary, not equal.
