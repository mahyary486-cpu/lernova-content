# Properties of Acids and Bases

Lemon juice is sour and soap is slippery. These everyday differences come from acids and bases.

## Key ideas
- **Acids** taste sour, turn blue litmus red, release **H+** in water and react with metals to give **hydrogen gas**.
- **Bases** taste bitter, feel slippery, turn red litmus blue and release **OH-** in water.
- Acids react with **carbonates** to give carbon dioxide, water and a salt.
- Both conduct electricity in solution.
- **Strong** acids and bases ionize almost completely; **weak** ones only partly.
- Never taste or touch unknown chemicals.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Identify.** Is vinegar an acid or a base?
1. Vinegar tastes sour and contains acetic acid.
2. It is a weak acid.

**Example 2: Metal and acid.** Zinc is placed in hydrochloric acid and bubbles form.
1. Acids react with metals to produce hydrogen gas.
2. The bubbles are hydrogen.

**Example 3: Ions.** Which ion is released by a base in water?
1. Bases release hydroxide ions.
2. The ion is OH-.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking all acids are dangerous. Many are weak and found in food.
- Thinking strong means concentrated.
- Testing an unknown substance by tasting it.
