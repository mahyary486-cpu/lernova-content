# Counting: Permutations and Combinations

## Fundamental Counting Principle
If one choice can be made in m ways and a second choice in n ways, the two choices together can be made in **m * n** ways.
- 3 shirts and 4 pants make 3 * 4 = **12** outfits.
- A license plate with 3 letters then 2 digits: 26 * 26 * 26 * 10 * 10 = **1,757,600** plates.

## Factorials
n! = n * (n - 1) * ... * 2 * 1. For example 6! = **720**. This is the number of ways to arrange n different objects in a row.

## Permutations (order matters)
The number of ways to arrange r objects chosen from n:
**nPr = n! / (n - r)!**
- 5P3 = 5 * 4 * 3 = **60**.
- Choosing a president, vice president and treasurer from 10 people: 10P3 = 10 * 9 * 8 = **720**.

## Combinations (order does not matter)
The number of ways to choose r objects from n:
**nCr = n! / (r! (n - r)!)**, which is nPr divided by r!.
- 5C3 = 60 / 6 = **10**.
- 8C2 = 8 * 7 / 2 = **28**.
- 10C4 = **210**.
- A 3-person committee from 7 people: 7C3 = **35**.

Note nCr = nC(n - r), so 8C6 = 8C2 = 28.

## Permutation or combination?
Ask: "Does the order matter?" A president and a vice president (different jobs) is a permutation. A committee of two (same job) is a combination.

## Arrangements with repeated letters
Divide by the factorial of each repeat. The letters of BOOK (two O's) can be arranged in 4! / 2! = **12** ways.

## Common mistakes
- Using permutations when order does not matter (the answer is too large).
- Forgetting to divide by r! in combinations.
- Not dividing for repeated letters.
