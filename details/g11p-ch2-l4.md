# Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle

Why does a steel ship float while a steel nail sinks? The shape decides how much water is displaced.

## Key ideas
- **Archimedes' principle**: **F_b = rho_fluid g V_displaced** = weight of displaced fluid.
- Sinks if **rho_object > rho_fluid**; floats if **rho_object < rho_fluid**.
- Floating: the buoyant force equals the object's **weight**.
- **Fraction submerged** = rho_object / rho_fluid.
- **Apparent weight** = true weight - buoyant force.
- A ship floats because its **shape** displaces a lot of water.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Buoyant force.** An object of volume 0.002 m^3 is fully under water (g = 9.8).
1. F_b = rho g V = 1000 x 9.8 x 0.002.
2. F_b = 19.6 N.

**Example 2: Floating block.** A wooden block floats with half of its volume submerged.
1. Fraction submerged = rho_wood / rho_water = 0.5.
2. rho_wood = 500 kg/m^3.

**Example 3: Apparent weight.** A rock weighs 30 N in air and 20 N in water.
1. The loss of weight equals the buoyant force.
2. F_b = 30 - 20 = 10 N.

## Common mistakes
- Using the object's volume above water as the displaced volume.
- Thinking heavy objects always sink.
- Thinking the buoyant force depends on the object's weight.
