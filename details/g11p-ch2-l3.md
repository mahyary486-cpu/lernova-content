# Pascal's Principle

A garage jack lets one person lift a car. A small force over a long distance becomes a large force over a short distance.

## Key ideas
- **Pascal's principle**: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is **transmitted equally** everywhere.
- **F1 / A1 = F2 / A2**, so **F2 = F1 (A2 / A1)**.
- The force is multiplied by the **area ratio**.
- The distances are in the **inverse ratio**: d1 / d2 = A2 / A1.
- Work in = work out (ideal machine); no energy is gained.
- Liquids are nearly **incompressible**, so hydraulics work well.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Hydraulic press.** Pistons have areas 0.01 m^2 and 0.5 m^2. A 100 N force acts on the small piston.
1. F2 = F1 x A2 / A1 = 100 x 0.5 / 0.01.
2. F2 = 5000 N.

**Example 2: Distance.** The large piston has 20 times the area of the small piston. The small piston moves 10 cm.
1. The volume moved is the same: A1 d1 = A2 d2.
2. d2 = 10 / 20 = 0.5 cm.

**Example 3: A jack.** Radii are 2 cm and 10 cm. What force lifts a 2500 N load?
1. The area ratio is (10 / 2)^2 = 25.
2. F1 = 2500 / 25 = 100 N.

## Common mistakes
- Using the radius ratio instead of the area ratio.
- Thinking a hydraulic machine creates energy.
- Forgetting that the distance moved is inversely proportional to the area.
