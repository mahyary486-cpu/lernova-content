# Kinetic and Potential Energy

A moving ball can knock over a pin, and a ball held high can fall and do work. Both have energy: one because of motion, the other because of position.

## Key ideas
- **Energy** is the ability to do work. Unit: joule (J).
- **Kinetic energy** is energy of motion: **KE = 1/2 m v^2**.
- Doubling the speed makes KE **four times** larger.
- **Gravitational potential energy**: **PE = m g h**.
- Other stored energy: **chemical** (food, fuel, batteries) and **elastic** (stretched springs).
- Mass in kg, speed in m/s and height in m give energy in joules.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Kinetic energy.** A 4 kg object moves at 3 m/s.
1. KE = 1/2 m v^2 = 0.5 x 4 x 3^2.
2. KE = 0.5 x 4 x 9 = 18 J.

**Example 2: Potential energy.** A 2 kg object is 10 m above the ground (g = 9.8 m/s^2).
1. PE = m g h = 2 x 9.8 x 10.
2. PE = 196 J.

**Example 3: Doubling the speed.** A car doubles its speed.
1. KE depends on v^2.
2. 2^2 = 4, so the kinetic energy becomes four times larger.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to square the speed in KE.
- Forgetting the 1/2 in the kinetic energy formula.
- Measuring height from different reference levels without noticing.
