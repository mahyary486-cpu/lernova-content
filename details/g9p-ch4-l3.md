# Kinetic and Potential Energy

A moving ball can knock down a pin, and a ball held high above the ground can fall and do work when it is released. Both have energy, but they have it for different reasons: one because it moves, and the other because of its position. Energy is one of the most important ideas in all of science.

## Key ideas

Energy is the ability to do work, and it is measured in joules, the same unit as work. Energy exists in many forms, and it is useful to separate them into energy of motion and stored energy.

Kinetic energy is the energy that an object has because it is moving. It is calculated with KE = 1/2 x m x v^2, where m is the mass in kilograms and v is the speed in meters per second. The speed is squared, which has a big effect: if the speed doubles, the kinetic energy becomes four times larger, and if the speed triples, it becomes nine times larger. This is why a fast car is much more dangerous in a crash than a slow one, and why the stopping distance grows quickly with speed.

Gravitational potential energy is the energy stored because of an object's height above a chosen reference level. It is calculated with PE = m x g x h, where h is the height in meters. The higher the object is, the more energy it has stored. The height is always measured from a reference level that you choose, such as the floor or the ground, and the same reference must be used throughout one problem.

There are also other kinds of stored energy. Chemical energy is stored in food, fuel and batteries. Elastic energy is stored in a stretched spring or rubber band. All of them can be transformed into motion or heat.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: Kinetic energy.** A 4 kg object moves at 3 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?
1. Use KE = 1/2 x m x v^2.
2. First square the speed: 3^2 = 9.
3. Then calculate 0.5 x 4 x 9 = 18. The kinetic energy is 18 J.

**Example 2: Potential energy.** A 2 kg object is 10 m above the ground. What is its gravitational potential energy? Use g = 9.8 m/s^2.
1. Use PE = m x g x h.
2. Substitute: PE = 2 x 9.8 x 10.
3. The potential energy is 196 J.

**Example 3: Doubling the speed.** A car doubles its speed. What happens to its kinetic energy?
1. Kinetic energy depends on the square of the speed.
2. If the speed is doubled, the factor is 2^2 = 4.
3. The kinetic energy becomes four times larger.

## Common mistakes

Students often forget to square the speed in the kinetic energy formula, or they forget the factor 1/2. Both mistakes change the answer a lot. Another error is to measure heights from different reference levels in the same problem without noticing. Finally, check that the mass is in kilograms, the speed in meters per second and the height in meters, so the energy comes out in joules.
