# Alkenes, Alkynes and Aromatic Compounds

Unsaturated compounds are more reactive because the double or triple bond can open up and add atoms.

## Key ideas
- **Alkenes**: **CnH2n**, C=C; **alkynes**: **CnH2n-2**, C-triple bond-C.
- **Addition** reactions: with **H2**, **Br2**, **HBr**, **H2O**.
- **Bromine water** is **decolorized** by alkenes.
- **Cis-trans isomers** arise from restricted rotation around C=C.
- **Benzene C6H6**: delocalized ring; **substitution** rather than addition.
- Number the chain to give the **double bond** the lowest number.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Addition.** Complete: CH2=CH2 + Br2 ->
1. The double bond opens and each C gets one Br.
2. CH2Br-CH2Br (1,2-dibromoethane).

**Example 2: Formula.** How many hydrogens are in an alkene with 5 carbons?
1. CnH2n with n = 5.
2. C5H10, so 10 hydrogens.

**Example 3: Test.** How can you tell an alkene from an alkane?
1. Add bromine water.
2. The alkene decolorizes it; the alkane does not.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking benzene undergoes addition easily.
- Forgetting cis-trans isomers.
- Using the alkane formula for alkenes.
