# Radical Expressions

## Rules for radicals
For non-negative a and b:
- **Product rule:** sqrt(ab) = sqrt(a) * sqrt(b)
- **Quotient rule:** sqrt(a / b) = sqrt(a) / sqrt(b)
- (sqrt(a))^2 = a

## Simplifying
Find the largest perfect square (or perfect cube for cube roots) inside the radical.
- sqrt(72) = sqrt(36 * 2) = **6 sqrt(2)**
- sqrt(3) * sqrt(12) = sqrt(36) = **6**
- The cube root of 54 = the cube root of (27 * 2) = **3 (cube root of 2)**
- (sqrt(5))^2 = **5**

## Adding and subtracting
Only **like radicals** (same number under the root) can be combined. Simplify first.
sqrt(50) + sqrt(18) = 5 sqrt(2) + 3 sqrt(2) = **8 sqrt(2)**.

## Multiplying
Use FOIL and the product rule.
(2 + sqrt(3))(2 - sqrt(3)) = 4 - 3 = **1**. This is a difference of squares, and the radicals cancel.

## Rationalizing the denominator
We do not leave a radical in the denominator.
- One term: multiply the top and bottom by the radical. 6 / sqrt(3) = 6 sqrt(3) / 3 = **2 sqrt(3)**.
- Two terms: multiply by the **conjugate**. 1 / (sqrt(5) - 2): multiply by (sqrt(5) + 2) / (sqrt(5) + 2). The bottom is 5 - 4 = 1, so the answer is **sqrt(5) + 2**.

## Common mistakes
- Writing sqrt(a + b) = sqrt(a) + sqrt(b). That is false.
- Adding unlike radicals, such as sqrt(2) + sqrt(3), into one radical.
- Forgetting to simplify before adding.
- Multiplying only the top by the conjugate. You must multiply both top and bottom.
