# Thermal Energy, Temperature and Heat

A cup of hot tea has a higher temperature than a bathtub of warm water, but the bathtub holds much more thermal energy.

## Key ideas
- **Temperature** = average kinetic energy of particles. **Thermal energy** = total energy of all particles.
- **Heat** is energy transferred from hot to cold.
- **Conduction** (contact), **convection** (moving fluid), **radiation** (waves, no medium needed).
- **Q = m c delta T**, with c in J/(kg C). Water: c = 4200 J/(kg C).
- Substances with a **high specific heat** (water) warm up and cool down slowly.
- Metals are good **conductors**; air and wood are good **insulators**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Heating water.** How much heat raises 2 kg of water by 5 C (c = 4200 J/kg C)?
1. Q = m c delta T = 2 x 4200 x 5.
2. Q = 42,000 J.

**Example 2: Specific heat.** A 0.5 kg metal absorbs 4500 J and warms by 10 C.
1. c = Q / (m delta T) = 4500 / (0.5 x 10).
2. c = 900 J/(kg C).

**Example 3: Mixing water.** 100 g at 80 C is mixed with 100 g at 20 C.
1. Equal masses of the same liquid share the heat equally.
2. The final temperature is the average: 50 C.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing heat with temperature.
- Forgetting to use kilograms in Q = m c delta T.
- Thinking cold flows from one object to another. Heat flows from hot to cold.
