# Newton's First Law: Inertia

Why does a passenger lurch forward when a bus brakes? The bus slows down, but the passenger keeps moving forward because of inertia.

## Key ideas
- **Newton's first law**: an object keeps its state of motion unless a net force acts on it.
- At rest stays at rest; moving keeps the same **velocity** (same speed and direction).
- **Inertia** is the resistance of an object to a change in its motion.
- **Mass** measures inertia: a bowling ball has more inertia than a tennis ball.
- Things stop on Earth because of **friction and air resistance**, not because motion needs a force.
- Seat belts protect you by providing the force that stops you with the car.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Braking bus.** A bus stops suddenly. Why do standing passengers lean forward?
1. The passengers were moving forward with the bus.
2. No force acts on them to stop them, so they keep moving forward.
3. This is inertia.

**Example 2: Constant velocity.** A hockey puck slides at constant velocity across smooth ice.
1. Constant velocity means no acceleration.
2. So the net force on the puck is almost zero.

**Example 3: Comparing inertia.** Which is harder to start moving: a 2 kg book or a 20 kg box?
1. Inertia depends on mass.
2. The 20 kg box has more mass, so it has more inertia and is harder to start.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking a force is needed to keep an object moving.
- Mixing up inertia with a force. Inertia is a property, not a force.
- Thinking heavier objects have no inertia in space.
