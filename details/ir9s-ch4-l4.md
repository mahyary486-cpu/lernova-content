# Motion graphs

Motion graphs show how position or velocity changes with time, so we can read motion from a picture.

## Key ideas

- In a distance-time graph, time is on the horizontal axis and distance (or position) is on the vertical axis.
- A horizontal line on a distance-time graph means the object is at rest, because the distance does not change.
- A straight sloping line means constant speed. A steeper line means a higher speed.
- The slope of a distance-time graph equals the speed: speed = change in distance / change in time.
- A curved line means the speed is changing, so the object is accelerating.
- In a velocity-time graph, time is on the horizontal axis and velocity is on the vertical axis.
- A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means constant velocity, and zero acceleration.
- A straight sloping line means constant acceleration. Upward means speeding up, and downward means slowing down.
- The slope of a velocity-time graph equals the acceleration: a = change in velocity / change in time.
- The area between the line and the time axis on a velocity-time graph equals the displacement. For a rectangle, area = height × width. For a triangle, area = ½ × base × height.

## Examples

**Example 1: Speed from a distance-time graph.** A line goes straight from the point (0 s, 0 m) to the point (10 s, 50 m).
- Slope = (50 − 0) / (10 − 0) = 5 m/s.
- Answer: the object moves at a constant speed of 5 m/s.

**Example 2: Acceleration and distance from a velocity-time graph.** The velocity rises in a straight line from 0 m/s to 12 m/s in 4 s.
- Acceleration = slope = (12 − 0) / 4 = 3 m/s².
- The shape under the line is a triangle: area = ½ × 4 × 12 = 24 m.
- Answer: a = 3 m/s² and displacement = 24 m.

**Example 3: A graph with three parts.** A car's velocity goes up from 0 to 8 m/s in the first 4 s, stays at 8 m/s from 4 s to 10 s, and then falls to 0 at 12 s.
- Part 1 (triangle): ½ × 4 × 8 = 16 m.
- Part 2 (rectangle): 6 s × 8 m/s = 48 m.
- Part 3 (triangle): ½ × 2 × 8 = 8 m.
- Total displacement = 16 + 48 + 8 = 72 m.
- Acceleration in part 3 = (0 − 8) / 2 = −4 m/s².

## Common mistakes

- Reading a horizontal line on a velocity-time graph as "at rest". It means constant velocity, and it is at rest only if the line lies on the time axis.
- Mixing up the two kinds of graph. Slope means speed on a distance-time graph, but acceleration on a velocity-time graph.
- Reading the height of a point as the slope. Slope is a ratio of changes, found by dividing.
- Forgetting the units. The area of a velocity-time graph is in metres, and the slope is in m/s².

## Remember

- Distance-time graph: slope = speed.
- Velocity-time graph: slope = acceleration, area = displacement.
- Flat line: at rest on a distance-time graph, constant velocity on a velocity-time graph.
