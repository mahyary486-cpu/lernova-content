# Fluid Flow and Bernoulli's Principle

Faster flow means lower pressure. This single idea explains how aircraft fly and how a perfume sprayer works.

## Key ideas
- **Continuity**: **A1 v1 = A2 v2**; flow rate **Q = A v** (m^3/s).
- A narrower pipe means a **faster** flow.
- **Bernoulli**: **P + 1/2 rho v^2 + rho g h = constant**.
- Horizontal flow: **faster speed = lower pressure**.
- **Torricelli**: speed from a hole at depth h: **v = sqrt(2 g h)**.
- Lift on a wing: air moves faster over the top, so the pressure is lower there.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Continuity.** A pipe narrows from 0.04 m^2 to 0.01 m^2. The speed in the wide part is 2 m/s.
1. A1 v1 = A2 v2, so v2 = 0.04 x 2 / 0.01.
2. v2 = 8 m/s.

**Example 2: Torricelli.** A hole is 5 m below the water surface (g = 9.8).
1. v = sqrt(2 g h) = sqrt(2 x 9.8 x 5).
2. v = sqrt(98) = 9.9 m/s.

**Example 3: Pressure change.** Water at 2 m/s and 150,000 Pa enters a narrow section where it moves at 6 m/s.
1. P2 = P1 + 1/2 rho (v1^2 - v2^2) = 150,000 + 500 x (4 - 36).
2. P2 = 134,000 Pa.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking faster flow means higher pressure.
- Using diameters instead of areas in the continuity equation.
- Forgetting that Bernoulli's equation applies along a streamline.
