# Scalars and Vectors

Some quantities need only a number and a unit. Others also need a direction. Physics sorts them into scalars and vectors, and the difference changes how we add and compare them.

## Key ideas
- A **scalar** has magnitude (size) only: distance, speed, time, mass, temperature, energy.
- A **vector** has magnitude and direction: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force.
- Distance is the total path length (scalar). Displacement is the straight-line change in position from start to finish (vector).
- Speed is distance / time (scalar). Velocity is displacement / time (vector).
- In diagrams a vector is an arrow. Its length shows the magnitude and its pointing shows the direction.
- Two vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and direction. The vector -A has the same magnitude as A but points the opposite way.
- Multiplying a vector by a positive number changes its magnitude only. Multiplying by -1 reverses it.
- In one dimension a plus or minus sign gives the direction, for example +20 m/s east and -20 m/s west.
- For perpendicular vectors, the magnitude is found with the Pythagorean theorem and the direction angle with tan(theta) = opposite / adjacent.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: perpendicular displacements.** A student walks 6.0 m east and then 8.0 m north.
1. Distance = 6.0 m + 8.0 m = 14.0 m.
2. Displacement magnitude = sqrt(6.0^2 + 8.0^2) = sqrt(100) = 10.0 m.
3. Direction: theta = tan^-1(8.0 / 6.0) = 53 degrees north of east.

**Example 2: a round trip.** A jogger runs once around a 400 m track and stops at the starting line in 80 s.
1. Distance = 400 m.
2. Displacement = 0 m, because the final position equals the starting position.
3. Average speed = 400 m / 80 s = 5.0 m/s, but average velocity = 0 m / 80 s = 0 m/s.

**Example 3: back and forth.** A hiker walks 5.0 km east and then 8.0 km west.
1. Distance = 5.0 km + 8.0 km = 13.0 km.
2. Take east as positive: displacement = +5.0 km + (-8.0 km) = -3.0 km.
3. The hiker ends 3.0 km west of the start.

## Common mistakes
- Saying displacement and distance are the same. Displacement can be smaller than the distance, or zero.
- Treating a negative sign as "slower". It only shows direction.
- Calling speed a vector. Speed has no direction.
- Adding perpendicular vectors by simple addition (6 + 8 = 14) instead of using the Pythagorean theorem.
