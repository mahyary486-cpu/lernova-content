# Natural and synthetic polymers

This topic explains what polymers are and compares polymers found in nature with those made in factories.

## Key ideas

A polymer is a very large molecule made of many small repeating units joined in a long chain. The small units are called monomers. The process of joining monomers is called polymerization. The word comes from "poly" (many) and "mer" (part).

Natural polymers are made by living things:
- Starch and cellulose: polymers of glucose (C6H12O6). Starch stores energy in plants; cellulose gives plant cell walls their strength and is the main part of cotton and wood.
- Proteins: polymers of amino acids. Examples are silk, wool, hair and muscle.
- DNA: a polymer of units called nucleotides that stores genetic information.
- Natural rubber: a polymer collected as latex from rubber trees.

Synthetic polymers are made by people, mostly from chemicals obtained from crude oil:
- Polyethylene: made from ethene (C2H4). Used in bags and bottles.
- PVC (polyvinyl chloride): used in pipes and cables.
- Polystyrene: used in packaging and foam cups.
- Nylon and polyester (PET): used in clothes, ropes and drink bottles.
- Teflon: used for non-stick pans.

Properties of polymers: Many are light, cheap, flexible, waterproof, and poor conductors of heat and electricity. These properties make them useful. Many synthetic polymers are not broken down by microbes, so they stay in the environment for a long time. This is a problem for waste, so we use recycling, reuse and reduce plastic use.

Natural fibers (cotton, wool, silk) are comfortable but may be costly. Synthetic fibers (nylon, polyester) are strong and cheap, but they do not absorb water as well as cotton.

## Examples

Example 1: Polyethylene from ethene.
- Monomer: ethene, C2H4. Molar mass = 2 × 12 + 4 × 1 = 28 g/mol.
- If a chain has 1000 monomer units, its molar mass is 1000 × 28 = 28 000 g/mol.
- A single polymer molecule is therefore far heavier than a monomer, which is why polymers form solids.

Example 2: Choosing a polymer for a task.
- A water pipe must be strong, waterproof and must not rust. PVC fits these needs better than a natural polymer such as cotton or wood.
- A shirt for hot weather should absorb sweat. Cotton, a natural polymer, is better than nylon.

Example 3: Starch and glucose.
- A starch molecule can contain thousands of glucose units.
- If a short piece has 500 glucose units, we can say about 500 monomers were joined.
- When we digest starch, the body breaks it back into glucose, and the cells use this glucose for energy.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that all polymers are plastics. Wool, DNA and cellulose are natural polymers.
- Thinking that all plastics are the same. Different polymers have different properties and uses.
- Mixing up monomer and polymer. The monomer is the small unit; the polymer is the long chain.
- Believing that synthetic polymers rot quickly in soil. Most do not.

## Remember

- A polymer is a long chain of many monomers.
- Natural polymers: starch, cellulose, proteins, DNA, natural rubber. Synthetic polymers: polyethylene, PVC, nylon and others.
- Reduce, reuse and recycle plastics to protect the environment.
