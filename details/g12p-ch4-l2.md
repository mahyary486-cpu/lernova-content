# Refraction, Lenses and Optical Instruments

Glasses, cameras and microscopes all use the thin-lens equation.

## Key ideas
- **Snell**: **n1 sin(theta1) = n2 sin(theta2)**.
- **1 / f = 1 / d_o + 1 / d_i**; **m = -d_i / d_o**.
- Converging lens: **f > 0**; diverging lens: **f < 0**.
- **Power P = 1 / f** (diopters; f in meters).
- Lenses in contact: **P = P1 + P2**.
- Short sight: **diverging** lens; long sight: **converging** lens.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Image from a lens.** An object is 20 cm from a lens with f = 10 cm.
1. 1 / d_i = 1 / 10 - 1 / 20 = 1 / 20.
2. d_i = 20 cm; m = -1: real, inverted, same size.

**Example 2: Lens power.** A lens has f = 25 cm.
1. f = 0.25 m.
2. P = 1 / 0.25 = 4 diopters.

**Example 3: Diverging lens.** An object is 30 cm from a lens with f = -15 cm.
1. 1 / d_i = -1 / 15 - 1 / 30 = -3 / 30.
2. d_i = -10 cm: a virtual, upright, smaller image.

## Common mistakes
- Using centimeters for diopters.
- Forgetting the negative f for a diverging lens.
- Mixing up near-sighted and far-sighted corrections.
