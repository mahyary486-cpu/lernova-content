# Newton's third law

Newton's third law says that forces always come in pairs: when one object pushes on another, the second object pushes back on the first.

## Key ideas

- Newton's third law: for every action force there is a reaction force that is equal in size and opposite in direction.
- The two forces of a pair act on two different objects. This is the most important point of the law.
- The two forces of a pair are the same type of force and they act at the same time.
- Action and reaction forces do not cancel each other, because they act on different objects. Only forces on the same object can balance.
- Examples of pairs:
  - A swimmer pushes the water backward, and the water pushes the swimmer forward.
  - A rocket pushes hot gas downward, and the gas pushes the rocket upward.
  - Your foot pushes the ground backward when you walk, and the ground pushes you forward.
  - A book pushes down on a table, and the table pushes up on the book.
- Equal forces do not always give equal accelerations. By the second law, a = F / m, so the object with the smaller mass has the larger acceleration.

## Examples

**Example 1: Two skaters.** Two skaters stand on smooth ice. Skater A has a mass of 60 kg and skater B has a mass of 40 kg. A pushes B with a force of 120 N.
- By the third law, B pushes A with 120 N in the opposite direction.
- Acceleration of A: a = F / m = 120 N / 60 kg = 2 m/s^2.
- Acceleration of B: a = F / m = 120 N / 40 kg = 3 m/s^2.
- The forces are equal, but the lighter skater accelerates more.

**Example 2: A falling apple.** The Earth pulls an apple of mass 1 kg downward with a force of about 10 N.
- The reaction force is that the apple pulls the Earth upward with 10 N.
- The apple accelerates at about 10 m/s^2 because its mass is small.
- The Earth has a huge mass, so its acceleration is far too small to notice.

**Example 3: Jumping from a boat.** A person jumps forward from a small boat on still water.
- The person pushes the boat backward.
- By the third law, the boat pushes the person forward with an equal force.
- The boat moves backward because of this push. This is why the boat can drift away from the dock.

**Example 4: A balloon.** When air rushes out of a balloon backward, the air pushes the balloon forward. The balloon moves in the direction opposite to the escaping air.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that action and reaction cancel. They act on different objects, so they cannot cancel.
- Thinking the bigger object pushes harder. In a pair, the two forces are always equal in size.
- Thinking the reaction comes after the action. Both forces exist at the same time.
- Mixing up a reaction pair with two balanced forces on one object, such as weight and the support force on a book at rest.

## Remember

- Forces come in pairs, equal in size and opposite in direction.
- The two forces of a pair act on different objects.
- Equal forces can cause different accelerations when the masses are different.
