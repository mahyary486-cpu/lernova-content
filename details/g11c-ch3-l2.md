# Solubility and the Factors Affecting It

Soda goes flat when warm or open because less gas can stay dissolved at higher temperature or lower pressure.

## Key ideas
- **Like dissolves like**.
- Most solids: **higher T = higher solubility**; gases: **higher T = lower solubility**.
- **Henry's law**: **S = k P**.
- Soluble: **nitrates**, **Group 1 salts**; usually insoluble: **carbonates**, **hydroxides** (except Group 1).
- **Saturated**, **unsaturated** and **supersaturated** solutions.
- A **precipitate** forms when ions combine into an insoluble compound.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Henry's law.** A gas has S = 0.02 mol/L at 1.0 atm. What is S at 3.0 atm?
1. S is proportional to P.
2. S = 0.02 x 3 = 0.06 mol/L.

**Example 2: Precipitate.** Does mixing AgNO3 and NaCl form a precipitate?
1. The ions swap: AgCl and NaNO3.
2. AgCl is insoluble, so it precipitates.

**Example 3: Temperature.** Why does soda go flat faster when warm?
1. Gas solubility decreases with temperature.
2. So the gas escapes more easily.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking all solids dissolve better when hot.
- Confusing saturated and concentrated.
- Forgetting Henry's law is for gases.
