# Angles, Radians and Degrees

An angle is formed by rotating a ray, called the terminal side, away from a fixed ray, called the initial side. In pre-calculus we measure angles in two units: degrees and radians.

## Key ideas
- **Standard position:** vertex at the origin, initial side along the positive x-axis. Counterclockwise rotation is positive and clockwise is negative.
- **Radian:** one radian is the angle at the center of a circle that cuts off an arc equal in length to the radius. A full turn is 360 degrees = 2pi radians, so 180 degrees = pi radians.
- **Conversions:** degrees to radians, multiply by pi/180. Radians to degrees, multiply by 180/pi.
- **Coterminal angles** share a terminal side. Add or subtract 360 degrees (or 2pi) any number of times.
- **Arc length:** s = r*theta, with theta in radians.
- **Sector area:** A = (1/2) r^2 theta, with theta in radians.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Convert.** Change 150 degrees to radians, and 7pi/4 to degrees.
1. 150 x pi/180 = 5pi/6.
2. 7pi/4 x 180/pi = 7 x 45 = 315 degrees.

**Example 2: Coterminal angles.** Find one positive and one negative angle coterminal with 400 degrees.
1. 400 - 360 = 40 degrees (positive).
2. 40 - 360 = -320 degrees (negative).
3. For radians, -pi/3 + 2pi = 5pi/3 is coterminal with -pi/3.

**Example 3: Arc and sector.** A circle has radius 6 and a central angle of pi/3.
1. Arc length: s = 6 x pi/3 = 2pi.
2. Sector area: A = (1/2)(36)(pi/3) = 6pi.

## Useful benchmarks
30 degrees = pi/6, 45 degrees = pi/4, 60 degrees = pi/3, 90 degrees = pi/2, 270 degrees = 3pi/2.

## Common mistakes
- Using degrees in s = r*theta or A = (1/2) r^2 theta. Convert to radians first.
- Multiplying by 180/pi when you should multiply by pi/180.
- Forgetting that a negative angle turns clockwise.
- Thinking coterminal angles must differ by 180 degrees. They differ by full turns of 360 degrees.
