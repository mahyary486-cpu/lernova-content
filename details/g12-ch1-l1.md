# Increasing, Decreasing and Extrema

## Increasing and decreasing
A function f is **increasing** on an interval if its graph rises from left to right: whenever x1 < x2, then f(x1) < f(x2). It is **decreasing** if the graph falls: whenever x1 < x2, then f(x1) > f(x2). It is **constant** if the outputs do not change.
We describe these **intervals of x-values**, using open intervals at turning points.

Example: f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3 has its vertex at (2, -1) and opens up.
- Decreasing on **(-infinity, 2)**.
- Increasing on **(2, infinity)**.

Example: f(x) = |x - 1| is decreasing on (-infinity, 1) and increasing on **(1, infinity)**.

Example: f(x) = x^3 is **increasing on its entire domain**. f(x) = x^2 and f(x) = |x| are not.

## Maximum and minimum values (extrema)
- **Absolute maximum:** the largest output on the whole domain.
- **Absolute minimum:** the smallest output on the whole domain.
- **Local (relative) maximum:** an output larger than all nearby outputs. A "peak" of the graph.
- **Local (relative) minimum:** an output smaller than all nearby outputs. A "valley".

Example: f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3 has an absolute minimum of **-1** at x = 2 and no maximum.
Example: f(x) = -x^2 + 4 has an absolute maximum of **4** at x = 0.

## Turning points of polynomials
Look at f(x) = x^3 - 3x. Some values: f(-2) = -2, f(-1) = 2, f(0) = 0, f(1) = -2, f(2) = 2.
The graph rises to a **local maximum at x = -1** (value 2), falls to a **local minimum at x = 1** (value -2), then rises again. A polynomial of degree n has at most n - 1 turning points, so a quartic has at most **3**.

## Using the idea
If f is increasing and f(1) = 5, then f(2) must be greater than 5 and f(0) must be less than 5.

## Common mistakes
- Giving the intervals as y-values. Increasing and decreasing are described with **x-values**.
- Saying the maximum is the x-value. The maximum value is the **output** (y), and it occurs at an x.
- Confusing a local maximum with the absolute maximum. A function can have several local extrema.
