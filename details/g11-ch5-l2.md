# Exponential Growth, Decay and the Number e

## Growth and decay models
**y = a(1 + r)^t** for growth and **y = a(1 - r)^t** for decay, where
- a is the starting amount,
- r is the rate per period (as a decimal),
- t is the number of periods.

Examples:
- A town of 5000 grows 4% a year. After t years: **P = 5000(1.04)^t**. After 3 years: 5000 * 1.124864 = about **5624**.
- A car worth $20,000 loses 15% a year. **V = 20000(0.85)^t**. After 2 years: 20000 * 0.7225 = **$14,450**.
- A population doubles every 5 years: **P = P0 * 2^(t/5)**.
- A substance has a half-life of 10 years. After 30 years, 3 half-lives have passed, so (1/2)^3 = **1/8** remains.

## Compound interest
**A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)**
- P: principal, r: annual rate (decimal), n: times compounded per year, t: years.
Example: $1000 at 6% compounded monthly for 2 years: A = 1000(1 + 0.06/12)^24 = 1000(1.005)^24, about **$1127.16**.

## Continuous compounding and e
The number **e ≈ 2.71828** is an irrational number. It is the limit of (1 + 1/n)^n as n gets very large.
**A = P e^(rt)** gives continuous growth.
Example: $500 at 4% compounded continuously for 5 years: A = 500 e^(0.2), about **$610.70**.

**Natural exponential function:** f(x) = e^x. Its graph passes through (0, 1) and has horizontal asymptote y = 0.

## Doubling time
For y = a * 2^(t/d), d is the doubling time. For 100 bacteria that double every 3 hours, after 9 hours there are 100 * 2^3 = **800**.

## Worked example
An account holds $2000 and grows 5% per year. After 4 years: 2000(1.05)^4 = 2000 * 1.21550625, about **$2431.01**.

## Common mistakes
- Using r = 5 instead of r = 0.05.
- For decay, writing 1 + r instead of 1 - r.
- Forgetting that t must match the period (monthly rates need months).
- Treating e as a variable. It is a constant.
