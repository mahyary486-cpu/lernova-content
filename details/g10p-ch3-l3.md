# Newton's Second Law

Newton's second law links force, mass and acceleration. It lets us predict exactly how an object moves when we know the forces on it.

## Key ideas
- **Second law:** the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass: a = F_net / m, or F_net = ma.
- The acceleration points in the same direction as the net force.
- Units: 1 N = 1 kg x 1 m/s^2.
- Doubling the net force doubles the acceleration. Doubling the mass halves it.
- Problem method: (1) draw a free-body diagram, (2) choose a positive direction, (3) add the forces with signs to get F_net, (4) apply F_net = ma.
- For several objects pushed together, treat them as one system with total mass to find a, then apply F = ma to a single part to find the force between them.
- Combine with kinematics: a = (change in velocity) / time.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: car acceleration.** A 800 kg car has a net forward force of 2400 N.
1. a = F_net / m = 2400 / 800.
2. a = 3.0 m/s^2 in the direction of the force.

**Example 2: pushing against friction.** A 25 kg box is pushed with 100 N across a floor with 30 N of friction.
1. Net force = 100 - 30 = 70 N.
2. a = 70 / 25 = 2.8 m/s^2.

**Example 3: force from a change in speed.** A 1000 kg car slows from 20 m/s to rest in 5 s.
1. a = (0 - 20) / 5 = -4 m/s^2.
2. F_net = ma = 1000 x (-4) = -4000 N.
3. The magnitude is 4000 N, directed opposite to the motion.

## Common mistakes
- Using the applied force instead of the net force. Subtract friction and other opposing forces first.
- Dividing mass by force, giving a = m / F. Mass belongs in the denominator.
- Thinking velocity points along the net force. Acceleration does, velocity need not.
- Forgetting units. Convert grams to kilograms before using F = ma.
