# Reptiles

Reptiles are vertebrates with dry, scaly skin that breathe air with lungs and are well adapted to life on land.

## Key ideas

- Lizards, snakes, turtles, tortoises and crocodiles are reptiles.
- Reptiles are ectothermic. They often warm themselves in the sun and cool down in shade or in burrows. Because they do not need extra food to heat their bodies, they eat much less than birds or mammals of the same size.
- The skin is dry and covered with hard scales made of keratin, the same protein found in our nails. The scales greatly reduce water loss, so reptiles can live in dry places such as deserts. Snakes and lizards shed their outer skin as they grow.
- Reptiles breathe with lungs for their whole lives. Their lungs have more folds inside than amphibian lungs, so they take in oxygen more efficiently. They do not breathe through the skin.
- Most reptiles have a three-chamber heart. Crocodiles have a four-chamber heart.
- Fertilization is internal. The male puts sperm inside the female. Most reptiles lay eggs with leathery shells on land. The shell and the membranes inside protect the embryo from drying out and hold a food supply (the yolk). Because of this, reptiles do not need water to reproduce. A few snakes and lizards give birth to live young.
- Most reptiles do not look after their eggs. Sea turtles bury their eggs in sand and return to the sea. Crocodiles are an exception because they guard their nests.
- Many reptiles are predators. Snakes may use venom to kill prey. Lizards often eat insects. Many tortoises eat plants.
- Reptiles help control the number of rodents and insects, so they are useful in ecosystems.

## Examples

- Lizard in a desert: A desert lizard has thick scales that reduce water loss. In the cool morning it lies on a warm rock until its body temperature rises, and at midday it hides under a stone. It behaves like this because it cannot make its own body heat.
- Sea turtle nesting: A female turtle comes onto a beach and digs a hole in the sand. She lays about 100 eggs, covers them and returns to the sea. The eggs have shells, so the embryos can develop on land without drying out. The warmth of the sand helps them develop.
- Comparing food needs: A python can survive for weeks between meals, but a mammal of the same mass needs food every day. A reptile uses less energy because it does not spend food energy to keep its body warm.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking reptiles are slimy. Their skin is dry, not slimy.
- Thinking a snake's skin is cold because it is not alive. A reptile's temperature simply changes with its surroundings.
- Thinking all reptiles lay eggs in water. Reptile eggs are laid on land.
- Mixing up reptiles and amphibians. Amphibians have moist skin and gelatinous eggs; reptiles have scaly skin and shelled eggs.

## Remember

- Reptiles: dry scaly skin, lungs, internal fertilization, shelled eggs on land, ectothermic.
- Their adaptations let them live far from water.
- They control their body temperature by their behavior, for example basking in the sun.
