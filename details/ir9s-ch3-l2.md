# The carbon cycle

The carbon cycle is the movement of carbon atoms between the air, living things, water, soil and rocks.

## Key ideas

Carbon is the main building element of living things. Carbon is found in the air as carbon dioxide (CO2), in organisms as sugars, fats and proteins, in fossil fuels, and in rocks such as limestone (CaCO3). The amount of CO2 in air is small, about 0.04 percent, but it is very important.

Processes that take CO2 out of the air:
- Photosynthesis: plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to change CO2 and water into glucose and oxygen.
 6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2
- Dissolving: some CO2 dissolves in ocean water. Shell-forming animals use it to build shells of calcium carbonate.

Processes that put CO2 into the air:
- Respiration: living cells break down glucose to get energy. C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O
- Decay: decomposers break down dead organisms and release CO2.
- Combustion: burning wood, coal, oil and natural gas releases CO2. For example, CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O
- Volcanoes also release CO2.

Carbon can stay out of the air for a very long time. Dead organisms buried for millions of years may become coal, oil or gas. When people burn these fuels, they release carbon that was stored for a long time. This adds extra CO2 to the air and strengthens the greenhouse effect, which is one cause of global warming. Forests and oceans take in CO2, so protecting forests helps to keep the cycle balanced.

## Examples

Example 1: CO2 from burning methane. Methane (CH4) burns: CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O. Find the mass of CO2 from 16 g of methane.
Step 1: Molar mass of CH4 = 12 + 4 × 1 = 16 g/mol.
Step 2: Moles of CH4 = 16 g ÷ 16 g/mol = 1 mol.
Step 3: The equation gives 1 mol CO2 for 1 mol CH4, so 1 mol CO2 forms.
Step 4: Molar mass of CO2 = 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 g/mol, so mass = 1 × 44 = 44 g.
The burning of 16 g of methane makes 44 g of CO2.

Example 2: CO2 used in photosynthesis. How much CO2 is needed to make 180 g of glucose?
Step 1: Molar mass of C6H12O6 = 6 × 12 + 12 × 1 + 6 × 16 = 180 g/mol.
Step 2: Moles of glucose = 180 ÷ 180 = 1 mol.
Step 3: The equation shows 6 mol CO2 for 1 mol glucose.
Step 4: Mass of CO2 = 6 × 44 = 264 g.
So 264 g of CO2 are needed.

Example 3: A forest and a car. A growing forest takes CO2 from the air through photosynthesis and stores carbon in wood. A car engine burns gasoline and sends CO2 into the air. Planting trees can help to balance some of the CO2 from vehicles, but it cannot replace the need to burn less fuel.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking that only animals release CO2. Plants also respire and release CO2, especially at night.
- Thinking that photosynthesis and respiration are the same reaction. They are opposite processes.
- Saying that burning fuels creates carbon. Carbon atoms are not created; the fuel already contains them.
- Forgetting to use molar masses when calculating masses of gases.

## Remember

- Photosynthesis removes CO2 from air; respiration, decay and combustion add it.
- Burning fossil fuels moves carbon from underground storage into the air.
- Carbon atoms are conserved: they change place and form, but they are not lost.
