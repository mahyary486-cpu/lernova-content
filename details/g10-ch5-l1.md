# Pythagorean Theorem

## The theorem
In a right triangle with legs **a** and **b** and hypotenuse **c** (the side opposite the right angle):

**a^2 + b^2 = c^2**

The hypotenuse is always the **longest** side.

## Pythagorean triples
Whole numbers that fit the theorem: **3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25**. Multiples also work (6-8-10, 9-12-15, 10-24-26).

## Converse and classification
Let c be the longest side of a triangle.
| If | Then the triangle is |
|---|---|
| a^2 + b^2 = c^2 | right |
| a^2 + b^2 > c^2 | acute |
| a^2 + b^2 < c^2 | obtuse |

## Worked examples
1. Legs 6 and 8: c^2 = 36 + 64 = 100, so the hypotenuse is **10**.
2. Hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5: b^2 = 169 - 25 = 144, so the other leg is **12**.
3. A ladder 10 feet long leans against a wall with its base 6 feet from the wall. It reaches the square root of (100 - 36) = **8 feet** up.
4. The diagonal of a rectangle 9 by 12 is the square root of (81 + 144) = square root of 225 = **15**.
5. The distance from (1, 2) to (4, 6): the legs are 3 and 4, so the distance is **5**.
6. Sides 7, 9, 12: 49 + 81 = 130 and 12^2 = 144. Since 130 < 144, the triangle is **obtuse**.

## Common mistakes
- Putting a leg in the place of the hypotenuse. c is always opposite the right angle.
- Forgetting to take the square root at the end.
- Using the theorem for a triangle that does not have a right angle.
