# Right Triangle Trigonometry

## The three ratios
For an acute angle θ in a right triangle, with sides named from θ's point of view:
- **sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse**
- **cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse**
- **tan θ = opposite / adjacent**

A memory aid is **SOH-CAH-TOA**. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle.

## Example: the 3-4-5 triangle
Angle A has opposite side 3, adjacent side 4 and hypotenuse 5.
- sin A = **3/5**
- cos A = **4/5**
- tan A = **3/4**

## Special triangles
**45-45-90 triangle** (sides 1, 1, sqrt(2)):
- sin 45° = cos 45° = **sqrt(2)/2**, and tan 45° = **1**.

**30-60-90 triangle** (sides 1, sqrt(3), 2; the side 1 is opposite 30°):
| Angle | sin | cos | tan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | 1/2 | sqrt(3)/2 | sqrt(3)/3 |
| 45° | sqrt(2)/2 | sqrt(2)/2 | 1 |
| 60° | sqrt(3)/2 | 1/2 | sqrt(3) |

## Finding a missing side
Choose the ratio that uses the side you know and the side you want.
- Hypotenuse 10, angle 30°. The opposite side is 10 * sin 30° = **5**.
- A tower is 50 ft away and the angle of elevation to its top is 30°. Height = 50 * tan 30° = 50 * sqrt(3)/3, about **28.9 ft**.
- A 20 ft ladder makes a 60° angle with the ground. It reaches 20 * sin 60° = 10 sqrt(3), about **17.3 ft** up the wall.

## Cofunctions
In a right triangle the two acute angles add up to 90°, so **sin θ = cos(90° - θ)**. For example sin 20° = **cos 70°**.

## Finding an angle
Use the inverse functions. If tan θ = 1, then θ = arctan(1) = **45°**.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up opposite and adjacent. They change depending on which angle you use.
- Using tan when the hypotenuse is involved.
- Using a calculator in the wrong mode (degrees or radians).
