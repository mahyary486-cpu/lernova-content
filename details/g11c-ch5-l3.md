# Activation Energy and the Arrhenius Equation

A small change in temperature can change a rate a lot because only particles with energy above Ea react.

## Key ideas
- **k = A e^(-Ea / RT)**.
- Higher **Ea** = slower; higher **T** = faster.
- **ln k = ln A - Ea / (R T)**: slope of ln k vs 1/T is **-Ea / R**.
- **ln(k2 / k1) = (Ea / R)(1 / T1 - 1 / T2)**.
- The **transition state** is at the top of the energy barrier.
- A catalyst lowers **Ea** for both directions.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Slope.** A plot of ln k against 1 / T has a slope of -6000 K (R = 8.31).
1. Slope = -Ea / R.
2. Ea = 6000 x 8.31 = 49,860 J/mol = 49.9 kJ/mol.

**Example 2: Effect of Ea.** Which reaction is faster at the same T: Ea = 40 kJ or Ea = 80 kJ?
1. A lower Ea gives a larger k.
2. The reaction with Ea = 40 kJ is faster.

**Example 3: Profile.** In an exothermic reaction, where are the products compared with the reactants?
1. delta H is negative.
2. The products are at a lower energy than the reactants.

## Common mistakes
- Using Celsius in the Arrhenius equation.
- Forgetting that Ea must be in joules with R = 8.31.
- Thinking a catalyst changes delta H.
