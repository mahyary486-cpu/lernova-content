# Uniform Circular Motion

A car on a circular track, a point on a spinning wheel and the Moon around Earth all move in circles. They share the same simple relations between radius, period and speed.

## Key ideas
- **Uniform circular motion**: constant **speed**, but the **velocity changes** because its direction changes.
- Velocity is always **tangent** to the circle.
- **Period T**: time for one revolution. **Frequency f = 1 / T** (hertz).
- **Speed**: **v = 2 pi r / T = 2 pi r f**.
- **Angular speed** omega = 2 pi / T (rad/s); **v = omega r**.
- One revolution = 2 pi radians = 360 degrees.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Speed from the period.** An object goes around a circle of radius 5 m once every 10 s.
1. v = 2 pi r / T = 2 x 3.14 x 5 / 10.
2. v = 3.14 m/s.

**Example 2: Frequency.** A wheel's period is 0.5 s.
1. f = 1 / T = 1 / 0.5.
2. f = 2 Hz.

**Example 3: Lap speed.** A runner completes a 400 m circular lap in 80 s.
1. v = distance / time = 400 / 80.
2. v = 5 m/s.

## Common mistakes
- Saying the velocity is constant because the speed is constant.
- Forgetting to use 2 pi r for the circumference.
- Mixing up period and frequency.
