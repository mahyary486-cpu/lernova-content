# Logistic Growth and Newton's Law of Cooling

## Logistic growth
Real populations cannot grow forever. A **logistic function** starts like exponential growth, then levels off at a **carrying capacity** L:

**P(t) = L / (1 + A e^(-kt))**

- L is the carrying capacity (the upper limit).
- A and k control the starting value and the speed.
- The graph is S-shaped. It grows fastest at about **half** the carrying capacity.

Example: P(t) = 1000/(1 + 9e^(-0.5t)).
- P(0) = 1000/(1 + 9) = **100**.
- As t gets large, e^(-0.5t) approaches 0, so P approaches **1000**.
- The horizontal asymptote y = 1000 is the carrying capacity.

## Newton's Law of Cooling
An object cools (or warms) toward the temperature of its surroundings:

**T(t) = Ts + (T0 - Ts) e^(-kt)**

- Ts: the surrounding temperature.
- T0: the starting temperature.
- k: the cooling constant.
As t grows, T approaches **Ts**.

Example: coffee at 90 degrees C in a 20 degree room, k = 0.1 per minute.
T(t) = 20 + 70 e^(-0.1t).
- After 10 minutes: 20 + 70 e^(-1) = 20 + 25.75 = about **45.8 degrees**.
- When is T = 50? 30 = 70 e^(-0.1t), so e^(-0.1t) = 3/7 and t = ln(7/3)/0.1, about **8.5 minutes**.

## Radioactive decay and half-life
**N(t) = N0 (1/2)^(t/h)**, where h is the half-life. Carbon-14 has h = 5730 years, so after 11,460 years (2 half-lives) the fraction left is (1/2)^2 = **1/4**.

## Choosing a model
- Exponential growth: keeps growing at a constant percent. Good for short periods.
- Logistic: growth that is limited by resources.
- Exponential decay toward a limit (cooling): use a temperature difference.

## Common mistakes
- Using the starting temperature T0 instead of the difference T0 - Ts in the cooling formula.
- Forgetting that the logistic function never exceeds L.
- Mixing up the half-life with the time to lose everything.
