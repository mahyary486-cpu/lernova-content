# Classifying Matter

Everything around you is made of matter, from air to water to rock. Scientists sort matter into groups to understand its behavior.

## Key ideas
- **Matter** has mass and takes up space.
- A **pure substance** has a fixed composition: it is an **element** or a **compound**.
- An **element** has one kind of atom (gold, oxygen). A **compound** is chemically combined elements (water, salt).
- A **mixture** is two or more substances not chemically combined; the parts keep their properties.
- **Homogeneous** mixtures look the same throughout; **heterogeneous** mixtures do not.
- Mixtures are separated by **physical methods**: filtering, distillation, evaporation, magnets.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Sorting examples.** Classify gold, water, air and muddy water.
1. Gold is an element.
2. Water is a compound.
3. Air is a homogeneous mixture and muddy water is a heterogeneous mixture.

**Example 2: Separating sand and water.** How can sand be separated from water?
1. Sand does not dissolve, so it can be caught in a filter.
2. Filtration separates the sand from the water.

**Example 3: Salt water.** How can pure water be obtained from salt water?
1. Boil the salt water.
2. The steam is collected and cooled; this is distillation.

## Common mistakes
- Calling a mixture a compound.
- Thinking a compound keeps the properties of its elements.
- Thinking air is a single pure substance.
