# Acids and Bases in Everyday Life

From the sour taste of a lemon to the cleaning of a kitchen, acids and bases are part of daily life, and so are the safety rules for handling them.

## Key ideas
- **Antacids** are bases that neutralize excess stomach acid.
- **Soaps and cleaners** are basic; **bleach** is a strong base.
- **Lime** (a base) neutralizes acidic soil.
- **Acid rain** comes from sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from burning fuels.
- **Safety**: add acid slowly to water; wear goggles; never taste chemicals.
- Many foods contain weak acids: citric (fruit), acetic (vinegar), lactic (yogurt).

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Indigestion.** Why does an antacid tablet help indigestion?
1. The stomach has too much acid.
2. The tablet is a base that neutralizes some of it.

**Example 2: Acid rain.** What causes acid rain?
1. Burning fuels releases sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
2. They dissolve in rain and make acids.

**Example 3: Soil.** A farmer's soil is too acidic.
1. Add a base to neutralize it.
2. Lime (calcium hydroxide or carbonate) is used.

## Common mistakes
- Adding water to concentrated acid instead of acid to water.
- Thinking all cleaners are acids.
- Thinking rainwater is neutral.
