# Diffraction, Polarization and Resolution

Polarizing sunglasses cut glare because light reflected from water and roads is partly polarized, and the lenses block that direction. This lesson looks at three more wave properties of light. Diffraction is the spreading of waves when they pass an opening or an edge, polarization is the direction of the vibrations, and resolution is the limit set by diffraction on how finely any instrument can see details.

## Key ideas

Diffraction occurs when a wave passes through an opening comparable in size to its wavelength. A single narrow slit of width a produces a central bright band with dark fringes on both sides. The dark fringes, called minima, appear at angles given by a sin(theta) = m lambda, with m = 1, 2, 3 and so on. A narrower slit gives a wider pattern, which is a surprising result: squeezing the light spreads it out more.

A diffraction grating is a surface with a very large number of equally spaced slits, such as 500 lines per millimeter. It gives sharp bright maxima at the angles d sin(theta) = m lambda, where the spacing is d = 1 / N, the reciprocal of the number of lines per unit length. Because each wavelength is deflected by a different angle, a grating separates light into its colors and is used in spectrometers to analyze light from stars and lamps.

Polarization shows that light is a transverse wave, because only transverse waves can be polarized. In unpolarized light the electric field vibrates in all directions perpendicular to the beam, and a polarizing filter lets through only one direction. When polarized light of intensity I0 passes a second polarizer whose axis is at angle theta to the plane of polarization, the transmitted intensity follows Malus's law: I = I0 cos^2(theta). At 0 degrees all the light passes, and at 90 degrees none passes. Sound waves in air are longitudinal, so they cannot be polarized.

Diffraction also limits resolution. Light from a point source passing through a circular aperture of diameter D spreads into a small disc, and two objects can be seen as separate only if their angular separation is larger than the Rayleigh criterion: theta = 1.22 lambda / D. A larger aperture and a shorter wavelength give better resolution, which is why large telescopes see finer details and why electron microscopes, with their tiny wavelengths, see much smaller things than light microscopes.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: A grating.** A grating has 500 lines per millimeter. Find the angle of the first-order maximum for light of 600 nm.
1. The spacing is d = 1 / 500 000 m = 2.0 x 10^-6 m.
2. sin(theta) = m lambda / d = 6.0 x 10^-7 / 2.0 x 10^-6 = 0.30.
3. The angle is theta = 17.5 degrees.

**Example 2: Malus's law.** Polarized light of intensity 8.0 W/m^2 passes a polarizer whose axis is at 60 degrees to the plane of polarization. Find the transmitted intensity.
1. Use I = I0 cos^2(theta) = 8.0 x cos^2(60) = 8.0 x 0.50^2.
2. The result is I = 8.0 x 0.25.
3. The transmitted intensity is 2.0 W/m^2.

**Example 3: Resolution.** A telescope of diameter 0.20 m observes light of 500 nm. Find the smallest resolvable angle.
1. Use theta = 1.22 lambda / D = 1.22 x 5.0 x 10^-7 / 0.20.
2. The numerator is 6.1 x 10^-7.
3. The angle is theta = 3.05 x 10^-6 rad.

## Common mistakes

A common error is to use the number of lines per millimeter directly as the slit spacing; the spacing is its reciprocal, in meters. Another is to forget to square the cosine in Malus's law. Students also think that a narrower slit gives a narrower pattern, but it gives a wider one. Finally, remember that sound waves cannot be polarized, because they are longitudinal.
