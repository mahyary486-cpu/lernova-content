# Diffraction, Polarization and Resolution

Polarizing sunglasses cut glare because reflected light is partly polarized.

## Key ideas
- **Single slit minima**: **a sin(theta) = m lambda**.
- **Grating maxima**: **d sin(theta) = m lambda**, d = 1 / N.
- **Polarization** proves light is **transverse**.
- **Malus**: **I = I0 cos^2(theta)**.
- **Rayleigh**: **theta = 1.22 lambda / D**.
- Larger **aperture** gives better resolution.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Grating.** A grating has 500 lines per mm. Light of 600 nm: find the first-order angle.
1. d = 1 / 500,000 = 2.0 x 10^-6 m.
2. sin(theta) = lambda / d = 0.3, so theta = 17.5 degrees.

**Example 2: Malus's law.** Polarized light of intensity 8 W/m^2 passes a polarizer at 60 degrees to its plane.
1. I = I0 cos^2(60) = 8 x 0.25.
2. I = 2 W/m^2.

**Example 3: Resolution.** A telescope of diameter 0.2 m observes light of 500 nm.
1. theta = 1.22 lambda / D = 1.22 x 5 x 10^-7 / 0.2.
2. theta = 3.05 x 10^-6 rad.

## Common mistakes
- Using lines per millimeter as the spacing.
- Forgetting to square the cosine.
- Thinking sound waves can be polarized.
