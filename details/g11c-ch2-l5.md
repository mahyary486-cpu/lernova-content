# Diffusion, Effusion and Real Gases

The smell of perfume spreads across a room because of diffusion, and helium balloons deflate faster than air-filled ones because helium effuses faster.

## Key ideas
- **Diffusion**: spreading; **effusion**: escaping through a small hole.
- **Graham**: **rate1 / rate2 = sqrt(M2 / M1)**.
- Lighter gases are **faster**.
- **v_rms = sqrt(3 R T / M)** (M in kg/mol).
- Real gases deviate at **high P** and **low T**.
- The **van der Waals** equation corrects for particle volume and attractions.

## Worked examples
**Example 1: Graham's law.** How much faster does H2 (2 g/mol) effuse than O2 (32 g/mol)?
1. rate(H2) / rate(O2) = sqrt(32 / 2) = sqrt(16).
2. H2 is 4 times faster.

**Example 2: Unknown gas.** A gas effuses 2 times slower than methane CH4 (16 g/mol).
1. rate(CH4) / rate(x) = 2 = sqrt(M / 16), so M / 16 = 4.
2. M = 64 g/mol.

**Example 3: Real gases.** When does a real gas behave least ideally?
1. Attractions and particle volume matter most when particles are close.
2. At high pressure and low temperature.

## Common mistakes
- Putting the molar masses the wrong way up in Graham's law.
- Using grams per mole in the rms speed formula.
- Thinking real gases are ideal at high pressure.
