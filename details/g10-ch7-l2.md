# Prisms and Cylinders

## Prisms
A **prism** has two parallel, congruent **bases** joined by **lateral faces** (parallelograms, rectangles for a right prism).

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Volume | **V = B x h** (base area times height) |
| Lateral area | **L = P x h** (base perimeter times height) |
| Total surface area | **S = L + 2B** |

For a rectangular box with length l, width w and height h: V = l w h and S = 2(lw + lh + wh).
A cube with side s: V = s^3 and S = 6 s^2.

## Cylinders
A **cylinder** has two parallel circular bases.
- **V = pi r^2 h**
- Lateral area **L = 2 pi r h**
- Total surface area **S = 2 pi r h + 2 pi r^2**

## Cavalieri's principle
Two solids with the same height and equal cross-section areas at every level have the **same volume**. This is why an oblique prism has the same volume formula as a right prism.

## Worked examples
1. A box 5 by 4 by 3 has volume **60** and surface area 2(20 + 15 + 12) = **94**.
2. A triangular prism has a base triangle with base 6 and height 4, and prism height 10. B = 12, so V = **120**.
3. A cylinder with r = 3 and h = 10 has V = pi x 9 x 10 = **90 pi**, about 282.7.
4. The same cylinder has S = 2 x pi x 3 x 10 + 2 x pi x 9 = 60 pi + 18 pi = **78 pi**, about 245.0.
5. A cube has volume 64, so its side is 4 and its surface area is **96**.
6. A cylinder has volume 200 and radius 4. Its height is 200 / (16 pi), about **3.98**.

## Common mistakes
- Using the slanted edge instead of the perpendicular height.
- Forgetting the two bases in total surface area.
- Writing volume in square units. Volume uses **cubic** units.
