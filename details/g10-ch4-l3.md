# Triangle Similarity: AA, SSS, SAS

## Shortcuts for similar triangles
| Shortcut | What you need |
|---|---|
| **AA** | two pairs of angles congruent (the third pair is then automatically equal) |
| **SSS~** | all three pairs of sides proportional |
| **SAS~** | two pairs of sides proportional and the **included** angles congruent |

## Notes
- AA is the quickest and is used most often.
- For SSS~ and SAS~ the ratios of the corresponding sides must be **equal**.
- Similar triangles have equal angles, but the sides do not have to be equal. That is the difference from congruence.

## Indirect measurement
Objects and their shadows at the same time of day form similar right triangles (the sun's angle is the same). Use a proportion to find a height you cannot measure directly.

## Worked examples
1. Triangle ABC has angles 40 and 60 degrees. Triangle DEF has angles 60 and 80 degrees. The third angle of ABC is 80 and the third angle of DEF is 40. Two angles match, so they are similar by **AA**.
2. Triangle 1 has sides 4, 6, 8. Triangle 2 has sides 6, 9, 12. The ratios are 6 / 4 = 1.5, 9 / 6 = 1.5, 12 / 8 = 1.5. They are similar by **SSS~**.
3. A person 6 feet tall casts a shadow 4 feet long. At the same time a tree casts a shadow 20 feet long. Then 6 / 4 = h / 20, so h = 6 x 20 / 4 = **30 feet**.
4. In triangle ABC and triangle ADE, angle A is shared, AD / AB = 1 / 2 and AE / AC = 1 / 2. Two sides are proportional and the included angle is the same, so they are similar by **SAS~**.
5. A 10 foot pole casts a 15 foot shadow. A building casts a 90 foot shadow. The building is 10 x 90 / 15 = **60 feet** tall.

## Common mistakes
- Using SSA or "one angle and one side" as a similarity shortcut. They do not work.
- Using SAS~ when the angle is not **between** the two proportional sides.
- Writing the similarity statement with the vertices in the wrong order.
